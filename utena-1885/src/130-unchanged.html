XIII

What did not change

The limits of a book, stated plainly

A novel can change what people take for granted, give a movement words and pictures, and tip a decision that was already close. It cannot stop a war, close a mill, or move votes that money and armies have already decided. Most of modern Japanese history runs the same way in this timeline as in ours, and the divergences only make sense against it.

The empire and its wars

Taiwan in 1895, Korea in 1910, Manchuria in 1931, China in 1937, Pearl Harbor in 1941, Hiroshima and Nagasaki in 1945: all happen as they did. documented for our history; inference that the serial changes none of them. The forces behind Japanese expansion were strategic, economic and ideological, and a girls' novel touches none of them. Its readers were, if anything, a little more likely than other people to oppose the wars, and a little more likely to be harassed for it. That is all. inference

The throne

The constitution of 1889, the Rescript on Education, the cult of the emperor in the schools, the lèse-majesté prosecutions: unchanged. The Imperial House Law of 1889 still restricts the succession to men; the serial's first effect on the law, as Part VI argued, was to give conservatives another reason to write it that way. inference The question returns only in 2005 (Part XI).

The mills and the quarters

The women who made Meiji Japan rich, the silk reelers and cotton spinners who were the majority of the country's factory workers, lived in this timeline as they did in ours: on contracts signed by their fathers, in locked dormitories, on twelve-hour shifts, dying of tuberculosis at rates the government knew and did not publish. documented (Part XVIII) Utena reached them late, read aloud, and as entertainment. The Factory Act of 1911 and its slow enforcement are the same. inference So is licensed prostitution, which lasted until 1956–58 in both timelines; the serial's world of girls' schools did not reach the brothel districts, and its readers' reformist energy went, as in our history, into the Christian temperance and purity societies. inference

The household

The Civil Code of 1898 is enacted in this timeline as in ours, and its household system governs Japanese families until 1947. What changes is one part of it, the wife's legal incapacity, abolished in 1931 instead of 1947 (Part IX), and the way the rest of it is talked about. The household head, primogeniture, the father's control of marriage before thirty and twenty-five, the double standard on adultery: all stay. inference

The political machine

The parties, the oligarchs and later the bureaucracy and the army decided Japan's politics, and in the post-war decades one party did. A cultural change moves votes at the margin, and in this timeline it moved enough for women's local suffrage to pass the Peers in 1931, which had been a close vote. It does not move the Liberal Democratic Party's factions, its rural base or its money. In this timeline as in ours the LDP governs for most of the years after 1955; in both, Japan's first woman prime minister takes office only in 2025. choice That is the hardest of this document's convergences to defend, and it is defended in Part XI.

The canon around it

Most of the books, plays and pictures of our timeline exist in this one. Takekurabe, Seitō, Flower Tales, the Takarazuka Revue, Princess Knight, The Rose of Versailles: all are made, by the same people, at roughly the same time. choice The counterfactual convention followed here is that people born after 1885 are still born and still do their work, unless the serial gives them a strong reason to do something else. Strictly, a change in the past changes which children are born; following that rule would empty the twentieth century of everyone we know, which would make the history unreadable and teach nothing. This document keeps the people and lets the book act on them.

Utena itself

The strangest convergence is the one the premise makes inevitable. In 1997, in this timeline as in ours, TV Tokyo broadcasts a thirty-nine-episode animated Revolutionary Girl Utena from April to December. documented for our history; choice for this one. In ours it is an original work. In this one it is an adaptation of a Meiji classic, and the most faithful one ever made (Part XI).
