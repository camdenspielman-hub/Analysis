XI

Chronicle, 1989–2026

The adaptation, the succession, the certificate, a prime minister, and the house law again

The last thirty-seven years bring the two timelines closer than at any time since 1885, and one legal change pulls them apart again. The book's afterlife in this period is mostly a matter of adaptation and scholarship, and the most famous adaptation is one that exists in our timeline too, under a different description.

1989–1999The faithful adaptation

1990s. The women elected in the "Madonna boom" do not last; the Socialists collapse in the 1990s and the LDP returns. In 1996 the government's Legislative Council recommends letting married couples keep separate surnames, and the Diet never acts on it; in 2015 and again in 2021 the Supreme Court upholds the single-surname rule and leaves the question to the Diet. documented In this timeline both happen too. inference The first Tokyo Lesbian and Gay Parade is held in 1994. documented In this timeline its women's contingent carries roses, and its men's, lilies (Part X). choice

2 April 1997. TV Tokyo broadcasts the first of thirty-nine weekly episodes of an animated Revolutionary Girl Utena, made by J.C.Staff for a group calling itself Be-Papas, directed by Ikuhara Kunihiko with series composition by Enokido Yōji, characters by Saitō Chiho, and choruses for the duels by J.A. Seazer, who had written music for Terayama Shūji's underground theatre in the 1970s. It runs until 24 December. documented In our history it is an original story. In this one it is an adaptation of the Meiji classic, and the first episode goes out on the hundred and twelfth anniversary, to the day, of chapter 1. choice

Critics in this timeline call it the most faithful adaptation the book has ever had, and they are right in a way that only readers of this document can fully appreciate: it follows the serial chapter by chapter, keeps every name and every unexplained thing, and takes a single liberty, the one every adapter since 1918 had taken. It brings the machines up to date. Akio's carriage becomes a red sports car; the magic lantern on the dome becomes a planetarium projector; the family register in chapter 31 becomes a chart of blood types; the phonograph becomes a tape recorder; the prize-riddle column becomes a late-night radio phone-in. choice Part IV's concordance, read from right to left, is a list of the adaptation's changes. inference Older viewers complained that the car spoiled the Carriage Book, since a man who drove his own carriage in 1886 was a spectacle and a man who drives himself in 1997 is a commuter. The defenders answered that the car, which in the series mostly drives nowhere, is as useless and as dangerous as the carriage, and the argument has not ended. choice

What the makers said about the series in our history belongs to our history, and this document does not invent their words for the other one. choice Some of what they said reads strangely from here. The group's name, Be-Papas, was glossed by its makers as "let's become adults". documented (makers' statement) The director, asked in 2000 what it meant to revolutionise the world, answered that it means "to break the eggshell". documented (makers' statement) In a timeline in which those words had been printed in Tokyo in 1885, the creed of the chick and the egg would need no explanation, and the series' debts to Takarazuka and to the underground theatre of the 1960s, which its makers acknowledged, would be debts to two of the book's own descendants. inference

17–29 December 1997. A stage musical of Utena plays at the Hakuhinkan Theatre in Tokyo with an all-female cast, in the manner of Takarazuka. documented In this timeline it is the 1,000th-odd stage version since November 1885, and the first to use the anime's songs. choice In August 1999 the director's feature film, Adolescence of Utena, opens in cinemas; it retells the story differently, as the series' makers chose. documented In this timeline it is the first film version to depart from the book's plot, and it is attacked for exactly that. choice

2000–2015The succession and the certificate

2004–2006. No boy has been born to the imperial family since 1965. In December 2004 Prime Minister Koizumi sets up an advisory panel on the Imperial House Law, and on 24 November 2005 it recommends opening the throne to women and to their descendants. The government prepares a bill. In February 2006 Princess Kiko's pregnancy is announced and the bill is dropped; her son Hisahito is born in September. documented In this timeline the question returns after a hundred and sixteen years with its old argument reversed. In 1886 the men who struck the female line out of the house law had not needed to say that a female succession would tell girls raised on Utena that they could be princes; the newspapers had said it for them. The supporters of 2005 say that every girl in Japan has read Utena at school for fifty years, and that the country has survived it. choice The outcome is the same. inference A birth decided it in both timelines, and no novel could have.

5 November 2015. Shibuya ward in Tokyo issues Japan's first certificate recognising a same-sex partnership, to Higashi Koyuki and Masuhara Hiroko. Higashi had played men's roles in Takarazuka's Flower Troupe in 2005–06. documented In both timelines the first official recognition of a couple in Japan went to a woman who had been trained to play the prince. In this one, nobody thinks it a coincidence. inference

2016–2026The courts, a prime minister, and the house law

In our history, from February 2019 couples sue the state in five cities for the right to marry. District and high courts mostly find the present law unconstitutional, or in a "state of unconstitutionality"; in March 2025 the Osaka High Court becomes the fifth high court of five to say so; in November 2025 the Tokyo High Court, in a second suit, becomes the first to uphold the law. On 25 March 2026 the Supreme Court refers all six cases to its Grand Bench. As of October 2026 it has not ruled, and Japan has neither same-sex marriage nor civil unions. documented

In this timeline the courts get there sooner, and the Diet moves. The suits are filed in 2012; the Grand Bench rules in June 2023 that the Civil Code's failure to provide any form of legal union for same-sex couples violates Articles 14 and 24; and in 2024 the Diet passes a partnership law that gives registered couples most of the rights of marriage under another name. choice It is the second largest divergence in this document, and the case for it:

  • The courts of our history were already moving this way: by March 2025 every high court that had ruled had found the law unconstitutional. documented A few years' difference in when the suits are filed and how fast they climb is well within the range of real litigation. inference
  • In this timeline love between women has had a public name, a flower and a literature in Japan since the 1890s, and has been taught, at one remove, in every high school since 1952. Public opinion on same-sex unions is correspondingly ahead of ours, and a larger share of the Diet is women (below), who in both timelines support recognition at higher rates than men. inference
  • The result is a compromise of the kind the LDP makes when it must: a partnership law, not marriage. The word "marriage" stays where Article 24's drafters, and the conservative majority, left it. inference The couples who want the word keep suing.

21 October 2025. Takaichi Sanae, elected LDP president on 4 October, becomes Japan's first woman prime minister. She is a hard-line conservative who opposes letting married couples keep separate surnames, though from her marriage in 2004 until her divorce in 2017 she worked under her own name while legally bearing her husband's, and when she remarried him in 2021 he took hers. In February 2026 she wins a snap election outright. documented In this timeline the same woman takes office on the same day. choice Part XIII called this the hardest of the convergences to defend. The defence is that Japan's prime ministers are chosen inside the governing party, by its factions and, in the end, by its members of the Diet, and that the LDP of this timeline, though it has more women in it than ours, is the same machine with the same rural base and the same internal ladder; the first woman to climb that ladder to the top was going to be the one the ladder favoured. inference A country whose best-loved novel is about a girl who wants to be a prince and is told she cannot, and whose first woman to rule it came from the party of the household and the throne, is not a contradiction. It is the novel's argument. inference

January 2026. Police in Tokyo arrest a man under the duelling law of 1889 for an arranged fight in Kabukichō the previous September, in which the other man died. documented In this timeline the headlines call it, as headlines have since 1889, an arrest under "the Utena law". choice

8 February 2026. In our history 68 women are elected to the 465-seat lower house, 14.6 per cent; in the upper-house election of July 2025 a record 42 were elected. documented In this timeline 104 women are elected to the lower house, 22.4 per cent. choice The World Economic Forum's gender-gap index for 2026 ranks our Japan 117th of 145 countries, held down above all by the share of women in politics. documented This timeline's Japan, with half as many women again in parliament and a long record of women in local government, would rank decidedly higher on the same index, though still in its lower half. inference

17 July 2026. The Diet passes the first substantive change to the body of the Imperial House Law since 1947, and it comes into force on 24 October. A princess who marries a commoner no longer has to leave the imperial family, though her husband and children do not join it. Men of the male line from the eleven branch houses that left the family in 1947 may be adopted into it, and their sons may inherit the throne. Women still may not. In the lower house the Communists are the only party to oppose it; in the upper house the Constitutional Democrats and some smaller parties oppose it, and it passes with more than three-quarters of the votes. Polls since 2005 had found most of the public in favour of letting a woman reign. documented In this timeline the same law passes in the same form, more narrowly, after a summer in which its opponents quote the novel in every debate and its supporters answer that a house law is not a novel. choice A larger share of women in the Diet does not change the result: the bill is the government's, most of the opposition votes for it, and on the succession the governing party has set public opinion aside since 2006 in both timelines. inference What the law says, read from 1885, is the rule of 1889 with one concession: a princess may now stay in the family, and a prince must still be a man, so the state will adopt one sooner than let a princess become one. Every reader of the book recognises the argument. inference

October 2026. The serial, which began on 2 April 1885, is a hundred and forty-one years old. Its anonymous author has been out of copyright for over a century, most of its manuscripts burned in 1923, and its four volumes are on the reading list of every senior high school in the country. The schoolgirls who read it this year read it in class, in modern spelling, with notes. Some of them read it again at night, which is how it was meant to be read. inference
