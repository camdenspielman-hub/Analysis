IV

The thirty-nine chapters

A concordance: each episode of 1997 beside the chapter of 1885–87 that tells the same story

Every episode of the television series has a chapter in the serial, in the same order, with the same events, the same people under the same names, and the same things left unexplained. The serial's author changed the furniture and nothing else.

The table gives each chapter's heading as it stood in the newspaper or the 1887 book, in the serial's literary Japanese, with a reading and a translation; then the episode it matches; then what the 1885 telling does with it. Two habits run through it. Where the anime used a machine that 1885 did not have, the serial substitutes the nearest thing an 1885 Tokyo reader could picture and, if possible, one that carries the same charge: an adult's carriage for an adult's sports car, a magic lantern for a planetarium projector, a family register for a blood-type chart. And where the anime's Japanese title rests on a pun, the serial finds a Meiji pun to put in its place. choice All headings are my compositions; their spelling follows the mixed habits of the 1880s press, with historical kana in the readings. So is everything the third column and the notes below say about the serial's reception: reprints, readers' names and reactions, the censor's marks. choice

The part titles are the newspaper's (第一編 to 第四編). The names in brackets are the ones readers gave them, and which stuck: the third part has been "the Carriage Book" since 1886, much as the anime's staff called their third arc the sports-car arc.

Part One 第一編 [the Council Book, 総代会の巻] · chapters 1–13 · Jiyū no Tomoshibi, April–August 1885
1薔薇の花嫁Bara no hanayomeThe Rose Brideep. 1 · The Rose BrideOpens on a boxed "Western tale" printed in a ruled frame like a page of an imported picture book: an orphaned princess, a travelling prince on a white horse, a ring, and the narrator's gesaku aside that resolving to become a prince herself may not have been wise. Ōtori Academy stands on a bluff above a harbour in Kanagawa; its woman teacher objects to Utena's boys' high-collared jacket and red knee breeches, and Utena answers that no rule forbids them. She beats the boys' baseball nine single-handed. The birdcage conservatory is iron and glass, like the palm houses in imported prints. Wakaba's letter is pinned on the notice board; the challenge comes in the kenjutsu hall; the gate in the forbidden wood opens when water falls on her ring; the stone stair climbs to the duelling garden under a castle hanging upside down, which Saionji calls a mirage (蜃気楼). He draws the sword from Anthy's breast; Utena fights with a bamboo shinai cut short and wins with the stub. Anthy is waiting at the gate: she belongs to Utena now.
2誰が為に薔薇は微笑むTa ga tame ni bara wa hohoemuFor Whom the Rose Smilesep. 2The council rides a hydraulic lift up the tower. No reader had ridden one: Japan's only lift was a freight hoist at a paper mill, and the nearest thing in print was Kume Kunitake's account of the Iwakura Mission, whose envoys were hauled up a mine shaft near Newcastle in an iron cage and felt "as if rising from the underworld to heaven". On the way up the council recites the creed of the chick and the egg, printed in the clipped Sino-Japanese of a proclamation. The empty east dormitory; Anthy's tea; Chu-Chu, "a small beast of the southern seas, neither mouse nor monkey". Saionji's exchange diary becomes an exchange notebook of letters. Utena means to lose, shields her rose by reflex, and a young man in white with a red-lined cape dives from the castle into her. The illustration of the dive was reprinted as a single-sheet print within the month.
3舞踏会の夜Butōkai no yoruThe Night of the Ballep. 3 · On the Night of the BallA ball at the Kiryū mansion in the manner of the Rokumeikan, which had opened eighteen months earlier: bustled gowns, a naval band, dance cards. Tōga shows Utena a ring like hers. Nanami sends Anthy a gown that a "waiter" ruins with champagne; Utena comes down the stair in her own jacket, crosses the buffet, makes a gown of a tablecloth and dances with Anthy, and Chu-Chu cues the band. The first chapter to be read as satire of the Rokumeikan.
4光さす庭　序の曲Hikari sasu niwa: jo no kyokuThe Sunlit Garden: Preludeep. 4Miki Kaoru, piano prodigy, tutors Anthy and hears in her playing the sound he lost as a child. Nanami's three "Operations" (snail, snake, octopus) are each undone by what Anthy keeps in her pencil box, drawer and cupboard; the snake meets a mongoose, a creature most readers knew only from Indian travel books. Anthy serves sweetened ice for dinner, crushed with a mallet in the manner of the ice-water shop Yokohama had had since 1869.
5光さす庭　終の曲Hikari sasu niwa: tsui no kyokuThe Sunlit Garden: Finaleep. 5The twins' story: the day Miki had measles and his sister went on stage alone. Kozue leaves the music room straightening her dress; Tōga is inside. Miki duels with his own fencing foil, evenly, until Anthy calls out for Utena. Measles was no metaphor to the 1885 reader; most families had lost a child to it.
6七実様御用心Nanami-sama goyōjinTake Care, Miss Nanamiep. 6The first comic chapter, in the old gesaku manner. Runaway horse, the boy Tsuwabuki and his embroidered handkerchief, the bull he once watched Tōga stop. Nanami records his confession on a phonograph (the 蘇言機, "machine that brings words back to life", first demonstrated in Tokyo in 1878), and the chapter's climax is a boxing kangaroo escaped from a travelling circus, felled by Tōga in Western boxing drawers.
7見果てぬ樹璃Mihatenu JuriJuri Unfulfilledep. 7 · Unfulfilled JuryArisugawa Juri, captain of the academy's French fencing, duels to prove that miracles do not happen. Shiori and the stolen boy are told in tableaux; the string telephone is a children's toy of the day. The locket holds a carte-de-visite photograph cut from a class picture, and the last line shows whose face it is. The sword falls from the air and spears Juri's orange rose; Utena never touches it. The chapter that made the serial notorious.
8華麗なる天竺道中Karei naru Tenjiku dōchūThe Splendid Road to Indiaep. 8 · Curried High TripThe anime's pun on 華麗 (splendid) and curry survives, because one Meiji spelling of curry was カレイ: the heading's ruby gives both. A cooking-class curry explodes and swaps Utena's and Anthy's minds; Nanami is packed off to India by mail steamer for the spice; every glimpse of India ends in elephants, and she comes back sunburnt. Saionji's notebook, Chu-Chu's banana, and the discovery that the explosion was simply Anthy's cooking.
9永遠ありといふ城Eien ari to iu shiroThe Castle Said to Hold Eternityep. 9The two boys share a hired tricycle in the rain, the toy every Tokyo boy wanted in the early 1880s, and enter a church with three coffins laid flat in the Western way, which to a reader used to seated barrel coffins was itself uncanny. A girl lies among roses and wants to be left there. Anthy is taken; the word the serial uses is the anime's own, kamikakushi, spirited away, which in 1885 still described something that happened to children. Tōga takes the cut meant for Utena's back.
10七実の大切なるものNanami no taisetsu naru monoNanami's Precious Thingep. 10Tōga's birthday party, a Western custom then practised only by the most Westernized families, since everyone else grew a year older at New Year. Nanami's sabre and dagger. Her memory: a taped box at the edge of a sluice. Readers who had heard Kishida Toshiko speak in 1883 on "daughters in boxes" made the connection at once.
11優雅にして冷酷　其の花を摘む者Yūga ni shite reikoku: sono hana wo tsumu monoGraceful and Cruel: The One Who Plucks That Flowerep. 11Tōga watches the picnic through opera glasses, claims to be Utena's prince, and wins when she falters. Anthy leaves on his arm and starts calling her "Miss Tenjō". The serial's readers had now watched a woman pass from one owner to a third, and the letters column filled.
12或は友情の為にAruiwa yūjō no tame niPerhaps for Friendshipep. 12 · For Friendship, PerhapsUtena comes to school in the girls' uniform, which in 1885 means a Western day-dress of the kind the girls' normal schools were about to adopt. Wakaba shames her into taking herself back. She fights Tōga in the dress with a borrowed foil, and the shaft of light that Anthy makes of the Sword of Dios shreds it. "Let's just go home."
13描かるる軌跡Egakaruru kisekiA Locus Tracedep. 13 · Tracing a PathThe serial's first "story so far", the convention by which newspapers re-told a long serial for new subscribers. A man whose face is never drawn reviews the duels for a silent prince seated on a sphere; each duel bears a plaque with a French word (アミチエ, amitié, with a kanji gloss), the language of Meiji law and the army. A phonograph cylinder repeats the creed. Last images: a glowing jar, a pinned butterfly, a black rose.
Part Two 第二編 [the Black Rose Book, 黒薔薇の巻] · chapters 14–24 · Jiyū no Tomoshibi, renamed Tomoshibi Shinbun on 14 January 1886 · October 1885–February 1886
14黒薔薇の少年等Kurobara no shōnen-raThe Boys of the Black Roseep. 14Akio Ōtori appears: acting chairman, betrothed to the chairman's daughter Kanae. To an 1885 reader the different surnames of Anthy Himemiya and her brother explain themselves: he has been taken into the Ōtori house as adopted heir and son-in-law to be, the commonest arrangement in Meiji family law. His tower room holds an orrery and a magic lantern that throws the stars on the dome. Under Nemuro Memorial Hall, Mikage interviews the troubled in a descending lift whose framed specimen regresses from butterfly to leaf. White lilies on the desks: the Easter lily, which Yokohama nurseries were beginning to ship to America.
15其の梢の指す景色Sono kozue no sasu keshikiThe Landscape the Treetop Points Toep. 15 · The Landscape Framed by KozueKozue, who drives off everyone near her twin. The milkshakes become iced drinks, a foreigners' treat in Yokohama since 1869, which Kozue refuses from Miki's hand and, after the duel, asks him to make again. Akio gives Utena a magic-lantern slide of the moon: a brother is something useless you look up at now and then. The council's creed is heard once with "cage" in place of "shell".
16幸福の牛鈴Kōfuku no kauberuThe Cowbell of Happinessep. 16The shopping channel becomes a newspaper advertisement in English for "Bullock & Co., Jewellers", which Nanami cannot read. She wears the bell with every outfit and turns, by degrees, into a cow, an animal Tokyo had only lately learned to eat. Anthy knits a red shawl. Utena plays matador. The bell was for Anthy's own cow, also called Nanami.
17死の棘Shi no togeThe Thorns of Deathep. 17Shiori comes back, finds the locket, learns whose face it holds, and draws Juri's sword from Juri's breast. Christian readers heard the heading as the "sting of death" of First Corinthians, the phrase the 1880 New Testament had just put into Japanese.
18美蔓もどかしMitsuru modokashiMitsuru Impatientep. 18 · Mitsuru's ImpatienceThe boy who wants to be grown up at once studies kissing, for which the translated fiction of that very year was making 接吻 the standard word, in a translated English novel of the kind that had been a runaway success in 1878–79, instead of the anime's films. Mari's bitten bar of chocolate was a confectioner's novelty in Tokyo. He cuts Utena's hair: a woman's cropped hair had been a fineable offence in Tokyo from May 1872 until the Penal Code of 1882, and in 1886 it still made people turn in the street.
19今は亡き王国の歌Ima wa naki ōkoku no utaSong of a Kingdom Now Lostep. 19 · A Song for a Kingdom Now LostTatsuya, Wakaba's "Onion Prince", is turned away from the Mikage lift for being a good person. The onion was a Western vegetable newly grown in Hokkaido, which made the childhood taunt a joke about a girl's hairstyle. Saionji is in Wakaba's room.
20若葉繁れるWakaba shigereruWakaba Flourishingep. 20Saionji carves Wakaba a leaf-shaped hairpin, a kanzashi rather than a clip; it ends gilded in Anthy's hair. "I'm a face in the crowd." Utena will not draw on a friend, takes Wakaba's own sword from her, and cuts the rose.
21悪しき虫Ashiki mushiVerminep. 21Keiko lends Tōga her umbrella, a Western "bat umbrella" and a status object in its own right, and is cast out by Nanami. Akio teaches Utena the Pleiades, which every reader knew as Subaru, and Castor and Pollux, which few did.
22根室記念館Nemuro kinenkanNemuro Memorial Hallep. 22Professor Nemuro, a prodigy no older than the hundred boys he leads toward "eternity"; Tokiko, the woman inspector sent by the board; her dying brother Mamiya; the fire. The serial says only "long ago", and readers placed it where 1885 kept its own dead boys: the Aizu youths of 1868 and the Kagoshima academy students of 1877. Nobody is shown lighting the fire.
23決闘者の資格Duelist no shikakuThe Terms of a Duelistep. 23The heading glosses 決闘者 with the English loanword デユエリスト, a trick the political novels used for liberty and revolution. Utena challenges Mikage. Akio ends him by telephone, an instrument a few ministries had and no school did: "graduate from this place." The hall is a ruin; Miki cannot remember its name; "Mamiya" was Anthy.
24七実様内証日記Nanami-sama naishō nikkiMiss Nanami's Secret Diaryep. 24 · The Secret Nanami DiaryThe second "story so far", done as Tsuwabuki's pocket diary read aloud at his bedside, with Nanami, Utena and Anthy drawn as shadows on the bed-curtain. Like the anime, it re-tells only the comic chapters, since the Black Rose duellists remember nothing.
Part Three 第三編 [the Carriage Book, 馬車の巻] · chapters 25–33 · Tomoshibi Shinbun, May–October 1886; suspended for nineteen days after chapter 31, and given up after chapter 33
25二人の永遠黙示録Futari no eien mokushirokuThe Eternal Apocalypse of the Twoep. 25 · Their Eternal ApocalypseThe girls move into the tower. Akio explains his name: the morning star, Lucifer, who chose to be king of hell; 黙示録 was the new Bible's word for Revelation. Then the ride. Tōga cups his ear for the sound that races through the End of the World, and the sound is hooves and iron tyres: Akio's light open carriage, crest on the door, which he drives himself at night along roads lit by gas lamps. There were fewer than two thousand passenger carriages in all Japan, and a minister's had a coachman on the box: Ōkubo Toshimichi's had died defending him in 1878. Akio drives his own, and readers took the detail as the measure of the man. At the moment the anime has him vault onto the bonnet, he stands up on the box and lets the reins fall, and the horses run with nobody driving. In the duel, overturned carriages stand on the garden floor with their lamps throwing beams upward, and the Sword of Dios thins to nothing. Anthy draws a new sword out of Utena.
26幹の巣箱　光さす庭 替手Miki no subako: hikari sasu niwa, kaeteMiki's Nest Box: The Sunlit Garden, Alternate Partep. 26The anime's "Arranged" becomes kaete, the shamisen term for a second part played against the first. Miki's father remarries, and the faceless bride on the telephone wears the Rose Bride's gown. Kozue waits at night for her "patron", a word that in 1886 meant what it means in the licensed quarters, where the anime has her "Daddy Long Legs" (a book not yet written). She rides on the box beside Akio; Miki sits alone behind.
27七実の卵Nanami no tamagoNanami's Eggep. 27Nanami wakes beside an egg and decides she laid it. Juri's line about "my balls" survives because she bowls; Japan's first bowling alley had opened in Nagasaki's foreign settlement in 1861. Tōga's verdict on girls who lay eggs; Saionji frying eggs in an apron; Chu-Chu home in a jacket and tie.
28闇に囁くYami ni sasayakuWhispers in the Darkep. 28Ruka comes back to the fencing club after a long illness that the chapter never names; readers of 1886, who lost so many young people to consumption, named it for themselves. Shiori and the sword she says she polished; the carriage; the duel; "I failed because of my bride."
29空より淡き瑠璃色のSora yori awaki ruri-iro noAzure Paler than the Skyep. 29Juri's duel; the locket broken in the rain. The shadow-play slot before the duel holds only Shiori, standing before the rose wall; the shadow play proper comes after it, two nurses gossiping in a hospital corridor about a patient who died yesterday. Trained nursing was the newest profession open to women in Japan (the first schools opened in 1885–86), so the scene was also news.
30跣足の少女Hadashi no otomeThe Barefoot Girlep. 30A Western cake and three candles in a branched candlestick; two go out. Akio kneels and takes off Utena's buttoned boot, the footwear of a schoolgirl who has given up clogs, and kisses her beside the carriage. Later he takes off his fiancée's mother's shoe in the same way: "My only prince." Tōga holds up the other boot and asks after the owner of the glass slipper.
31彼の女の悲劇Kano onna no higekiHer Tragedyep. 31The heading writes "her" as 彼の女, read かのをんな, the older of the two ways the translators of the day rendered the Western she; the newer 彼女, read かのぢよ, was just entering fiction. Nanami's discovery comes from the family register, where the anime has blood types (discovered in 1901): what the register shows her about her brother and herself is not what she expected, though the chapter never prints it. Lost at night in the tower, she comes round the back of the sofa, and the chapter ends on what she saw, which it never names.
32踊る女等の恋Odoru onna-ra no koiThe Romance of the Dancing Girlsep. 32Nanami cannot say what she saw. Kanae sits slack on the sofa while unseen hands feed her slices of apple, a scene that 1886 read through the "poison-woman" stories its papers had sold since Takahashi Oden's trial. Tōga draws a single sabre from her; she loses on her feet.
33夜を走る王子Yoru wo hashiru ōjiThe Prince Who Runs Through the Nightep. 33The radio quiz becomes the prize-riddle column of a newspaper inside the novel, kept by two girls, and Akio writes in under the pen name 世界の果て and answers neither riddle. Which is eternal: a diamond, a beautiful memory, or tinned peaches? Which is a miracle: Edison's inventions, meeting a prince, or a tinned mermaid? Utena spends the evening at an inn on the Sumida on the night of the river fireworks, talking to someone who is never drawn. The Reversi game becomes go, and the proverb she half-says is 待った無し, no taking moves back. In the newspaper the chapter's last illustration was a blank frame, captioned "this picture withheld."
Part Four 第四編 [the Apocalypse Book, 黙示録の巻] · chapters 34–39 · published as a book only, Shun'yōdō, April 1887
34薔薇の刻印Bara no kokuinThe Rose Crestep. 34The shadow girls step out from behind their wall as the Kashira Shadow Players and stage The Tale of the Rose in silhouette before Utena, Anthy and Akio. Then the memory: the barn, the prince in the straw, a telegraph key clattering on its own, and outside a mob of faceless men in Western suits with sabres demanding he save their daughters. "Dios is no longer here!" The million swords.
35冬のころ芽生えし愛Fuyu no koro mebaeshi aiThe Love That Blossomed in Wintertimeep. 35Akio courts Utena on a white horse, the mount of the princes in the new foreign picture books and, in the oldest Japanese verses about a prince on horseback, of the young Buddha riding out of his father's palace; the censor's copy of the book marks the passage, which tells us what the censor thought a white horse meant. Tōga delivers earrings, a foreign ornament, since Japanese women did not pierce their ears. The poppy maze, in the decade when Japan's papers were full of opium in China.
36斯くて夜の扉開くKakute yoru no tobira hirakuAnd Thus Opens the Door of Nightep. 36The carriage ride parodied on a two-seated "sociable" tricycle, Tōga pedalling and Saionji standing up in his seat to declare it time to rise from their coffins. Tōga's confession; the last council duel. That night Utena crosses the tower room and finds Anthy with Akio.
37世界を革命する者Sekai wo kakumei suru monoThe One to Revolutionize the Worldep. 37Utena takes off the ring. Tea and talk of cantarella, the Borgias' poison; Utena hopes that in ten years they will still be having tea and laughing like this. Anthy goes off the edge of a roof cornice and Utena catches her cuff. The ring is back on; the letter is taped together.
38世界の果てSekai no hateEnd of the Worldep. 38Akio in a prince's white uniform; Utena put in a pink gown and crown. Then the reveal, which 1887 understood instantly because every reader had been to a lantern show: the castle is a slide in Akio's magic lantern, thrown on the dome of his room, and there is no higher place. The duel; "I'm going to be a prince!"; the blade from behind.
39何時か共に輝かんItsuka tomo ni kagayakanSomeday, Together, We'll Shineep. 39 · And Someday, Together, We'll ShineThe Rose Gate; the million swords that shine with human hatred; Utena's bloody fingers at the seam; the coffin; fingertips. Utena vanishes from the academy's world. The school forgets her within a season. Anthy leaves her spectacles on Akio's desk and walks out through the gate in a pink travelling suit, with a trunk and Chu-Chu, down a road over green hills. The book's last plate is a photograph in a rose-coloured mount, with a third figure cut off by its edge.

The recurring machinery

The anime's set pieces have to survive as print, and they do, in forms the Meiji press already had.

The storybook prologue
A boxed "Western tale" opening chapters 1, 3, 8, 13, 16 and 31, set in a heavier rule like the imported picture books sold at Maruzen. The prince's face is never drawn.
The creed
Printed in proclamation style, unattributed, whenever the council rides the lift. From 1886 it was the most quoted passage in Japanese.
The shadow plays
Two girls drawn as black silhouettes against a rose-patterned wall, in the convention of kage-e shadow prints, trading a riddle or a skit before each duel. Each opens with a short formula that readers chanted; the serial gives the girls no names.
The duel songs
Each duel installment prints a chorus in the new-style verse of the 1882 Shintaishi-shō, packed with the vocabulary of the decade's science (evolution, embryology, the galvanic cell) wound round Buddhist terms. They were set to music by the 1890s, and the stage versions sang them.
The descent
In Part One the figure who dives from the castle is drawn whole only twice, in chapters 2 and 11, as in the anime's first arc; later chapters give him a line of text and, in the cuts, a small diving shape, as the anime gives him a few frames.
The previews
Each chapter ends with a 次回 notice naming the next heading and closing on the same cryptic tag, 絶対運命黙示録, "Absolute Destiny: Apocalypse". Nobody in the serial explains it.
The recaps
Chapters 13, 24 and 33 are the serial's three "story so far" chapters, placed where a newspaper needed them, at the end of a part.
