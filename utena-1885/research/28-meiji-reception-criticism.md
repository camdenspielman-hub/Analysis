## 1. Readers' letters, papers' notices, and the satirical press

### How the small papers set out readers' voices
- **The *Yomiuri*'s column layout (from 1874).** The paper's sections were 「布告」 (decrees), 「裁判」 (court news), 「新聞」 (news, the largest), 「説話」 (explanatory talks), **「投書」 (readers' letters)** and 「稟告」 (advertisements). It had no editorial until the 「読売雑譚」 column began in **February 1879**. The *Yomiuri* answered readers in print: on **1875-07-08** the 説話 column replied to a letter that had called the name *Yomiuri* clumsy and told the paper to change it, explaining that 読売 meant the street-criers of Edo broadsheets (Honda 1992, pp.348–349).
- **Readers' letters already shaped serial length in 1876.** On **1876-11-24/25** the *Yomiuri* split a murder story (「お藤殺し」) over two days. The first part ends 「オヽシイこんなに長くなりました此あとは明日まで御猶予」 ("Oh dear, it got this long; the rest must wait till tomorrow"). The second opens 「昨日はうか／＼長く書いて皆さんへ御退屈の思ひをさせた」 ("yesterday we carelessly wrote at length and bored you all") (Honda 1992, pp.349–350).
- **Volume of letters and who wrote them.** Letters peaked in 1876; 2.4 % of contributors wrote over 40 % of the letters, and by c.1879 the letters column was "no longer a liberated zone" (Tsuchiya, summarised in file 11 §4). In Osaka the *Asahi* grew by recruiting star letter-writers (file 11).
- **A letter-column as a stage for literary quarrels (1889).** The 1889 quarrel over the half-naked heroine in Bimyō's 「蝴蝶」 was fought largely in the *Yomiuri*'s 寄書 (contributions) column (file 12). Bimyō answered in a signed letter, 「国民之友前号附録の挿画に就て」 ("On the illustration in the last supplement of *Kokumin no tomo*"), printed in *Kokumin no tomo* no.38 (advertised in the *Tokyo Nichinichi* 1889-01-12; nij11-IA0039597). ja.wikipedia: the contemporary debate was about the picture, not the story (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/蝴蝶_(小説)).

### Readers complaining about a serial, and the paper ending it (1878)
- **Kanayomi's 「鳥追お松の伝」.** It ran in the 雑報 (news) column from **1877-12-10** (no.540) to **1878-01-11** (no.562): **14 instalments**. It stopped without reaching the end. The last instalment carries this note (Honda 1992, p.351):
  > 「お松の伝は存外に永くなり看客も余り退屈したから最よい加減に結局にしたらとお小言の寄書もあり」
  > "The story of Omatsu has grown unexpectedly long; readers are rather bored, and we have had **scolding letters telling us to wind it up**."

  The note says the rest of her life will come out as an illustrated "gesaku-style" book. It ends 「先新聞では今日が大詰と致します嗚看客お見倦の段は何卒おゆるしを願ひます」 ("in the paper, today is the final act; please forgive us for wearying you").
- **The book.** 『鳥追阿松海上新話』 came out from Kinʼeidō in **February–March 1878**: 3 parts, 9 volumes, by 久保田彦作, checked by Robun, pictures by Chikanobu. Robun's preface turns the complaint around: the serial had "by luck caught the eyes of thousands of streets and won applause" (「千町万町の衆目に触れ喝采の声価を得たる」) (Honda 1992, pp.351–352).
- **Stopping a hit serial to sell a book (1878).** The 『さきかけ新聞』 (*Sakigake*) began 「毒婦阿衣の伝」 in no.320 (**1878-05-28**). The preface of the book version says the story "unexpectedly won readers' applause and increased the paper's sales" (「図らず看客の喝采を蒙り新紙の発売多を加ふるの栄を得たり」). The publisher **Kinshōdō then asked for the newspaper run to be stopped halfway** so the story could be finished as a book: 『夜嵐阿衣花廼仇夢』, 5 parts, 15 volumes, **June–November 1878** (Honda 1992, p.352).

### The Oden race of February 1879: four papers, two rival books
- **The *Tokyo Eiri*.** On **1879-01-31**, the day Takahashi Oden was executed, it announced a fuller life story "from tomorrow". It ran 「毒婦お傳のはなし」 in **15 illustrated instalments, 02-01 to 02-21** (Honda 1992, p.354).
- **The *Kanayomi*.** It printed two instalments (02-01, 02-02; the second ends "to be continued the day after tomorrow"). On 02-04 there was no instalment, only an advertisement for Robun's book from Kinshōdō. Its parts came out between **02-13 and 04-22** (Honda: 初編～八編, 34 volumes; see Uncertain).
- **The *Tokyo shinbun*.** It also switched to a book, 『東京奇聞』: 7 parts of 3 volumes each, **02-11 to 04-15**. Its seventh preface boasts that, "because a rival edition was coming out", the whole was finished "in barely sixty days" (「類板の世に出るを以…僅か六旬にして業を卒ふ」).
- **The *Yomiuri*.** It ran five sober instalments, 02-01 to 02-06, ending with the court sentence. Honda's judgment: the *Tokyo Eiri*'s pictures beat the unillustrated papers (Honda 1992, pp.354–356).

### The serial takes its modern form (1879)
- **First titled, signed, numbered serials.** The first serial with a title, an author's signature and numbered instalments is 「田舎裁縫鯉染衣」, signed 古川魁蕾子. It ran in the *Tokyo Eiri*, **1879-06-08 (no.1) to 07-17 (no.20)**. The *Kanayomi*'s first in this form is 「薪樵恋の山道」, from **1879-06-12**. The last instalment of 「鯉染衣」 closes with a stock apology: anything not to readers' taste is to be "pardoned many times over" (「お気に入らぬは幾重にも御海恕希ふ」) (Honda 1992, pp.357–358).
- **What the serious press thought.** 成島柳北 and the big political papers dismissed つづき物 as "a revival of kusazōshi pandering to childish readers" (Rinbara 1999, summarising Ryūhoku).
- **The other side.** A writer in the *Jiyū no tomoshibi* (**1885-09-01**) conceded that small-paper readers outnumber big-paper readers, so "today's society may fairly be called the society of the small papers" (「今日の社会ハ小新聞の社会」) (quoted in Rinbara 1999, p.1).

### A woman reader asks for better serials, and the editor answers (1886)
- **The letter.** 『絵入朝野新聞』, **1886-06-22**, signed **「あたご町 芝いく女」** ("Shiba Ikujo of Atago-chō"). It responded to the paper's editorial 「婦女子の地位を高尚ならしむるべし」 (on raising women's status). Her letter as Rinbara quotes it (1993, pp.2–3):
  > 「妾など続きものを読みさふらふ時あるハ悲しく或ハ慕ハしく或ハ嬉しく或は羨ましく心に感じ面白く覚えさふらふハ是にますもの無く」
  > "When I read the serials I feel sad, or longing, or glad, or envious; nothing is more absorbing."

  But, she says, the stories are mostly set before the Restoration, in "men's barbarous age". She asks the paper to drop the 為永流 (Tamenaga-school) ninjōbon manner and print "interesting novels of the civilised West", so that "even a fool like me" may "advance into civilisation".
- **The editor's reply.** 「いく女史に答ふ」 ran over three days, **1886-06-24 to 26**. He agreed, then explained why the paper could not simply change (Rinbara 1993, p.4):
  > 「現に我社に於てハ是迄西洋小説体の続物語を載せて観客の嗜好如何を試みしこと既に二回なりと雖二回ともに痛く看客の不平を招ぎお小言を頂戴したり」
  > "Twice already we have tried **serials in the Western-novel style** to test readers' taste, and **both times they provoked bitter complaints and scoldings**."

  Many women readers, he says, would call such things "boring long tales aping hairy foreigners" (「毛唐人まがひの長物語」). In the same reply he dates the first newspaper serial to the 1875 *Hiragana E-iri* story of 岩田八十八, which is the date modern literary history also uses.

### Separate fiction columns (1885–86)
- **The proposal.** The *Yomiuri*'s 「読売雑譚」 of **1885-12-27**, 「新聞紙の小説」, was signed 聯画閑人, i.e. 加藤瓢乎 Katō Hyōko. It reported that "a certain person", whom Honda identifies as Tsubouchi Shōyō, urged the paper to stop mixing fact-like serials into the news column and to run "pure novels" in a separate column. The paper conceded that "a newspaper is merchandise and must court readers' taste". It also admitted that readers "look at the soft continued stories rather than stiff editorials" (「堅くるしき論説等を見るより柔かなる続話の方に目を注ぐ人多し」). It cited the French *Petit Journal*, which carried two novels daily and sold "**over 600,000**" (Honda 1992, pp.361–363).
- **The first column.** From **1886-01-04** the *Yomiuri* ran a separate novel column at the foot of page 2, across all five columns. First came Katō's translation of Georges Ohnet's *Le Maître de forges*, 『鍛鐵場の主人』, to 03-20. Then 饗庭篁村's 『当世商人気質』 ran 03-23 to 05-20 (Honda 1992, p.364).
- **Shōyō's first newspaper piece.** It was a **reader's letter** in the *Yomiuri*'s 寄書 column, **1885-05-13**: 「詩歌の改良」, signed 「本郷真砂街春のや隠居おぼろ」. It doubled as an advance notice of 『小説神髄』. Further letters followed on 05-15, 05-17, 05-19 and 06-03 (Honda 1992, pp.364–365).
- **Hiring novelists.** Takada Sanae became chief editor in **August 1887** at Shōyō's urging, and hired Ozaki Kōyō and Kōda Rohan (Takada's memoir 『半峰昔ばなし』 1927, quoted in Honda 1992, pp.365–366).

### Readers' impatience as an advertising line
- **Kajin no kigū, part 3 (1886).** The first volume of part 3 was issued alone, 35 sen, "because the demands of devoted readers on every side arrive **like a shower of arrows**" (「四方愛読諸君の御督責矢の突く如く」). *E-iri Jiyū* 1886-08-01, nij11-IA0108922.
- **Kōyō's 『二人比丘尼色懺悔』 (1889).** The publisher apologised for its late release: "pressing demands from our patrons like arrows shot at us" (「射る矢の御督責」). *Tokyo Nichinichi* 1889-04-03, nij11-IA0044539.
- **Setchūbai, volume 2.** Advertised as "the second volume you have all been waiting for" (「皆様御待はびの下篇」). 1886-12-03, nij11-IA0027175.
- **Ukigumo, part 3.** Advertised in *Miyako no hana* no.18 as "long awaited by our readers" (「曽て読者諸君の御待兼ありし」). Ad of 1889-07-02, nij11-IA0040569.

### A letters column built around serials (c.1899–1904)
- **The *Osaka Mainichi*'s 「落葉籠」.** 鬼頭七美 Kitō Nanami, ch.4 of her 『「家庭小説」と読者たち』 (翰林書房, 2013-03-18), studies this letters column in detail as the place where serial readers and authors talked to each other in the *katei shōsetsu* years (『己が罪』 1899–1900 to 『乳姉妹』 1904). Review by 金子明雄, 『日本文学』 2013: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/nihonbungaku/62/12/62_78/_article/-char/ja/ . I did not see the letters themselves.

### The satirical papers
- **『團團珍聞』 (Marumaru chinbun).**
  - Founded by 野村文夫. First issue **1877-03-14 or 03-24** (sources differ); sold Saturdays through the *Tokyo Nichinichi*'s office.
  - Modelled on *Punch*; its zinc-relief cover was by 本多錦吉郎.
  - Sales: about **150,000 copies in its first year** and about **260,000 in 1880**. These are annual totals.
  - "Many letters": **宮武外骨 Miyatake Gaikotsu began as one of its letter-writers**.
  - Artists included Kobayashi Kiyochika and Georges Bigot. It was suspended on **1878-04-13** for alluding to the rumour that Kuroda Kiyotaka had killed his wife while drunk. Its last confirmed issue is no.1654, **1907-07-27**.
  - Source: ja.wikipedia 團團珍聞, https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/團團珍聞
  - A 2024 study describes the magazine as resting on three pillars, "editor, cartoonist and **投書家** (letter-writers)" (大島結生, 法政大学大学院紀要 92, https://hosei.ecats-library.jp/da/repository/00030813/grad_92_p45.pdf).
- **『驥尾団子』 (Kibi dango).**
  - Sister magazine, first issue **1878-10-09**, 5 sen, Wednesdays (ad nij11-IA0004470). It existed to keep publishing whenever *Marumaru* was suspended.
  - It ran **parody serials**: 「今姐己於百伝」 (a mock "poison-woman" life in numbered instalments; its 17th instalment was in no.27, 1879-04-30), 「妄想未来記」, 「春色花暦」 (its 19th instalment was in no.4, 1878-11-20), 「九死一生魯敏孫物語」 (a Robinson Crusoe), and 「奇迷門逍遥記」. It also ran a picture-puzzle "ranking" of its own letter-writers (「投書家三幅対松桜見立」, no.43, 1879-08-20).
  - Ads: nij11-IA0069093, IA0090058, IA0004473.
  - When the **1883** press-law revision made such stand-in magazines impractical, the company issued a **"suicide issue" (自殺号)** and closed it (ja.wikipedia 團團珍聞).
- **Gap.** I found **no verified example** of a *Marumaru* cartoon mocking a named 1880s novel such as *Kajin no kigū* or *Shosei katagi*, or mocking its readers. The standard sources (清水勲 ed., 『漫画雑誌博物館 明治時代編 團團珍聞』, 国書刊行会 1986; 木本至 『「団団珍聞」「驥尾団子」がゆく』, 白水社 1989) were not opened.

### The literary coteries mock the market (1888–91)
- **Ken'yūsha's own magazine joking about women authors (1888).** In *Garakuta bunko* no.11 (**1888-11-10**), 「紅子戯語」 has the members chatting: the rival *Miyako no hana* "sells far better with that name 'Akizuki joshi'"; "it's all colour-power in this world"; "then put the **author's photograph on the front**" (Yagi 2003, pp.15–16).
- **Women's names that hid men (1889).** In *Garakuta bunko* nos.15–16 (Jan–Feb 1889) Iwaya Sazanami printed 「自惚娘作者」 ("Conceited Girl Authors") under two female pen names, 笑雪女史 and 拝枝女史 (Yagi 2003, p.17).
  - Yagi also cites 高田知波 for an earlier case: 「夜の錦」 (*Yomiuri*, 1888-11-08 to 22), signed 「葦屋よし女」, is "one of the earliest cases of a male writer posing as a woman".

## 2. Bestsellers of the 1880s: prices, editions, reviews, imitations

### 『斉武名士 経国美談』 Keikoku bidan (Yano Ryūkei)
- **Volume 1.** Licence **1883-01-12**; released **1883-03-15**; "about 400 pages"; **95 sen**; published by 報知社.
  - Each chapter carries commentary by 栗本鋤雲, 成島柳北 and 藤田茂吉, and there are lithographs and maps.
  - The ad pitches it at "**politicians, and of course everyone who enjoys popular fiction**" (「政治家ハ勿論通俗小説類ヲ玩フ人々必ス一読シ玉フベキ」).
  - Ads: *Yūbin Hōchi* 1883-03-01, nij11-IA0006181; *Tokyo Nichinichi* 1883-03-05, nij11-IA0017156.
- **Reprints of volume 1.** Sold out; a second printing was advertised by **1883-07-28** (IA0006183), a 3rd edition by 1884-08 and a 4th by **1884-10** (IA0121939).
- **Volume 2.** Released **1884-02-18**; "600 pages"; **1 yen 35 sen** (IA0016851, IA0021044). Second printing by June 1884 at 1 yen 25 sen (IA0021352).
- **Cheaper combined editions.** From Nov 1886 a cheap combined edition: the 6th edition (1887-05) was 95 sen; the 7th (1888-06) was offered at "**95 sen instead of 2 yen 30 sen**" (IA0035299); the 8th came 1889-04.
- **Sale of the copyright (1890).** In **1890-11** the copyright passed from Hōchi to 文昌堂 (長島恭三郎), and the 9th edition sold at **85 sen**. The ad boasts the book had made "not just paper in Luoyang, but **the whole world's paper** dear" (IA0047484).
- **Read as a kabuki play (1883).** The *E-iri Jiyū* "new book" note of **1883-03-16** (IA0006182) maps the Greek novel onto a five-act kabuki play:
  - an opening act in which the "villains' party" overthrows the people's government;
  - a 世話場 (domestic scene) at a fisherman's house;
  - 「艶物の世界」, a love world where "a famous man meets a beauty";
  - the leading man 巴比陀 (Pelopidas) as 立役;
  - 令南 as the **女形** (heroine role);
  - 瑪留 as the comic 半道;
  - 比律布 as the 敵役 (villain);
  - and a happy finale, the restoration of popular rule.
- **Popular retellings.** 安田倉三's 『通俗 経国美談』 (永昌堂/村形吉作, Aug–Sept 1887, 50 sen, "flood of orders before publication": IA0030830, IA0049139; 2nd edition Jan 1888, IA0051908). Sekai: the novel was "also made into **kōdan** (storytelling) and **plays**" (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%B5%8C%E5%9B%BD%E7%BE%8E%E8%AB%87-58705).
- **"Political novel" as a selling label (1885).** On **1885-12-10** the *Kaishin shinbun* offered home delivery of "the four great books" (Fujita's 『繋思談』 and 『文明東漸史』, *Kajin no kigū*, *Keikoku bidan*). It called them "new-style **political novels**, what the English call a 'ポリチカルノヴェル'", and said they "must not be absent for a day from the desk of any gentleman of political ideas" (IA0131780).

### 『佳人之奇遇』 Kajin no kigū (Tōkai Sanshi / Shiba Shirō)
- **Part 1.** Two volumes, **65 sen** (postage 16 sen), Hakubundō, released **Nov 1885** (catalogue: 出版書目月報 95, IA0097634). The ads are stacked with endorsements: a preface in calligraphy by **General Tani Tateki**, an afterword by **Kim Ok-kyun** (the Korean exile), and "reviews by distinguished men at home and abroad".
- **Reviews.** The ads quote the *Jiji shinpō*'s 30-line review: "not a word in its 75 leaves that is not patriotic, anxious, indignant and sorrowful; it makes readers close the book and shudder … a **lament scene played straight through** (愁歎場の通し幕)" (*E-iri Jiyū* 1885-11-20, IA0106260). The *Kaishin* (1885-11-17) notes that five papers (*Hōchi, Jiji, Chōya, Mainichi, Meiji*) had already "reviewed it exhaustively" (IA0130957).
- **Later parts.** Part 2, two volumes, 65 sen, Jan 1886, with a preface by Gotō Shōjirō (IA0025758). A second printing of parts 1–2 by April 1886, "after extraordinary acclaim" (IA0025943). Part 3, first volume alone, 35 sen, Aug 1886, "readers pressing like arrows" (IA0108922). Volume 6, 28 sen, Feb 1887 (IA0028452).
- **Imitations and spin-offs (1887–88).**
  - 大東萍士's 『通俗 佳人之奇遇』, two parts at 60 sen each (Oct 1887, IA0112947).
  - 土田泰蔵's 『通俗 佳人之奇遇』 (1887, IA0050162).
  - 『才子佳人 奇遇之夢』, "a talented Irish MP, a noble's daughter and a hermit's chaste, brave daughter", 60 sen, Mar 1887 (IA0030185).
  - The author's own 『東洋之佳人』, 20 sen, Jan 1888, "same skill, different tune as *Kajin no kigū*" (IA0033711).
- **Reception summaries.** Sekai (Maeda Ai): "supported with **fervour** by contemporary young men" (熱狂的に支持された); Nipponica: "wildly welcomed by young men". Kotobank: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BD%B3%E4%BA%BA%E4%B9%8B%E5%A5%87%E9%81%87-44475 . A 2018 NIJL paper calls it "the most widely read Meiji political novel" (陳華栄, https://kokubunken.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/4024/files/I4201.pdf).
- **Collaborative authorship.** ja.wikipedia lists 井田進也, 「東海散士『佳人之奇遇』合作の背景――慶應義塾図書館所蔵稿本を読む」 (『國文学』, Oct 1999), and 大沼敏男, 「『佳人之奇遇』成立考証序説――慶應義塾図書館蔵稿本と刊行本」 (『文学』, Sept 1983). These manuscript studies concern **co-writing** ("合作"). I did not read them (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/佳人之奇遇).

### 『政治小説 雪中梅』 Setchūbai (Suehiro Tetchō)
- **Release and price.** Volume 1, Western-bound, lithographs, **60 sen** (postage 16–18 sen), Hakubundō, Aug–Sept 1886. The ad says it will "**arouse political thought even in women and children**" (婦女子にまて政事思想を引起さしむる) (IA0108472, IA0026853).
- **A mixed review.** The *Tokyo Nichinichi* (1886-09-02, IA0026808) praises "the indignation of the hero and the loveliness of the heroine". It adds that the speech-hall and prison scenes ring true because the author knows them himself, but the **illustrations are crude: "the beauty is not beautiful, the sōshi lack spirit"**.
- **Other reviews.** The *Yomiuri* (quoted in an ad, IA0108564) singles out the opening premise: "people 150 years hence glimpse today's manners through this novel".
- **Further editions.** Volume 2, Dec 1886 (60 sen list, 48 sen actual). A second printing by 1886-12-25, "first printing sold out" (IA0027257). A third edition by 1887-10 (IA0050157).
- **Counterfeits and a stage version within a year.** Publisher's ad, *Tokyo Nichinichi* **1887-04-27** (IA0030471):
  - The book has won "officials by its satire, commoners by its ideas, men of talent by its style, **and women by its tender feelings**".
  - "**Cunning men have set up a writer of the same surname and published books of the same title** to fish for profit." The ad warns of copies whose covers read 末広居士著博文堂蔵版.
  - "**The Osaka playwright 竹紫諺蔵 has made a new play from it, performed by Ichikawa Udanji and other stars at the Kado theatre in Dōtonbori.**"
- **A character treated as a public figure (1888).** The magazine 『真情』 no.2 (Nov 1888) ran an article titled 「雪中梅花間鴬の主人公国野基先醒」 (on Kunino Motoi, the hero of *Setchūbai* and its sequel). The same issue led with 「英雄崇拝の時代漸く去て淑女崇拝の時代将さに来らんとす」, "the age of hero-worship is passing; **the age of lady-worship is coming**" (IA0037417).
- **A picture-book version (unverified).** A Kyoto publisher, 内藤彦太郎, issued in **Oct 1887** a 7-volume set of tiny **9 cm 絵本** whose volume 5 is titled 『雪中梅』. Its companions are stage stories (朝顔日記, 伊賀上野仇討, 宮本無三四伝記…). NDL: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/en/books/R100000039-I883482 . FLAG: I have not confirmed that this 雪中梅 is Tetchō's novel.

### 『一読三歎 当世書生気質』 Tōsei shosei katagi (Tsubouchi Shōyō as 春のやおぼろ)
- **Part-issue format and price.** It came out in parts at **7 sen** each, **three a month**, 17 parts in all, "**1 yen 15 sen** for the whole set, postage included". The parts were sold "at every bookshop and **picture-book shop** (絵双紙屋)". Publisher 晩青堂 (ads 1885-06-25, IA0114475; 1885-08-27, IA0122891).
- **Reprints and colour.** No.1 was reprinted by **1885-08-29** "because of many orders from all quarters" (IA0122900, IA0122907). No.9 (Oct 1885) came "with a beautiful **colour frontispiece**" (IA0123038).
- **Collected editions.** A deluxe two-volume edition (**1 yen 30 sen**, April 1886) had Takada Sanae's critique appended (IA0026443, IA0099361). A one-volume edition followed (1 yen 25 sen, 1887). By **1889** a discount house, 共和書店, sold it at **18 sen** among cheap novels (IA0118272).
- **First review.** 高田早苗 (半峰居士), 「当世書生気質を評す」, in 『中央学術雑誌』 no.21 (dated Jan 1886, delayed to **1886-02-01**; IA0132163, IA0132225). ja.wikipedia, citing the 1926 reprint, gives the title as 「当世書生気質の批評」. Takada called it "**a novel without a hero**", comparing it with Thackeray's *Vanity Fair* (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/当世書生気質).
- **A reader who changed his name.** Medical student 野口清作 changed his name to **英世 (Hideyo)** for fear of being taken for the dissolute medical student 野々口精作 in the novel. Shōyō later called the resemblance coincidence (ja.wikipedia, same page).
- **Students reading male-love fiction, inside the novel.** One of the students, 桐山勉六, a self-styled "hero" and 男色家, is a devoted reader of the Satsuma male-love romance 『賤のおだまき』 (ja.wikipedia, same page).
- **The *Yomiuri* in the picture.** Honda notes that the first edition's frontispiece shows **a geisha reading the *Yomiuri shinbun*** (Honda 1992, p.365).

### 『浮雲』 Ukigumo (Futabatei Shimei, issued under Tsubouchi Yūzō's name)
- **Parts and prices.** Part 1, **55 sen**, Kinkōdō (June 1887; catalogue July 1887, IA0048691). Part 2, **50 sen**, Feb 1888 (IA0115047).
- **Early reviews.**
  - 『出版月評』 (1887-09-25, IA0045222): in a world where every novel carries a label ("political novel, literary novel…"), "this one belongs to **no jurisdiction but fiction's own**".
  - **石橋忍月, 「浮雲の褒貶」**, *Jogaku zasshi* **no.79 (1887-10-08)**, continued in no.80 (10-15) (IA0030991, IA0031027).
  - *Kokumin no tomo* no.16 (1888-02-17) reviewed part 2 under the name **大江逸** (IA0033201; not confirmed here as Tokutomi Sohō's pen name).
  - The *E-iri Jiyū* (1888-02-04, IA0115085): "anyone who reads this and does not praise it may be dismissed with the one word *fool*."
- **Authorship exposed in a review (1888).** The *Kaishin shinbun* reviewer (**1888-08-28**, IA0129542) wrote that when part 1 appeared he had praised it as Harunoya's (Shōyō's) masterpiece. But when he discussed it with Shōyō, Shōyō replied that **he had only lent his name, and the author was Futabatei Shimei, i.e. 長谷川辰之助** (printed as 辰三郎). So the reviewer "now transfers the praise to the Futabatei master".
- **Out of stock, and part 3.** Part 1 was "**long out of stock**" until a reprint in **Aug 1888**, sold "at bookshops or picture-book shops" (IA0129364). Part 3 was serialized in *Miyako no hana* from no.18 (**1889-07-07**) (IA0040569).
- **A traditionalist's verdict.** 依田学海's long review, 「浮雲の評」, *Shuppan geppyō* no.25, **1889-10-25** (IA0058403), opens by contrasting it with "our old fiction, which makes one man embody all eight virtues and another all evil".
- **A hostile pamphlet.** In 1888, 鉄筆将軍's 『明治之細君』 (2nd edition reviewed 1888-12-04) "**reviles every novel in the world to death**, capturing *Shosei katagi*, *Ukigumo* and 『一夜ぎり』 alive". The reviewer mocks the author for printing his own portrait and a **marriage advertisement** at the back (IA0129906).
- **Title borrowing.** A novel called 『月愁花怨 浮雲巷』 (35 sen, 1889/90; IA0058584).

### Kōyō's breakthrough: 『二人比丘尼色懺悔』 (April 1889)
- **Format.** No.1 of 吉岡書籍店's 『新著百種』, a **one-novel-per-issue** magazine modelled on Western "library" series, **12 sen**, preface by Shōyō, pictures by 芳年, 省亭 and 緑芽 (IA0039903, IA0044539).
- **Reviews.**
  - *Kokumin no tomo* nos.48–49 (Apr–May 1889), by "福洲学人" (Ishibashi Ningetsu's pen name).
  - *Shuppan geppyō* (1889-04-25).
  - 「色懺悔盲評」 by 石橋思案 in *Bunko* no.20.
  - A letter in *Shuppan geppyō* (1889-05-22) by 京童子 calls Kōyō "**the Fukusuke of the literary world, the Konishiki of the art world**", comparing him to a star onnagata and a top sumo wrestler (IA0045491).
  - 『新著百種』 no.2 (June 1889) printed **critiques of 『色懺悔』 as its supplement** (IA0040417).
- **Plot (two women).** Two young women, the widow and the fiancée of the same dead samurai, meet as nuns. A later ad for Kōyō's next book quotes *Kokumin no tomo* as calling it "**the best thing since 『色懺悔』**" (IA0041176).

### 『藪の鶯』 (Tanabe Kaho, Kinkōdō, June 1888)
- **Reception.** Yagi: "because a 19-year-old schoolgirl of the Tokyo Higher Girls' School wrote it, **many newspapers and magazines took it up and praised it**, and it went through printings". It was the second novel by a woman in book form, after 中島湘煙's 『善悪の岐』 (Jogaku zasshi-sha, 1887) (Yagi 2003, p.11). Fee and other details: file 11 §6.
- **A critic's verdict.** In 「閨秀小説家の答を読む」 (*Kokumin no tomo*, **1890-04-13**), Ningetsu called it "the source that **roused women writers** and brought about today" (Yagi 2003, p.11).

### Hototogisu (for scale, 1900)
- **Sales.** 『小説 不如帰』, Min'yūsha, **1900-01-15**.
  - First printing **1,000 copies**, sold out within a month.
  - **10,000** in two years.
  - By Roka's death in 1927, **190 printings / 500,000 copies**.
- **Readers' responses.** Takahama Kyoshi's haiku 「小説に涙を落とす火鉢かな」 ("over a novel, dropping tears onto the brazier"). Lovers called each other "Takeo" and "Namiko" (ja.wikipedia 不如帰 says "武男と信子"; see Uncertain). The model family (the Mishimas) resented the book.
- Source: ja.wikipedia 不如帰 (小説), citing 神立春樹 1991 and 竇新光 2014: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/不如帰_(小説)

### Reading aloud, reading circles, boarding houses
- **Maeda Ai's thesis.** Maeda Ai's 「音読から黙読へ」 (in 『近代読者の成立』, 1973) argues from readers' responses that *Keikoku bidan* and *Kajin no kigū* still belonged to a culture of reading aloud and communal performance, while *Ukigumo* and Futabatei's 「あひびき」 created a new, modern, private reading. This summary is from the 1974 review by 木村幸雄, 『日本文学』 23(2) (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/nihonbungaku/23/2/23_KJ00010011870/_article/-char/ja/). English translation: "From Communal Performance to Solitary Reading", in *Text and the City* (Duke UP, 2004), ed. James A. Fujii, p.223. FLAG: I could not open the essay itself, and found **no open source quoting a dated anecdote** of students chanting *Kajin no kigū*'s verses (see Uncertain).
- **A fictional reading club (1890).** Rohan's 「日くらし物語」 (*Yomiuri*, **1890-04-08 to 29**) depicts a 「小説博聞会」. Each member reads aloud a piece he has brought; the company judges it; the loser must write a confession and be **struck three times on the back with a bamboo whip**. The judges spare the woman writer 「繍蓮女史ゆかり式部」, and only "Rohan" is punished (Yagi 2003, note 6).
- **Schoolgirls' fiction clubs (1890).** In 「黒衣魔」 (『日本之文華』 nos.4–5, 1890), Iwaya Sazanami has his novelist-hero say he read in the paper that "**at a certain girls' school five or six students have formed a group and are making a fiction magazine, or holding a fiction-study society**" (「小説研究会」) (Yagi 2003, pp.17–18).
- **A satire of a novel-mad girl (1891).** 秋城居士's 「小説娘」 (*Miyako no hana* nos.67–69, Sept–Oct 1891): a bookseller's daughter advertises that she will **marry whoever writes a novel to her taste**. "Novel fever" spreads to girls' schools, where pupils "gossip about her and critique novels whenever they meet" (Yagi 2003, p.19).
- **Shōyō's reading-aloud society (1891).** Shōyō founded a **朗読研究会 (reading-aloud study group)** with students of the Tōkyō Senmon Gakkō literature course. It was Japan's first study of the art of reading aloud and a seed of the 文芸協会 theatre movement (Sekai, 朗読, https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9C%97%E8%AA%AD-662823).
- **The word 輪読.** Kokugo Daijiten's earliest literary example of **輪読** (taking turns to read and discuss a text) is from Uchida Roan's 『社会百面相』 (1902): a "woman scholar" going "to Miss ABC's for **a Shakespeare reading circle**" (「ミス、ヱビシイの許へ沙翁劇の輪読をしに行く」) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%BC%AA%E8%AA%AD-660163).
- **Who read what, by town (late 1890s; Nagamine 1990, pp.17–18, from readers' letters in the youth magazine 『文庫』).**
  - **Nagasaki:** 『太陽』 went to officials and teachers; 『中学世界』 to middle-school and normal students; 『女学雑誌』 and 『女子之友』 to **schoolgirls**; the literary magazines 『文芸倶楽部』 and 『新小説』 "sell mostly to **medical students, petty clerks, and geisha and prostitutes**" (「医学生俗吏芸娼妓に多く買はれる」).
  - **Kyoto:** higher-school students read 『帝国文学』 and 『早稲田文学』.
- **Magazines in Tokyo girls' homes (1897).** A June 1897 survey of all **361 pupils** of a Tokyo prefectural higher girls' school counted the magazines taken in their households: 『女鑑』 114, 『少年世界』 92, 『太陽』 64, 『女学雑誌』 9 (OCR of the table is partly unreadable). A 1899 survey at the same school found the pupils' own favourites were 『女子之友』 (70+) and 『女鑑』 (50+), "and hardly any others". 山川菊栄's family borrowed 『太陽』 monthly from relatives, and her elder siblings discussed Keigetsu and Chogyū. A reader from Higo recalled his uncle scolding him for reading 『太陽』: "at your age, reading such things will keep you from your studies" (Nagamine 1990, pp.19–21).

## 3. The new criticism, 1885–1900: dates and venues

### Ken'yūsha and *Garakuta bunko* (1885)
- **Founding.** Ken'yūsha was founded **February 1885** by Ozaki Kōyō, Yamada Bimyō and 石橋思案 (students at the University Preparatory School) with 丸岡九華 (Sekai, quoted in https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%88%91%E6%A5%BD%E5%A4%9A%E6%96%87%E5%BA%AB-47070 ).
- **The magazine.** 『我楽多文庫』 began **May 1885** as a **hand-copied circulating magazine**. It became a printed, not-for-sale magazine from no.9 (**Nov 1886**) and went on public sale from **May 1888** (renumbered from 1). It was renamed 『文庫』 in **March 1889** and ended with no.27 in **October 1889**: **43 issues** in all.
- **Contents.** It printed fiction, kanshi, senryū, kyōka, dodoitsu, even rakugo, plus group reviews (合評). It is called the first Japanese literary 同人雑誌 (Nipponica, Sekai, Yamakawa; same Kotobank page).

### 『小説神髄』 and 「小説総論」 (1885–86)
- **Publication.** It was due out in March 1885, but the first publisher (東京稗史出版社) failed. It appeared in **9 parts at 7 sen** from 松月堂, **Sept 1885 – April 1886**. Parts 1–2 are catalogued Sept 1885 at 14 sen (IA0096961) and part 9 in May 1886 (IA0099771). A combined two-volume edition came May 1886; a second edition, 2 vols, **63 sen**, in 1887 (IA0049576) (Nipponica: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%B0%8F%E8%AA%AC%E7%A5%9E%E9%AB%84-79441 ; Honda 1992, p.367).
- **Key phrases** (Yamakawa, same page):
  - current fiction is "the dregs of Bakin and Tanehiko, or fakes of Ikku and Shunsui" (「馬琴種彦の糟粕ならずは一九春水の贋物」);
  - fiction must be rescued from being treated as "**playthings of women and children**" (「婦女童幼の玩弄物」);
  - 「小説の主脳は人情なり、世態風俗これに次ぐ」 ("the heart of the novel is human feeling; manners and customs come next").
- **A reader's sense of its novelty.** Sekai (Maeda Ai) quotes Shinzui's call to make "inner feelings invisible on the surface plainly visible" (「外面に見えざる衷情をあらはに外面に見えしむべし」).
- **Futabatei's response.** Futabatei called on Shōyō in **January 1886** to set out his own views. At Shōyō's urging he published **「小説総論」 in 『中央学術雑誌』, 1886**. It distinguishes 形 (form) and 意 (idea): the novel should express the idea through depicted forms. It criticises 勧善懲悪 (reward-good-punish-evil) (Sekai, quoted in https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%B0%8F%E8%AA%AC%E7%B7%8F%E8%AB%96-1339609 ; ja.wikipedia 小説総論).
- **Ōgai on Shinzui.** In 「『志がらみ草紙』の本領を論ず」 (Oct 1889) Ōgai judged that no one had "grasped how to apply" Shinzui and that it left readers "having got Long wishing for Shu" (unsatisfied) (Ogura 1986, p.17).

### *Kokumin no tomo* (1887) and its literary supplements
- **Founding and format.** Founded by Tokutomi Sohō's Min'yūsha, **February 1887**. The title comes from the American *Nation*. The cover reads "THE NATION'S FRIEND / 政治社会経済及文学之評論". Its literary section was **「藻塩草」**. It ended Aug 1898 with **no.372** (Kotobank: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9B%BD%E6%B0%91%E4%B9%8B%E5%8F%8B-64285).
- **Price and frequency.** 8 sen, twice monthly (1888); 6 sen, three times monthly (from 1889). Ads: IA0035022, IA0039597.
- **Circulation.**
  - **Peak 20,000** (Sekai). Nagamine: usually "**around 7,000**", whereas political magazines normally sold "a few thousand".
  - A Sept 1894 trade note ranked 『少国民』 at about 40,000, then 『国民之友』, 『幼年雑誌』 and 『家庭雑誌』.
  - After 1895 it collapsed: even the summer supplement fell to the 6,000s. It went monthly at **15 sen** in Sept 1897 (Nagamine 1990, pp.2–5).
- **The supplements.** The **New Year and summer literary supplements**, begun **January 1889**, "drew the literary world's notice and were imitated by other magazines" (Yagi 2003, citing Nagamine 1997).
  - 1889 New Year supplement (no.37, **1889-01-02**): Bimyō's 「蝴蝶」 with Watanabe Seitei's nude (file 12).
  - **Summer supplement no.58 (1889-08-02)**, "40-odd pages": the translated poems **『於母影』** (by "S.S.S.", Ōgai's group) with an illustration by **Harada Naojirō** engraved by **合田清**; Aeba Kōson's 「良夜」; Morita Shiken's essay; and 北邨散士's (Kitamura Tōkoku's) 「流転」 (ad IA0043105).
  - ja.wikipedia (citing 十川信介) notes that **appearing in a *Kokumin no tomo* literary supplement was "proof of first-rank status"** (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/山田美妙).
  - Ōgai's 「舞姫」 appeared in *Kokumin no tomo*, **January 1890** (Kotobank 舞姫論争).
- **Min'yūsha print runs (Dec 1889).** 『平民政治』 sold its **first printing of 1,500 in under 20 days**, and a second 1,500 was printed. The John Bright biography had a first printing of **2,000**, sold out (IA0044288).

### *Jogaku zasshi*, Iwamoto Yoshiharu, and women's fiction (1885–1892)
- **Early views (1886).** Iwamoto complained that the fiction women liked to read offered nothing but "men lost in lust, or women who sell themselves for them … **mere instruments of lewdness**" (no.27, 1886). He urged **women themselves to write better novels** (no.32, 1886). In 「女子と文筆の業」 (nos.79–80, Oct 1887) he listed women writers and editors of East and West and attacked Article 7 of the Press Ordinance for limiting newspaper owners and editors to men (Okada 2009, pp.243–244).
- **Who read it.** In 1886 the magazine's first prize-essay contest on "women's reform" drew **101 entries, 15 from women** (no.34, 1886), evidence of many male readers. From **1888-08-04** its cover imitated ***Godey's Lady's Book*** (Okada 2009, p.243).
- **A woman writer's restraint (1888).** 中島俊子 (Kishida Toshiko), 「婦人の文章」 (no.128, 1888): women's books "should not plainly depict the doings and talk of **geisha and palanquin-bearers**". Women should keep "the brush of elegance and purity" (Okada 2009, p.245).
- **An editorial on women novelists (March 1889).** 「女流小説家の本色」, *Jogaku zasshi* no.153 (dated 1889-03-06 in Yagi; see Uncertain). It lists the women who had published: 湘煙, 花圃, 秋月女史 (大島千代子), 曙女史 (木村曙 / 岡本栄子), 竹柏園女史. It ends: "**schoolgirls in a hurry to become novelists are especially numerous**" (「早く小説家たらんずる女学生特に多し」) (Yagi 2003, p.12).
- **A survey of women novelists and a reply (1890).** *Jogaku zasshi* nos.205–209 (Mar–Apr 1890) printed answers from five women writers (小金井きみ, 木村栄子, 若松しづ子, 竹柏園女史, 田辺龍子), titled 「閨秀小説家答」. Ningetsu reviewed it in *Kokumin no tomo* (**1890-04-13**) (Yagi 2003, p.11).
- **The 1889 "Literature and Nature" debate, in order** (Ogura 1983, pp.41–42):
  1. Iwamoto, 「姦淫の空気、不純潔の空気」, *Jogaku zasshi* no.150, **1889-02-23**.
  2. 森林太郎 (Ōgai), 「売笑の利害」, 『衛生新誌』 no.1, **1889-03-25**: "the advocates of abolishing licensed prostitution rate human beings too highly."
  3. *Jiji shinpō* editorial 「市川団十郎」, **1889-03-30**, praising Danjūrō for refusing to play the courtesan Okaru as degrading.
  4. 「市川団十郎と衛生新誌」, *Jogaku zasshi* no.156, 1889-04-06.
  5. Tokutomi Sohō (unsigned), 「言論の不自由と文学の発達」, and "局外生" (Ishibashi Ningetsu), 「時事新報と女学雑誌に質す」, both *Kokumin no tomo* no.48, **1889-04-22**.
  6. しのぶ (Iwamoto), 「国民之友第四十八号文学と自然」, *Jogaku zasshi* no.159, **1889-04-27**: 「最大の文学は自然の儘に自然を写し得たるもの也。極美の美術なるものは決して不徳と伴ふことを得ず」 ("the greatest literature copies nature as it is; supreme art can never go with immorality").
  7. Ōgai, 「『文学ト自然』ヲ読ム」, *Kokumin no tomo* no.50, **1889-05-11**.
  8. Iwamoto's reply, *Jogaku zasshi* no.162, 05-18.
  9. Ōgai, 「再び自然崇拝者に質す」, *Kokumin no tomo* no.52, 06-01.
  10. Iwamoto, 「自然崇拝者の答」, and 丸山通一, 「森林太郎君に横槍を呈す」, *Jogaku zasshi* no.165, **1889-06-08**.
- **The 「小説論略」 debate (June–October 1889).** Iwamoto then argued with Uchida Roan and Ishibashi Ningetsu over "idealist" against "realist" fiction (search summaries of a Hiroshima University paper and of Ogura 1984; FLAG, details not read).
- **Uchida Roan's early verdict (1889).** In *Jogaku zasshi* no.151 (**1889-03-02**), Roan wrote "**the bluestocking is about to appear in Japan**" (「ブルユーストッキングは将に日本に顕はれんとす」). He judged a new story by 竹柏園女史 thin in plot and "even weak in probing human feeling, the very strength proper to women" (Yagi 2003, pp.18–19).
- **Roan's satire on ghost-writing (1889).** 「当世文学通」 (*Miyako no hana* no.17, **1889-06-16**) satirises **ghost-writing (代作)** for "girl authors" (Yagi 2003, p.18).

### *Miyako no hana* (1888–93), a commercial fiction magazine that recruited women
- **Run.** Kinkōdō, first issue **1888-10-21**; ended **1893-06-18** with no.109. Editor (主幹) Yamada Bimyō, moved over from the women's magazine 『以良都女』 (founded July 1887, 成美社).
- **Women writers.** It printed women writers almost every issue from no.2 (1888-11-04) to no.12. Garakuta bunko said its looks "make it **must-reading for young ladies**" (「令嬢必読といふ可き体裁なり」).
- **Suspension (1891).** It was suspended June–July 1891 for 風俗壊乱 (corrupting morals) over no.63 (1891-05-17), which carried Bimyō's 「大恥辱」 and 藤本藤蔭's 「やぶだゝみ」. Yagi suggests women writers then kept away from it for a year.
- **First anthology of women's fiction (Dec 1891).** 『閨秀六家撰』 (Kinkōdō) collected 6 stories. Four of the six end in a talented-man/beautiful-woman wedding.
- **Selling Ichiyō (1892–93).** It ran Ichiyō's 「うもれ木」 (nos.95–97, from **1892-11-20**) as "**a master's new work**". It presented 「暁月夜」 (no.101, **1893-02-19**) as "a tale **that does not bend to frivolous love**". Ichiyō blushed at the puffery (diary, 1893-02-22). Tayama Katai recalled editor Fujimoto Tōin saying "**she writes like a man, for a woman**".
- Source: Yagi 2003, passim.

### *Shigarami zōshi* (1889–94) and Ōgai's polemics
- **Ōgai's 「小説論」.** *Yomiuri* New Year supplement, **1889-01-03**; the first Japanese account of Zola's tendency (ja.wikipedia 森鷗外).
- **The magazine.** 『しがらみ草紙』, monthly, first issue **October 1889** (advertised 1889-10-12/25, IA0041209), paid for with the fees from 『於母影』. The title means "**to throw a weir across the stream of the literary world**". **59 issues**, ending **Aug 1894** when Ōgai went to the Sino-Japanese War (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/しがらみ草紙).
  - First issue: the manifesto 「柵草紙の本領を論ず」 (signed S.S.S.); 「演劇改良論者の偏見に驚く」; translations by Ōgai and 小金井きみ子; and a detailed critique of *Kokumin no tomo*'s 「菩提樹畔の逍遥」.
- **Ōgai's survey essays.** 「現代諸家の小説論を読む」 (Nov 1889) and 「明治二十二年批評家の詩眼」 (Jan 1890). The latter answered Shōyō's year-end surveys in the *Yomiuri* (1890-01-13 to 15) (Ogura 1984, p.1).
- **The 「舞姫」 debate (Feb–May 1890).** Ishibashi Ningetsu reviewed 「舞姫」 in **February 1890** under the pen name **気取半之丞**, faulting the hero's weakness of will. Ōgai replied **under the name of a character in the story, 相沢謙吉**, in 「気取半之丞に与ふる書」. Daijisen calls it "the first full-scale modern literary debate in Japan" (https://kotobank.jp/word/「舞姫」論争).
- **Verse reviews of novels (1890).** *Shigarami zōshi* no.6 (March 1890) printed a 「詩月旦」: short kanshi reviewing recent novels by their three-character titles (「浮雲痕」, 「風流仏」, 「黒衣魔」…) (IA0046340).

### The Botsurisō debate (1891–92)
- **Who and where.** Shōyō in 『早稲田文学』 (first issue **October 1891**; organ of the Tōkyō Senmon Gakkō literature course; 156 issues to Oct 1898) against Ōgai in 『しがらみ草紙』 (his 「山房論文」).
  - Shōyō, starting from notes on Shakespeare, praised "没理想" (ideal-less, objective depiction) and inductive criticism.
  - Ōgai, drawing on Hartmann's aesthetics, insisted on ideals and standards of value.
  - Dates: **1891 to 1892** (Daijisen); Sept 1891 – June 1892 (ja.wikipedia しがらみ草紙).
  - Sekai: "known as **the first real literary debate** of modern Japan".
- Kotobank: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B2%A1%E7%90%86%E6%83%B3%E8%AB%96%E4%BA%89-630865 ; Waseda bungaku: https://kotobank.jp/word/早稲田文学 (entries quoted from the fetched page)

### Kitamura Tōkoku, 「厭世詩家と女性」 (1892)
- **Publication.** *Jogaku zasshi* **no.303 (1892-02-06) and no.305 (1892-02-20)** (Aozora Bunko, from 『現代日本文學大系 6』: https://www.aozora.gr.jp/cards/000157/card45237.html).
- **Opening.** 「恋愛は人世の秘鑰なり、恋愛ありて後人世あり、恋愛を抽き去りたらむには人生何の色味かあらむ」 ("**Love is the secret key of human life**; human life exists only after love; take love away and what colour would life have?").
- **Further lines.**
  - 「恋愛は一たび我を犠牲にすると同時に我れなる『己れ』を写し出す明鏡なり」 ("love… is a clear mirror that reflects one's own self").
  - 「男女相愛して後始めて社界の真相を知る」 ("only after a man and a woman love each other does one know society's true face").
  - 「婚姻は人を俗化し了する者なり」 ("marriage makes a person worldly").
  - The famous split between 「想世界」 (the world of imagination) and 「実世界」 (the real world).
  - It even claims that a child "**reading kusazōshi with the help of kana already imagines what love is**".
  - It ends 「嗚呼不幸なるは女性かな」 ("Ah, how unfortunate is woman").
- **Impact.** Sekai: its "bold view of love **shocked the young intellectuals** of the day" (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%8E%AD%E4%B8%96%E8%A9%A9%E5%AE%B6%E3%81%A8%E5%A5%B3%E6%80%A7-1710482 ). ja.wikipedia: the first sentence "shocked 島崎藤村 and 木下尚江". Okada quotes 木下尚江 on the age as one when "**things between men and women were thought somehow dirty**" (Okada 2009, p.246).
- **Aftermath.** In 1893 Tōkoku argued with 山路愛山 (the 人生相渉論争, "does literature engage with life?") and wrote 「内部生命論」. He hanged himself on **1894-05-16** (ja.wikipedia 北村透谷).

### 『文學界』 Bungakukai (1893–98)
- **Run.** Founded **January 1893**. It grew out of the literary "white-cover" issues of *Jogaku zasshi*, which nos.1–4 still published. It ended Jan 1898: **58 issues** plus an extra, 『うらわか草』 (May 1896). Sekai: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%96%87%E5%AD%A6%E7%95%8C-128322
- **First-issue sales.** The **first issue, 1,500 copies, sold out on the day; a reprint of 1,000 sold out within a week**. 小川 (1974), quoted by Okada (2009, p.254), argues that the start of Japanese "pure literature" rested on schoolgirl and *Jogaku zasshi* readers. FLAG: secondary citation; the 小川 work was not identified more exactly.

### 1894: a scandal and a prize
- **Bimyō's scandal.** The *Yorozu chōhō* exposed Yamada Bimyō's affair with the Asakusa geisha 石井留女 (money troubles). His rebuttal turned opinion further against him. **Shōyō** wrote in 『早稲田文学』: 「小説家は実験を名として不義を行ふの権利ありや」 ("**Does a novelist have the right to commit immorality in the name of experiment?**"). Bimyō was isolated from the literary world (ja.wikipedia 山田美妙, citing 塩田良平 1989).
- **Chogyū's prize novel.** 高山樗牛's 『滝口入道』, a tragic love story drawn from the *Heike*, was a winner of the ***Yomiuri*'s prize-novel contest** in **1894**, written while he was a Tokyo Imperial University student (Sekai, https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%AB%98%E5%B1%B1%E6%A8%97%E7%89%9B-18632).

### 1895: *Taiyō*, *Bungei kurabu*, *Teikoku bungaku*
- **Hakubunkan's merger (January 1895).** Hakubunkan merged its magazines and launched **『太陽』** (to Feb 1928), **『文芸倶楽部』** and **『少年世界』**.
  - *Taiyō*'s first issue was advertised as **285,000 copies**. Police statistics give an average of about **100,000** in its first year.
  - The war magazine 『日清戦争実記』 (from Aug 1894) printed **1.2 million copies** in 1894, about 100,000 an issue (Nagamine 1990, pp.2–4).
  - Takayama Chogyū became *Taiyō*'s chief writer (主筆) in **1897** and preached 日本主義 (Japanism) (Sekai, Nipponica).
- **『帝国文学』.** Founded **January 1895** by the 帝国文学会 of the Tokyo Imperial University literature faculty, formed **December 1894**. Ran to January 1920, 296 issues. Academic, "**conservative and enlightening at once**" (Sekai, https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%B8%9D%E5%9B%BD%E6%96%87%E5%AD%A6-100043).
- **New critics' labels, 1895–96.** **観念小説** ("idea novels": Kyōka's 「夜行巡査」, 「外科室」; Bizan's 「書記官」) and **深刻小説 / 悲惨小説** ("dark" or "tragic" novels: Ryūrō's 「黒蜥蜴」, 「今戸心中」). Kokugo's first example of 観念小説 is Ōgai's 「鷸翮掻」 (1896): 「所謂観念小説は狭き世界観若くは或る世界観の一片を写せる小説なり」 ("so-called idea novels depict a narrow world-view, or a fragment of one") (https://kotobank.jp/word/観念小説 , https://kotobank.jp/word/深刻小説).

### The *Bungei kurabu* 「閨秀小説」 special issue (December 1895)
- **The issue.** *Bungei kurabu* vol.1 no.12, special extra number **「閨秀小説」**, **December 1895**: women writers only, including Ichiyō's 「十三夜」 (file 11 §6). Its frontispiece carried **photographs** of 小金井喜美子, 若松賤子 and 樋口一葉 (JapanKnowledge's *Bungei kurabu* index: "小金井喜美子君／若松賎子君／樋口一葉君（＊写真版）（明治28年12月）", https://japanknowledge.com/contents/bungeikurabu/topics.html ).
- **Its effect.** Yagi: the issue, "together with the portrait photographs at its front, **suddenly fanned public interest in 'keishū' (women writers)**" (Yagi 2003, p.26). 関礼子 (『樋口一葉をよむ』, 1992) calls it the moment when "the 'keishū' (female) writer was **for the first time turned into a market category**" (Yagi, note 14).
- **Roan's review.** Uchida Roan, 「『閨秀小説』を評す」, *Mainichi shinbun*, early 1896 (dates uncertain). He recalls how people once laughed at the Ken'yūsha's "monotonous talented-man-and-beauty love stories". He then holds women to a stricter rule: "**when a woman writes of love she needs ten times the caution of a man**… writing frivolous love stories is even more unsightly in a woman" (Yagi 2003, p.26).
- **Sales.** I found **no figure** for this issue's sales or reprints (see Uncertain). The magazine as a whole sold about **30,000 c.1897** (file 11).

### *Mezamashi-gusa* and the 「三人冗語」 (1896)
- **The magazine.** 『めさまし草』, January 1896 – February 1902, **56 issues**, ed. Ōgai; successor to *Shigarami zōshi* (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E3%82%81%E3%81%95%E3%81%BE%E3%81%97%E8%8D%89-141306).
- **The column.** **「三人冗語」**, nos.3–7 (**March–July 1896**): group reviews by Ōgai, Kōda Rohan and Saitō Ryokuu.
  - The format copies the **kabuki actor-critique book (評判記)**: a "頭取" (master of ceremonies, Ōgai) introduces each work, then speakers called **「ひいき」 (the fan)** and **「さし出」 (the butter-in)** argue.
  - Sekai calls it the "first anonymous round-table review". It "**savaged many works**".
  - From no.8 it widened, with 依田学海, 饗庭篁村, 森田思軒 and Kōyō, into 「雲中語」, which "**ruled the critical world**" (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%89%E4%BA%BA%E5%86%97%E8%AA%9E-1170269).
- **Ōgai on 「たけくらべ」 (no.4, 1896)**, after the story was reprinted whole in *Bungei kurabu* on **1896-04-10**. Quoted by Okada (2009, p.253, from 森 1896:48):
  > 「唯だ不思議なるは、この境に出没する人物の…人の形したる畜類ならで、吾人と共に笑ひ共に哭すべきまことの人間なることなり。われは作者が捕へ来りたる原材とその現じ出したる詩趣とを較べ見て、此人の筆の下には、灰を撒きて花を開かする手段を知り得たり」

  In English: "the only wonder is that the people who come and go in this quarter are not beasts in human shape, as Zola and Ibsen are used to writing and the so-called naturalists strive to copy, but **true human beings with whom we may laugh and weep**… comparing the raw material the author has caught with the poetry she has drawn from it, I see that under this person's brush **ashes are scattered and flowers bloom**."

  (Rohan's line about wanting critics and novelists "to swallow five or six characters of this as a charm for improving their craft" is widely quoted but I did not find it in an open source; see Uncertain.)
- **Ichiyō's reaction (diary, 1896).** As quoted by Okada (2009, p.254): the praise came "only because they **see me merely as a woman**… they never point out a flaw, nor say what is good; they only say 'Ichiyō is clever', 'skilful', 'most men should bow their heads to her craft', 'just clever, skilful'. Have they no other words?" (「我れをただ女子と計見るよりのすさび…いとあやしき事ども也」).
- **Ichiyō's salon.** Saitō Ryokuu began corresponding with Ichiyō in **January 1896** and visited her to talk about Edo literature. She wrote that he was "interesting as an enemy; probably more interesting as an ally" (ja.wikipedia 斎藤緑雨).
- **A later re-reading (1985).** The novelist **佐多稲子** proposed in **1985** that Midori's sudden change at the end of 「たけくらべ」 meant **a secret first client (初店)**, not first menstruation, the long-standard reading. **Maeda Ai** answered in defence of the menstruation reading. 瀬戸内晴美, 野口冨士男 and 吉行淳之介 joined in. Both readings still have supporters (ja.wikipedia たけくらべ; 小谷野敦 2010, chapter 「『たけくらべ』論争 1985–2005」: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/たけくらべ).

### Price collapse and the 1887 Publication Ordinance
- **Before the new ordinance.** A bookseller's ad of **1888-01-13** says that, with the new 出版条例 just issued (late December 1887), the "**unprecedented collapse of book prices**" was about to reverse; prices would rise "about 20 %". It offered a last clearance before the rise:
  - *Kajin no kigū*, 7 vols, **1 yen 75 sen**;
  - *Setchūbai*, 2 vols, **82 sen**;
  - other political novels from 16 to 80 sen.
  - IA0033659
- **Copyright as a tradable asset.** The *Keikoku bidan* copyright was sold from Hōchi to a cheap-edition house in 1890 (above). Fake *Setchūbai*s used look-alike covers (above).

### Print runs, as far as found
- **Book runs.**
  - Min'yūsha books, 1889: **1,500** and **2,000** first printings, sold out in weeks (above).
  - *Hototogisu*, 1900: **1,000** first printing; 10,000 in two years (above).
  - *Kajin no kigū* and *Keikoku bidan* advertised multiple "editions" (版) in their first years, 8th and 9th editions by 1889–90. Edition size is unknown; see Uncertain.
- **Magazine runs.**

  - *Taiyō*: ~100,000 average (1895).
  - *Bungei kurabu*: 30,000 (c.1897).
  - *Marumaru chinbun*: 150,000 a year in 1877, 260,000 a year in 1880.

### Lending libraries and second-hand circulation
- See file 11 §4 (Edo's 656 lenders; decline from c.1883; いろは屋貸本店 for students in Kanda, 1886–c.1913; scrapbooked newspaper serials rented at 3 sen and taken round boarding houses in the early 1900s).
- **Students and the general public.** Nipponica: lending libraries "flourished in Meiji too and contributed greatly to popular reading"; **lending libraries for students, renting scholarly books and textbooks**, appeared and were later displaced by public libraries. Popular lenders shifted from carrying books door to door to over-the-counter lending (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%B2%B8%E6%9C%AC%E5%B1%8B-462164).
- **Picture-book shops.** Novels were sold at 絵双紙屋 (picture-book shops) as well as bookshops. *Shosei katagi* and *Ukigumo* ads say so explicitly (IA0122891, IA0129364).

### Girls, women, fiction, and the moralists
- **Novels as women's reading.** Okada: it was "the common understanding of Meiji intellectuals, Iwamoto included, that before Western fiction arrived, **the novel was a despised genre read mainly by women**". Shōyō set out to raise it above "**playthings of women and children**" (Okada 2009, p.243; Yamakawa on 小説神髄).
- **The "harm of novels" as mainstream.** Okada calls 小説有害論 "**the mainstream view in education at the time**" (2009, p.254).
- **A newspaper campaign against schoolgirls (1890).** In February 1890 the *Yomiuri* ran a campaign "**passing sentence**" (筆誅) on **schoolgirls of the Christian girls' schools**: 1890-02-20, 21 and 23–28. It blamed educators ("女学生の醜聞あるは教育家の罪なり", "schoolgirl scandals are the educators' fault"). It sneered that "before you know it, theories of the equality of the sexes and of **free marriage** arise, and so-called refined religious ladies preach abolition of prostitution, laying out before the public the secrets of prostitutes **that even men do not know**" (*Yomiuri* 1890-02-20, quoted in Okada 2009, p.245).
- **"Girl authors" as a joke, 1888–91.** The derisive term **「娘作者」** ("girl author") was in use; see the Garakuta, Sazanami, Roan and 「小説娘」 satires above (Yagi 2003, pp.17–19).
- **The schoolgirl as a sexual cliché (1890s–1900s).** Hiraishi Noriko traces it: in the 1890s, under the "good wife, wise mother" ideal, higher girls' schools multiplied. Schoolgirls came to stand for Western-style urban beauty, but **their longing for Western-style romance and the lax supervision of boarding houses** earned them a reputation for sexual looseness. **Newspaper serials and scandal articles fixed the image** of the schoolgirl as Western-beautiful, assertive and sexually free (平石典子, 「『女学生神話』の誕生を巡って」, 『人文論叢』 (Mie Univ.) 18, 2001-03-25; abstract at https://mie-u.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/1355 ; full text is a scanned image I could not read).
- **The 1906 Ministry instruction** (just after the period, but the end-point of the 1890s worry). 文部省訓令第一号, 「学生生徒ノ風紀振粛ニ関スル件」, **1906-06-09** (MEXT, 『学制百年史』 資料編: https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317975.htm):
  - It opens with concern that "**among young women** spirits are sinking and morals decaying" (「近来青年女子ノ間ニ往々意気銷沈シ風紀頽廃セル傾向アル」). Some students are "tormented by fancies" (空想ニ煩悶シテ).
  - It says that among recent publications and pictures, some "print dangerous arguments, **preach pessimism (厭世ノ思想)**, or depict base conditions".
  - It orders schools to examine what students read, recommend the good, and "strictly forbid, **whether inside or outside school**", whatever may lead to bad results.
  - It does not name 小説 (novels) as such.
- **Women readers as a market (1888–91).** *Miyako no hana* courted "young ladies"; Yomiuri serials appeared under women's names (曙女史 1889–90; 清花女史, 花圃女史, 三茂女史 in 1890). The *Yomiuri*'s women's serials stopped after **July 1890**, when it began running **Kōyō and Rohan side by side** (Yagi 2003, pp.14–15).

## 5. Later scholarship a modern critic would cite

### Japanese
- **柳田泉 Yanagida Izumi, 『政治小説研究』.** 春秋社 / 松柏館, vol.1 **May 1935** … completed **July 1939** (3 vols); reissued 1967–68. The foundational survey of political fiction (NDL: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000136-I1971712334721968299 ). His 「『佳人之奇遇』と東海散士」 is in vol.1. He guessed that Itagaki Taisuke brought back Stepniak's *Underground Russia* in 1882 (file 11).
- **前田愛 Maeda Ai, 『近代読者の成立』.** 有精堂出版, **1973-11-20** (NDL; Kimura's review says 昭和48年12月); reissued in 前田愛著作集 (筑摩, 1989) and 岩波現代文庫 (2001-02, 391 pp.) (NDL: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000094-I1100825 ).
  - Essays include 「明治初期戯作出版の動向」 (woodblock giving way to type), 「明治立身出世主義の系譜」 (readers of 『西国立志編』 and 『学問のすすめ』 across three generations: "fathers", "elder brothers", "younger brothers"), **「音読から黙読へ」**, two essays on Taishō–Shōwa popular readers, and a 「読者論小史」.
  - Argument: modern literature cannot be understood without the history of its readers. *Keikoku bidan* and *Kajin no kigū* were not yet modern novels in how they were read; *Ukigumo* and 「あひびき」 were (review by 木村幸雄, 『日本文学』 1974).
- **山本武利 Yamamoto Taketoshi, 『近代日本の新聞読者層』.** 法政大学出版局, 1981 (5th printing 1985; 436 pp.). Newspaper readership by class and region (NDL: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000001-I1423127801 ). See also his 「明治三十年代前半の新聞読者層」, 『新聞学評論』 16, 1967.

## Telling details

- **Readers' letters already shortened serials in 1878.** The *Kanayomi* cut off 「鳥追お松の伝」 after 14 instalments because "readers are rather bored and we have had scolding letters telling us to wind it up". It promised the rest in an illustrated book, and the book's preface then boasted of the serial's "applause from thousands of streets" (Honda).
- **Publishers stopped hit serials halfway to sell the book.** Kinshōdō asked the *Sakigake* to halt the Kinu serial in 1878. In February 1879 the *Kanayomi* ran two Oden instalments and on the third day printed only an advertisement for Robun's book (Honda).
- **The 60-day race.** Two rival Oden books raced each other; one boasted of finishing all seven parts "in barely sixty days because a rival edition was coming" (Honda).
- **A reader's honest letter.** 「芝いく女」 (1886) wrote that serials made her "sad, longing, glad, envious; nothing is more absorbing", and still asked for Western novels to civilise her. The editor answered that he had tried Western-style serials twice and both times been scolded by readers who found them "boring long tales aping hairy foreigners" (Rinbara 1993).
- **The *Yomiuri* apologised for boring readers.** It ran a murder story at greater length than usual in 1876 and said so: "yesterday we carelessly wrote at length and bored you all" (Honda).
- **Shōyō's first newspaper appearance was a reader's letter** (*Yomiuri*, 1885-05-13), signed "the old recluse Harunoya Oboro of Masago-chō, Hongō". It doubled as an advance notice for 『小説神髄』 (Honda).
- **A Greek history novel read as kabuki.** The *E-iri Jiyū*'s 1883 notice cast *Keikoku bidan* as a kabuki play: Pelopidas the leading man, a 女形 heroine, a comic sidekick, a villain, a love scene, and a "happy grand finale" (IA0006182).
- **Ads built on readers' impatience.** "Readers' demands like a shower of arrows" (*Kajin no kigū*, 1886); "pressing demands like arrows shot at us" (Kōyō, 1889).
- **Kajin no kigū's ad stacked endorsements:** a preface by General Tani Tateki, an afterword by **Kim Ok-kyun** and five newspapers' reviews. The *Jiji* called it "a lament scene played straight through".
- **Within eight months of publication *Setchūbai* had counterfeits** "by a writer of the same surname" with a look-alike publisher's mark, and an Osaka stage version with Ichikawa Udanji (1887).
- **Setchūbai's own ad said it would make women and children political**, and later claimed women loved it for "its tender feelings".
- ***Shosei katagi* sold for 7 sen a part, three a month, at picture-book shops.** No.1 was reprinted within two months. Four years later a discount house sold the whole for 18 sen.
- **Ukigumo's real author was revealed through a review.** The *Kaishin*'s reviewer reported that Shōyō told him "I only lent my name; the author is Futabatei Shimei" (1888).
- **An author answered his critic in the name of his own character.** Ningetsu attacked 「舞姫」 as "Kidori Hannojō"; Ōgai answered as **Aizawa Kenkichi**, a character in the story (1890).
- **Kōyō as kabuki and sumo star.** A reader called him "the Fukusuke of the literary world, the Konishiki of the art world" (1889). His breakthrough 『二人比丘尼色懺悔』 is about two women, the widow and the fiancée of one dead man, who meet as nuns.
- **Reviews as a selling supplement.** 『新著百種』 no.2 printed reviews of no.1 as a bonus (1889).
- **The Sannin jōgo was staged like an actor-critique book**, with "the fan" and "the butter-in" as speakers. Its praise made Ichiyō famous, and she wrote that the critics only saw her "as a woman" and had "no other words than 'clever, skilful'" (1896).
- **The girl-author jokes.**
  - Ken'yūsha members joked in print that a magazine sold better with a woman's pen-name and should "put the author's photograph at the front" (1888).
  - Seven years later *Bungei kurabu* did exactly that, with photographs of three women writers in its "keishū" issue (1895).
  - A man published as "Ashiya Yoshijo" in the *Yomiuri* (1888); Sazanami wrote as two women to mock "girl authors" (1889).
  - Roan satirised ghost-writing for women and hailed "the bluestocking about to appear in Japan" (1889).
- **The *Jogaku zasshi*'s editorial of March 1889** listed every published Japanese woman novelist; there were six. It added that schoolgirls in a hurry to become novelists were "especially numerous".
- **Schoolgirl fiction clubs were reported in the papers by 1890.** Five or six girls at one school were "making a fiction magazine or holding a fiction-study society" (Sazanami, 「黒衣魔」).
- **Rohan imagined a reading club whose worst reader is whipped three times with bamboo.** The woman writer is let off by majority vote (1890).
- **A novel-mad bookseller's daughter offers to marry whoever writes a novel she likes**, and a German doctor of letters lectures on her advertisement (「小説娘」, 1891).
- **In Nagasaki (late 1890s) the literary magazines sold mostly to medical students, petty clerks, and geisha and prostitutes**; girls bought *Jogaku zasshi* (Nagamine).
- **A *Kokumin no tomo* literary supplement was a writer's badge of rank (1889–90).** The magazine itself printed 1,500 and 2,000 copies of its books.
- **Bungakukai's first issue (1893): 1,500 copies sold out on the day**, and the 1,000-copy reprint sold out within a week.
- **Tōkoku's essay** began "Love is the secret key of human life" in a Christian women's magazine (1892), at a time when, as Kinoshita Naoe put it, things between men and women were "thought somehow dirty".
- **Takada Sanae's first review called *Shosei katagi* "a novel without a hero"** (1886). A real medical student, Noguchi Seisaku, changed his name to Hideyo to escape the resemblance to the novel's dissolute 野々口精作.
- **The *Yomiuri*'s 1890 campaign against Christian schoolgirls** linked them to sex-equality talk, "free marriage" and abolitionist ladies who "lay out the secrets of prostitutes that even men do not know".
- **The 1906 Ministry instruction worried about "young women"** whose spirits were sinking. It ordered schools to police students' reading "inside or outside school".
- **Late readers still fight over the classics.** In 1985 a novelist (Sata Ineko) and a scholar (Maeda Ai) fought publicly over what happened to Midori at the end of 「たけくらべ」, and others joined in. Both readings still have champions.

## Uncertain / conflicting

- **Date of 『近代読者の成立』.** NDL gives 有精堂出版 **1973-11-20**; 木村幸雄's 1974 review says 昭和48年**12**月.
- **Robun's 『高橋阿伝夜叉譚』.** Honda (1992) says 初編～八編, **34 volumes**, 1879-02-13 to 04-22. File 11 cites an NDL record of **24 volumes**, Meiji 12–13. Not reconciled; the NDL count may reflect the holdings of one set.
- ***Marumaru chinbun*'s first issue.** 1877-03-14 or 03-24 (ja.wikipedia gives both). The 150,000 / 260,000 figures are **annual copies**, not per issue.
- **Ken'yūsha's founding.** February 1885 (Sekai) vs May 1885 (Yamakawa, which dates the society together with the first manuscript magazine).
- ***Garakuta bunko* numbering and issue counts.** Accounts differ on numbering, but all give 43 issues in total.
- **Takada's review title.** 「当世書生気質を評す」 (1886 advertisements) vs 「当世書生気質の批評」 (ja.wikipedia and the 1926 reprint; 明治文學全集 79).
- ***Jogaku zasshi* no.153 and "1889-03-06".** Yagi dates 「女流小説家の本色」 to no.153, 明治22年3月6日. But no.151 was dated 1889-03-02 in the same paper, and the magazine was then weekly (no.150 = 02-23), which would make no.153 c.03-16. The OCR may have dropped a digit.
- **Uchida Roan's 「『閨秀小説』を評す」.** The OCR of Yagi's note reads "明二九・一・三1-．八 『毎日新聞』". Possibly 1896-01-31 to 02-08, or 01-03 to 01-08. Unresolved.
- **Sales of the December 1895 「閨秀小説」 issue.** Not found. Secondary literature (Seki Reiko 1992; 小川昌子 1999; 菅聡子 1999) discusses it as a market event; no copy figure was located.
- **Who wrote under 大江逸** (Kokumin no tomo's 1888 Ukigumo review)? Possibly Tokutomi Sohō, who used 大江逸郎 early on. Not verified.
- **Rohan's "霊符" remark and Ryokuu's lines in 「三人冗語」.** Widely quoted, but not found in an open source. Ōgai's praise is quoted here from Okada 2009, itself citing the original (森 1896:48). Wording should be checked against 『めさまし草』 no.4 or a modern edition.
- **Students reciting *Kajin no kigū*.** That young men chanted its kanshi-style passages is a commonplace (Maeda Ai and others), but I found **no open primary anecdote with date and place**. Treat any specific scene as invention.
- **Rindokukai (reading circles) in the 1880s.** No dated 1880s example was found. Kokugo's first literary example of 輪読 is 1902 (Roan). The 1890 report of schoolgirl "fiction-study societies" comes from inside a novel (Sazanami's 「黒衣魔」), which says it was "seen in the newspapers".
- **The 1887 Kyoto miniature picture book 『雪中梅』.** Its identity with Tetchō's novel is unconfirmed.
- **竹紫諺蔵 and the 1887 Osaka stage version of *Setchūbai*.** The name and theatre come from the publisher's advertisement, which reports it as hearsay ("と聞く"). No playbill found.
- **ja.wikipedia's *Hototogisu* claim** that lovers called each other "武男" and "信子". Nobuko was the real model's name, Namiko the heroine's. Possibly a slip in the cited secondary work (竇新光 2014); check.
- **The *Kokumin no tomo* 20,000 peak** (Sekai) vs Nagamine's "around 7,000, fairly stable". The 20,000 probably refers to supplement or peak issues.
- **Bungakukai's 1,500 + 1,000.** Known only from 小川 (1974) as quoted by Okada; the original source was not seen.
- **The 「小説論略」 debate (June–October 1889).** Known only from search-result summaries of a Hiroshima University paper (repository moved) and of Ogura; the details and the participants' texts were not read.
- **Marumaru chinbun cartoons on specific novels.** None verified. If the alt-history needs one, invent it, but note that real *Marumaru* satire of the 1880s aimed chiefly at officials and politics. Its sister *Kibi dango* parodied the poison-woman serial genre itself (「今姐己於百伝」, 1879).
