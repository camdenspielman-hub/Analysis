## 1. Serial to stage: speed, freedom, and the author's role

### 1a. The pattern before 1880 (Edo precedent)
- **Kabuki dramatised novels and storytelling long before Meiji.**
  - Heibonsha: early-Edo playwrights disliked dramatising novels or kōdan and insisted on the stage's autonomy.
  - From the 文化 era (1804–18) on, this changed. Stage versions were made of 山東京伝's yomihon 『昔話稲妻表紙』, 馬琴's 『南総里見八犬伝』, 『自来也説話』, the gōkan 『しらぬひ譚』, the kōdan of 松林伯円, and Enchō's ninjōbanashi.
  - Heibonsha adds: 「明治期には新聞小説の脚色が流行した」.
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/脚色
- **Newspaper serials on stage from about 1877.** Nipponica: 「1877年（明治10）ころからは新聞連載の評判小説の劇化も盛んになった」. Britannica: in the Meiji 30s (1897–1906), shinpa often staged dramatisations of newspaper novels. https://kotobank.jp/word/脚色
- **Hakkenden on stage while the novel was still coming out.**
  - First staging: October 1834, Osaka 若太夫芝居 『金花山雪曙』. It flopped.
  - Then 『花魁莟八総』 (8 acts, 西沢一鳳), Osaka 中の芝居, January–April 1836.
  - Then a jōruri version, July 1836, which was 「ほぼ原作の筋に近い」. The wording implies the others were not close to the book.
  - Edo première: April 1836, 森田座.
  - The novel itself was finished only in 1842.
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/南総里見八犬伝
- **『偐紫田舎源氏』 (1829–42).**
  - Itself a 翻案 of Genji moved to the Muromachi period.
  - Spawned 「類似擬古典作」 and a fashion for Genji-e nishiki-e.
  - Was staged as the dance 『名夕顔雨の旧寺』.
  - Was banned in 1842 and left unfinished (parts 39–40 survive only as manuscript).
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/偐紫田舎源氏

### 1b. Crime news → serial → kabuki in weeks or months (1877–1888)
- **Takahashi Oden 高橋お伝: the fastest documented chain.**
  - Sentenced and beheaded 31 Jan 1879.
  - From 1 Feb, the small papers (仮名読新聞, 有喜世新聞) all ran gesaku-style stories about her. Street sellers sang her story as a kudoki song. ja.wp calls this the start of 「毒婦物」.
  - Serials: 「毒婦高橋お伝」, 東京曙新聞, 1–7 Feb 1879; 「毒婦お伝のはなし」, 東京絵入新聞, 1–21 Feb 1879.
  - Books (NDL catalogue):
    - 『邪婦高橋お傳の物語』 (吉田清三郎, 1879.3) https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000136-I1971149384746720549
    - 仮名垣魯文 『高橋阿傳夜刄譚』 (金松堂, ill. 守川周重, 1879) https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000136-I1970304959941488920
    - 岡本勘造 『東京奇聞 其名も高橋 毒婦の小傳』 (1879) https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000136-I1971993809807719833
  - **Kabuki:** 河竹新七 (Mokuami) 『綴合於伝仮名書』, 新富座, May 1879 (明治12.5), Kikugorō V as Oden. Heibonsha: "four months after the execution". https://kotobank.jp/word/綴合於伝仮名書
  - **In the same month, two rival picture-book versions of the play (正本写) were published:**
    - one by 篠田仙果 with 楊洲周延 (山村金三郎, 明治12.5 届);
    - one by 武田交来 with 3rd 国貞 and 周延 (錦栄堂, preface 1879.5);
    - plus 豊原国周 prints of the production.
    - Sources: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R000000025-I013010005864292 ; https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000136-I1970586434803804714 ; https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000136-I1971149384851060163
  - **The legend grew in retelling.** Heibonsha: in later 読物 and novels by 町川黙鶯, 矢田挿雲 and 邦枝完二, "Oden the poisoner" was steadily enlarged. Invented victims were added: her father, her husband 波之助 and others. https://kotobank.jp/word/高橋阿伝夜叉譚
  - **The theatre paid for her grave.** Kan'ya XII, Kikugorō V, Sadanji I, Enchō and 3rd Enshō funded a Yanaka grave in April 1881, reportedly in thanks for their Oden hits. Even today shamisen students visit it. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/高橋お伝
  - **Films by 1940:** 福宝堂 1912; 『お伝地獄』 in three parts (Shōchiku 下加茂, 1925); 1926 (中央映画社); 1929 (河合映画); 1935 (新興キネマ). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/高橋お伝
- **Torioi Omatsu 鳥追お松.** 久保田彦作's serial in the 仮名読新聞 (1877–78) was staged in April 1878. Heibonsha and Nipponica treat this serial as a prototype of the newspaper novel. https://kotobank.jp/word/新聞小説 ; file 16.
- **Hanai Oume 花井お梅.**
  - She stabbed her employee 峰吉 on 9 June 1887.
  - The 東京絵入新聞 serialised a half-invented 「花井於梅酔月奇聞」.
  - Part of it became a shinnai-bushi, 『梅雨衣酔月情話』 (5th 富士松加賀太夫), March 1888.
  - Mokuami's kabuki 『月梅薫朧夜』 opened at the 中村座 in April 1888, about 10 months after the crime.
  - After her pardon (明治36 = 1903) Oume toured, **acting her own murder on stage**.
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/花井お梅
- **Genre name.** Heibonsha (毒婦物): these Meiji "true stories" of Omatsu, Oden and Oume were staged as 毒婦物 / 悪婆物, continuing the Edo stock type of the "wicked woman" role. https://kotobank.jp/word/毒婦物

### 1c. Novels and foreign stories onto the Meiji stage (1872–1890)
- **『西洋道中膝栗毛』.**
  - 仮名垣魯文's sequel-parody of 一九 (1870–76; parts 12–15 by 総生寛). The grandsons of Yaji and Kita go to the London Exhibition.
  - Staged in July 1872 at the 村山座, with Robun credited as author and Kawatake Shinshichi II as assistant.
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/西洋道中膝栗毛
- **Self-Help on stage (Kyoto, 1872).** 『鞋補童教学』 by 佐橋富三郎 (book form 1873). https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000136-I1971993809712278167 ; file 16.
- **Mokuami adapted Bulwer-Lytton's *Money*** as 『人間万事金世中』 (1879). Heibonsha says he also planned a *Hamlet* adaptation in 1879. https://kotobank.jp/word/月梅薫朧夜
- **Shakespeare, 1885.**
  - 『何桜彼桜銭世中』 (*The Merchant of Venice*, adapted by 宇田川文海, staged by 勝諺蔵) opened in May 1885 (明治18.5) at the 戎座, Osaka, with the 中村宗十郎 company.
  - Heibonsha calls it the first Shakespeare performance in Japan.
  - Its source was 井上勤's translation from Lamb's *Tales*, 『人肉質入裁判』 (1883).
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/何桜彼桜銭世中
- **『当世書生気質』 (1885–86).**
  - Issued in 17 numbered fascicles by 晩青堂, from June 1885 to January 1886.
  - Staged in August 1887 (明治20.8) at Osaka's 中の芝居, with 坪内逍遙 as author and 勝諺蔵 as 補作者.
  - Heibonsha credits 勝諺蔵's stagings of novels with influencing shinpa's later "novel-dramatisation movement".
  - Sources: https://kotobank.jp/word/当世書生気質 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/何桜彼桜銭世中 ; fascicle dates https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000140-I156759 and following records.
- **Political novels.**
  - 『経国美談』 (前編 March 1883, 後編 February 1884, 報知社). Heibonsha: 「講談や演劇にもなった」. https://kotobank.jp/word/経国美談
  - Kawakami Otojirō's 書生芝居 opened in February 1891 at Sakai's 卯の日座 with 『経国美談』 and 『板垣君遭難実記』. https://kotobank.jp/word/壮士芝居
- **Enchō onto the stage.**
  - First: 『業平文辞松達摂』, 春木座, April 1879. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/三遊亭圓朝
  - 『西洋噺日本写絵』 (from Enchō's *Hard Cash* adaptation, see 2a): Mokuami, 新富座, January 1886 (明治19.1). https://kotobank.jp/word/西洋噺日本写絵
  - 『塩原多助一代記』: Kabuki-za, January 1892 (3rd 河竹新七, Kikugorō V). https://kotobank.jp/word/塩原多助一代記
  - 『怪異談牡丹燈籠』: Kabuki-za, July 1892. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/牡丹灯籠

### 1d. Shinpa and the newspaper novel (1895–1910)
- **The first newspaper-novel dramatisation by a new-theatre troupe.**
  - Kawakami's 『滝の白糸』 (from 鏡花's 『義血侠血』), 浅草座, 4 Dec 1895 (the abstract misprints "1985").
  - Inoue counts 金色夜叉, 己が罪, 無花果, 不如帰 and 婦系図 as the repertoire that "established" the new theatre.
  - Source: 井上理恵, KIUI bulletin abstract, https://kiui.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/1018/files/KJ00004881170.pdf
- **『金色夜叉』 was staged while the serial was still running.**
  - The serial ran 1 Jan 1897 – 11 May 1902 in six runs, totalling 236 instalments (ja.wp counts 32+52+49+38+51+14).
  - First staged March 1898 at the 市村座 (Nipponica, Britannica). Inoue: by the Kawakami troupe, from the end of March to about mid-April 1898.
  - So it reached the stage **15 months** after the serial began, with **four more years** of the novel still to come.
  - It became a staple 当り芸 of 伊井蓉峰 and 高田実.
  - Sources: https://kotobank.jp/word/金色夜叉 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/金色夜叉 ; Inoue abstract above.
- **How the stage rewrote Konjiki yasha.**
  - The line everyone "remembers", 「来年の今月今夜のこの月を僕の涙で曇らせてみせる」, is **the stage and film simplification**. Kōyō's text is a long, broken speech: 「来年の今月今夜になつたならば、僕の涙で必ず月は曇らして見せるから、月が……月が……月が……」.
  - The famous kick: Kōyō never says what Kan'ichi wore on his feet. The first-edition kuchi-e (武内桂舟, 『金色夜叉 前編』, 春陽堂, July 1898) shows **leather shoes**. The 1985 Atami statue shows **geta**.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/金色夜叉
- **『不如帰』: first a loose version, then a "faithful" one the author liked.**
  - Serial: 国民新聞, 29 Nov 1898 – 24 May 1899. Book: 民友社, 15 Jan 1900.
  - First staged February 1901, Osaka 朝日座, adapted by 並木萍水, with 秋月桂太郎 (Takeo) and 喜多村緑郎 (Namiko).
  - Britannica: 柳川春葉 later wrote 「原作に忠実な脚本」. Kitamura played it at the 本郷座 in April 1908 to the praise 「原作者をはじめ多くの人々」. 真山青果 later made another version.
  - Kabuki version: 東京座, September 1904 (補作 竹柴晋吉).
  - Sources: https://kotobank.jp/word/不如帰-133743 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/不如帰_(小説)
- **『婦系図』: the stage version changed the novel.** The 湯島境内 scene was invented for the stage (Shintomi-za, 1908), and Kyōka later wrote it himself. Nipponica via file 16: https://kotobank.jp/word/婦系図-42006
- **Kikuchi Yūhō 菊池幽芳.**
  - 『己が罪』 (大阪毎日, 1899–1900; book 1900–01, 春陽堂) ends happily: the heroine's devotion restores the marriage. https://kotobank.jp/word/己が罪
  - 『乳姉妹』 (1903) went to kabuki at the 東京座, January 1905 (明治38.1), credited to 菊池幽芳 as author. https://kotobank.jp/word/乳姉妹
- **Playbills credit the publisher.** A 1909 新富座 playbill reads: 「紅葉山人原著 春陽堂發行 小栗風葉氏脚色 第壹番目 金色夜叉 七幕七場 柳川春葉氏補作」. https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000002-I032279182
  - An earlier 宮戸座 bill (c. 1902) credits 「紅葉山人原作 花房栁外脚案 金色夜叉 十五場」. https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000002-I032017410
- **Were authors asked or paid?** I found no source giving fees for any Meiji dramatisation.
  - The documented behaviour: playbills name author, publisher and adapter; authors sometimes collaborated (Kyōka) or praised the result (Roka in 1908); Kōyō's own disciples (風葉, 春葉) wrote the authorised-looking versions.
  - The law gave novelists **no performance right** (see section 4), so permission was courtesy or contract, not legal necessity.
  - Cross-reference (file 16): Hōgetsu's 「カチューシャの唄」 earned no royalties (1914).

## 2. Oral and print spin-offs

### 2a. San'yūtei Enchō's 三遊亭圓朝 adaptations and the sokkibon
- **Enchō's Western adaptations (翻案物).** Main source: 閑田朋子 2018 (Nihon Univ. 研究紀要 96), cited on ja.wp.

  | Title | Year | Source |
  |---|---|---|
  | 『西洋人情噺 英国孝子ジョージスミス之伝』 | 1885 | Charles Reade, *Hard Cash* (1863) |
  | 『松の操美人の生埋』 | 1886 | Dumas père, *Pauline* (1838); plot told to him by 福地桜痴 |
  | 『蝦夷錦古郷の家土産』 | 1886 | Wilkie Collins, *The New Magdalen* (1873) |
  | 『名人長二』 | 1887 | Maupassant, "Un parricide" (1882) |
  | 『欧州小説 黄薔薇』 | 1887 | Dumas fils, *Le roman d'une femme* (1849) |
  | 『錦の舞衣』 / 『名人競』 | 1891 | Sardou, *La Tosca* (1887) |

  - A posthumous 『英国女王イリザベス伝』 (1923) is said to come from Scott's *Kenilworth*.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/三遊亭圓朝
- **How the stories reached him.**
  - Enchō: 「有名るべき官員さんが円朝に口うつしに教へて下さいました」, i.e. a notable official recited the plot to him (『円朝全集』 vol. 4, p. 3).
  - In 『黄薔薇』 the French story is transposed into Meiji Japan. Its ending involves the 西南戦争 and the resignation of a 元老院議長.
  - Source: 森裕介, 「円朝『黄薔薇』原作梗概ならびに若干の考察」 (Iwanami, 2022), https://www.iwanami.co.jp/moreinfo/0927441.pdf
- **『死神』** is Enchō's adaptation (明治20年代) of a European Death story: either Grimm's 『死神の名付け親』 or the Ricci brothers' opera *Crispino e la comare*, "probably" via 福地桜痴. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/死神_(落語)
- **Chain of adaptation: 牡丹灯籠.**
  - Ming 『剪灯新話』 「牡丹灯記」 → 浅井了意 『伽婢子』 (1666) → Enchō's ninjōbanashi (c. 1861–64) → sokkibon (1884) → kabuki (1892) → films.
  - Enchō added revenge, murder and a mother–son reunion to the ghost-romance.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/牡丹灯籠
- **The first sokkibon (1884).**
  - 『怪談牡丹燈籠』, 三遊亭円朝 演述, 若林玵蔵 筆記, 東京稗史出版社, published in parts from July–August 1884. NDL holds an 11-fascicle set (20 cm). https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000136-I1970023484963573311 ; https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000039-I891188
  - Heibonsha: the shorthand writers 若林玵蔵 and 酒井昇造, newly graduated from 田鎖綱紀's course, did it to advertise shorthand. Its success led to sokkibon of 『塩原多助一代記』, 『英国孝子之伝』 and then kōdan and rakugo generally. https://kotobank.jp/word/速記本
  - **Rival and repeat editions came fast:** 鶴声社, October 1884; 東京稗史出版社 again, September–December 1884 and February 1885; 文事堂, May 1885 and a "5版" in 1886 (286 pp.). https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000136-I1970586434890899144 ; https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000002-I000000924969
  - Wakabayashi was in 田鎖's first class (1882) and became a House of Representatives stenographer in 1890. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/若林玵蔵
- **The sokkibon as a medium.**
  - In 1889 金蘭社 launched 『百花園』, a magazine devoted to rakugo and kōdan transcripts. https://kotobank.jp/word/速記本
  - Newspapers then serialised kōdan by 2nd 松林伯圓, 松林伯知 and 3rd 一龍斎貞山. After the Russo-Japanese War, "書き講談" (written imitations of oral kōdan) took over. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/速記本
- **Language effect.** 二葉亭四迷 modelled 『浮雲』's colloquial style on Enchō's transcripts at 坪内逍遥's suggestion (Futabatei, 『余が言文一致の由来』). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/三遊亭圓朝
- **A spin-off rewrote history.**
  - 『塩原多助一代記』 was taken from a real merchant, 塩原**太**助 (1743–1816). Enchō researched it from 1876 and completed it in 1878. The sokkibon came out in 1885.
  - Enchō performed it before the Meiji Emperor at 井上馨's house on 24 Apr 1891.
  - It entered the national 修身 textbooks **under Enchō's spelling 「多助」**.
  - Sources: https://kotobank.jp/word/塩原多助一代記 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/三遊亭圓朝
- **Enchō in the newspapers.** From 1886 the やまと新聞 ran his tales by dictation. In June 1891 he left the yose and worked only through newspaper shorthand. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/三遊亭圓朝 ; file 11.

- **Kōdan magazines, and the fight that created "new kōdan".**
  - 『講談倶楽部』 was founded in 1911 by 大日本雄弁会 (later Kodansha). It was selling about 8,000 copies by 1912.
  - In 1913 its special issue 「浪花節十八番」 angered the storytellers, who resented being ranked with naniwabushi. Their shorthand supplier 今村次郎 demanded exclusivity, was refused, and attacked the magazine.
  - The magazine responded by commissioning writers to produce **「新講談」**, kōdan written from scratch. Readers preferred them, and shorthand kōdan was purged.
  - Sales: about 20,000 by 1915, about 150,000 in 1924, 400,000–500,000 in the 1930s.
  - In 1923 the magazine made peace with the storytellers.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/講談倶楽部
  - Heibonsha: the name ran 〈書き講談〉 → 〈新講談〉 → 〈読物文芸〉 → 〈大衆文芸〉 → 〈大衆文学〉. The writers' group 二十一日会 was founded in autumn 1925. https://kotobank.jp/word/大衆文学

## 3. Continuations by other hands, prequels, side-stories, parodies

### 3a. Edo precedents
- **『東海道中膝栗毛』 (1802–09)** was followed by 『続膝栗毛』 (to 1822): 21 years of sequels. Heibonsha: 「模倣作も多い」.
  - First kabuki riff: 4th 鶴屋南北's 『独道中五十三駅』 (河原崎座, June 1827).
  - The famous later stage version: Kabuki-za, August 1928 (木村錦花).
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/東海道中膝栗毛
- **『児雷也豪傑譚』: the model case of a serial continued by successive hands.**
  - 43 parts, 173 fascicles, 1839–1868, **unfinished**.
  - Parts 1–5 by 美図垣笑顔; 6–11 by 一筆庵 (渓斎英泉); 12–39 by 柳下亭種員; 40–43 by 柳水亭種清.
  - Lineage: Song-dynasty thief 我来也 → 感和亭鬼武's yomihon 『自来也説話』 → this gōkan.
  - Nipponica: because the authors kept changing and 「読者の続編への要求が強く無理に延ばした」, the design is inconsistent.
  - Mokuami staged it in July 1852 (河原崎座, Danjūrō VIII).
  - Imitations include 『女自来也』. It became a children's idol through Taishō films.
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/児雷也豪傑譚
- **『犬の草紙』.** A gōkan retelling of Hakkenden by 2nd 柳亭種彦 (笠亭仙果) and others, 紅英堂, 1848–1866. https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R000000050-I000015418

### 3d. Hon'an: foreign originals made Japanese
- **Definitions.**
  - Nipponica: 翻案 changes society, events and characters to Japanese ones; merely changing proper names is not 翻案. It sits between translation and plagiarism (剽窃).
  - 紅葉's 『夏小袖』 (1892, from Molière's *L'Avare*) loses the satire and becomes an Edo 茶番; 『隣の女』 (1893, from Zola) drops the darkness.
  - 福地桜痴 openly labelled 『あはれ浮世』 as from *Les Misérables*. 岡本綺堂's 『半七捕物帳』 took its idea from Holmes.
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/翻案
- **Kuroiwa Ruikō 黒岩涙香.**
  - Hon'an serials: 『法廷の美人』 (1888/89), 『鉄仮面』 (Boisgobey, 1892–93), 『幽霊塔』 (1899–1900), 『巌窟王』 (Dumas, 1901–02), 『噫無情』 (Hugo, 1902–03).
  - Heibonsha: he **read the original through, then wrote without looking at it again**, giving the characters Japanese names.
  - His 『萬朝報』 sold for 1 sen against others' 1.5 sen, reaching 50,000 copies by the Sino-Japanese War, about twice the 東京朝日's 25,000.
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/黒岩涙香
- **Konjiki yasha's own source.**
  - 堀啓子 identified (2000) Bertha M. Clay's dime novel *Weaker than a Woman* (serialised in the *Family Herald*, 17 Aug – 23 Nov 1878).
  - 巌谷小波 recalled Kōyō saying 「面白いから飜案して、今度の讀賣の小説に、使つてやらう」 (『金色夜叉の真相』, 1927).
  - Hori: the second half departs from the source and is Kōyō's own.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/金色夜叉 ; https://www.u-tokai.ac.jp/uploads/sites/8/2021/04/9290c2a063df1290021266f732a6d13b.pdf

## 4. Law: dramatisation, adaptation, translation

### 4a. 1887: scripts protected, novels not
- **脚本楽譜条例 (明治20年12月28日 勅令第78号).** My transcription from 法令全書:
  - **Art. 1:** scripts and scores may be published and hold 版権 under the 出版条例 and 版権条例.
  - **Art. 2:** a published, 版権-holding script or score also carries the **興行権** (「即チ利益ノ為メ公衆ノ前ニ演スルノ権」) for the 版権 term, **only if the five characters 「興行權所有」 are printed on it**.
  - **Art. 3:** the 興行権 may be sold or transferred, with or without limits.
  - **Art. 4:** infringers owe damages. So does anyone who stages an **unpublished** script or score without the consent of the author or heirs.
  - **Art. 5:** claims lapse one year after the last month of infringement.
  - Source: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/787970/1/333
  - **Consequence: a novel had no performance right at all.** Only scripts that their authors had chosen to publish and mark were protected.
- **Playwrights used it.** A Mokuami catalogue, 『脚本楽譜条例・楽譜名題目録：吉村新七著作長唄浄瑠理』 (1890.8), lists his registered works. https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000002-I000000491328
- The 1887 版権条例's Art. 15 two-year bar on compiling serials into books without the **editor's** consent: see file 25.

### 4b. 1899 Copyright Act (明治32年法律第39号, in force 15 Jul 1899)
- **Art. 1(2):** 「文藝學術ノ著作物ノ著作權ハ翻譯權ヲ包含シ各種ノ脚本及樂譜ノ著作權ハ興行權ヲ包含ス」.
  - Translation is reserved to the author.
  - Performance rights still attach only to scripts and scores.
  - There is **no express right of dramatisation (脚色) or adaptation (翻案)** of a novel.
- **Art. 7:** the translation right lapses if the author publishes no translation within **10 years** of the original (the "ten-year reservation").
- **Art. 19:** 「原著作物ニ…修正增減ヲ爲シ若ハ翻案シタルカ爲新ニ著作權生スルコトナシ但シ新著作物ト看倣サルヘキモノハ此ノ限ニ在ラス」. In other words, an adaptation earns no new copyright unless it counts as a new work.
- **Art. 21:** a lawful translator is deemed an author.
- **Art. 30(4):** inserting phrases from a literary work into one's own script, or setting them to music, is not infringement (source must be named).
- **Art. 50 (transitional):** a performance that had already been staged or begun before the Act, and was not infringing at the time, could go on being performed for **5 years**.
- **Art. 46** repealed the 版権法, 脚本楽譜条例 and 写真版権条例.
- Source for all of 4b: https://ja.wikisource.org/wiki/著作権法_(明治32年法律第39号). File 25 warns that the Wikisource text is corrupt in Arts. 4–6; the articles quoted here were not cross-checked against the 官報.
- **The drafter's commentary** (水野錬太郎, 『著作権法要義』, May 1899):
  - **On 翻案** (p. 81): 「翻案トハ例ヘハ演劇脚本ヲ小説ニ書替ヘ、小説ヲ浄瑠璃本ニ書直シ、或ハ英國ニアリタル事件ヲ日本ノ事情ニ仕組ムカ如キヲ云フ」. He continues (p. 82): 「是等ハ原著作者ノ工夫意匠ヲ剽竊スルモノナレハ新奇ノ著作物ト云フコトヲ得ス、從テ翻案ハ新ニ著作權ヲ生セサルナリ」, unless the adapter creates "a new framework" (新機軸). https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/796622/1/47 ; https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/796622/1/48
  - **On alteration** (pp. 76–77): a work may not be altered 「一字一句ト雖モ」 without the author's consent, even by someone who has acquired the copyright. https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/796622/1/45
  - **On newspaper serials** (p. 87): if a newspaper pays an ordinary 原稿料 and the author accepts it, 「其ノ原稿料ハ著作權ノ代償ニシテ著作者ハ之ニヨリテ其ノ著作權ヲ讓渡シタルモノト解釋スルヲ適當ナリト信ス」, absent other agreement. So, in the drafter's view, **the newspaper owned the serial**. https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/796622/1/50
  - **On Art. 30(4)** (p. 124): his examples are putting lines of 『日本外史』 into a play, or setting 君が代 to music. Such copying is still 複製, but is exempted because it does no harm. By implication, wholesale dramatisation of a protected novel was not covered by the exemption. https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/796622/1/69
- **Practice despite the law.** No Meiji lawsuit over a stage version of a novel turned up. See Uncertain.
- **Public domain.** Once terms expired, or for works never protected (all Edo works; unregistered pre-1899 works, per Mizuno's Art. 47 commentary in file 25), adaptation was entirely free. That is how 60 straight Chūshingura films could be made by 1926 (section 5).

### 4c. Later changes
- **Tōchūken Kumoemon case (桃中軒雲右衛門事件).**
  - Kumoemon's rōkyoku records were issued by ライロフォン (三光堂) on 19 May 1912: five double-sided discs, 72,000 pressed.
  - Unauthorised dubs followed.
  - Lower courts found infringement. The **Great Court of Cassation (大判大正3・7・4) reversed it**: naniwabushi improvised without a fixed score was not a musical work.
  - After the judgment, 横田昇一 (editor of 『蓄音機世界』) lobbied 鳩山一郎 and others, which produced the **1920 (大正9) amendment**. That it added 演奏・歌唱 to the protected categories is from memory, not checked against the text.
  - Even earlier (September 1911), a Kobe firm had sold 「桃中軒雲右衛門節」 discs marked 「奏演者匿名」, sung by someone else.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/桃中軒雲右衛門
- **1931 amendment: film rights. Unverified.** It is said to have added film rights, including 「脚色シテ映画ト為ス場合」, and to have made films protected works (old Arts. 22ノ2–22ノ4). Only seen in a search summary of a court judgment; not opened. See Uncertain.
- **Kamishibai (1930s): no copyright consciousness.** ja.wp: 「当時の零細な紙芝居業界に著作権意識は存在しなかったため、多種多様な黄金バットが勝手に作られた」. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/黄金バット

## 5. New media, 1908–1940

### 5a. Rensageki 連鎖劇
- **Definition.** Live drama alternating with filmed scenes. A screen was lowered for the film scenes, and the same actors spoke the lines from behind it.
- **Dates.**
  - First: September 1908, 宮戸座 (Tokyo), 『女さむらひ』 with 4th 沢村源之助.
  - Shinpa actors 山崎長之輔 and 井上正夫 staged many in early Taishō.
  - Ended by the revision of the entertainment regulations: Heibonsha says 1917, Britannica 1916.
  - A 1939 talkie-era revival called "キノドラマ".
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/連鎖劇
- **Scale.**
  - 権田保之助's survey (end of March 1917) counted 13 rensageki houses in Tokyo's 15 wards.
  - In Asakusa film dominated; elsewhere live acting dominated.
  - Content: mostly shinpa tragedy spliced with serial-film action.
  - Korea's first film, 『義理的仇討』 (1919), was a rensageki.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/連鎖劇

### 5b. Film remakes of hit novels and classics (counts to 1940)
- **『金色夜叉』: 22 titles by 1937.**
  - 1912 (Yokota); 1912 (Yoshizawa).
  - 1918 Nikkatsu, plus 『続』 (**Omiya played by the onnagata 衣笠貞之助**).
  - 1921 Nikkatsu, plus 『続』; 1921 小松商会.
  - 1922 Shōchiku (two titles).
  - 1923 Makino 『宮の巻』 and 『貫一の巻』.
  - 1924 Nikkatsu; 1924 Teikine.
  - 1925 『絵巻金色夜叉』.
  - 1930 『剣戟から生れた金色夜叉』; 1930 Nikkatsu 『続金色夜叉』.
  - 1932 Shōchiku (林長二郎 / 田中絹代); 1932 不二映画.
  - 1933 Nikkatsu (山田五十鈴); 1933 新興 『間貫一』.
  - 1934 赤沢キネマ.
  - 1937 Shōchiku Ōfuna.
  - ja.wp list; it may be incomplete. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/金色夜叉
- **『不如帰』: 19 Japanese titles by 1934, plus a 1926 Chinese film.**
  - 1909 (two); 1910 Yoshizawa; 1911 福宝堂 (two); 1914; 1915 天活.
  - 1919 Nikkatsu (Namiko played by 衣笠貞之助).
  - 1921; 1922 (three); 1924 『不如帰浪子』; 1926 Teikine.
  - 1932 (three, incl. 五所平之助's for Shōchiku); 1934 P.C.L. 『浪子の一生』.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/不如帰_(小説)
- **Chūshingura.**
  - 御園京平's 1966 count, excluding side-stories and variants: **22 films in late Meiji, 38 in Taishō** (= 60 by 1926), and 22 more "after the start of Shōwa" (to 1966).
  - First: 『忠臣蔵五段目』, 吉沢商店, 1907, a kabuki film with 11th 片岡仁左衛門 in three roles.
  - First complete telling: 牧野省三 / 尾上松之助, 横田商会, 1910. 牧野 made six in all.
  - First talkie: Shōchiku 1932 (衣笠貞之助).
  - Heibonsha: the industry saying 「ヒット作の企画に困れば忠臣蔵」, and its use as every studio's all-star 顔見世.
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/忠臣蔵映画-1185354
- **『四谷怪談』: no pre-1945 count found.** The ja.wp article lists only postwar films; Enchō also had a rakugo 『四谷怪談』. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/四谷怪談
- **Other cross-media figures, 1912:** Tokyo's 観物場 admissions reached **12 million, three times the previous year; 8.51 million of them for films**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ジゴマ

### 5c. Zigomar: a foreign hit, local imitations, a ban (1911–12)
- **Japanese release.** French serial (*Le Matin*, 1909); Éclair film 1911; Tokyo release as 『探偵奇譚ジゴマ』, 金龍館, Asakusa, **11 Nov 1911**.
- **Follow-ups:**
  - 『女ジゴマ』 (December 1911 – March 1912).
  - 『ジゴマ後編』 (May 1912).
  - Yoshizawa's **『日本ジゴマ』** (August 1912).
  - M. Pathé's 『新ジゴマ大探偵』 (September 1912).
  - **23 Zigomar novels** published July–December 1912.
- **The ban.** The 警視庁 banned Zigomar and similar films on **9 Oct 1912**, then **all** Zigomar films on 20 Oct. This is said to have pushed film censorship forward.
- **Afterwards.** A 「ヂゴマ団」 gang appeared in 1915. The ban was lifted in 1924.
- Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ジゴマ

### 5d. Asakusa Opera (1917–1925)
- **Start.** 『女軍出征』, 常磐座, 22 Jan 1917.
- **Repertoire** (dates per ja.wp):
  - 『天国と地獄』, 日本館, from 23 Oct 1917.
  - 『カルメン』 and 『椿姫』, 20 Mar 1922. Enoken's chorus début was in the *Carmen*.
- **End.** Killed by the 1923 earthquake. 根岸大歌劇団 disbanded in March 1924; the last production was in October 1925.
- **Legacy.** Its performers carried over into カジノ・フォーリー and 笑の王国.
- Parody details are in 3c.
- Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/浅草オペラ

### 5e. Kamishibai and Ōgon Bat (1929–1937)
- **Origins.**
  - 立絵 (cut-out puppet) kamishibai came out of 写し絵.
  - Its maker, "新さん", was a former **disciple of Enchō** who had painted magic-lantern slides. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/紙芝居 ; Heibonsha https://kotobank.jp/word/紙芝居
  - In 1929 about 40 Tokyo tachi-e performers broke from the tekiya bosses and formed 東京写絵業者組合 (Nipponica).
- **The flat-picture (平絵) form came out of a police ban.**
  - Tachi-e was banned in Asakusa: 1929 according to Heibonsha and ja.wp (菊屋橋署); 1931 according to Nipponica.
  - Performers switched to showing a sequence of pictures in a frame. Mypedia credits 永松武雄 with the first 平絵 in 1930.
- **Ōgon Bat.**
  - Story by 鈴木一郎. It began in 1930 as the hero who defeats the skull-faced villain in the **final episode of the villain series 『黒バット』**. The 19 pictures were drawn by 永松健夫.
  - It took over as its own series in early 1931 and ran to 1934.
  - Scripts were not written down; performers told it from memory, so 「同じ紙芝居でも演者によって内容に差異があるのが普通だった」.
  - The pictures were hand-painted single copies, hired out from the 貸元. Most pre-war sets burned in April 1945.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/黄金バット
- **Scale.**
  - 1935 Tokyo city survey: about 2,000 performers (ja.wp, Heibonsha); Nipponica gives about 2,500.
  - Nationwide 30,000: Nipponica says 1935, Heibonsha says 1936 (the peak).
  - Picture size grew from about 10 × 14 cm to about 18 × 25 cm within 2–3 years.
  - Sources: https://kotobank.jp/word/紙芝居 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/紙芝居
- **Source material.** Before Ōgon Bat the repertoire was kabuki-derived ghost stories, kyōkaku stories and 『西遊記・孫悟空』 (Nipponica). https://kotobank.jp/word/紙芝居
- **Respectable kamishibai.**
  - First printed kamishibai: 今井よね's 『クリスマス物語』 (1933).
  - 「幼稚園紙芝居」 in 10 volumes (1935).
  - 日本教育紙芝居連盟 (1937), later absorbed into wartime propaganda.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/紙芝居

### 5f. Radio, 1925–
- **Broadcasting began.** Per ja.wp: test transmissions 1 Mar 1925, then the provisional service (仮放送) 22 Mar 1925, JOAK. Regular service from 愛宕山 on 12 Jul 1925 is from memory.
- **Early drama.**
  - 11 Mar 1925: the benshi 熊岡天堂 performed 『噫無情』 alone as a 「映画物語」.
  - 12 Jul 1925: kabuki actors (5th 中村歌右衛門 among them) did part of 『桐一葉』 as a 「ラジオ劇」, essentially the stage performance.
  - The next week: shinpa's 井上正夫 and 水谷八重子 in 『大尉の娘』.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ラジオドラマ
- **First radio-native drama.**
  - **『炭坑の中』, 13 Aug 1925**: an adaptation of Richard Hughes's BBC play 《Danger》 (1924). Heibonsha: it established radio drama as a 「音の世界」 art.
  - Sources: https://kotobank.jp/word/炭坑の中 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ラジオドラマ
  - Translator: 小山内薫, with 築地小劇場 actors, per Weblio and ja.wp (see Uncertain).
- **Fees.** From 1925 NHK paid leading novelists **500 yen** for one-off radio plays ("500円ドラマ"). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ラジオドラマ

## 6. How a single hit spawned a genre

- **毒婦物 (1879–).** Oden's story → 鳥追お松, 夜嵐お絹, 花井お梅. Nipponica calls 『鳥追阿松海上新話』 (1878) and 『高橋阿伝夜刃譚』 (1879) the typical gōkan-format 毒婦物. https://kotobank.jp/word/毒婦物
- **当世〜気質 (1886–90).** See 3c: about ten imitation titles within three years of Shōyō's fascicles.
- **観念小説 and 悲惨/深刻小説 (1895).**
  - Works: 鏡花's 「夜行巡査」 and 「外科室」; 眉山's 「書記官」, 「うらおもて」 and 「大盃」; 柳浪's 「変目伝」, 「黒蜥蜴」 and 「今戸心中」; also 一葉's 「にごりえ」 etc.
  - **Names:** 田岡嶺雲 first wrote of 「悲酸文学」 and 「深刻の悲哀文学」 (『日本人』, July 1895). 島村抱月 coined **「観念小説」** (読売新聞, 26 Aug 1895). 「悲惨小説」 appeared in 『青年文』 (October 1895), probably by 佐々醒雪.
  - **Why readers wanted them:** 後藤宙外 said such novels 「天颷海を渡る勢をもて小説界を風靡」, because readers 「人生問題に煩悶する」.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/社会小説 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/観念小説 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/悲惨小説
  - NKD's first citation for 観念小説 is 鴎外's 『鷸翮掻』 (1896). https://kotobank.jp/word/観念小説
- **家庭小説 (c. 1898–1912), "the reaction to the dark novels".**
  - Precursors: 金色夜叉 and 不如帰.
  - Peak: 菊池幽芳's 『己が罪』 (1899–1900) and 『乳姉妹』 (1903).
  - Then: 草村北星 『浜子』 (1902) and 『相思怨』 (1904); 田口掬汀 『女夫波』 (1904) and 『伯爵夫人』 (1905); 大倉桃郎 『琵琶歌』 (1905); 柳川春葉 『生さぬ仲』 (1912).
  - Hopeful versions were called 〈光明小説〉 (中村春雨, 『無花果』, 1901).
  - Heibonsha ties its rise to shinpa dramatisation: 「その脚色による新派劇の興隆と相まって」.
  - Sources: https://kotobank.jp/word/家庭小説 ; Inoue abstract (shinpa built its position on 家庭小説 dramatisations).
- **The publishing scale behind a hit.**
  - 『不如帰』: first printing 1,000 copies sold out in a month; 10,000 in two years; 79th edition by 25 Sep 1907; 100th edition preface dated 2 Feb 1909; **190 editions and about 500,000 copies by 1927**. Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/不如帰_(小説) ; https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000134-I000250906
  - 『金色夜叉』: 春陽堂's edition reached a **189th printing by 10 Oct 1926**. https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000134-I000025953
- **Tachikawa bunko 立川文庫 and the ninja boom.**
  - About 200 titles (Britannica: about 240 by c. 1925), 1911 – c. 1924, from 立川文明堂, Osaka (publisher 立川熊次郎).
  - Format: 四六半截, cloth. List price 25 sen; actual retail about 10 sen, trade price 6–7 sen. Nicknamed 「こちょう本」 for the butterfly cover pattern, against 袖珍文庫's 「いちょう本」.
  - Written collectively by the storyteller 2nd 玉田玉秀斎, his wife 山田敬 and her son 阿鉄 under pseudonyms (雪花山人, 野花散人).
  - It began as written-down kōdan and moved to original writing.
  - **No. 40, 『(真田三勇士忍術名人) 猿飛佐助』**: an invented hero who mixes 『真田三代記』 with 『西遊記』. Nipponica: it 「映画などにおける忍術ブームを巻き起こした」, and imitators followed (『袖珍大川文庫』, 『武士道文庫』, 『忍術文庫』).
  - Britannica: some titles went through **1,000 printings**.
  - Heibonsha: the 真田十勇士's exploits are 「すべて〈立川文庫〉による創作」.
  - Sources: https://kotobank.jp/word/立川文庫 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/立川文庫 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/猿飛佐助
- **Ninja on film.**
  - 尾上松之助 ("目玉の松ちゃん"): over 1,000 films for 横田商会 / Nikkatsu. He became an idol to children through 牧野省三's trick-shot 忍術映画.
  - 『豪傑児雷也』 (1921) survives in about 21 minutes. He played 児雷也 five times.
  - A child reportedly **stood in front of an oncoming train making ninja hand-signs**. Critics called ninjutsu films 「世を惑わすもの」, and Makino turned to educational films.
  - 南部僑一郎 dates the ninjutsu-film vogue to 1912.
  - He played many 立川文庫 characters.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/尾上松之助 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/尾上松之助
- **From 書き講談 to 大衆文学.** 1913 新講談 (2b) → 二十一日会 (autumn 1925) → the term 大衆文学. 白井喬二 is usually credited with it but denied coining the full phrase. The genre was first identified with sword-fighting 時代小説. https://kotobank.jp/word/大衆文学
- **Ōgon Bat spawned imitators at once** (5e). The postwar surge 「さまざまな亜流を、紙芝居だけではなく児童雑誌、ラジオなどの物語にも発生させた」 (Nipponica). https://kotobank.jp/word/紙芝居

## Telling details

- **The line everyone quotes is not in the book.** 「来年の今月今夜のこの月を僕の涙で曇らせてみせる」 is the stage and film version. Kōyō wrote a stammering 「月が……月が……月が……」. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/金色夜叉
- **Shoes or geta?** In the 1898 frontispiece Kan'ichi kicks Omiya in **leather shoes**; the 1985 Atami statue gives him **geta**. The text never says. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/金色夜叉
- **A reader's grave.** Hori Keiko retells the story of a dying young woman who asked that only a copy of the 『讀賣新聞』 be laid at her grave, so she could keep up with 『金色夜叉』. Kōyō was then paid as much as the paper's president. When the Yomiuri let him go, readers boycotted it. https://www.u-tokai.ac.jp/uploads/sites/8/2021/04/9290c2a063df1290021266f732a6d13b.pdf
- **Fiction remade a landscape.**
  - 小栗風葉's 金色夜叉 monument at Atami was put up on 15 Aug 1919.
  - The nearby 「羽衣の松」 came to be called 「お宮の松」 (the date is garbled in the source; see Uncertain).
  - Atami holds a 尾崎紅葉祭 every 17 January, the date of the parting.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/金色夜叉
  - At Zushi, a temple's Fudō image became 「浪子不動」 after the heroine of 『不如帰』. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/浪子不動
- **A novel coined a word.** NKD: 「金色夜叉」 came to mean a moneylender (高利貸). https://kotobank.jp/word/金色夜叉
- **A novel made a gesture.** After 『不如帰』, the handkerchief waved at a departure became the standard farewell prop (林えり子, cited on ja.wp). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/不如帰_(小説)
- **Braille editions.** In May 1916 a news report announced libraries for the blind at 小石川 and 本郷, offering 不如帰 and 金色夜叉 in braille. https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R000000035-I2050014094_0100362028
- **Two picture-books of a play in the same month.** In May 1879, while Kikugorō played Oden, rival publishers issued two illustrated 正本写 of the play. https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R000000025-I013010005864292 ; https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000136-I1970586434803804714
- **The murderess played herself.** Hanai Oume, pardoned in 1903, toured re-enacting her own crime. https://kotobank.jp/word/花井お梅
- **The textbook used the storyteller's spelling.** 塩原太助 became 塩原「多助」 in the 修身 textbooks because of Enchō. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/三遊亭圓朝
- **A bureaucrat at the storyteller's elbow.** Enchō's French and English plots were recited to him by 「有名るべき官員さん」, probably 福地桜痴. https://www.iwanami.co.jp/moreinfo/0927441.pdf
- **The kamishibai lineage runs back to Enchō.** The man who invented the cut-out kamishibai had been an Enchō apprentice who gave up rakugo to paint lantern slides. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/紙芝居
- **A hero born in the villain's last episode.** Ōgon Bat first appeared to destroy the undefeatable 黒バット in the final episode, then took over the series. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/黄金バット
- **A prequel in 1913.** 『前篇不如帰：未婚時代の武男と浪子』 told the lovers' story before the novel begins. https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000002-I000000531522
- **A sequel to a knock-off.** 篠原嶺葉's unauthorised 『新不如帰』 (1906) got its own sequel, 『後の新不如帰』 (1907). The same hack later wrote a 『女金色夜叉』 (1925). https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000002-I000000512314 ; https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000002-I000000542982
- **The "real hero" answers back.** 巌谷小波, the supposed model for Kan'ichi, published 『金色夜叉の真相：所謂る間貫一の告白』 in 1927. https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000002-I000000924937
- **A future naturalist wrote a parody.** 正宗白鳥's 『吾輩ハ鼠デアル』 (1907) (per the NDL record). https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000002-I000000513197
- **Konjiki yasha as chanbara.** 『剣戟から生れた金色夜叉』 (1930). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/金色夜叉
- **Men played the heroines on screen.** Omiya (1918) and Namiko (1919) were both played by the onnagata 衣笠貞之助, who later directed the first talkie Chūshingura. Katyusha (1914) was 立花貞二郎. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/金色夜叉 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/不如帰_(小説) ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/立花貞二郎
- **A sake-shop handbill borrowed the novel.** The Zushi farewell scene of 『不如帰』 decorated a Tokushima fish-and-sake shop's 1912 handbill. https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000094-I233959
- **Folk song versions.** In 1932 Victor recorded 『不如帰』 as a じょんがら節 ("浪子の生立") and as a よされ節. https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000039-I1321079
- **Fake records under a star's name.** In 1911 a Kobe firm sold 「桃中軒雲右衛門節」 discs marked "performer anonymous" before Kumoemon had recorded at all. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/桃中軒雲右衛門
- **A ninja-struck child.** A boy faced a moving train with ninja hand-signs; critics said ninjutsu films 「世を惑わす」. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/尾上松之助
- **Copying was itself a performance practice.** Kamishibai scripts were not written down; each teller's Ōgon Bat differed. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/黄金バット
- **Readers demanded sequels, and the story suffered.** Nipponica on 『児雷也豪傑譚』: four successive authors over 30 years, 「読者の続編への要求が強く無理に延ばしたため、趣向が一貫していない」. https://kotobank.jp/word/児雷也豪傑譚

## Uncertain / conflicting

- **First staging of 『金色夜叉』, March 1898.**
  - Nipponica and Britannica: March 1898, 市村座.
  - Inoue (abstract): Kawakami troupe, end of March to about mid-April 1898.
  - A search-engine summary of a Hiroshima University paper (not opened; URL now 404) gave **25 March 1898, Kawakami troupe at the 市村座**, with the script printed in a newspaper beforehand and the Atami scene set at 「吉浜海岸」. That would reconcile the two, but it is unverified.
- **Sarutobi Sasuke (立川文庫 no. 40): 1913 or 1914?**
  - 1914: Heibonsha, Nipponica, Yamakawa.
  - 1913 (大正2): 日本人名大辞典+Plus and a ja.wp image caption. The ja.wp body text says 「1913年（大正4年）」, which is internally inconsistent.
  - Sources: https://kotobank.jp/word/猿飛佐助 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/立川文庫
- **Ninjutsu-film boom vs. Sarutobi.** Ja.wp (尾上松之助) cites 南部僑一郎 dating the vogue to 1912, which is before the Sarutobi volume. The trick-film genre may predate and then merge with the 立川文庫 boom (Jiraiya was an Edo stage and gōkan staple). Nipponica credits Sarutobi with starting the 忍術ブーム.
- **Hototogisu sequels by 菱花生.** NDL dates 『川島武男：不如歸外傳』 to January 1913. ja.wp cites 『不如帰外伝 片岡浪子』 (東盛堂, 1913) and 『後の不如帰』 as 博盛堂 1909. NDL has 『後の不如帰』 (紅葉堂, 1908.3) and 『続後の不如帰』 (博盛堂, 1909.3), so ja.wp may conflate the two.
- **小栗風葉's ending: did he use Kōyō's notes?**
  - Hori (2020): Fūyō wrote 『終篇』 「紅葉の遺した覚書を参考にして」.
  - Ja.wp: Kōyō's 創作メモ were *discovered* around 1940, while 中央公論社 was preparing a complete works.
  - These need not conflict (Fūyō may have known the plan privately), but they are not reconciled.
- **Atami 「お宮の松」 date.** ja.wp writes 「1924年（昭和9年）頃から」, which is impossible (1924 = Taishō 13; Shōwa 9 = 1934).
- **Rensageki end date.** Heibonsha: the 1917 revision of the entertainment law. Britannica: 1916. Ja.wp: "banned" without a date.
- **Kamishibai dates and numbers.**
  - Asakusa ban on tachi-e: 1929 (Heibonsha, ja.wp) or 1931 (Nipponica).
  - First 平絵: 1930 (Mypedia, 永松武雄).
  - Ōgon Bat: late 1930 as a character, its own series from early 1931 (ja.wp).
  - Tokyo performers in 1935: 2,000 (ja.wp, Heibonsha) or 2,500 (Nipponica).
  - Nationwide 30,000: 1935 (Nipponica) or 1936 (Heibonsha).
  - 永松武雄 (Mypedia) and 永松健夫 (ja.wp) appear to be the same artist under two names; not checked.
- **First Japanese newspaper serial.** 前田香雪's 『岩田八十八の話』, 平仮名絵入新聞, 1875, for 3 days: NKD says from **28 October**, Britannica says from **28 November**.
- **1931 amendment.** The film-adaptation clauses (old Arts. 22ノ2–22ノ4, 「脚色シテ映画ト為ス場合ヲ包含ス」) were seen only in a search-engine summary of a court judgment; the PDF was not reachable. Also unverified: what the **1910 (明治43)** amendment did. A summary of 酒井麻千子's paper links it to the 1908 Berlin conference on film and photography; the PDF was unreadable.
- **Dramatisation of a novel under the 1899 Act.** The statute grants translation and (for scripts) performance rights only. Mizuno classes turning a novel into a jōruri text as 翻案 that 「剽竊」s the original. **No Meiji lawsuit** over an unauthorised stage version of a novel was found. That is evidence of absence only; specialist histories (e.g. 大家重夫, 倉田喜弘) were not consulted. The real practice may have run on newspaper and publisher consent; the 1909 playbill credits 春陽堂.
- **Payment of novelists for stage versions.** No figures found for any Meiji or Taishō dramatisation. The NHK "500円ドラマ" fee from 1925 is from ja.wp only.
- **Radio drama 1925.**
  - Kotobank: 『炭坑の中』 adapts Hughes's 《Danger》 (1924).
  - The translator 小山内薫 rests on ja.wp and a Weblio search snippet. A WebFetch summary mis-rendered the name as "Komaki Kaoru", and the play title as "Kiki"; I trust Kotobank for the title.
- **「カチューシャ」 as the word for a hair-band.** The popular etymology (from Matsui Sumako's Katyusha in 1914) is widely repeated but called 「確定的ではない」 by the one dictionary site found. Unverified.
- **「武男」と「信子」.** Ja.wp (citing 竇新光 2014) says lovers took to calling each other 「武男」 and 「信子」. 信子 is the real model's name, not the heroine's (浪子), so this may be a slip in the source. Unverified.
- **Konjiki yasha and Hototogisu film lists** are from ja.wp and may be incomplete. A 1922 Shōchiku 「傑作集粋（明治文壇海岸の悲劇）」 appears under both novels, perhaps as one double bill.
- **Hiroshima "media-mix" paper.** Per the search summary, it uses 関肇's notion of 「メディア・ミックス」 (theatre, 新体詩, painting, naniwabushi, postcards, songs, film) for 金色夜叉. 関肇's 『新聞小説の時代』 (新曜社, 2007) is the standard study and was not consulted.
- **Sugoroku / karuta on specific novels.** None found for 金色夜叉 or 不如帰. Do not assume absence.
- **『吾輩ハ鼠デアル』 by 正宗白鳥.** The NDL authority record links the work to 正宗白鳥 (1879–1962). I did not confirm this from a literary-history source; it may be a pseudonym collision.
