# 12. Meiji press and publication censorship; laws on dress, swords and duels (1868–1912)

Real history only. Every bullet gives the page it came from. Primary statute texts come from the Nagoya University Faculty of Law historical statute database (法令データベース, jahis.law.nagoya-u.ac.jp, transcribed from 官報/法令全書/御署名原本), Japanese Wikisource, and e-Gov. Reference works come from Kotobank (Sekai Daihyakka 世界大百科事典, Nihon Daihyakka Zensho/Nipponica 日本大百科全書, Yamakawa 日本史小辞典, Britannica 小項目, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten), plus ja.wikipedia, J-STAGE and university repository papers, the National Archives of Japan, NDL pages, and Okamoto Kidō's memoirs on Aozora Bunko.

**Calendar note.** Dates before 1 Jan 1873 (= Meiji 5/12/3) are given as lunar date → Gregorian. I computed the conversions with the `lunar-javascript` calendar library and checked it against ten known Japanese anchor dates (Meiji 5/12/3 = 1873‑01‑01; Meiji 4/8/9 = 1871‑09‑23; Meiji 5/11/9 = 1872‑12‑09; Keiō 4/1/3 = 1868‑01‑27; Meiji 1/9/8 = 1868‑10‑23; Meiji 4/7/14 = 1871‑08‑29; Meiji 2/6/17 = 1869‑07‑25; Japan's leap 4th month of 1868 and leap 10th month of 1870). It matched all of them. Treat the conversions as reliable to the day for 1868–72.

**Legal-name glossary.** 太政官布告 Dajōkan proclamation; 勅令 imperial ordinance; 法律 statute passed by the Diet (from 1890); 発行禁止 permanent ban on a periodical; 発行停止 suspension of a periodical; 発売頒布禁止 ban on selling or distributing one issue or book (the usual 発禁); 安寧秩序妨害/治安妨害 "disturbing public order"; 風俗壊乱 "corrupting public morals"; 朝憲紊乱 "subverting the constitutional order"; 保証金 deposit.

## 1. Press law

### Before 1875
- **1868 bans.** After Toba–Fushimi the Dajōkan ordered newspapers closed, by proclamations of **Keiō 4/4/28 (= 20 May 1868)** and **Keiō 4/6/8 (= 27 July 1868)**. Most early papers were pro-Tokugawa. Papers run by foreigners under extraterritoriality, such as the 万国新聞 and 横浜新報もしほ草, kept circulating. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新聞紙条例 (dates); conversions mine.
- **1869 licensing.** The 新聞紙印行條例 (学校官, 布告第135号) of **Meiji 2/2/8 (= 20 Mar 1869)** allowed papers to restart under licence. Two copies of every issue had to go to the government on the day of publication, and the 開成学校 began censoring at this point. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新聞紙条例 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/出版条例
- **1873.** The 新聞紙発行条目 (太政官布告第352号, **19 Oct 1873**) kept licensing in place and banned slander of the 国体 and criticism of laws. The government also promoted newspapers at this time, setting up public newspaper-reading rooms (新聞縦覧所) in each prefecture. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新聞紙条例 ; Sekai Daihyakka in https://kotobank.jp/word/新聞紙条例-82522

### 新聞紙条例, 1875 (太政官布告第111号, 28 June 1875) and 讒謗律 (第110号, same day)
- **Contents.** 16 articles (Nipponica). Publication needed a licence. Owners and editors had to be Japanese nationals (Art. 4, said to be aimed at John Black's 日新真事誌). Writers had to give their name and address, and **pen names were prohibited** (筆名を禁止). Corrections had to be printed in the next issue. Unauthorised petitions could not be printed. It introduced criminal penalties for: inciting crime (Art. 12); "articles arguing for the overthrow of the government or the state, or seeking to stir up disorder" (「政府ヲ変壊シ国家ヲ顛覆スルノ論ヲ載セ騒乱ヲ煽起セントスル者」, Art. 13); and "slandering the laws" (成法誹毀, Art. 14). — Nipponica (春原昭彦) in https://kotobank.jp/word/新聞紙条例-82522 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新聞紙条例
- **Nishiki-e.** Under the 1875 rules, colour prints had to carry the filing date, the artist's name and the publisher's address and name. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新聞紙条例
- **Senate passage.** The 元老院 (Sano Tsunetami and others) first sent the bill back as oppressive. It returned, Art. 5 was deleted, and it passed with few votes against. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新聞紙条例
- **讒謗律.** 8 articles. Art. 1 defines 讒毀 (publishing acts that damage a person's honour, "whether true or not") and 誹謗 (attaching a bad name to someone without stating facts), when committed by "writings, pictures or portraits displayed, sold or posted." Arts. 2–5 rank the victims, with penalties falling in this order: the emperor (乗輿), the imperial family, officials, then commoners. The maximum is 禁獄 3 years (Yamakawa). It was abolished when the 1880 Penal Code took effect on 1 Jan 1882. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/讒謗律 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/讒謗律-71473
- **Arrests.** 7 people were arrested under the 讒謗律 by the end of 1875, and 40 in 1876. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/讒謗律 (no source given)
- **Journalists jailed.** "From 1875 to 1880, 203 journalists were imprisoned" (禁獄). — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新聞紙条例 (no source given; the bibliography lists 伊藤正徳『新聞五十年史』1943, 西田長寿, and 彌吉光長「新聞紙条例と讒謗律の犠牲者」『出版研究』16, 1985). FLAG: I could not see the primary statistic.
- **Administrative suspension.** On **5 July 1876** (太政官布告第98号) the 内務卿 gained the power to ban or suspend papers for 「国安妨害」. On **12 Oct 1880** (太政官布告第45号) a second ground, 「風俗ヲ壊乱スル」, was added. Mitsuishi says this is the first time the words 風俗壊乱 appear in law. From then on, administrative suspension rather than prosecution was "the central weapon." — Sekai Daihyakka (内川芳美) in https://kotobank.jp/word/新聞紙条例-82522 ; Mitsuishi Ayumi, 名古屋大学国語国文学 93, p. 88 (https://nagoya.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/2006591). The 1869 出版条例 already banned writing that "leads to lewdness" (淫蕩ヲ導ク).
- **First daily suspended.** On **15 May 1878** the 朝野新聞 printed the assassins' manifesto (斬奸状) of 島田一郎 and others, who had killed Ōkubo Toshimichi the day before. The paper was suspended for 7 days. ja.wikipedia calls this "the first suspension of a daily." — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/発禁. FLAG: "first" is unverified, since the suspension power existed from July 1876.

### Famous 1870s cases
- **Suehiro Tetchō 末広鉄腸** (東京曙新聞) was the first person punished under the 1875 laws. In August 1875 he printed a reader's letter attacking them and received 自宅禁錮 2 months. ja.wikipedia gives the fine as "罰金二千円", which is implausible (FLAG; probably 20 yen). — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/末広鉄腸 ; Dajisen in https://kotobank.jp/word/新聞紙条例-82522
- **February 1876, 朝野新聞.** The paper mocked the laws' drafters 井上毅 and 尾崎三良. Owner **成島柳北** got 禁獄 4 months and 100 yen. Editor 末広鉄腸 got 8 months and 150 yen. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/末広鉄腸
- **小松原英太郎 Komatsubara Eitarō.** In 1876, writing in the 評論新聞, he published an editorial titled 「圧制政府転覆すべし」 ("The despotic government should be overthrown"). He was arrested under the 新聞紙条例 and spent about two years in prison, through the Satsuma Rebellion, until released in 1878. He later became 内務省警保局長 (head of the national police bureau, who ran the 1892 election interference), Vice-Minister of Home Affairs, and **Minister of Education 1908–11** under the second Katsura cabinet, which handled the aftermath of the High Treason case. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/小松原英太郎
- **English-language sources** sometimes give the date of the 1875 press laws as 28 July. That is an error for 28 June. — e.g. California Western International Law Journal article at https://scholarlycommons.law.cwsl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1729&context=cwilj (search snippet only; not used for facts)

### 1883 revision (太政官布告第12号, 16 April 1883)
- **Contents.** A complete rewrite that created the **deposit system (発行保証金)**. It widened the circle of people legally liable, banned "substitute newspapers" (身替わり新聞, a new title issued to evade a ban), let the Foreign and Army/Navy ministers forbid reporting, gave prefectural governors administrative powers, and created a power to seize newspapers. — Sekai Daihyakka and Nipponica in https://kotobank.jp/word/新聞紙条例-82522 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新聞紙条例
- **Effect.** "Within a month 47 papers closed; from 355 the previous year to 199 by year's end." The law was nicknamed 「新聞撲滅法」 ("newspaper extermination law"). — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新聞紙条例 (no source given; FLAG)
- **Book law tied in.** The 出版条例 was amended on 29 June 1883 to apply the newspaper law's Art. 31 (no printing of unpublished official documents or petitions without permission). — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/出版条例

### 1887 ordinance (勅令第75号; signed 28 Dec, promulgated 29 Dec 1887)
- **Notification system.** Licensing was replaced by notification (届出): a paper had to notify the Home Ministry, through the 警視庁 in Tokyo, two weeks before its first issue (Art. 1). — Nagoya DB full text https://jahis.law.nagoya-u.ac.jp/lawdb/l/120i0075
- **Art. 6: no women as publisher or printer.** Only "a Japanese national who is a male of full 20 years or more" (「内国人ニシテ満二十歳以上ノ男子」) could be publisher (発行人) or printer (印刷人). — same
- **Art. 8: deposits, exact text.** Tokyo **1,000 yen**; Kyoto, Osaka, Yokohama, Hyōgo, Kōbe and Nagasaki **700 yen**; elsewhere **350 yen**; papers appearing three times a month or less pay half. The deposit could be paid in government bonds or National Bank deposit notes. Papers carrying only scholarship, arts, statistics, official notices or prices were exempt. — same. FLAG: one search summary attributes the same amounts to the 1883 law, but the page it cites is the 1887 text. I did not verify the 1883 figures.
- **Deposit seized.** Fines and costs unpaid within a week came out of the deposit, and the paper was stopped until the deposit was topped up (Art. 26). — same
- **Art. 19: administrative ban or suspension.** 「治安ヲ妨害シ又ハ風俗ヲ壊乱スルモノト認ムル新聞紙ハ内務大臣ニ於テ其発行ヲ禁止シ若クハ停止スルコトヲ得」: the Home Minister may ban or suspend a paper he "deems" to disturb public order or corrupt morals. Art. 20 adds seizure of the issue. — same
- **Arts. 32–33: penalties.** Advocating changing the polity or subverting the constitutional order: 2 months–2 years light imprisonment plus a 50–300 yen fine for publisher, editor and printer, and the press is confiscated. A "**猥褻ノ新聞紙**" (obscene paper): 1–6 months or a 20–200 yen fine. — same
- **Senate review.** The 元老院 reviewed the 1887 ordinances together on 13 Feb 1888 (第562号議案): newspaper law, 出版条例, 版権条例, 脚本楽譜条例 and the photograph copyright law. — same

### 1897 revision (法律第9号; sanctioned 19 Mar, promulgated 24 Mar 1897): administrative suspension abolished
- **Text.** Arts. 19 and 20 (the Home Minister's ban, suspension and seizure powers) were "削除" (deleted). Banning a paper now required a court judgment, and only for military/diplomatic violations (Art. 22) or Arts. 32–33. New Art. 32: 「皇室ノ尊厳ヲ冒瀆シ政体ヲ変壊シ又ハ朝憲ヲ紊乱セントスルノ論説」. New Art. 33: 「社会ノ秩序又ハ風俗ヲ壊乱スル事項」, punished by 1–6 months or a 20–300 yen fine. When a prosecution was filed, the Home Minister could still provisionally halt sales and seize an issue (Art. 23). — Nagoya DB https://jahis.law.nagoya-u.ac.jp/lawdb/l/130a0009 ; Sekai Daihyakka in https://kotobank.jp/word/新聞紙条例-82522
- **Trigger.** 高橋健三, Chief Cabinet Secretary in the Matsukata cabinet, had his own magazine 『二十六世紀』 banned by the Home Ministry for attacking the peerage and the Imperial Household Minister. He then pushed the amendment. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新聞紙条例

### 新聞紙法, Newspaper Law (法律第41号, 6 May 1909): Meiji's final press law, in force until 1949
- **Origin.** It began as a press-friendly private member's bill (村松恒一郎 and others, 25th Diet) and was turned into a harsher law. 45 articles. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新聞紙法 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/新聞紙法-82523
- **Art. 2.** Publishers and editors must reside in the empire and may not be serving soldiers, minors, incompetents, or people serving a sentence. **There is no sex requirement**, unlike 1887. — Nagoya DB https://jahis.law.nagoya-u.ac.jp/lawdb/l/142a0041
- **Art. 12: deposits roughly doubled.** Tokyo and Osaka and anywhere within 3 ri: **2,000 yen**; cities or wards of 70,000+ people and within 1 ri: **1,000 yen**; elsewhere **500 yen**; half for papers appearing three times a month or less. — same
- **Art. 23: the 発禁 power restored.** 「内務大臣ハ新聞紙掲載ノ事項ニシテ安寧秩序ヲ紊シ又ハ風俗ヲ害スルモノト認ムルトキハ其ノ発売及頒布ヲ禁止シ必要ノ場合ニ於テハ之ヲ差押フルコトヲ得」. The minister may also forbid future articles "of the same purport." — same
- **Art. 27.** The Army, Navy and Foreign ministers may forbid or limit military and diplomatic reporting. — same
- **Art. 41.** Printing matter that disturbs order or harms morals: publisher and editor ≤6 months or ≤200 yen. — same
- **Art. 42.** Matter that "profanes the dignity of the imperial house, changes the polity or subverts the constitutional order": publisher, editor and printer ≤2 years **and** ≤300 yen. — same
- **Art. 43.** For Arts. 40–42 the **court** may ban the paper outright. — same
- **Pre-publication gag orders.** The Home Minister also issued 掲載差止 orders forbidding papers in advance to print particular stories. Sekai Daihyakka says these had no legal basis. — Sekai Daihyakka (内川芳美) in https://kotobank.jp/word/新聞紙法-82523
- **Deposits paid in railway shares.** By ministerial ordinance, Home Minister 平田東助 let deposits be paid in railway shares as well as bonds. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新聞紙法

## 2. Book law and the obscenity standard

### 出版条例 (publication ordinances)
- **First steps.** Book licensing began with a 太政官 proclamation of **Keiō 4/閏4 (leap 4th month, = 22 May–19 June 1868)**: 「新著并翻刻書類……以後総テ官許ヲ不経候品売買堅被差停候事」 (no sale of new or reprinted books without official licence). A 行政官達 of **Meiji 2/1/27 (= 9 Mar 1869)** followed. — Nipponica (清水英夫) in https://kotobank.jp/word/出版法-77915 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/出版条例
- **1869 出版条例** (行政官達第444号, **Meiji 2/5/13 = 22 June 1869**). 20 articles. Five copies had to be deposited. It banned leaking state secrets, slander, and anything that "leads to lewdness" (淫蕩ヲ導ク). Censorship was given to the 昌平学校 and 開成学校, and licensing also protected the publisher's monopoly (an early copyright). — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/出版条例 ; Britannica and Sekai Daihyakka in https://kotobank.jp/word/出版条例-77913
- **Revision of Meiji 5/1/13 (= 21 Feb 1872).** 14 articles under the 文部省. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/出版条例 ; Yamakawa in https://kotobank.jp/word/出版条例-77913
- **1875 出版条例** (**3 Sep 1875**). Moved to the 内務省. Publication became a matter of notification, but the ministry could call in manuscripts and stop works judged harmful. 28 articles plus 8 penalty articles, with fines up to 100 yen. The same law created **版権** (copyright): 「三十年間専売ノ権」. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/出版条例 ; Yamakawa and Sekai Daihyakka in https://kotobank.jp/word/出版条例-77913
- **1887 出版条例** (勅令第76号, signed 28 Dec 1887). 34 articles; copyright split off into the 版権条例. — Nagoya DB https://jahis.law.nagoya-u.ac.jp/lawdb/l/120i0076
  - **Filing.** Ten days before issue, with **3 bound copies**, to the Home Ministry (Art. 3).
  - **Author's name.** The filing is jointly sealed by author and publisher. **If the author cannot be known, the publisher files and says so** (Art. 5). A pseudonymous book was therefore legal, but the publisher carried the filing.
  - **Who may publish.** Publishers must be professional booksellers, or the author (Art. 6). Printer and publisher names and addresses must be printed in the book (Art. 7).
  - **Serials.** Every instalment of a numbered serial must be filed separately (Art. 9).
  - **Art. 16.** The Home Minister may ban sale and distribution of, and seize the plates and copies of, any book or picture deemed 治安妨害 or 風俗壊乱.
  - **Art. 25.** "**猥褻ノ文書図画**" (obscene writings or pictures): author and publisher, as joint offenders, 1–6 months light imprisonment or 20–200 yen.
  - **Art. 26.** Photographs of such writings or pictures are treated the same way.

### 出版法, Publication Law (法律第15号, 14 April 1893), in force until 1949
- **Origin.** Drafted by 有松英義 and passed as a private member's bill (加賀美嘉兵衛) in the 4th Diet. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/出版法 ; Nagoya DB https://jahis.law.nagoya-u.ac.jp/lawdb/l/126a0015
- **Filing.** 3 days before issue (not counting transit time), with **2 copies** (Art. 3). The author-unknown provision survives (Art. 5). Publisher and printer must appear in the colophon (Arts. 7–8). — Nagoya DB, same
- **Art. 19.** 「安寧秩序ヲ妨害シ又ハ風俗ヲ壊乱スルモノト認ムル文書図画ヲ出版シタルトキハ内務大臣ニ於テ其ノ発売頒布ヲ禁シ其ノ刻版及印本ヲ差押フルコトヲ得」 — same
- **Art. 26.** Works aiming to "change the polity or subvert the national constitution": author, publisher and printer 2 months–2 years plus a 20–200 yen fine. — same
- **Art. 27.** 「風俗ヲ壊乱スル文書図画」: author and publisher 11 days–6 months or 10–100 yen. — same
- **Statistics.** Book bans numbered **124 in 1893** and **1,096 in 1913**, "mostly for 風俗壊乱" in the Taishō period. — Sekai Daihyakka 禁書 entry (彌吉光長), quoted in https://kotobank.jp/word/出版法-77915
- **Which officials banned what.** "Only 風俗壊乱 bans were delegated to prefectural governors; 安寧秩序 bans were issued in the Home Minister's name." 発売頒布禁止 was an administrative act; 発行禁止 was a judicial one. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/発禁
- **Penal codes.** 1880 Penal Code Art. 259: displaying or selling "booklets, pictures and other obscene objects harmful to morals": 4–40 yen fine. Art. 258: public indecency: 3–30 yen. 1907 Penal Code Art. 175: distributing, selling or displaying obscene documents or pictures: ≤500 yen or a petty fine. — Wikisource https://ja.wikisource.org/wiki/刑法_(明治13年太政官布告第36号) ; Nagoya DB https://jahis.law.nagoya-u.ac.jp/lawdb/l/140a0045
- **Ban statistics after the Russo-Japanese War** (Mitsuishi, from 内務省統計報告):
  - Banned items as a share of all publications: under 1% until 1904; **6.19% in 1905, 6.51% in 1906, 3.51% in 1907, 4.72% in 1908, 1.28% in 1909**.
  - Bans for 安寧秩序妨害 peaked at **75 in 1905** and were mostly under 40 in other years.
  - Bans for 風俗壊乱: **204 (1904), 1,578 (1905), 1,773 (1906), 916 (1907), 1,029 (1908), 1,259 (1909)**.
  - Mitsuishi explains the rise by the post-war boom in publishing, picture postcards and Chinese erotica, alongside naturalist fiction. — Mitsuishi, pp. 90–91, https://nagoya.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/2006591/files/nagujj_93_88.pdf

### The 1889 Kochō (蝴蝶) nude illustration controversy
- **Publication.** Yamada Bimyō's 山田美妙 『蝴蝶』 appeared in the New Year supplement (附録) to **『国民之友』 no. 37, dated 2 Jan 1889**, published by 民友社 at 徳富蘇峰's request. It is set at Dan-no-ura (1185). The court lady Kochō, loyal to the boy emperor Antoku, discovers that her lover 二郎春風 is a Genji spy and kills him, then learns that the emperor died the day before. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/蝴蝶_(小説) ; Dajisen in https://kotobank.jp/word/胡蝶-502271
- **The illustration.** 渡辺省亭 Watanabe Seitei drew Kochō half-naked on the shore in wet, half-shed clothing. Bimyō is said to have directed the design. It shows the influence of Seitei's teacher 菊池容斎's 「塩冶高貞妻の浴後の図」 in 『前賢故実』. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/蝴蝶_(小説) ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/渡辺省亭
- **The debate (裸蝴蝶論争).** Letters in the 『読売新聞』 attacked art as 風俗壊乱. Mori Ōgai defended the nude from Western aesthetics. Bimyō replied in 国民之友 no. 38. Iwaya Sazanami attacked Sohō for printing a poor nude. Ozaki Kōyō printed a criticism in 『我楽多文庫』, and Yoda Gakkai also objected. Scholar 山田俊治 calls the Yomiuri campaign "a censorious gaze subordinating art to worldly logic." — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/蝴蝶_(小説)
- **No ban found.** I found no evidence that the issue was banned. The controversy was carried on in the press. FLAG.

### Kuroda Seiki's 朝妝 (Morning Toilette) and later nude incidents
- **The painting.** Painted in Paris in 1892–93, oil, 178.5 × 98.0 cm. It shows a European model, who according to Kuroda was a provincial schoolgirl who had eloped to Paris with a student, standing nude before a mirror. It was accepted at the 1893 Salon (Société nationale) and well received at the 6th 明治美術会 exhibition, Tokyo, Oct 1894. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/朝妝
- **The 1895 Kyoto exhibition.** At the **4th 内国勧業博覧会, Kyoto Okazaki (1 Apr–31 July 1895; over 1.13 million visitors)**, juror 雨森菊太郎 (chief editor of the 日出新聞) condemned it as damaging public morals. On **8 April** the newspaper 『日本』 called it a kind of 春画 (erotic print) that should not be shown in public. Chief judge 九鬼隆一 wrote to the police chief, and the picture stayed on show with part of it covered by cloth. It won a 妙技二等賞. Saionji Kinmochi (Education Minister) had his brother 住友友純 buy it. It burned in the Kōbe air raid of June 1945. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/朝妝
- **Caricatures.** Georges Bigot drew the scene: a middle-aged man peering at the nude's buttocks, a woman hiding her face in her kimono skirt so that her legs show, and **a policeman who has forgotten his duty**. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/朝妝
- **Background.** ja.wikipedia says Tokyo issued a 裸体禁止令 against public nudity in 1871 and that police policed nude paintings. The Tokyo 違式詿違条例 of 1872, Art. 22, punished nakedness or bared thighs as 醜体 (see §4). — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/朝妝 ; https://note.com/glad_briony9143/n/n539bb8b59d41
- **The "loincloth incident" (腰巻事件).** Police objected to Kuroda's 『裸体婦人像』 at the 白馬会 exhibition, and the lower half of the canvas was covered with cloth. ja.wikipedia dates it "October 1900." FLAG: the painting is usually dated 1901; check. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/黒田清輝
- **1907.** At the Tokyo 勧業博覧会, 九鬼隆一 himself attacked a nude by Nakamura Fusetsu. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/朝妝

### Banned fiction, 1900–1910
- **Uchida Roan 内田魯庵, 「破垣」.** Published in 『文芸倶楽部』, Jan 1901; banned for 風俗壊乱. Roan wrote: 「我が作『破垣』は内務大臣より風俗壊乱と認められて発売禁止を命ぜらる」. — Nihon Kokugo Daijiten quoting 『社会百面相』 (1902), https://kotobank.jp/word/発売禁止-602936 ; Mitsuishi p. 89
- **Other bans listed by Mitsuishi.** 島崎藤村「旧主人」(1902); 生田葵山「富美子姫」(1906) and「虚栄」(1907); 佐藤紅緑「復讐」(1907); 小栗風葉「姉の妹」(1909); 水野葉舟「おみよ」(1910), which was banned **because of an inserted nude illustration of a woman**. Mitsuishi notes that works depicting adultery, prostitution or infidelity, that is women's sexuality, were the most likely to be punished. — Mitsuishi pp. 89–90
- **The 「都会」 case (1908).**
  - The Feb 1908 issue of 『文芸倶楽部』 was banned for 風俗壊乱 under the newspaper law.
  - Author **生田葵山 Ikuta Kizan was fined 20 yen**. Publisher-editor **石橋思案 was fined 40 yen** (20 as publisher plus 20 as editor), a double administrative and judicial punishment.
  - The prosecution turned on **adultery (姦通)**. The 1880 Penal Code Art. 353 punished a married woman and her lover with 6 months–2 years. The 1907 code Art. 183 set ≤2 years, only on the husband's complaint.
  - Press coverage: 萬朝報 and 東京二六新聞, 28 Feb 1908.
  - It was Kizan's third ban. — Mitsuishi pp. 91–92 ; Wikisource 旧刑法 Art. 353 ; Nagoya DB 140a0045 Art. 183
- **Mori Ōgai, 『ヰタ・セクスアリス』.** Published in 『スバル』 no. 7 (July 1909) under the signature 森林太郎. **That issue was banned about a month after publication.** Ōgai, then Surgeon-General of the Army, was disciplined by Vice-Minister of the Army **石本新六**. Some read the ban as aimed at Ōgai's official position, since no sex act is directly described. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ヰタ・セクスアリス. FLAG: the exact ban date, often given as 28 July 1909, is not on the page I read.
- **Nagai Kafū.** 『ふらんす物語』 (博文館, March 1909) was **banned the moment it was filed**. 『歓楽』 (1909) was also banned for 風俗壊乱. 『夏姿』 was banned in Jan 1915. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/永井荷風
- **Socialist books banned under the 出版法.** Kōtoku and Sakai's translation of the 『共産党宣言』 (1904); 『社会民主党建設者ラサール』 (1909); Sakai's 『婦人問題』 (1909); Katayama's 『万国社会党』 (1909); Sakai and Morichika's 『社会主義綱要』 (1910). — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/出版法

## 3. Political repression

### 集会条例, Public Meetings Ordinance (太政官布告第12号, 5 April 1880)
- **Contents.** 16 articles. Political lectures and associations had to notify the police **three days** in advance and obtain approval. Meetings had to be indoors. Uniformed police attended and could dissolve meetings. **Soldiers, police, teachers and students of all schools, and agricultural or craft apprentices** were barred from political meetings and associations. Advertising, and links between political societies, were forbidden. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/集会条例 ; Yamakawa, Britannica in https://kotobank.jp/word/集会条例-76769
- **Amendments.** December 1880: governors gained the power to dissolve societies and to ban speaking for up to a year. **3 June 1882**: the Home Minister could ban a speaker nationwide for 1 year, and branch offices were forbidden. The amended law had 19 articles. — Sekai Daihyakka, Yamakawa, same
- **1882 figures.** **86 violators; 351 dissolutions of meetings or associations.** — Sekai Daihyakka (大日方純夫), same
- **Students.** On **10 June 1882** the 文部省 banned even academic speeches by students. Students of the 明治法律学校 got round it by withdrawing from school for the duration of a meeting and re-enrolling afterwards. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/集会条例
- **Named cases.** 植木枝盛 (Nov 1879). **川上音二郎**, the future founder of 新派 theatre and husband of Sadayakko: on **13 Sept 1883** the Home Ministry banned him from political speaking for one year under Art. 6; in 1888 Kyoto police stopped his rakugo show 「獄中手枕の夢」. **岸田俊子 (中島湘烟)**: on **12 Oct 1883**, in Ōtsu, she was arrested and jailed after a speech titled **「箱入娘」 ("Daughters in boxes")**. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/集会条例 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/川上音二郎 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/中島湘煙
- **Women's school closed.** 福田（景山）英子's school for women, the 蒸紅学舎, was closed under the 集会条例 in 1884. — https://kotobank.jp/word/福田英子-124086
- **集会及政社法** (法律第53号, 25 July 1890).
  - **Art. 4:** 「……学校ノ教員学生生徒未成年者及女子ハ政談集会ニ会同スルコトヲ得ス」: **women**, minors, teachers and students may not attend political meetings.
  - **Art. 25:** they may not join political associations.
  - **Art. 7:** outdoor gatherings need approval 48 hours ahead, but "**sports by students**" (学生生徒ノ体育運動), funerals and festivals are exempt. — Nagoya DB https://jahis.law.nagoya-u.ac.jp/lawdb/l/123a0053
- **治安警察法** (法律第36号, 10 Mar 1900). Art. 5 keeps women and minors out of political associations and meetings. Women won the right to attend political meetings only in 1922. — Nagoya DB https://jahis.law.nagoya-u.ac.jp/lawdb/l/133a0036 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/治安警察法

### 保安条例, Peace Preservation Ordinance (勅令第67号, 25 December 1887, effective the same day)
- **Text.**
  - 7 articles.
  - Art. 1 bans secret societies and meetings.
  - Art. 2 lets police ban outdoor gatherings.
  - Art. 3 orders confiscation of the presses used for seditious printing.
  - **Art. 4:** anyone living within **3 ri (about 11.8 km) of the Imperial Palace** whom the police chief deems to be plotting rebellion or "likely to disturb public order" can be ordered out and barred from returning for up to 3 years. Refusal: 1–3 years plus up to 5 years of surveillance.
  - Art. 5 allows quasi-martial law in a district: prior police censorship of newspapers, a ban on carrying swords and firearms, and passports for travellers. — Nagoya DB https://jahis.law.nagoya-u.ac.jp/lawdb/l/120i0067 ; Sekai Daihyakka in https://kotobank.jp/word/保安条例-131568
- **The expulsions.** **570 people** were ordered out between the night of 26 Dec and 28 Dec 1887; **224 of them were from Tosa**. Named people include 尾崎行雄, 星亨, 中江兆民, 片岡健吉, 林有造, 中島信行 and 中島湘煙. **片岡健吉, 星亨 and others refused and were jailed at 石川島** (Sekai Daihyakka: 11 refused). Most went to Yokohama and Urawa, and the Akasaka temporary palace was guarded by two battalions of guards artillery. That evening half of Tokyo's patrolmen were at a year-end party in Shiba Park and were recalled. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/保安条例 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/保安条例-131568
- **Later use.** 54 people expelled 13 Jan 1891 and 8 more on 11 Feb 1891. Applied in Kōchi in 1892 during the election, then 143 and 37 people expelled in May 1892. Repealed 25 June 1898 (法律第16号). — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/保安条例

### Lese-majesté (不敬罪) and high treason (大逆罪)
- **1880 Penal Code** (太政官布告第36号, promulgated 17 July 1880, in force 1 Jan 1882). — Wikisource https://ja.wikisource.org/wiki/刑法_(明治13年太政官布告第36号)
  - **Art. 116:** 「天皇三后皇太子ニ対シ危害ヲ加ヘ又ハ加ヘントシタル者ハ死刑ニ処ス」 (harming or attempting to harm the emperor, the three empresses or the crown prince: death only).
  - **Art. 117:** 「天皇三后皇太子ニ対シ不敬ノ所為アル者ハ三月以上五年以下ノ重禁錮ニ処シ二十円以上二百円以下ノ罰金ヲ附加ス」; para. 2 applies the same to 「皇陵」 (imperial tombs).
  - **Art. 119:** other imperial family members: 2 months–4 years plus 10–100 yen.
  - **Art. 120:** surveillance for 6 months–2 years.
- **1907 Penal Code** (法律第45号, promulgated 24 Apr 1907; in force 1 Oct 1908). — Nagoya DB https://jahis.law.nagoya-u.ac.jp/lawdb/l/140a0045 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/大逆罪-556705
  - **Art. 73** (大逆): harm or attempted harm to the emperor, grand empress dowager, empress dowager, empress, crown prince or imperial grandson heir: **death only**. It covered planning and conspiracy as well as the act, and was tried at the 大審院 (Great Court of Cassation) only, with no appeal.
  - **Art. 74** (不敬): 3 months–5 years penal servitude; **神宮** (Ise) added beside the imperial tombs.
  - **Art. 76:** other imperial family members, 2 months–4 years.
- **Scope of 不敬.** A 1911 大審院 ruling held that lese-majesté was complete once disrespect was expressed, "whether or not anyone perceived it." A diary entry could count. — Sekai Daihyakka in https://kotobank.jp/word/不敬罪-124222 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/不敬罪
- **宮武外骨 Miyatake Gaikotsu.** On **4 Mar 1889** his 『頓智協会雑誌』 printed a parody of the Constitution's promulgation ceremony, 「頓智研法発布式」. He was convicted of lese-majesté and sentenced to **3 years** (October 1889). Time on remand was not counted, so he was in prison 3 years 8 months. By 1927 he had **43 bans** and was hailed as "the senior of the banned"; **17** of his magazines died after a single issue. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宮武外骨 ; Yomiuri 11 Nov 1892 quoted in https://kotobank.jp/word/不敬罪-124222
- **The High Treason Incident (大逆事件).** On 25 May 1910 a bomb plot by 宮下太吉 and others was uncovered. 26 people were indicted under Art. 73, and the 大審院 tried them in closed session over 16 hearings (10–29 Dec 1910). On **18 Jan 1911** 24 were sentenced to death and 2 to prison terms; 12 death sentences were commuted the next day. **11 were hanged on 24 Jan and 管野スガ on 25 Jan 1911**. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大逆事件 ; Sekai Daihyakka in https://kotobank.jp/word/大逆罪-556705

## 4. Laws on dress and the body

### 違式詿違条例 (minor-offence ordinances)
- **Tokyo version.** Promulgated by Tokyo-fu notice on **Meiji 5/11/8 (= 8 Dec 1872)**; in force **Meiji 5/11/13 (= 13 Dec 1872)**. Sekai Daihyakka (吉井蒼生夫) counts **5 general articles + 23 違式 (deliberate offences) + 25 詿違 (negligent offences) = 53 articles**. — Kotobank https://kotobank.jp/word/違式〓違条例-1145633 ; CRD https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?page=ref_view&id=1000141975
- **Penalties** (Arts. 1–3). 違式: 75–150 sen. 詿違: 6 sen 2 rin 5 mō–12 sen 5 rin. Those who could not pay received 10–20 strokes of the 笞 (違式) or 1–2 days detention (詿違). From 1876 the 笞 was replaced by 8–15 days penal labour, and from 1878 by 5–10 days detention. Police inspectors judged cases summarily. — lawyer 齊藤雅俊's annotated transcription http://donttreadonme.blog.jp/archives/1078601732.html
- **Sample Tokyo articles** (modern paraphrase):
  - Art. 9: selling 春画 (erotic prints).
  - Art. 11: tattooing.
  - Art. 12: running a mixed-sex bathhouse.
  - Art. 22: nakedness, bared shoulders or bared thighs (醜体).
  - **Art. 25: "男女相撲並蛇遣ヒ其他醜体ヲ見世物ニ出ス者"** (women's and men's sumo, snake-handlers and other "ugly" shows).
  - **Art. 39 (詿違): "婦人ニテ謂レナク断髪スル者"** (women cutting their hair short without good reason). — https://note.com/glad_briony9143/n/n539bb8b59d41 (paraphrase list; Art. 39 numbering from this page only) ; Art. 25 wording from donttreadonme.blog.jp, above
- **National version.** **各地方違式詿違条例**, 太政官布告第256号, **19 July 1873**: 90 articles (37 違式, 48 詿違). It includes **"婦人ニテ謂レナク断髪スル者"** as a 詿違 offence. Prefectures could add or delete articles after consulting the 警保寮. — Sekai Daihyakka at https://kotobank.jp/word/違式〓違条例-1145633

### The cross-dressing article
- **Text.** Added to the **Tokyo** ordinance as **Art. 62** by **司法省布達第131号 of 12 August 1873**:
  > 「第六十二条　男ニシテ女粧シ女ニシテ男粧シ或ハ奇恠ノ粉飾ヲ為シテ醜体ヲ露ス者　但シ俳優歌舞妓等ハ勿論女ノ着袴スル類此限ニ非ス」
  ("Men who dress as women, women who dress as men, or anyone who puts on a bizarre costume and so exposes an ugly appearance. Proviso: this does not apply, of course, to actors, kabuki performers and the like, nor to such cases as women wearing hakama.")
  On 27 Aug 1873 司法省布達第138号 corrected 粉飾 to **扮飾**. It was classed as **違式** (the heavier class), despite its position among the 詿違 articles. — transcription from the 法令 text in http://donttreadonme.blog.jp/archives/1078601732.html. The same wording, with 醜態, is in Hirayama Asako, https://note.com/shobunsha/n/n21c3eb21b38e
- **Exceptions.** Actors and kabuki performers (俳優歌舞妓), and **women wearing hakama** (女ノ着袴). The hakama exception mattered later, when schoolgirls adopted the hakama from the 1880s onward.
- **Reading of the offence.** The offence requires both cross-dressing and "exposing an ugly appearance" (醜体ヲ露ス). The lawyer reads it as an offence against public seemliness, not a religious taboo. — same
- **Tokyo only.** The cross-dressing article was **not** in the 1873 national ordinance. Prefectures added their own versions:
  - **Ōita**: male and female dress added as a 詿違 offence from 1 Jan 1877 (警第35号, 1876).
  - **Kyoto**: Art. 52 of the 京都府違式詿違条例 (布達第385号, 2 Oct 1876) has the same offence, with the exemption for 舞妓.
  - **Tochigi**: Art. 11 (乙第104号, 17 Apr 1878, in force 1 June 1878), 「奇怪ノ扮装ヲ為シテ徘徊スル者」. — same blog, citing 春田国男, 別府大学短期大学部紀要 13 (1994) pp. 46–47; 西村兼文『京都府違式詿違条例図解』(1876); 平野長富編『栃木県違式詿違条例図解』(1878)
- **Not carried into the 1880 Penal Code.**
  - All the 違式詿違 ordinances lapsed when the 1880 Penal Code took effect on 1 Jan 1882. Its 違警罪 (police offences, Arts. 425–429, 71 items) contain **no cross-dressing or nudity offence**. I checked the full text: no 粧, 扮, 裸 or 袒.
  - The 1877 French-language draft (Art. 478 no. 16), the Boissonade draft (Art. 486 no. 4) and the Japanese draft of Nov 1877 (Art. 477 no. 16: 「男ニシテ女装シ女ニシテ男装シ其他奇怪ノ扮装ヲ為シテ徘徊シタル者」, 5–50 sen) all included it. It was dropped from the final code.
  - However, **Art. 430 let each locality define additional 違警罪** (「各地方ノ便宜ニヨリ定ムル所ノ違警罪」). Some prefectural police-offence rules may have kept a cross-dressing clause after 1882. FLAG: unverified.
  - — Wikisource 旧刑法 https://ja.wikisource.org/wiki/刑法_(明治13年太政官布告第36号) ; blog above (citing 西原春夫等編『旧刑法（明治13年）(2)-Ⅱ』pp. 803, 850–851)
- **Not revived in 1908.** The 警察犯処罰令 (内務省令第16号, 29 Sep 1908, in force 1 Oct 1908) has no cross-dressing clause. It did revive nudity under 3条2号: 「公衆ノ目ニ触ルヘキ場所ニ於テ袒裼、裸裎シ又ハ臀部、股部ヲ露ハシ其ノ他醜態ヲ為シタル者」. Its penalties are 1–29 days detention or a fine of 10 sen–under 20 yen, imposed by the police chief under the 違警罪即決例 (1885). — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/警察犯処罰令 ; blog above ; https://kotobank.jp/word/違警罪即決例-30255
- **A recorded enforcement case.** 『読売新聞』, **13 April 1881**: お秀, a fishmonger's daughter of 本芝二丁目, wore full male dress (a 大紋附 half-coat, 盲縞 tight trousers, a 三尺帯 sash), cropped "jangiri" hair and male speech. She had been "summoned to the police station and thoroughly admonished" (「警察署へ呼出され厚く御説諭」) under the 違式詿違条例. She then eloped with お駒, a waitress at a tea-stall in the grounds of 芝神明. The reporter wrote: 「女が女に惚るというは余りおかしい話だ」. — Hirayama Asako, https://note.com/shobunsha/n/n21c3eb21b38e

### Women's short hair
- **散髪脱刀令** (太政官第199号, **Meiji 4/8/9 = 23 Sept 1871**). Text: 「散髪制服略服脱刀共可為勝手事　但礼服ノ節ハ帯刀可致事」 (cropping the hair, uniform or informal dress, and going without a sword are all at one's discretion; but swords must be worn with formal dress). It permitted short hair; it did not order it. — Wikisource https://ja.wikisource.org/wiki/散髪制服略服脱刀随意ニ任セ禮服ノ節ハ帶刀セシム ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/散髪脱刀令
- **Women's reaction.** Some women took it as applying to them. Kanazawa's 『開化新聞』 (No. 4, New Year 1872) reported many women there cutting their hair and **praised it** as saving the cost of combs, hairpins and oil. — https://ribiyo-news.jp/?p=38411
- **The press backlash.** 『新聞雑誌』, **Meiji 5/3 (= 8 Apr–6 May 1872)**: 「近頃、府下にて往々女子の断髪する者あり。固より我古俗にもあらず、また西洋文化の諸国にも未だ曽て見ざることにして、その醜態陋風見るに忍びず……女子は従順温和を以って主とする者なれば、髪を長くして飾りを用ゆるこそ万国の通俗なる……あたら黒髪を切り捨て開化の姿とか色気を離るるとか思ひて、すまし顔なるは実に片腹痛き業なり」. — https://www.riyo.or.jp/緑の黒髪にも変革の波～新風を巻き起こす女性に世間はびっくり～/ (barbers' federation history page; modernised kana)
- **Tokyo-fu notice: date and number.** "Ban on women cutting their hair" (女子断髪禁止), **東京府達第32号, Meiji 5/4/5 (= 11 May 1872)**. The 公文録 holds Tokyo-fu's request to the Dajōkan, 「婦人断髪ノ儀ニ付伺」, which argues that hairstyles making it hard to tell men from women should be banned. — National Archives of Japan, https://www.archives.go.jp/naj_news/13/anohi.html. NWEC's chronology gives "5月11日・東京府、女子の断髪禁止の告諭", which is the same date converted to Gregorian. — https://w-archive.nwec.go.jp/contents/nwec/chronological.html
- **Tokyo-fu notice: wording.** I could not obtain the verbatim text. The barbers' federation gives a modern paraphrase: 「婦女子のザンギリと男装はひっきょう『散髪の儀は勝手たるべし』とのかねての布告の趣旨のとり違えであるから婦女は従前のとおりにせよ」. This explicitly couples women's cropped hair with **male dress**. — https://www.riyo.or.jp/… (above). FLAG: paraphrase, not the original.
- **In the ordinances.** It then entered the Tokyo 違式詿違条例 (Dec 1872; Art. 39 per one paraphrase) and the national version (1873) as 「婦人ニテ謂レナク断髪スル者」. — Kotobank above
- **Workarounds.** Women with legitimate reasons, such as illness or widowhood, were told to obtain permission in advance. A **用捨証** (a certificate that the fine had been paid) was issued so that a cropped woman would not be fined every time she went out. — https://ribiyo-news.jp/?p=38411, citing 石井研堂『明治事物起原』. FLAG: secondary.
- **Hair reform later.** A 「婦人断髪禁止令」 dated **13 Feb 1873** is mentioned by the barbers' federation page without detail (FLAG: unidentified). The 婦人束髪会 (Western-style bun society) was founded in 1885. A survey in Osaka in 1888 found one woman in five of 89 wearing the bun. — https://www.riyo.or.jp/… ; NWEC chronology
- **Men's topknots.** The emperor cut his hair in March 1873. 島津久光 and 忠義 and the swordsman **榊原鍵吉** kept their topknots; 忠義 attended the 1889 Constitution ceremony in one. Iwakura Tomomi left Yokohama in a topknot in 1871 and had it cut in Chicago. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/散髪脱刀令

## 5. Swords and duels

### Wearing swords
- **森有礼's proposal.** Mori proposed abolishing swords for all but officials and soldiers at the 公議所 in 1869. It was rejected unanimously, Mori was told to resign, and opponents said abolition would "sap the spirit of the imperial land." The month is given as Meiji 2/3 (ja.wikipedia; = from 12 Apr 1869) or Meiji 2/5 (Yamakawa; = 10 June–8 July 1869). FLAG: conflict. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/廃刀令 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/散髪脱刀令-514608
- **Commoners.** Commoners were barred from wearing swords from **Meiji 3/12/27 (= 16 Feb 1871)**. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/散髪脱刀令 (Gregorian date as given there; conversion confirmed)
- **The 廃刀令** (帯刀禁止令; 太政官布告第38号, **28 March 1876**). Officially titled 「大礼服並軍人警察官吏等制服着用ノ外帯刀禁止ノ件」. It bans wearing swords except with court dress (大礼服) and the regulation uniforms of soldiers and police. It was proposed by Army Minister Yamagata (Dec 1875) on the ground that conscription and a police force made private swords unnecessary. **Ownership was not banned; only wearing.** It also ended the custom of commoners wearing a wakizashi at weddings, funerals, New Year visits and on journeys, and newspapers reported many arrests of commoners for this. Formally repealed 1 July 1954 (法律第203号). — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/廃刀令 ; Sekai Daihyakka, Yamakawa, Nipponica in https://kotobank.jp/word/廃刀令-112989 and https://kotobank.jp/word/帯刀禁止令-91672
- **Reactions.** Some former samurai carried swords in cloth bags, or **on the shoulder** ("as long as I'm not wearing it"). The 神風連 rising (1876) followed. ja.wikipedia claims that heroes who fight with their fists in Meiji adventure fiction (e.g. 星塔小史「蛮カラ奇旅行」, 1907) reflect the sword ban. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/廃刀令 (FLAG: interpretive claim)
- **1887 emergency powers.** The 保安条例 Art. 5(3) allowed a district-wide ban on carrying, transporting or selling "guns, pistols, gunpowder, swords and sword-canes" (仕込杖). — Nagoya DB 120i0067

### The duel law: 決闘罪ニ関スル件 (明治22年法律第34号)
- **Legislative history.** Passed by the 元老院 in readings of **21, 26 and 27 Dec 1889**; sanctioned **28 Dec 1889**; promulgated **30 Dec 1889**, under Prime Minister Yamagata Aritomo and Justice Minister Yamada Akiyoshi. It predates the first Diet. — Nagoya DB https://jahis.law.nagoya-u.ac.jp/lawdb/l/122a0034
- **Original text:**
  - **Art. 1:** 「決闘ヲ挑ミタル者又ハ其挑ニ応シタル者ハ六月以上二年以下ノ重禁錮ニ処シ十円以上百円以下ノ罰金ヲ附加ス」 (issuing or accepting a challenge: 6 months–2 years heavy imprisonment plus 10–100 yen).
  - **Art. 2:** 「決闘ヲ行ヒタル者ハ二年以上五年以下ノ重禁錮ニ処シ二十円以上二百円以下ノ罰金ヲ附加ス」 (fighting a duel: 2–5 years plus 20–200 yen).
  - **Art. 3:** killing or wounding in a duel is judged under the Penal Code.
  - **Art. 4:** seconds and witnesses, "under whatever name, such as 証人 or 介添人", and anyone who knowingly lends the ground: 1 month–1 year plus 5–50 yen.
  - **Art. 5:** defaming someone **for refusing a challenge** is punished as defamation.
  - **Art. 6:** where the Penal Code penalty is heavier, the heavier applies.
  - — same
- **Origin of the law.**
  - **June 1888:** 松岡好一 published 「三菱高島炭鉱の惨状」 on Mitsubishi's Takashima coal mine in the magazine 『日本人』.
  - **Sept 1888:** 犬養毅 (then a journalist) denied it in the 『朝野新聞』. Matsuoka sent Inukai a challenge, with **三宅雪嶺 (雄二郎) and 志賀重昂 as seconds**. Inukai refused, calling it "a barbarous survival" (野蛮な遺風).
  - **30 Sept 1888:** at the 3rd joint debate of the five private law schools (第3回五大法律学校連合大討論会) in the 帝国大学 lecture hall, the Paris-trained prosecutor **光妙寺三郎** declared 「決闘は文明の華」 ("the duel is the flower of civilisation").
  - A run of copycat challenges followed, along with debate in the press and at law schools. The Sangiin Legislative Bureau: the law was passed "for fear that the Western custom of dueling would take root."
  - Unlike contemporary German law, it gave no leniency to duels fought by the rules.
  - — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/決闘罪ニ関スル件 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/光妙寺三郎 ; https://houseikyoku.sangiin.go.jp/column/column070.htm ; blog summary https://indoor-mama.cocolog-nifty.com/turedure/2007/12/post_d32d.html (Matsuoka's challenge wording: 「如何にも天下の公道に相背き候と心外無念に存じ候」)
- **People behind the challenge.** Matsuoka (1865–1921) had come to Tokyo at 15 and **studied swordsmanship under 榊原鍵吉**. Kōmyōji was 千歳米坡's husband for a period (see §6). — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/松岡好一 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/千歳米坡-1365680
- **Later prosecutions and status.**
  - A 大審院 ruling of **14 Oct 1907** (明治40年(れ)第916号) defined 決闘 as fighting "by agreement, with violence capable of injuring body or life, regardless of the number of parties or whether the weapons are equal." So the law was being prosecuted in late Meiji.
  - The law is **still in force**. The Supreme Court ruled on it on 16 Mar 1951. Prosecutors received **34** suspects under it in 2005. Two Tokyo high-school students who arranged a fight on social media were arrested in 2019.
  - On **7 Jan 2026** police arrested a 26-year-old man after an agreed fight in Kabukichō on 23 Sept 2025; the other man died on 12 Oct.
  - The 2022 penal reform changed only the penalty names: Arts. 1–2 now read 拘禁刑 6 months–2 years and 2–5 years.
  - — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/決闘罪ニ関スル件 ; https://www.bengo4.com/c_1009/n_19867/ ; e-Gov https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/122AC0000000034

## 6. Theatre

- **1872 instruction to kabuki.** In 1872 kabuki people were summoned to the Tokyo-fu office. They were told to make their plots moral and fit for **nobles and foreigners to watch**, and to stop "fanciful fabrications" (狂言綺語). — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/演劇改良運動. FLAG: the source gives no exact date or text. Nipponica: the Tokyo-fu and police made kabuki stress **勧善懲悪** (reward virtue, punish vice). — search snippet from NTJ 文化デジタルライブラリー; not opened
- **1878 meeting.** On **28 Apr 1878** Itō Hirobumi and Yoda Gakkai, at the house of 内務大書記官 松田道之, urged 守田勘弥, 市川團十郎 and 尾上菊五郎 to reform the theatre. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/演劇改良運動
- **演劇改良会.** Founded **Aug 1886** by 末松謙澄, with 渋沢栄一, 外山正一 and others, backed by Itō. Its manifesto appeared in the 歌舞伎新報 on 6 Aug 1886. It called for **abolishing onnagata in favour of actresses**, abolishing the 花道, reforming theatre buildings and teahouses, and later a 2,000-seat theatre costing 200,000 yen. Suematsu called kabuki 「猥褻野鄙」 (obscene and vulgar). It produced the **天覧劇** at Inoue Kaoru's house on **26–29 Apr 1887**, with the 義太夫 chanters and ツケ打 removed and the texts edited. It dissolved in 1888. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/演劇改良運動 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/演劇改良会-821446 ; NDL column https://www.ndl.go.jp/scenery/column/tokyo/theatrical_3.html
- **Police licensing and rules.** All Tokyo; from Okamoto Kidō's 『明治演劇年表』, https://www.aozora.gr.jp/cards/000082/files/50705_42624.html:
  - **Oct 1886:** a 警視庁令 limited performances to **8 hours**.
  - **Aug 1890:** revised 劇場取締規則 created large and small theatres, capped at **10 large and 12 small**. The **actor tax** for large-theatre actors was set at 3, 2, 1 yen, 50 sen or 10 sen by rank; small-theatre actors paid 1 yen, 50 sen or 10 sen.
  - **15 Jan 1900:** the cap rose to 27 theatres and performances to 9 hours.
  - **Nov 1891:** at the Kabukiza, the Korean legation protested a scene in 「太閤軍記朝鮮巻」 showing the Korean queen and princes captured, and it was cut.
  - **July 1897:** the Kabukiza's 「小猿七之助」 drew charges of being 卑猥残忍 (obscene and cruel) and **closed mid-run after a police warning**.
- **Unlicensed theatres.** The 1880 Penal Code made it a police offence to open a theatre or show without a licence or to break its rules (違警罪, Art. 428 item 5). — Wikisource 旧刑法
- **Script censorship.** The 警視庁令第41号 「演劇取締規則」 (**Nov 1900**) governed play censorship until 1921. By the 1921 rules, scripts had to be submitted 10 days before performance, and content against 勧善懲悪, obscenity, cruelty or open political criticism could be cut or banned. A police inspector also attended performances, a second layer of censorship. — 藤井なつみ「大正期東京における演劇検閲の変容」『史学雑誌』134-2 (2025), https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/shigaku/134/2/134_1/_article/-char/ja
- **The censor 船茂芳五郎.** He handled Tokyo theatre censorship "from mid-Meiji onward." He joined the 警視庁 as a clerk and police inspector in **1893**, and as 保安課風俗係長 controlled theatres and cinemas as well as **licences for restaurants, assignation houses and geisha houses**. He retired 5 Mar 1917. Managers praised him for censoring "with tact." — same, p. 9 ff.
- **Banned words in titles.** The playwright 岡鬼太郎 recalled in 1922 that at one time the words **恋 (love), 心中 (double suicide), 殺 (kill) and 血 (blood) were banned from play titles**, and "colour and love" were sometimes struck even from dialogue. — same. FLAG: period not stated, probably late Meiji–Taishō.
- **Women performers.**
  - **女役者.** Women's kabuki troupes (女芝居), heirs to the お狂言師 who had performed for the shogun's and daimyō's women's quarters, played **all roles, including men's**. The first recorded troupe is 坂東桂八, as 岩井粂八, at the 薩摩座 in spring 1868. — Nipponica in https://kotobank.jp/word/女役者-168179
  - **市川九女八 (1847–1913).** Nicknamed "the female Danjūrō" (女団洲). She became Danjūrō IX's only female pupil in 1888 and was expelled in 1893 for staging 『勧進帳』 without permission. She taught at the actress schools of 1906 and 1908. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/市川九女八
- **Mixed casts permitted, August 1890.** Okamoto Kidō: "in August of that year the 警視庁 newly authorised that **mixed male–female performances (男女合併興行)** in theatres would henceforth be permitted." The ban dated from the Edo period, and the change came "as a bolt from the blue," apparently argued from Western practice. The press was cool and no major theatre hired actresses. Even in the 1890s 川上音二郎 at first refused to take on young actresses "for the sake of backstage morals." — 『明治劇談 ランプの下にて』, https://www.aozora.gr.jp/cards/000082/files/49526_42385.html (section 「男女合併興行の許可」, in the Meiji 23 chapter)
- **済美館 (済美団).** 伊井蓉峰's company, billed as "男女合同改良演劇", opened at the 吾妻座, Asakusa, with 依田学海's 『政党美談淑女之操』 and the former geisha **千歳米坡** as actress. Date: **Nov 1891** (Sekai Daihyakka; Kidō's chronology) or **Oct 1891** (Kidō's 明治劇談). FLAG. 千歳米坡 (1855–1918) was "for a time the wife of the politician 光妙寺三郎." — https://kotobank.jp/word/済美館-1350297 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/千歳米坡-1365680 ; Kidō, both texts above
- **Later Meiji actresses.** 川上貞奴 and 音二郎 founded the 帝国女優養成所 in 1908, for girls aged 16–25 with higher-elementary schooling and two guarantors. The **Imperial Theatre** staged its first actress production in May 1911 (森律子, 村田嘉久子). — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/川上貞奴 ; Kidō 年表
- **Musume gidayū (娘義太夫, 女義太夫).**
  - **Legal status.** Banned in the Tenpō reforms, when 36 were jailed. Women were admitted to the yose by the revised **寄席取締規則 of 1877**. — https://kotobank.jp/word/女義太夫-456030 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/女義太夫
  - **Stars.** Ayanosuke reached Tokyo in 1885 or 1887 (sources differ) and Kotosa in 1886. Both were early teenagers who **performed in a "boyish style" (ボーイッシュ・スタイル)**. Ayanosuke was called 「八丁荒し」 for emptying the yose within eight chō. She sold hundreds of photographs a day and retired in 1898 to marry. — Nipponica in https://kotobank.jp/word/女義太夫-456030 ; NDL 本の万華鏡 https://www.ndl.go.jp/kaleido/entry/34/3.html

  - **Official and press attacks.** In **1898** Education Minister **外山正一** remarked on disorder at the yose, and managers began barring fans from dressing rooms. In **Aug 1900** the 『万朝報』 ran 「娘義太夫荒し 青年の腐敗」, **printing the names, addresses and occupations of leading fans**. In Nov 1900 it ran 「風俗壊乱営業娘義太夫」. — same ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/女義太夫 (quote: 「腐敗々々、青年道心の大腐敗。危険々々、社会風教の大危険」)

## 7. Christian, socialist and women's publications

- **Christianity tolerated in 1873.** The anti-Christian notice boards were taken down on **24 Feb 1873**, and abroad the government declared Christianity permitted. At home no such announcement was made. Release of the exiled Urakami Christians was ordered on 14 Mar 1873; of 3,394 exiled, 662 had died. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/浦上四番崩れ
- **Uchimura Kanzō's "disrespect" (内村鑑三不敬事件).** On **9 Jan 1891**, at the 第一高等中学校's ceremony for reading the Imperial Rescript on Education, Uchimura bowed but did not make the **deepest bow (最敬礼)** before the imperial signature. Colleagues and students denounced him, and he resigned in February. This was a social sanction, not a prosecution. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/内村鑑三不敬事件
- **Inoue Tetsujirō's attack.** Inoue began in an interview in 『教育時論』 (late 1892; FLAG on exact date). The book 『教育ト宗教ノ衝突』 followed (敬業社, 1893). He concluded that 「耶蘇教は元と我邦に適合せざるの教なり」 ("Christianity is inherently unsuited to our country"). — Nipponica in https://kotobank.jp/word/井上哲次郎-31928 ; publisher from 東洋大学 paper https://toyo.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/6473/files/enryocenternenpo22_023-049.pdf
- **社会民主党.** Founded 20 May 1901 and banned at once. — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/赤旗事件
- **Weekly 『平民新聞』**
  - Ran 15 Nov 1903–29 Jan 1905, 64 issues, typically 3,000–4,000 copies.
  - **Issue 20's editorial 「嗚呼増税」 (27 Mar 1904)** earned Sakai Toshihiko 2 months' light imprisonment.
  - Issue 53 (13 Nov 1904), the first anniversary issue, carried the **first Japanese translation of the Communist Manifesto** and was banned.
  - Fines, sale bans and finally **confiscation of the printer's presses** followed. The last issue, no. 64, was **printed entirely in red ink**, imitating Marx's 『新ライン新聞』.
  - — Sekai Daihyakka, Nipponica in https://kotobank.jp/word/平民新聞-129042 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/平民社
- **Daily 『平民新聞』**
  - Ran from 15 Jan 1907. Issue 63's 「青年に訴ふ」 was prosecuted as 朝憲紊乱.
  - The Japan Socialist Party was banned on 22 Feb 1907.
  - The paper ended on **14 Apr 1907**, with issue 75 again printed all in red (Sekai Daihyakka, Nipponica); Yamakawa says it was banned 13 Apr and ended with issue 74. FLAG.
  - 山口孤剣's 「父母を蹴れ」 ("Kick your parents"), attacking the family system, earned him 3 months (1 year 2 months with other counts).
  - — same ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/赤旗事件
- **The Red Flag Incident (赤旗事件), 22 June 1908.**
  - At the 錦輝館 cinema in Kanda, at a party for Yamaguchi's release, 荒畑寒村, 大杉栄 and others unfurled red flags lettered in white 「無政府共産」, **「社会革命」** and "SOCIALISM", sang revolutionary songs and ran into the street. Police were waiting and seized the flags.

  - Sentences: 大杉 2½ years heavy imprisonment plus 25 yen; 堺, 西川 and 森岡 2 years plus 20 yen; 荒畑 and 宇都宮 1½ years.

  - — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/赤旗事件
- **Fukuda Hideko's 『世界婦人』.**
  - Founded **1 Jan 1907**, with 石川三四郎's help and contributions from 安部磯雄, 木下尚江 and 幸徳秋水. NWEC calls it Japan's first socialist women's magazine.

## 8. Official language about "dangerous" ideas, novels, girls and students

- **The 1906 Makino instruction.** 「学生生徒ノ風紀振粛ニ関スル件」, **文部省訓令第一号, 9 June 1906**, Education Minister 牧野伸顕. Full text on MEXT. Key passages:
  - 「近来青年女子ノ間ニ往々意気銷沈シ風紀頽廃セル傾向アルヲ見ル」 (young men and women are listless and their morals decaying).
  - Students 「或ハ小成ニ安シ奢侈ニ流レ或ハ空想ニ煩悶シテ処世ノ本務ヲ閑却スル」 (settle for small success, drift into luxury, or **agonise over fantasies** and neglect their duties).
  - 「近時発刊ノ文書図画ヲ見ルニ或ハ危激ノ言論ヲ掲ケ或ハ厭世ノ思想ヲ説キ或ハ陋劣ノ情態ヲ描キ教育上有害ニシテ断シテ取ルヘカラサルモノ尠シトセス」 (recent writings and pictures carry **violent language, pessimistic thought, or depictions of base passions**).
  - Schools must vet students' reading, **encourage** what is useful, and 「苟モ不良ノ結果ヲ生スヘキ虞アルモノハ学校ノ内外ヲ問ハス厳ニ之ヲ禁遏スル」 (strictly forbid anything that might have bad results, **inside or outside school**).
  - Also: 「極端ナル社会主義ヲ鼓吹スルモノ……教員生徒等ヲ誑惑セムトスル」, a danger that might 「建国ノ大本ヲ藐視シ社会ノ秩序ヲ紊乱スル」 (scorn the foundations of the state and disorder society).
  - — https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317975.htm
- **Background to the instruction.** The suicide of student 藤村操 at the Kegon Falls (May 1903) made 「煩悶青年」 ("anguished youth") a byword. — Mitsuishi p. 91
- **Girls' schools.** On **16 Sept 1902** the 文部省 sent all higher girls' schools a confidential instruction (内訓) to tighten moral and conduct training and to police girls' daily lives, and inspectors were sent to check. — NWEC chronology https://w-archive.nwec.go.jp/contents/nwec/chronological.html. FLAG: a blog claims an **1899 national conference of girls'-school principals banned girls from reading novels**; I could not verify it (search snippet only, https://bunken-magazine.amebaownd.com/posts/4946237/).
- **"Kakumei" (革命).** I found **no** official or police statement singling out the word 革命 as dangerous in Meiji. What I did find:
  - the statutory formulas 「政体ヲ変壊シ朝憲ヲ紊乱セントスル」 (1887–1909 press and book laws) and 「皇室ノ尊厳ヲ冒瀆」 (added 1897);
  - the 1906 instruction's 「危激ノ言論」 and 「極端ナル社会主義」;
  - police seizure of red flags reading 「社会革命」 in 1908;
  - the playwright's report of banned title words (恋, 心中, 殺, 血).
  - FLAG: absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.
- **Students and politics.** From 1880 students and teachers were excluded from political meetings by statute (集会条例; 1890 Art. 4; 1900 Art. 5). — see §3

## Telling details

- **The cross-dressing ban was Tokyo's alone, lasted about eight years, and exempted actors and women in hakama.** The wording paired 「男ニシテ女粧シ女ニシテ男粧シ」 with "bizarre costume that exposes ugliness." In 1877 the Japanese drafters of the national penal code wanted it ("men dressed as women, women dressed as men… loitering": 5–50 sen) and then dropped it. (donttreadonme.blog.jp)
- **お秀 of Shiba, 1881.** A crop-haired fishmonger's daughter in a workman's coat and tight trousers, nicknamed 「男女」 by her neighbours, was hauled in under the ordinance and "admonished." She ran off with the tea-stall girl お駒 from the shrine grounds at 芝神明. The Yomiuri found it "too strange, a woman falling for a woman." The ordinance had nine months left to live. (Hirayama)
- **Other cross-dressing cases.** A female thief, 「異人お鉄」, travelled in false whiskers in 1892 until a fake testicle fell out of her loincloth at a public bath (Yomiuri, 11 Nov 1892). In 1884 a woman raised as a son, 仲次郎, married お亀, who was legally male as 亀太郎 (Yomiuri, 13–14 June 1884). (Hirayama)
- **Short hair ban.** Tokyo-fu's 1872 request to the Dajōkan argued against hairstyles that blur the line between men and women. The modern summary of the notice pairs **「ザンギリと男装」** (cropped hair and male dress) as one misunderstanding of the 1871 decree. A cropped woman could buy a **用捨証** so as not to be fined every time she stepped outside. (NAJ; riyo.or.jp; ribiyo-news)
- **The duel as "the flower of civilisation."** Kōmyōji Saburō, the first Japanese to hold a Paris law degree, said so in the Imperial University's lecture hall on 30 Sept 1888, before the five private law schools. Fifteen months later the Senate made it a crime merely to **accept** a challenge, and a crime to **mock someone for refusing** one. The law still stands, and police used it in January 2026. Kōmyōji was for a time married to 千歳米坡, the geisha who became the first actress of the 1891 mixed-cast company. (ja.wikipedia; Kotobank; Nagoya DB)
- **The challenger's swordsman teacher.** Matsuoka Kōichi, who sent the challenge to Inukai with two famous journalists as seconds, had trained under 榊原鍵吉, the swordsman who refused to cut his topknot. (ja.wikipedia)
- **What the 1876 sword ban banned.** It banned the **wearing** of swords, not owning them. Some samurai carried theirs on the shoulder, and commoners were arrested for wearing a wakizashi to weddings. (ja.wikipedia 廃刀令)
- **No woman could publish a newspaper, 1887–1909.** The 1887 press ordinance required publisher and printer to be **male**, 20 or over, and Japanese. The 1909 law dropped the sex requirement. (Nagoya DB)
- **Pen names.** The 1875 press law banned pen names for newspaper writers. Book law instead let an author stay unknown, provided the publisher (a licensed bookseller) filed for them, and every instalment of a serial had to be filed 10 days before sale (1887) or 3 days (1893). (ja.wikipedia; Nagoya DB)
- **The censor became the censor-in-chief.** In 1876 Komatsubara Eitarō went to prison for the editorial 「圧制政府転覆すべし」. Thirty-two years later he was the Education Minister presiding over schools in the year of the High Treason trial. (ja.wikipedia 小松原英太郎)
- **The constitution parody.** Miyatake Gaikotsu parodied the Constitution's promulgation three weeks after the real ceremony and served 3 years 8 months. Seventeen of his magazines died after a single issue. (ja.wikipedia)
- **Red ink.** The anti-war 平民新聞 went out in red ink twice, in 1905 and 1907. In 1908 the red flags with 「社会革命」 brought down a cabinet. (Kotobank; ja.wikipedia)
- **「箱入娘」.** Kishida Toshiko was arrested after a speech under that title, "Daughters in boxes," in Ōtsu, 12 Oct 1883. From 1890 women could not even attend a political meeting. (ja.wikipedia; Nagoya DB 1890 Art. 4)
- **Bigot's policeman.** In Bigot's cartoon of the 1895 nude scandal, the policeman sent to guard 朝妝 is gaping at it. Kuroda said his model was a schoolgirl who had eloped to Paris with a student. (ja.wikipedia 朝妝)
- **One censor for a generation.** From 1893 to 1917 one police officer, 船茂芳五郎, censored Tokyo's plays **and** licensed its geisha houses and assignation inns. (藤井 2025)
- **A woman playing Danjūrō's roles.** 市川九女八, "the female Danjūrō," played men's roles in all-women kabuki. In 1890 the police legalised mixed casts, and no major theatre would hire her. (Kotobank; Kidō)
- **Boyish girl chanters.** Ayanosuke and Kotosa performed gidayū in a "boyish style" as young teenagers in the 1880s and drew student fan clubs. In 1900 the 万朝報 printed the fans' names and addresses as "the corruption of youth." (Nipponica; NDL)
- **The 1906 ministerial complaint.** Students who 「空想ニ煩悶シテ」 ("agonise over fantasies") and read works 「厭世ノ思想ヲ説キ」 ("preaching pessimism") were to be stopped "inside or outside school." (MEXT)

## Uncertain / conflicting

1. **1887 press ordinance date.** 28 Dec (signature, and some encyclopedias) vs 29 Dec (promulgation). Both are correct for different acts. The 1897 amendment likewise: sanctioned 19 Mar, promulgated 24 Mar 1897 (Nipponica says 19 Mar; Sekai Daihyakka and ja.wikipedia say 24 Mar).
2. **What 1909 restored.** Britannica says the 1909 law restored administrative 発行禁止・停止 (permanent ban and suspension) and dates promulgation to 30 Apr 1909. The statute text shows the Home Minister regained only **sale/distribution bans and seizure** (Art. 23); banning a paper (Art. 43) was for the courts. Promulgation was 6 May 1909 per Nagoya DB and ja.wikipedia.
3. **Unsourced press statistics.** 203 journalists jailed 1875–80; 47 papers closed within a month of April 1883; 355→199 papers; 讒謗律 arrests 7 (1875) and 40 (1876). All from ja.wikipedia without footnotes. Suehiro Tetchō's 1875 fine of "二千円" is almost certainly wrong.
4. **1883 deposit amounts.** Not verified. The 1,000/700/350-yen figures are the 1887 text.
5. **Tokyo 違式詿違条例 article count.** 53 (Sekai Daihyakka: 5+23+25) vs 54 (Hirayama; the paraphrase list runs to Art. 54). Article numbers in the modern paraphrase (e.g. Art. 39 for women's hair) are not checked against the original.
6. **Date of the cross-dressing Art. 62.** 12 Aug 1873 per the lawyer's citation of 司法省布達第131号, vs "the end of the following year" per Hirayama. I prefer the dated citation. No primary image seen.
7. **Survival of cross-dressing bans after 1882.** Art. 430 of the 1880 Penal Code allowed local police offences; whether any prefecture kept a cross-dressing clause is unknown.
8. **Women's hair notice wording.** I did not obtain the verbatim text of 東京府達第32号. A "婦人断髪禁止令" of 13 Feb 1873 is unidentified. "4/5" (lunar) and "11 May" (Gregorian) are the same day, not a conflict.
9. **Duel law text and date.** Wikisource prints Art. 2 as 「三年以上五年以下」 and the sanction as 18 Dec. The Nagoya DB (from the original) and e-Gov have **二年**, and the Nagoya DB has 28 Dec. Wikisource is wrong on both, since the Senate was still debating on 27 Dec.
10. **The duel fad.** Beyond Matsuoka→Inukai (Sept 1888), I found no named later challenges. Every source says only that "challenges followed one after another." Student participation is unverified, and I could not search further (search budget exhausted).
11. **Mori Arinori's sword proposal.** Meiji 2/3 vs Meiji 2/5.
12. **Kuroda's 腰巻事件.** ja.wikipedia says Oct 1900; the painting 『裸体婦人像』 is commonly dated 1901. ja.wikipedia's statement that Kuki wrote to "警視総監 小倉信" in 1895 is odd, since the exhibition was in Kyoto.
13. **Kochō (1889).** No evidence of an official ban; the controversy was in the press.
14. **Vita Sexualis.** The exact ban date (often given as 28 July 1909) and the form of Ōgai's discipline need a primary source.
15. **済美館 opening.** Oct vs Nov 1891 (Kidō contradicts himself). Mixed casts permitted Aug 1890 is from Kidō's memoir; I did not see the police order itself.
16. **Daily 平民新聞.** Ended with issue 75 on 14 Apr 1907 (Sekai Daihyakka, Nipponica), vs banned 13 Apr and ended with issue 74 (Yamakawa).
17. **The 1872 kabuki summons.** Exact date and wording not found.
18. **Girls and novels.** The 1899 principals' ban on novel-reading is unverified. The 1902 内訓 is from the NWEC chronology only.
19. **"First suspension of a daily" (朝野新聞, May 1878).** The claim is unverified.
20. **Inoue's 『教育時論』 interview.** The date (Nov 1892?) is unverified.
21. **Calendar library.** `lunar-javascript` follows the Chinese calendar. It matched all Japanese anchors tested for 1868–72, but the Japanese Tenpō calendar can differ from the Chinese by a day at month boundaries in some years. Treat conversions within ±1 day of a month start with slight caution.
