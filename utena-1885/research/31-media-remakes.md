# 31 — Japanese media formats by decade, and how classics and public-domain stories were remade in them, 1900–2026

**What this file covers.** Real history only. It shows how a famous story that anyone may use gets remade again and again in Japan, format by format. It covers film, radio, TV, animation, manga, fan works, theatre and games.

**How it relates to file 27.** File 27 (`27-adaptation-practices.md`) covers 1870s–1930s practice in detail: rensageki, Zigomar, kamishibai, early radio and pre-1940 film counts. This file repeats only what is needed, and carries the story to 2026.

**Conventions.**
- Dates are Gregorian.
- Counts are what the cited page lists. "ja.wp list" means a list that may be incomplete.
- Each wikitext source was read raw via `action=raw`. The URL given is the normal article URL.

## 0. Timeline: new formats, and the first time each was used to retell an old story

- **1907: first Chūshingura film, a recording of a kabuki performance.**
  - 『忠臣蔵五段目』 recorded the 11th 片岡仁左衛門's name-taking performance.
  - The first fiction-film Chūshingura is 牧野省三 / 尾上松之助 『実演・忠臣蔵』, 1910.
  - https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/赤穂事件を題材とした作品
- **1909–1926: the 尾上松之助 era.** He appeared in **over 1,000 films** for 牧野省三 / 日活, mostly 立川文庫 hero stories. Film then had two branches: 〈旧劇〉 (period) and 〈新派〉 (contemporary). https://kotobank.jp/word/時代劇映画 (世界大百科事典)
- **1917–1918: first manga versions of a modern novel.**
  - 岡本一平 『坊ちやん繪物語』 (1917).
  - 近藤浩一路 『漫画坊つちやん』 (1918).
  - https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/坊つちやん
- **1923: the word 時代劇 is coined.**
  - Shōchiku billed 『女と海賊』 (伊藤大輔 script) as a 〈新時代劇映画〉.
  - After it was "a big hit" the 新 was dropped.
  - The word copies the kabuki division into 時代物 and 世話物.
  - Source: Itō, 《時代映画》 May 1955, quoted at https://kotobank.jp/word/時代劇映画
- **1925: radio begins** (JOAK). Details are in file 27 §5f.
- **1928: a manga anthology of literary classics.**
  - 『現代漫画大観 第2編 文芸名作漫画』, 中央美術社, published 1 Apr 1928, edited by 代田収一. NDL records: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000098-Icm_itemM331708 ; https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000001-I01211001000430461
  - The volume includes 北澤楽天's manga 『不如歸』. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/不如帰_(小説)
- **1931: first full talkie,** 『マダムと女房』 (五所平之助, Shōchiku Kamata). Details in §1b. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/マダムと女房
- **1953: television.**
  - NHK Tokyo began regular broadcasts at **14:00 on 1 Feb 1953**.
  - The opening-day schedule included a **kabuki scene, 「道行初音旅」 (吉野山の場)**.
  - NTV, the first commercial station, began on **28 Aug 1953**.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHK総合テレビジョン ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本テレビ放送網
- **1957: the word 劇画 first appears.** Late 1957, in 辰巳ヨシヒロ's 「幽霊タクシー」, 『街』 no. 12 (セントラル文庫, Nagoya). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/劇画
- **1958: first Japanese colour feature cartoon,** 『白蛇伝』, Toei Dōga, released 22 Oct 1958. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/白蛇伝_(1958年の映画)
- **1961 and 1963: NHK's two flagship drama series begin.**
  - Morning serial (連続テレビ小説) from **3 Apr 1961**: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/娘と私
  - Taiga drama from **7 Apr 1963**: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/花の生涯_(NHK大河ドラマ)
- **1975: fan conventions and anime adaptations of literature take off.**
  - Comic Market first held 21 Dec 1975: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/コミックマーケット
  - World Masterpiece Theater (『フランダースの犬』) from 5 Jan 1975: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/世界名作劇場
  - 『まんが日本昔ばなし』 from 7 Jan 1975: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/まんが日本昔ばなし
- **1992–1997: games become a format for retelling.**
  - Chunsoft "sound novels" 『弟切草』 (1992) and 『かまいたちの夜』 (1994).
  - 『サクラ大戦』 (27 Sep 1996).
  - Leaf's "visual novels" (1996–97).
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ビジュアルノベル ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/サクラ大戦
- **2003: first "2.5-dimensional" musical,** 『ミュージカル・テニスの王子様』, Tokyo Metropolitan Theatre, from 30 Apr 2003. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/2.5次元ミュージカル
- **2010s: writers and objects become game characters, with tie-ins to literary museums.**
  - 『文豪ストレイドッグス』 (manga from the Jan 2013 issue of 『ヤングエース』).
  - 『刀剣乱舞』 (14 Jan 2015).
  - 『文豪とアルケミスト』 (1 Nov 2016).
  - See §3.

## 1. Film

### 1a. How many times each classic was filmed

- **Chūshingura (忠臣蔵)**
  - 世界大百科事典: film versions of all kinds number **"well over 100"** (優に100本を超える).
  - 御園京平's 1966 count of main-story films only (no side-stories or variants): **22 in late Meiji, 38 in Taishō, 22 after the start of Shōwa**.
  - Industry saying: 「ヒット作の企画に困れば忠臣蔵」 ("stuck for a hit? do Chūshingura").
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/忠臣蔵映画-1185354
  - ja.wp says "over 80 main-story films alone" from prewar to postwar. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/赤穂事件を題材とした作品
- **Chūshingura: every major studio made its own all-star version in the 1950s and early 60s.**
  - Shōchiku: 1954 『忠臣蔵 花の巻・雪の巻』. This was the first postwar film allowed to use the word 忠臣蔵 in its title after the Occupation ended.
  - Daiei: 1958, 長谷川一夫 as 大石.
  - Toei: 1959, 片岡千恵蔵.
  - Tōhō: 1962, 稲垣浩. This was 原節子's last film.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/赤穂事件を題材とした作品
- **Chūshingura was banned under the Occupation.**
  - In **Nov 1945** GHQ issued rules forbidding revenge plots (仇討ち) in films. Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/時代劇映画
  - Kabuki 『仮名手本忠臣蔵』 returned to the stage in **1947**. Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/忠臣蔵
- **『四谷怪談』 (Yotsuya kaidan)**
  - English Wikipedia: filmed "over 30 times"; first film 1912; "some 18 times between 1913 and 1937". These numbers have no citation; see Uncertain. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yotsuya_Kaidan

    - A 2006 version set in the present day.
  - Spin-offs listed separately include a 1976 sex film (大蔵映画) and 三池崇史's 『喰女』 (2014).
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/四谷怪談
- **『金色夜叉』: 24 films, 1912–1954; 8 TV versions, 1955–1990.**
  - Films: 1912 ×2, 1918 ×2, 1921 ×3, 1922 ×2, 1923 ×2, 1924 ×2, 1925, 1930 ×2, 1932 ×2, 1933 ×2, 1934, 1937, 1948, 1954.
  - Female roles were played by men at first: in 1918 Omiya was played by the onnagata 衣笠貞之助.
  - 1930: 『剣戟から生れた金色夜叉』, a swordfight version.
  - 1963: TBS comedy 『笑説金色夜叉』.
  - 1990: Fuji 『新金色夜叉 百年の恋』.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/金色夜叉
- **『不如帰』: 19 Japanese films, 1909–1958, plus a Chinese film in 1926.**
  - The 1958 Shintōhō version was the last.
  - One NHK TV version, 1963.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/不如帰_(小説)
- **『坊っちゃん』: 5 films; about 14 TV dramas; 2 TV anime.**
  - Films: 1935 (P.C.L., 山本嘉次郎), 1953, 1958, 1966 (坂本九), 1977 (中村雅俊).
  - TV dramas 1957–2016. Among them: the comedy troupe The Drifters in 1970, 本木雅弘 in 1994, 二宮和也 in 2016.
  - Anime: Fuji 1980 (supervised by 出崎統, character originals by モンキー・パンチ, Botchan voiced by the pop singer 西城秀樹); NTV 1986.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/坊つちやん
- **『伊豆の踊子』: 6 films, each starring the leading girl-idol of its day.**
  - 1933 (田中絹代, silent), 1954 (美空ひばり), 1960 (鰐淵晴子), 1963 (吉永小百合), 1967 (内藤洋子), 1974 (山口百恵).

### 1b. Sound film
- **1927: 小山内薫's 『黎明』.** Made with the De Forest Phonofilm system; reportedly never released for technical reasons.
- **1929: Nikkatsu part-talkies.** 『大尉の娘』 and 『藤原義江のふるさと』 (溝口健二), using the Mina Talkie system.
- **1931: first full talkie.**
  - **『マダムと女房』, released 1 Aug 1931**, using the 土橋式 sound-on-film system.
  - At that time about 80% of Japanese films were still silent.
- **The changeover was slow.**
  - 成瀬巳喜男 made his first talkie in 1935, 小津安二郎 in 1936.
  - Even in **1938 one third of Japanese films were silent**, because the benshi kept silent film popular.
- Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/トーキー ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/マダムと女房
- **Who gained and lost with sound** (ja.wp, marked "citation needed"): 田中絹代's voice helped her; 入江たか子's aristocratic diction "provoked resentment". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/トーキー

### 1c. Studio genres
- **1910s: 旧劇 and 新派.**
  - In the 1910s, period films (〈旧劇〉) and shinpa melodrama films (〈新派〉) were the two branches of fiction film.
  - 1914 都新聞 review: 〈活劇か変化物でなければ夜も日も明けぬ〉 (audiences want nothing but action or transformation films).
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/時代劇映画
- **Shinpa repertoire.** Shinpa's "classics" were stage versions of newspaper novels, many of which later became films: 『金色夜叉』『乳姉妹』『不如帰』『己が罪』『婦系図』. https://kotobank.jp/word/新派劇 (Nipponica)
- **Kyoto as Japan's period-film capital.**
  - After the 1923 earthquake Nikkatsu and Shōchiku moved production to Kyoto, then called 〈日本のハリウッド〉.
  - 阪東妻三郎 built his studio at 太秦 in 1926.
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/時代劇映画
- **The 1930s "topknot modern drama".**
  - 山中貞雄's 『丹下左膳余話 百万両の壺』 (1935) opened with **a title card saying its story had nothing to do with 林不忘's original**.
  - It turned the swordsman hero into a comic layabout, an early case of a famous character re-imagined against its source.
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/時代劇映画
- **1930s shōjo films (girls' films).**
  - Many 吉屋信子 novels were filmed. Among them was **新興キネマ's 「乙女シリーズ」** (1935): 『その一 花物語 福寿草』 and 『その二 釣鐘草』, from her 『花物語』 girls' stories.
  - Also 『乙女橋』 (1936) and 『花つみ日記』 (東宝京都, 1939, from 『小さき花々』).
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/吉屋信子
  - 成瀬's 『乙女ごころ三人姉妹』 (1935) is based on 川端康成's 『浅草の姉妹』. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/乙女ごころ三人姉妹
- **1950s idol vehicles: the girl-singer star in a Meiji classic.**
  - 美空ひばり starred in 『伊豆の踊子』 (1954) and 『たけくらべ』 (1955). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/伊豆の踊子 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/たけくらべ
  - With 江利チエミ and 雪村いづみ she formed the "三人娘" of Tōhō's 『ジャンケン娘』 (1955) series. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/三人娘
  - Nikkatsu's 1960s 吉永小百合 / 松原智恵子 / 和泉雅子 trio is described as the prototype of the "idol film". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/三人娘
- **The 1950s boom.**
  - Toei (founded 1951) called itself the 〈時代劇王国〉. Its run of star series included 美空ひばり's period films.
  - **Cinema attendance passed 1.1 billion in 1958**, the all-time peak.
  - From 1963 Toei switched to yakuza films and period drama moved to TV.
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/時代劇映画

### 1d. 1950s–60s remakes
- **A star remakes his own hit.**
  - 『雪之丞変化』 (Kinugasa, 1935–36, three parts, 長谷川一夫).
  - Remade by 市川崑, released **13 Jan 1963**, as **長谷川一夫's 300th film**.
  - Daiei president 永田雅一 phoned Hasegawa from the meeting and got a yes on the spot.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/雪之丞変化
- **The same story as art film, then as cartoon.**
  - 溝口健二's 『山椒大夫』 (released **31 Mar 1954**, from 鷗外's 1915 story) won the **Silver Lion at Venice**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/山椒大夫
  - Seven years later Toei made it a cartoon with animal sidekicks (see §3). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/安寿と厨子王丸_(映画)
- **Hasegawa Kazuo in many classics.** He played Genji (1951), Kaoru (1957), 大石 in film (1958) and on TV (1964 taiga), and Iemon (1959). He then **directed the first Takarazuka 『ベルサイユのばら』 (1974)**. Sources: ja.wp pages above; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ベルサイユのばら_(宝塚歌劇)

## 2. Radio (from 1925) and television (from 1953)

### 2a. Radio
- **The first radio dramas (1925)** were literary and stage adaptations: a benshi performing 『噫無情』 alone, kabuki 『桐一葉』, and shinpa 『大尉の娘』. See file 27 §5f. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ラジオドラマ
- **Postwar daily drama serials.**
  - 『向う三軒両隣り』 (July 1947–1953) and 『鐘の鳴る丘』 (to 1950) "established the listening habit" of daily serials.
  - In Feb 1953 NHK had **137 staff radio actors in 7 troupes**.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ラジオドラマ
- **『君の名は』 (菊田一夫): radio drama, then novel, film, fashion craze and remakes.**
  - **Broadcast.** NHK Radio 1, **10 Apr 1952 – 8 Apr 1954, 98 live episodes**, Thursdays 20:30–21:00. 古関裕而 improvised the score live on a Hammond organ.
  - **Ratings.** It was not popular for its first six months. In the winter 1953–54 survey it reached **59% of listeners in Kantō**.
  - **The bathhouse legend.** "Women's baths emptied when it aired." This may have been invented by Shōchiku publicity; sources disagree.
  - **Tie-ins.** A novel (宝文館), then a Shōchiku film (1953). The heroine's scarf style became the 「真知子巻き」 fashion.
  - **Remake.** NHK's 1991 morning-serial remake **flopped**: average 29.1%, the lowest ever at the time. Mid-run, NHK changed the theme song and lightened the story.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/君の名は

### 2b. Television
- **NHK morning serial (連続テレビ小説, "asadora").**
  - **First series:** 『娘と私』 (3 Apr 1961 – 30 Mar 1962), from 獅子文六's novel. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/娘と私
  - **The early series adapted novels:** 壺井栄 (1962), 武者小路実篤 (1963), 林芙美子 (1964), 川端康成 (1965). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/連続テレビ小説
  - **A trial run before the format.** NHK aired 『伊豆の踊り子』 in three nights, 1–3 Jan 1961, under the 連続テレビ小説 label; ja.wp says "its success laid the groundwork" for the series. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/伊豆の踊子
  - **Recent series about Meiji literary figures:**
    - 2014 『花子とアン』, about 村岡花子, the Japanese translator of *Anne of Green Gables*.
    - Autumn 2025, about 小泉セツ, the wife of Lafcadio Hearn.
    - Autumn 2026, about 宇野千代.
    - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/連続テレビ小説
- **Taiga drama (大河ドラマ).**
  - **First series:** 『花の生涯』, from 7 Apr 1963, based on 舟橋聖一's novel. It was planned as a one-off.
  - **Made a yearly series** after 『赤穂浪士』 (5 Jan – 27 Dec 1964, from 大佛次郎's novel). NHK's drama chief 長沢泰治 ordered: 「国民的文学をやれ」 ("do national literature").
  - **Ratings.** Over 30% throughout. **The raid episode drew 53.0%**, still the taiga record.
  - **The name.** Yomiuri coined 「大河ドラマ」 during that run.
  - **The theme music was recycled.** 芥川也寸志 reused his score for the 1955 film 『たけくらべ』.
  - **Original scripts.** The first fully original taiga script was the 18th series, 『獅子の時代』 (1980). Since 2010 nearly all are originals.
  - **The taiga's Chūshingura.** It returned to Chūshingura in 1975, 1982 and 1999. 2024's 『光る君へ』 is about Murasaki Shikibu.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大河ドラマ ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/赤穂浪士_(NHK大河ドラマ) ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/赤穂事件を題材とした作品
- **Period drama moves to TV (1963–70s).**
  - 1963: NHK's first taiga and Fuji's 『三匹の侍』.
  - Through the late 1960s and 70s "every popular period subject" was serialised on TV: 鞍馬天狗, 宮本武蔵, 忠臣蔵, 銭形平次 and others.
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/時代劇映画
- **Chūshingura on TV.** Many versions retell it from a new angle:
  - The women: 『女たちの忠臣蔵』 (TBS 1979, 橋田壽賀子), and two follow-ups (1987, 1988).
  - A dramatist manipulating the rōnin: 『つか版忠臣蔵』 (1982).
  - Hired assassins working behind the scenes: 『必殺忠臣蔵』 (1987).
  - A cowardly Ōishi pushed into leadership: TBS 1990.
  - The shogun 綱吉's view: 2004.
  - Lady Yōzei-in as the plot's mastermind: 2007.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/赤穂事件を題材とした作品
- **Late-night and comedy versions.**
  - 『ドリフ大爆笑』's 「松の廊下」 sketch (1979).
  - 『ルパン三世』 episode 113 「作戦名は忠臣蔵」 (1979).
  - 『ドラえもん』 「チューシン倉」 (1980).
  - 『クレヨンしんちゃん』 300th-episode special (1998).
  - The ten-hour 吉本新喜劇 all-star live broadcast (NHK BS1, 1991).
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/赤穂事件を題材とした作品
- **Anthology slots for literary classics.**
  - Kansai TV 「川端康成名作シリーズ」 (1973).
  - TV Tokyo 「日本名作ドラマ」 (1993): 『門』, 『伊豆の踊子』, 『美徳のよろめき』.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/伊豆の踊子
- **NHK puppet serial 『新八犬伝』.**
  - **Run:** 2 Apr 1973 – 28 Mar 1975, **464 episodes** of 15 minutes, weekdays, **average rating 20%**.
  - **How freely it adapted.** It moved the story's period and grafted in plots from more than ten other Bakin works. A one-year run was extended to two.
  - **Lost.** **Only 4 episodes survive**; NHK erased the master tapes.
  - **Narrator.** 坂本九, as a kuroko figure. His line 「因果は巡る糸車…」 became a playground catchphrase.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新八犬伝
- **NTV 『西遊記』 (1978–80) and the female Sanzō.**
  - 夏目雅子 played Sanzō, a male monk.
  - **"Since then every Japanese TV remake of Saiyūki has cast an actress as Sanzō."**
  - Some child viewers believed Sanzō was a woman. The band Godiego wrote a love song for 悟空 and Sanzō, which was rejected.
  - Average rating about 19.5%; final episode 27.4%.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/西遊記_(1978年のテレビドラマ)

- **Toei Dōga feature films: how freely they changed their sources.**

    - Production: 7 months of drawing, **¥40,471,000 budget, 16,474 key drawings, 65,213 in-betweens**, 42 new animators.
    - Changes: added **a giant panda and a red panda** as sidekicks, at a time when Japan had never seen a live panda (the first arrived in 1972).

  - **『安寿と厨子王丸』 (19 Jul 1961), Toei's 10th-anniversary film.**
    - According to an editor's note in the ja.wp source, the credits name no source author (鷗外 is not credited); not verified on screen. The script by 田中澄江 had been serialised in 「よみうり少年少女新聞」.
    - Additions:
      - Pet animals: a mouse, a bear cub, a dog.
      - A maid who drowns and becomes a mermaid.

      - Zushiō slays a monster for the emperor.

    - Chūshingura with stray dogs against a tiger called "Killer".

    - Sequels: a Western (1972) and an *Around the World in 80 Days* (1976). Pero became Toei Animation's mascot.
    - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/長靴猫シリーズ ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東映アニメーション

## 4. Manga

- **Before the war.**
  - 『坊っちゃん』: 岡本一平's picture-book version (1917) and 近藤浩一路 (1918). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/坊つちやん
  - 1928 anthology 『文芸名作漫画』 (中央美術社), including 北澤楽天's 『不如歸』. NDL records as in §0; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/不如帰_(小説)
- **Kashihon manga (rental-library manga), 1948–1969.**
  - **Origin.** The postwar rental shop is traced to 「ろまん文庫」, Kobe, 1948. Rental manga books reached Tokyo around 1952–53.
  - **Peak, late 1950s – 1960.** About **3,000 shops in Tokyo and 30,000 nationwide**, counting stationers and sweet shops that also rented.
  - **Prices.** Joining fee 20 yen; **10–20 yen for 2 nights**. The books (100–150 yen) were not sold in bookshops.
  - **Economics.** A specialist shop needed 1,000–1,500 members and 150–200 customers a day.
  - **Readers.** Mostly working-class young men and women in factory jobs, older than magazine-manga readers.
  - **Decline.** TV and the new weekly magazines (Sunday, Magazine) took the market. Rental-publisher manga effectively ended **at the end of 1969**.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/貸本漫画
- **Gekiga (劇画), from 1957.**
  - **Name.** First used in 『街』 no. 12 (late 1957).
  - **Gekiga Kōbō (1959).** Founded by seven artists, 辰巳 and さいとう・たかを among them. They mailed **150 postcard "manifestos"** to newspapers, publishers and artists; Tezuka got one.
  - **Techniques taken from Hollywood film:** camera angles, close-ups for inner feeling, bold sound effects, speed lines.
  - **The boom.** In 1965, after Tezuka left 『週刊少年マガジン』, the magazine turned to gekiga artists. Adult manga magazines followed (1966 Comic Magazine; 1967 Manga Action and Young Comic; 1968 Big Comic). Student radicals in 1970 declared 「われわれは明日のジョーである」.
  - **Decline** after 1972.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/劇画
- **Kashihon versions of classics.** 小島剛夕's 『忠臣蔵』 first appeared in the kashihon series 『長編大ロマンシリーズ』. 双葉社 reprinted it from Aug 1970. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/赤穂事件を題材とした作品
- **Tezuka's adaptations of world classics.**
  - **『ファウスト』** (Fuji Shobō, **15 Jan 1950**). Influences: Takarazuka (the Easter scene), Fleischer cartoons, and the Soviet cartoon *The Humpbacked Horse*.
  - **『罪と罰』** (Tōkōdō, **5 Nov 1953**). His last book for an Osaka publisher, partly drawn on trains to Tokyo. **Its ending differs completely from Dostoevsky's: the Russian Revolution breaks out.** Tezuka had acted in a student stage 『罪と罰』 in 1947.
  - 『ベニスの商人』 came later.
  - Tezuka adapted literature "as one way to root manga in Japan as culture".
  - He later redid Faust twice: 『百物語』 (1971) and 『ネオ・ファウスト』 (1988, unfinished). He also did his own 『丹下左膳』.
  - Sources: https://tezukaosamu.net/jp/manga/418.html ; https://tezukaosamu.net/jp/manga/282.html
- **『まんがで読破』 (East Press; now Gakken). Slogan: 「みんな知ってる。でも読んだこと、ある?」.**
  - **First titles, Aug 2007:** 『人間失格』 (MD001), 『破戒』, 『こころ』. Oct 2007: 『罪と罰』, 『羅生門』, 『蟹工船』.
  - **139 titles by Dec 2020.**
  - Some change the plot (『人間失格』, 『変身』).
  - Mascots: the comedian 劇団ひとり (2008) and the TV personality 優木まおみ (2009).
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/まんがで読破
- **Manga versions of Genji.**
  - **『あさきゆめみし』 (大和和紀).**
    - Serialised in 『mimi』 from the **Dec 1979 issue**, ending 1993 in 『mimi Excellent』 no. 27. 13 volumes.
    - **18 million copies (Nov 2021).**
    - Treated as required reading for the classics section of university entrance exams; **reprinted every December and April**.
    - Takarazuka staged it in 2000.
  - **How 大和 changed the source:**
    - An original opening (the courtship of the Kiritsubo emperor) to hook girls'-manga readers.
    - **Hanachirusato, "thin and shabby" in the original, drawn plump**, so that modern readers would read her as unglamorous.
    - Borrowed scenes from 田辺聖子's novelised Genji, and art resembling 岡田嘉夫's illustrations.
    - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/あさきゆめみし
  - **The genre that followed** (all from https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/源氏物語):
    - Comic: 赤塚不二夫 (1983); 『パタリロ源氏物語!』 (2004–08), with the cast of 『パタリロ!』 playing Genji.
    - Faithful: 牧美也子 (1988–90); 長谷川法世 (1996–97, imitating scroll-painting style, with home-made kimono screentone).
    - Modern-day settings: 『月下の君』 (2001–04); 『源君物語』 (2011–19).
    - 江川達也 (2001–05).
    - 『百合源氏』 (2014).
    - 『いいね！光源氏くん』 (2015–21): Genji in present-day Tokyo; NHK drama 2020.
- **『大奥』 (よしながふみ): gender-reversed Edo.**
  - Serialised in MELODY, **Aug 2004 – Feb 2021**; **7 million copies** (Jun 2023).
  - A plague kills young men, so **the shoguns from the third on are women**, and the Ōoku houses "3,000 handsome men". Every chapter is labelled 「男女逆転!パラレル時代劇」.
  - Award: James Tiptree Jr. Award.
  - Adaptations: films 2010 and 2012; NHK drama 2023; Netflix anime 2023.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大奥_(漫画)
- **Gender-swapped Botchan.** 大和田秀樹's gag manga 『坊っちゃん♥』 (2016): Botchan is a tomboyish woman nicknamed "Botchan". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/坊つちやん
- **A publisher's classics series in manga.**
  - Chūō Kōron's 「マンガ日本の古典」 used famous artists: 石ノ森章太郎 (古事記), 横山光輝 (平家物語, vols 10–12), さいとう・たかを (太平記, vol 19), 長谷川法世 (源氏物語, 1996–97).
  - Dates and volume count are not confirmed; see Uncertain.

## 5. Fans: dōjinshi, Comic Market, parody, and the law

- **Literary coterie magazines (同人誌) before manga.**
  - The first literary 同人誌 is said to be **硯友社's 『我楽多文庫』, 1885**: 尾崎紅葉, 山田美妙, 巖谷小波 and others.
  - School pupils' coterie magazines were common enough that **in 1908 the Ministry of Education ordered them controlled** on moral grounds.
  - Until the 1960s manga 同人誌 were mostly hand-drawn originals passed round (肉筆回覧誌). Offset printing spread from about 1968.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/同人誌
- **Comic Market: the first one.**
  - **C1: 21 Dec 1975**, a meeting room of 日本消防会館, Toranomon. **32 circles** (about half selling by consignment or only exhibiting); **about 700 visitors**.
  - Organised by the critics' circle 「迷宮'75」. Announced in the info column of 『別冊少女コミック』 (Jan 1976 issue).
  - Nearly half the circles were school manga clubs; next came **少女漫画 fan clubs, mostly for 萩尾望都**.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/コミックマーケット
- **Growth, from Comiket's own 2014 overview:**
  - 1975–81 (C1–C18): 32 → 500 circles; 700 → 10,000 visitors.
  - 1981–86 (C19–C30, Harumi): 600 → 4,000 circles; 9,000 → 35,000 visitors.
  - 1986–90 (C31–C39): 4,400 → 13,000 circles; 40,000 → 250,000.
  - 1991–95 (C40–C49): 11,000 → 22,000 circles; 200,000 → 250,000.
  - 1996–99 (C50–C57, Big Sight): 18,000 → **35,000 circles (the limit of the whole hall over 3 days)**; 350,000 → 400,000.
  - 2000–06: 430,000 → 510,000.
  - 2007–13: 510,000 → **590,000**. C84 (Aug 2013): about 35,000 circles from about 51,000 applicants.
  - Source: https://www.comiket.co.jp/info-a/WhatIsJpn201401.pdf
  - ja.wp adds a peak of **750,000 at C97 (Dec 2019)**. After Covid: 260,000 (C102, 2023); 270,000 (C103); **300,000 (C107, Dec 2025)**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/コミックマーケット
- **The first organiser resigned over fan works.**
  - Co-founder 原田央男 hoped for original comics; instead **derivative work (二次創作) became the mainstream**.
  - He believed Comiket "should not exclude derivative work", so he resigned after C12 (Jul 1979).
  - 米沢嘉博 led from C14 (1980).
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/コミックマーケット
- **Which series drove the parody booms** (Comiket's own list; male or female fandom marked as in the source):
  - Girls' manga of the "Year 24 group" (female).
  - Yamato '77 and Gundam '79 (both).
  - The 1980 "lolicon" boom (male).
  - **『キャプテン翼』 from 1985 (female)**.
  - 『聖闘士星矢』 1987 and 『サムライトルーパー』 1989 (female).
  - Sailor Moon 1992 (both).
  - Evangelion and dating games 1995 onward (male).
  - Jump series 2000 onward (female).
  - Touhou, Kancolle and others.
  - Source: https://www.comiket.co.jp/info-a/WhatIsJpn201401.pdf
- **"Yaoi" (1979).**
  - Coined inside 坂田靖子's circle ラヴリ as a joke: 「ヤマなし・オチなし・イミなし」 (no climax, no punchline, no meaning), mocking editors who rejected their manuscripts on those grounds.
  - **First printed in 『らっぽり』 「やおい特集号」, dated 20 Dec 1979**, edited by 波津彬子. At first it had no sexual meaning.
  - **Captain Tsubasa** yaoi dōjinshi multiplied from about 1984. They reportedly made up **half of sales at summer Comiket 1986**, with nearly 1,000 Tsubasa circle applications.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/やおい
- **Policing, 1989–1991.**
  - The 1989 Miyazaki case and the 1990 "harmful comics" panic followed.
  - **Makuhari Messe cancelled C40 just before it opened (summer 1991)**. Comiket returned to Harumi and **began checking sample copies of every book**.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/コミックマーケット ; https://www.comiket.co.jp/info-a/WhatIsJpn201401.pdf
- **The law.**
  - **Parody can infringe.** In the **Parody Montage case** the **Supreme Court ruled on 28 Mar 1980** that Mad Amano's photomontage of 白川義員's ski photograph infringed. After two retrials the parties settled in **1987**, 16 years after the suit was filed. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/パロディ・モンタージュ写真事件
  - **First arrest over a fan comic: the Pokémon case.** Nintendo and two other rights holders filed a complaint on 5 Jan 1999. **Kyoto police arrested a Fukuoka woman on 13 Jan 1999** for a sexual Pokémon comic. She had sold 5 copies at 600 yen and held about 100 more. She was jailed 22 days, fined, evicted, and left over ¥1 million in debt. In March the printer was also charged with aiding (460 copies printed for ¥73,000). Comiket's 米沢 warned of the chill. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ポケモン同人誌事件
  - **Other cases:** the Tokimeki Memorial video case (1998); the fake "Doraemon last episode" dōjinshi (warned, 2006). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/同人誌
  - **The 「同人マーク」 (2013).** Proposed by 赤松健, issued by Commons Sphere, first used on 『UQ HOLDER!』 (28 Aug 2013). It signals that the author permits fan works, as a hedge against TPP making copyright crime prosecutable without a complaint. That change took effect **30 Dec 2018**, limited to sales of unaltered copies, so derivative work stayed complaint-only. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/二次創作 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/同人誌
  - **The working position:** fan works exist in a grey zone of rights holders' **黙認** (tacit tolerance). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/同人誌
  - **After copyright expires.** Copyright Act art. 60 still forbids acts that would have violated the author's moral rights. Art. 116 lets **relatives within the second degree** (or a named executor) sue. Art. 120 sets a fine of **up to ¥5 million**. (My inference, not a source's: for a long-dead pseudonymous author with no traceable relatives, nobody is left to bring a civil claim against a parody of a public-domain classic. The art. 120 criminal penalty remains in theory.) https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/著作者人格権

## 7. Free retellings and parodies of classics as a practice

### 7a. Precedents before 1900
- **『偐紫田舎源氏』 (柳亭種彦, illustrated by 国貞; 1829–1842; 38 parts in 172 booklets).**
  - Moves Genji to the Muromachi shogun's court: Mitsuuji, a shogun's son, fakes womanising to foil 山名宗全. The first half is a detective story.
  - **Every part sold over 10,000 copies**, the biggest seller of the Edo period.
  - It was planned to end around 「紅葉賀」 but ran on because of its success.
  - **Banned under the Tenpō reforms** on rumours that it showed the shogun's Ōoku. The author was ordered to stop writing.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/偐紫田舎源氏
- **Hakkenden spin-offs, during and right after its serialisation:**
  - Kabuki versions from **1836** (Osaka 中の芝居; Edo 森田座).
  - **An all-female rewrite**, 『貞操婦女八賢誌』 (為永春水, 1834–48), with "eight wise women".
  - **An erotic parody**, 『恋のやつふぢ 男壮里見八見伝』 (1837, illustrated by 国貞).
  - Digest versions, 『犬の草紙』 (1848–1881) and 『仮名読八犬伝』, which competed with each other.
  - **A sequel about the heroes' children and grandchildren**, 『八犬伝後日譚』 (1853–57).
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/南総里見八犬伝
- **A side-story built into a classic.** Nanboku's 『東海道四谷怪談』 (1825) was written as a ghost-story companion to 『仮名手本忠臣蔵』, and it spawned a separate genre. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/忠臣蔵 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/四谷怪談

## Telling details

- **The rōnin's music was a Meiji girl's.** NHK's 1964 Chūshingura taiga, the one that drew 53.0%, used 芥川也寸志's recycled score from the 1955 film of 『たけくらべ』. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/赤穂浪士_(NHK大河ドラマ)
- **Classic-film heroines were played by men.** In 1918 the onnagata 衣笠貞之助 played Omiya in 『金色夜叉』. In 1919 he played Namiko in 『不如帰』. He later became a major director. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/金色夜叉 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/不如帰_(小説)
- **A 1930s period film told viewers to forget the book.** 山中貞雄's 1935 Tange Sazen opened by saying its story had nothing to do with 林不忘's original. https://kotobank.jp/word/時代劇映画
- **"Stuck for a hit? Make Chūshingura."** The film industry's saying, and every studio's all-star showcase. https://kotobank.jp/word/忠臣蔵映画-1185354
- **Tezuka on his Chūshingura outline:** 「ボクの感じはなにひとつ残っていません」 ("not one thing of my feeling is left"). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/わんわん忠臣蔵
- **Animators fought the studio over an ending.** Toei's staff opposed the 1961 Anju film's ending, in which the hero forgives the slave-master. The film was finished amid a dispute between the head office and the union. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/安寿と厨子王丸_(映画)
- **Pandas before pandas.** Toei's 1958 『白蛇伝』 gave its Chinese heroes a giant panda and a red panda, 14 years before any panda came to Japan. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/白蛇伝_(1958年の映画)
- **Hasegawa Kazuo's chain of classics.** He was Genji (1951), Ōishi in film and on TV (1958, 1964), Iemon (1959). He remade his own 『雪之丞変化』 as his 300th film (1963). Then he directed Takarazuka's first Oscar (1974). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/雪之丞変化 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ベルサイユのばら_(宝塚歌劇)
- **A woman Hikaru Genji.** In 2001 Toei cast 天海祐希, a former Takarazuka male-role star, as Genji opposite 吉永小百合's Murasaki Shikibu. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/源氏物語
- **One casting choice became a rule.** After 夏目雅子's Sanzō (1978), every Japanese TV Saiyūki cast a woman as the monk. The band Godiego wrote a love song for Gokū and Sanzō, which was rejected. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/西遊記_(1978年のテレビドラマ)
- **A lost hit.** NHK's 464-episode puppet Hakkenden averaged 20%. Only 4 episodes survive because the tapes were reused. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新八犬伝
- **The bathhouse legend may be advertising.** "The women's baths emptied for 『君の名は』" is attributed in different books to a Shōchiku executive or to the director's actress wife, and may be invented. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/君の名は
- **Remaking a beloved classic can fail.** NHK's 1991 morning-serial remake of 『君の名は』 had the lowest ratings in the series' history up to then, despite forecasts of 50%. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/君の名は
- **Exam students read Genji in manga.** 『あさきゆめみし』 is reprinted every December and April for students, and stocked in cram schools. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/あさきゆめみし
- **A plain lady drawn plump.** 大和和紀 drew Hanachirusato fat, because to modern readers thin had become beautiful. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/あさきゆめみし
- **"Yaoi" began as a self-deprecating joke.** It mocked editors' rejection slips ("no climax, no punchline, no meaning"). It first appeared in print on 20 Dec 1979. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/やおい
- **Comiket's founder quit because fans would rather rewrite than invent.** He decided he could not ban derivative work, so he left after C12 in 1979. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/コミックマーケット
- **The first fan-comic arrest was for a sexual parody bought by mail order.** A Fukuoka woman spent 22 days in custody for five 600-yen copies (1999). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ポケモン同人誌事件
- **A game sent fans to library volunteering.** Players of 『文豪とアルケミスト』 started typing little-read writers into Aozora Bunko (NHK, Jun 2017). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/文豪とアルケミスト
- **Fan characters on the covers of the real books.** Kadokawa Bunko put 『文豪ストレイドッグス』 characters on the covers of the real authors' novels, 16 titles by 2019. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/文豪ストレイドッグス
- **A 30-year wait.** 小池修一郎 asked 萩尾望都 in 1985 to let him stage 『ポーの一族』 and finally did it in 2018. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ポーの一族
- **The first night of 2.5D.** Tenimyu's opening night (2003) was 70% full. Fans sat "silently assessing" in the front row until the first number got applause; in the interval they went to the lobby and sent their verdicts by mobile phone. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/2.5次元ミュージカル
- **A banned bestseller.** 『偐紫田舎源氏』 sold over 10,000 per part. It was stopped in 1842 on the rumour that it showed the shogun's harem. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/偐紫田舎源氏
- **Genji's "national classic" status is partly institutional.** The 2008 「源氏物語千年紀」 led to 1 November becoming 「古典の日」 by law. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/源氏物語
- **TV's first day included a classic.** NHK's first broadcast (1 Feb 1953) included a kabuki scene from 『義経千本桜』 (「道行初音旅」). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHK総合テレビジョン
- **A Meiji anniversary staging.** 2018: 『坊っちゃん』 staged at 明治村's 呉服座 for the Meiji 150th anniversary, as a prequel to the story. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/坊つちやん
- **A kaidan's face in modern horror.** Sadako's final look in 『リング』 (1998) is cited as a direct homage to Oiwa: cascading hair, a drooping eye. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yotsuya_Kaidan

- **Lists marked "ja.wp list" are probably incomplete,** especially for early films (1909–1925) and TV dramas before 1970. Treat the 金色夜叉 (24), 不如帰 (19) and 坊っちゃん TV (about 14) counts as minimums.
- **Genji film count.** The 2000 NHK 『映像詩 源氏物語』 appears in both the film and the TV lists. It was a hi-vision TV production also shown as a film.
- **The 1961 『伊豆の踊り子』 "連続テレビ小説" (1–3 Jan 1961)** is listed on ja.wp's Izu page as a precursor of the morning serial. NHK's official numbering starts with 『娘と私』 (3 Apr 1961). Not checked against NHK.
- **The 『君の名は』 bathhouse story** is contested even on ja.wp (Shōchiku publicity vs. an actress's remark). Treat it as legend.
- **Captain Tsubasa "half of sales at summer Comiket 1986"** and "nearly 1,000 Tsubasa applications" are reported claims (ja.wp やおい). Comiket's own document says only "huge boom from '85 (female)".
- **Comiket attendance.** Comiket changed its counting method from C85 (Dec 2013), so later figures are not directly comparable with earlier ones. The 750,000 peak at C97 is from ja.wp, not checked against Comiket's release.
- **2.5D audience** of 2.89 million (2023) and 1.5 million (2016): seen only in second-hand reports, not the PIA or association release. The ¥28.3 billion market figure is from ITmedia, which links PIA.
- **「マンガ日本の古典」 (Chūō Kōron):** the publication years (mid-1990s) and the total of 32 volumes are from memory and not confirmed. Confirmed only: Ishinomori (Kojiki, vol 1), Yokoyama (Heike, vols 10–12), Saitō (Taiheiki, vol 19), Hasegawa (Genji, 1996–97).
- **The 1919 Takarazuka 『源氏物語 賢木の巻』** date comes from ja.wp citing the company's 90-year history. It is the earliest Genji staging found for the company.
- **『坊ちゃんのその後』 by 「三四郎」, "191?"**: the author and year are unknown on ja.wp.
- **Kurosawa's 『悪い奴ほどよく眠る』 (1960) as a Hamlet adaptation** is often said in English criticism. It was not confirmed on its ja.wp page, so it is left out of §7e.
- **Nikkatsu Roman Porno total** (often quoted as about 1,100 films, 1971–88): not confirmed. ja.wp gives start (Nov 1971) and end (May 1988) only.
- **The first-anime-feature claim** for 『白蛇伝』 is "first colour feature". The wartime 『桃太郎 海の神兵』 (1945, 74 min, black and white) was longer than any earlier work. Sources call 白蛇伝 the first colour feature, not the first feature.
- **Hakkenden's 1913 film actor:** ja.wp's caption shows the onnagata 立花貞二郎 in a 1913 『八犬伝』, but lists the film as Makino / Matsunosuke. Both may be right (Matsunosuke as lead, an onnagata in a female role).
