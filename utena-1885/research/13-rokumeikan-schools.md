## 1. The Rokumeikan 鹿鳴館

### Site, building, cost
- **Site.** The former Satsuma domain's 装束屋敷 in 内山下町 (today 千代田区内幸町), 8,532 tsubo. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- **Earlier use of the site (en.wiki).** In 1872 it housed the secretariat preparing for the 1873 Vienna Exhibition. From July 1875 to October 1881 it held the "Yamashita Monnai Museum" (山下門内博物館), a zoological and botanical garden that was then moved to Ueno. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokumeikan
- **Previous guest house.** Before the Rokumeikan, state guests stayed at the 延遼館 at Hama-rikyū, a converted building of the old 築地海軍操練所 (in use from 1870). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- **Architect, builder, cost.**
  - Designed by Josiah Conder ジョサイア・コンドル.
  - Built by 土木用達組, a joint venture of 大倉喜八郎 and 堀川利尚.
  - Total cost 180,000 yen. Total floor area 467 tsubo; the 条約改正 article gives 466 tsubo, and Nipponica gives 1,450 m².
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/条約改正 ; Nipponica at https://kotobank.jp/word/鹿鳴館-152691
- **Budget overrun (en.wiki).** The original budget was 100,000 yen and rose to 180,000. For comparison, the Foreign Ministry building cost only 40,000 yen. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokumeikan
- **Site supervisor.** Sekai says construction was supervised by 伊集院兼常, a Satsuma man formerly of the 工部省営繕局. https://kotobank.jp/word/鹿鳴館-152691
- **Start of construction (sources conflict).** ja.wiki says 1880 (明治13). Nipponica says January 1881. Sekai and en.wiki say 1881. All agree it was finished in July 1883. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/鹿鳴館-152691
- **Style (sources disagree).**
  - ja.wiki: Jacobian (James I) base with colonial verandas for the humid climate; brick, two storeys.
  - Nipponica: neo-Baroque. The 条約改正 article: neo-Renaissance. en.wiki: French Renaissance with a Mansard roof and an arched portico.
  - en.wiki: Conder's wish to add Japanese elements was overruled (he claimed "pseudo-Saracenic" touches), and only the garden was Japanese.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/鹿鳴館-152691 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokumeikan
- **Layout.**
  - Ground floor: great dining room, 和所 (salon), library.
  - First floor: ballroom, a room for the imperial family (皇族室), 貴顕室, a dining room for dignitaries, a special smoking room, a bar and a billiard room.
  - On ball nights the ground floor had cloakrooms separated by sex and rank.
  - About 20 guest rooms. The German architects Ende & Böckmann stayed there in 1887.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- **Name.** From the 鹿鳴 ode of the Shijing (小雅), meaning hospitality to guests. It was chosen by 中井弘 (号 櫻洲). https://kotobank.jp/word/鹿鳴館-152691 (NKD, Sekai); https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
  - en.wiki adds that Nakai was the *first husband of Inoue's wife Takeko* 井上武子. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokumeikan
- **Management.** Run by the Foreign Ministry. First director 松平忠礼, a Foreign Ministry 御用掛. Second director 吉田要作, a diplomat who had studied in France. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/鹿鳴館-152691

### Opening, balls, charity bazaar
- **Opening fête, 28 November 1883 (明治16年11月28日).**
  - About 1,200 guests were invited, which was Inoue Kaoru's birthday. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
  - Of these, 400 were foreigners. There was military band music and fireworks, and dancing went on into the night. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/条約改正
  - Inoue's speech: "この鹿鳴館を国内外の紳士がともに交わり、国境を越えた友情を結ぶ場にしたい" (the 条約改正 article's paraphrase-quotation). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/条約改正
  - Inoue and his wife Takeko presided. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokumeikan
- **Couples invited together.** Sekai: more than 1,200 invitations were sent "in the Western form of inviting husband and wife together (夫妻招待), which was not the Japanese custom." https://kotobank.jp/word/鹿鳴館-152691
- **First printed use of the name.** NKD's first citation of the word is 東京日日新聞, 28 Nov 1883: "山下町の鹿鳴館は予て記せし如く愈々今廿八日に開館式を行はれ". https://kotobank.jp/word/鹿鳴館-152691
- **Program and dances.**
  - Waltz, polka, quadrille and mazurka, played by Army or Navy bands. Menus were written in French. Foreign residents were hired as dancing tutors. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokumeikan
  - Sekai lists 園遊会, 舞踏会, 音楽会 and ladies' charity bazaars. https://kotobank.jp/word/鹿鳴館-152691
- **Charity bazaar, 12–14 June 1884.** Japan's first full-scale charity bazaar, the 婦人慈善会, was held there.
  - Ladies including 伊藤梅子, 井上武子 and 大山捨松 personally kept stalls and sold about 3,000 items of art and handicraft.
  - The proceeds founded Japan's first nurse-training school, the 有志共立東京病院看護婦教育所, the following year.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- **Tenchōsetsu ball, 3 November 1886.** The 天長節祝賀大夜会 brought together Prince Arisugawa Taruhito and his consort, Prince Kitashirakawa Yoshihisa and his consort, other imperial princes, and the foreign ministers. ja.wiki calls it the peak of the Rokumeikan era. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- **Who danced.**
  - Few Japanese women could dance, so geisha trained in dancing were mobilised to make up the numbers; this is shown in a Bigot cartoon.
  - Pupils of a girls' higher school were also reportedly mobilised.
  - Foreign diplomats enjoyed the balls but called the Japanese "滑稽" in private letters and diaries.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- **Tokyo Club 東京倶楽部 (1884).** A men-only, introduction-only club founded inside the Rokumeikan in 1884, with Itō Hirobumi as first president.
  - Only English could be spoken; Japanese and other foreign languages were banned.
  - In 1896 it moved to its own Conder-designed Victorian Gothic building next door.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- **Battōtai march (1885).** The march 「抜刀隊」 was first performed at the Rokumeikan in 1885, at a 大日本音楽会 concert.
  - Lyrics by 外山正一, published in 『新体詩抄』, August 1882.
  - Music by the French bandmaster Charles Leroux シャルル・ルルー.
  - It became the first popular song in Japan with a Western melody and later the Army's march-past (陸軍分列行進曲).
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/抜刀隊_(軍歌)

### The fancy-dress ball, 20 April 1887 (明治20年4月20日)
- **Venue.** It was held at Prime Minister Itō's official residence (首相官邸), not at the Rokumeikan. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館 ; Sekai at https://kotobank.jp/word/鹿鳴館-152691
- **Guests and costumes.** 350 people were invited. Costumes fell into three groups: Western historical dress, European or Asian folk dress, and Japanese historical or kabuki figures.
  - 伊藤博文 and his wife: Venetian nobles. Itō's second daughter 生子: an Italian peasant girl.
  - 井上馨: a 三河萬歳 street entertainer. 山縣有朋: a soldier of the Kiheitai.
  - 鍋島直大 and his wife: 18th-century French nobles.
  - 戸田極子 (Countess Toda Kiwako): a peasant girl offering yamabuki flowers, an allusion to the Ōta Dōkan legend. Her husband 戸田氏共: 太田道灌.
  - Albert Mosse: a Turk. 渋沢栄一: a kabuki character.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- **Real host.** It was held in the name of Itō's wife Umeko. ja.wiki says the British minister Francis Plunkett and his wife actually organised it, and Itō merely lent the residence.
  - Nationalists called it "亡国の兆し".
  - 勝海舟 was moved to submit a 21-article memorial on the evils of the time.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- **First of its kind.** Nipponica: this was the first fancy-dress ball in Japan. https://kotobank.jp/word/仮装舞踏会-463110

### Inoue's treaty-revision strategy and its collapse
- **Inoue's reasoning.** Foreign residents had witnessed crucifixions and beheadings only a few years earlier and would not accept Japanese jurisdiction. Inoue therefore set out to prove Japan "civilised" through Westernised manners and a Western-style social hall. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
  - His stated logic: treaty revision could come only by force or by making Westerners *feel* Japan's civilisation; force was impossible, so Westernisation was the only road. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/条約改正
- **Steps in the negotiation.**
  - Preliminary conference: 21 sessions between 25 January and 27 July 1882 at the Foreign Ministry.
  - 5 April 1883: Inoue declared that foreigners who submitted to Japanese law would be granted the interior (内地開放).
  - Formal conference opened 1 May 1886 with 12 countries.
  - 15 June 1886: the Anglo-German proposal. In return for abolishing consular jurisdiction and raising tariffs from 5% to 11%, Japan would open the interior within 2 years and enact Western-style codes within 2 years, communicating their English text to the powers 16 months before enforcement. Japan would appoint foreign judges and procurators, foreigners' cases would go straight to the appeal courts, those courts and the Great Court of Cassation would have a majority of foreign judges, and English would be recognised as an official language.
  - The conference ended at its 26th session on 22 April 1887.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/条約改正
- **Opposition.**
  - Boissonade argued that foreign judges would spread the settlement's injustices across the whole country. He also announced that he would refuse all ministers' banquets because "this is no time for luxury".
  - Agriculture and Commerce Minister 谷干城 submitted his opposition memorial and resignation in July 1887. On 1 August 1887 a "谷君名誉表彰運動会" at Yasukuni shrine paraded flags reading "国家の干城" to Tani's house.
  - The memorials were secretly printed and circulated. Coming after the Normanton incident (1886), they inflamed opinion. 板垣退助 submitted a memorial of more than 18,000 characters.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/条約改正
- **Collapse.**
  - 29 July 1887: the government told the powers the conference was postponed indefinitely.
  - 17 September 1887: Inoue resigned and Itō took over the Foreign Ministry.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/条約改正 ; ja.wiki 鹿鳴館 gives simply "1887年9月".
- **The label "Rokumeikan era".** NKD dates the 鹿鳴館時代 to roughly 1883–1887. Its first citation of the phrase is 生方敏郎, 1926: "いはゆる鹿鳴館時代には女性方面にもハイカラ簇出し". https://kotobank.jp/word/鹿鳴館時代-414211
- **Contemporary insults.**
  - Newspapers at home and abroad called the soirées "茶番劇" and "猿真似", and the policy "媚態外交" or "軟弱外交".
  - 徳富蘇峰 called Inoue's line "貴族的欧化主義".
  - The fad even produced arguments for adopting Christianity as the state religion and for "racial improvement" (人種改良論).
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/条約改正
- **After Inoue: Ōkuma's attempt (1888–89).**
  - Ōkuma Shigenobu's 1888–89 revision kept foreign judges, but only in the Great Court of Cassation.
  - When news of it leaked through *The Times*, opposition included a memorial from Gakushūin president 三浦梧楼 and the "貴族七人組".
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/条約改正

### Satire: Bigot, Loti, Akutagawa
- **Bigot in Japan.** Georges Bigot ジョルジュ・ビゴー (1860–1927) lived in Japan from 1882 to 1899.
  - From October 1882 to October 1884 he was employed as a drawing teacher.
  - He taught French at 中江兆民's 仏学塾 in 1885 and again in 1886–87, and Chōmin's circle supplied the Japanese captions for his satirical magazine.
  - Because of police surveillance he moved to Mukōjima around 1887–1890.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ジョルジュ・ビゴー
- ***Tôbaé* (second series).** Founded 15 February 1887; ceased 15 December 1889.
  - 80 sen a copy; lithographed; twice a month; 70 issues in all.
  - Each issue was seven sheets of cartoons only, with French and Japanese captions written inside the pictures.
  - Its regular subjects were the "現代日本" section (e.g. the superficial Westernisation at the Rokumeikan) and the "時事" section on politics (treaty revision, the Peace Preservation Ordinance).
  - A first, one-issue *Tobaye* appeared in December 1884.
  - It was published in the Yokohama settlement, where Japanese censorship did not reach.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/トバエ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Ferdinand_Bigot
- **The monkey-mirror cartoon.** Bigot's best-known Rokumeikan cartoon shows a stylishly dressed Japanese man and woman admiring themselves in a mirror that reflects a pair of monkeys. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokumeikan ; Yoshimura Kazuma's analysis of the "monkeys going to the Rokumeikan" picture is summarised at https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ジョルジュ・ビゴー
- **Bigot on Japanese women.** He wrote in *Tôbaé*: "日本で一番いいもの、それは女性だ…日本の女性に生まれたのだから、どうぞ日本の女性のままでいてもらいたい". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ジョルジュ・ビゴー
- **Pierre Loti.** He was in Japan in 1885: at Nagasaki from 9 July (the *Madame Chrysanthème* "marriage"), then at a Rokumeikan party that autumn.
  - His account "Un bal à Yeddo" is in *Japoneries d'automne*.
  - He likened the building to a mediocre casino in a French spa town and the ball to a "monkey show".
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ピエール・ロティ ; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Loti ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokumeikan
- **Akutagawa's 『舞踏会』 (*Shinchō*, January 1920).** Based on Loti.
  - Set on the night of 3 November 1886 (the Tenchōsetsu ball). The 17-year-old 明子 goes with her father to the chrysanthemum-decked Rokumeikan in a rose-coloured ball dress and waltzes with a French naval officer.
  - Mishima's play 『鹿鳴館』 (1956) grew from it.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/舞踏会_(小説)

### Later fate
- **1889–1898.**
  - 1889: transferred from the Foreign Ministry to the 十五銀行, the bank capitalised on the peers' pension bonds.
  - 1890: lent to the 華族会館 (Peers' Club). Sekai says November 1890.
  - 20 June 1894: damaged in the Meiji Tokyo earthquake, then sold to the Kazoku Kaikan.
  - 1898: Conder remodelled it, doubling the width of the mansard front. The building was turned 90°, so the old east-facing front now faced south.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/鹿鳴館-152691 ; en.wiki dates Conder's repairs to 1897.
- **1927–1945.**
  - 1927: the Kazoku Kaikan moved out and the site was sold to 日本徴兵保険. Sekai and Nipponica date the Kazoku Kaikan's use to 1933.
  - Demolished between March and the end of June 1940 (ja.wiki). Sekai says March 1941; Daijisen and en.wiki say 1941.
  - The old Satsuma 黒門 gate, designated a National Treasure on 28 December 1934, burned in the 1945 air raids.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/鹿鳴館-152691
- **Surviving fragments.**
  - The staircase and newel posts are at the University of Tokyo's architecture department.
  - An Italian bronze chandelier is at 燈明寺 (平井聖天) in Edogawa.
  - A great mirror is at the 霞会館. Furniture is at Meiji-mura.
  - Ivory-handled cutlery passed to peers' descendants after the Kazoku Kaikan's 1927 auction.
  - Conder's original drawings are lost.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- **Demolition protests.** 谷口吉郎 lamented the demolition in the 東京日日新聞 (8 November 1940). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館 ; en.wiki says this loss eventually led him to found Meiji-mura (1965). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokumeikan
- **1912 interfaith meeting.** The 三教会同 of 25 February 1912 was held at the 華族会館, i.e. in the old Rokumeikan building. Home Minister 原敬 met representatives of Shinto (13), Buddhism (50 or 51) and Christianity (7). https://kotobank.jp/word/三教会同-70519 (Sekai, Britannica, Yamakawa)

## 2. The 1884 Peerage Act (華族令) and the imperial houses

- **The old kazoku.** 華族 was created on 明治2年6月17日, which NAOJ converts to 25 July 1869. It began with 427 houses, and 76 more were added before 1884. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/華族 ; NAOJ calendar DB
- **The Act.** 華族令 was promulgated on 7 July 1884 (明治17年7月7日) as an imperially sanctioned Imperial Household notice (宮内省達, 奉勅達). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/華族令
  - Itō Hirobumi led it as head of the 制度取調局. Yamakawa says it was drafted by 井上毅.
  - Titles were male-hereditary and under the Imperial Household Minister. Peers had to educate their sons suitably and forfeited the title on a sentence of imprisonment or worse.
  - It started with 10 articles, was expanded piecemeal, and was wholly revised in 1907 (28 articles).
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/華族令 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/華族令-1290096 (Yamakawa, Sekai)
- **Five ranks (五爵).**
  - 公爵 kōshaku (prince/duke), 侯爵 kōshaku (marquis), 伯爵 hakushaku (count), 子爵 shishaku (viscount), 男爵 danshaku (baron).
  - The ranks were taken from the 礼記 王制 and Mencius ("公侯伯子男").
  - Life peerages were abolished, so all titles became hereditary.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/華族
- **Ranking criteria (叙爵内規).**
  - 公爵: princes and kings received into subject status, the five regent houses (旧摂家), the Tokugawa main line, and men of "great merit" (偉勲).
  - 侯爵: the 清華家, the Tokugawa 三家, former domains of 150,000 koku or more (*real* tax rice, 現米), and men of merit.
  - 伯爵: houses that habitually reached 大納言, the 三卿, and domains of 50,000 koku or more.
  - 子爵: lesser court nobles and small domains.
  - 男爵: branch houses, new creations, priests, and similar.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/華族
- **Merit princes in 1884.** 三条 (Sanjō), 島津 (Shimazu), 毛利 (Mōri) and 岩倉 (Iwakura). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/華族
- **Numbers created (sources conflict).**
  - ja.wiki 華族: 117 houses on 7 July and 387 on 8 July, 504 houses in all: 公11, 侯24, 伯73, 子322, 男74. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/華族
  - ja.wiki 華族令: 509 titled peers by the end of July. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/華族令
  - en.wiki: 11 princes, 24 marquesses, 76 counts, 324 viscounts, 74 barons = 509. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazoku
  - Sekai (via 公爵 entry): 11 公爵 houses and 24 侯爵 houses. For 1884–87 as a whole, 566 persons became kazoku, of whom 483 were old kazoku and 83 new, more than half of the new ones from Satsuma and Chōshū. https://kotobank.jp/word/公爵-495521
- **Where the peers lived.** In 1884, 433 of the 510 kazoku houses (80%) had their main residence in Tokyo Prefecture. 麹町区 alone had 77, mainly in 番町 and 富士見町. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/華族
- **Privileges.**
  - Under the 1889 貴族院令, every 公爵 and 侯爵 aged 30 or over sat in the House of Peers; counts, viscounts and barons elected members from among themselves.
  - The 華族世襲財産法 protected peers' estates, and the 十五銀行 served as a "peers' bank".
  - Marriages of peers and their children needed the Imperial Household Minister's permission.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/華族令
- **Sons of peers and the army.** In 1880 the government asked that peers' sons become officers, but at first this was unpopular. After the 1884 creations, 9 entered the army (including 松浦厚) and 2 the navy (including 小笠原長生). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/華族
- **"Prince" in English.**
  - 公爵 = prince, 侯爵 = marquess, 伯爵 = earl/count, 子爵 = viscount, 男爵 = baron. ja.wiki notes that a British "prince" is a royal title, so 公爵 Konoe Fumimaro was sometimes mistaken in Anglo-American writing for a member of the imperial family. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/華族 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazoku ("Prince, the equivalent of a European Duke")
  - NKD notes that 公爵 is also the word for European "duke/duc/Herzog", and cites a 1867 first use for the Dutch king's Luxembourg and Limburg titles. https://kotobank.jp/word/公爵-495521
- **Adopted heirs.** en.wiki: unlike European custom, an *adopted* heir could succeed ahead of a senior blood heir. A 1904 amendment allowed minor imperial princes (王) to become peers or heirs to childless peers. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazoku

### Imperial princely houses (宮家)
- **The four hereditary princely houses (四親王家).** These carried into Meiji: 伏見宮 (Fushimi), 桂宮 (Katsura), 有栖川宮 (Arisugawa) and 閑院宮 (Kan'in). https://kotobank.jp/word/宮家-139517 (Nipponica, Sekai)
  - In the Edo period a princely house with no heir could not adopt from another princely house; an emperor's son had to be sent in.
- **Meiji growth.** The imperial family grew from 4 houses to 15. This was because sons who under the 1884 rules should have become kazoku had already been made imperial princes for their services in the Restoration. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/華族
  - New Meiji houses included 山階, 梨本, 久邇, 賀陽, 朝香, 東久邇, 小松, 華頂, 北白川, 東伏見 and 竹田. https://kotobank.jp/word/宮家-139517 (NKD)
  - 久邇宮 was founded in 1875 by 朝彦親王, 4th son of 伏見宮邦家親王. The name comes from 恭仁 in Yamashiro. https://kotobank.jp/word/久邇宮-484594
- **Arisugawa (有栖川宮).**
  - Founded 1625 as 高松宮 by 好仁親王. The house taught calligraphy and waka.
  - 9th head 熾仁親王 was 東征大総督 in the Boshin War, later 左大臣, and died in office as Chief of Staff during the Sino-Japanese War (ja.wiki dates this to 1894; see the conflicts section).
  - 10th head 威仁親王 was a fleet admiral and guided the young crown prince (later Taishō).
  - Extinction: 威仁 died in 1913 (大正2年). His only son 栽仁王 had died young, and the 皇室典範 did not let a non-lineal heir succeed to the name. Taishō gave his 3rd son 宣仁親王 the old name 高松宮 to carry on Arisugawa's rites and property.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/有栖川宮 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/有栖川宮-28011
- **Arisugawa palace.**
  - Conder's two-storey Western mansion at 霞が関 was built from 1881 to July 1884 at about 470,000 yen. That is about 2.6 times the cost of the Rokumeikan.
  - It doubled as a reception house for foreign envoys, and the 『コンドル博士遺作集』 calls it the first imperial-family palace in pure Western style.
  - Bought by the Imperial Household as the 霞関離宮 in 1896–1904, with the family allowed to stay until 1903.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/有栖川宮
- **Other Western-style palaces.** The 伏見宮 house (1880) was the first Western-style imperial residence. The Arisugawa and Kitashirakawa houses followed in 1884, both by Conder. Peers' Western houses multiplied in the 1890s, largely in hope of an imperial visit (行幸). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/華族

### The Saionji family and Saionji Kinmochi 西園寺公望
- **Birth and adoption.** Born 嘉永2年10月23日 (7 December 1849), second son of 徳大寺公純 of the 清華 house Tokudaiji.
  - Adopted at two into the Saionji, also 清華 rank, whose house art was the biwa. He inherited the same year when his adoptive father 西園寺師季 died.
  - He learned swordsmanship from 戸田栄之進, "Kyoto's foremost swordsman", and his nephew.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/西園寺公望
- **Western dress at court.** He was reportedly the first court noble to appear at court with cut hair and Western clothes. When 大原重徳 rebuked him, he replied that within a year court dress would be Western, and he would commit seppuku if it were not. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/西園寺公望
- **France.**
  - Left in 明治3年12月 (NAOJ: 21 January – 18 February 1871) and reached Paris in February 1871, just before the Commune, which he despised.
  - Returned 21 October 1880 after nearly ten years. Friends included Clemenceau and 中江兆民.
  - 18 March 1881: became president of 『東洋自由新聞』. He was forced out by imperial command on 8 April.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/西園寺公望
- **Titles and posts.** 侯爵 on 7 July 1884. 公爵 on 7 September 1920 for heading the delegation at Versailles. Minister of Education from 1894. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/西園寺公望 ; en.wiki dates the elevation of both Tokudaiji and Saionji: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazoku
- **Household.**
  - Never married. His companions included 小林菊子, the former Shinbashi geisha 玉八.
  - Daughter 新子 born 1887; he had her learn English or French from the age of two.
  - Heir 八郎 was the 8th son of 毛利元徳. Adopted in 1899, he married Shinko, so he was a *mukoyoshi* into a marquisate.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/西園寺公望
- **Education views.** As Education Minister Saionji urged "science, English and women's education". He drafted a "second Rescript on Education" without 忠孝 or 愛国, and in 1901 was a founding sponsor of 日本女子大学校. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/西園寺公望

## 3. Western dress, hair and the backlash (elite women)

- **Court dress before 1884.** Women's court dress was the 袿袴, a simplified Heian 小袿.
  - As 宮内卿 from 1884, Itō pressed the Empress through her chamberlain 香川敬三 to adopt Western dress.
  - The Empress said "国ノ為メナレハ何ニテモ可致" (for the country's sake I will do anything). The Emperor said "ナラヌ" (not allowed).
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/昭憲皇太后
- **Permission, June 1886.** At the palace dinner of 19 May 1886 she was still in Japanese dress. On 23 June 1886 the Emperor finally consented. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/昭憲皇太后
- **The edict of 23 June 1886.** A 宮内省内達 of 明治19年6月23日 fixed Western dress for women's court ceremonial:
  - 大礼服 = *manteau de cour*, "for the New Year ceremony".
  - 中礼服 = *robe décolletée* and 小礼服 = *robe mi-décolletée*, both "for soirées and dinners".
  - 通常礼服 = *robe montante*, "made long in the skirt, for daytime palace luncheons".
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大礼服
- **First public appearance, 30 July 1886.** The Empress first wore Western dress in public on a visit to the 華族女学校. Ladies-in-waiting followed.
  - From 1886 she wore only Western clothes, except her nightwear.
  - A saying attributed to her: a kimono, not divided into upper and lower garments, restricts women's movement.
  - On 26 November 1886 she went aboard the new cruisers *Naniwa* and *Takachiho* in Western dress, watched gunnery and a torpedo test, and wrote her first martial waka.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/昭憲皇太后
- **The Empress's 思召書, 17 January 1887.** Her memorandum argued that present Japanese women's dress was a bad remnant of the wars after the Nanbokuchō era. It was unsuited to civilisation and unlike ancient Japanese dress, which, like Western dress, combined an upper garment (衣) with a skirt (裳). Western dress therefore *accorded with* Japanese tradition. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/昭憲皇太后
  - It also urged the use of domestic materials: "勉めて我が國産を用ひんの一言なり。もし、能く國産を用ひ得ば、傍ら製造の改良をも誘ひ、美術の進歩をも導き…". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大礼服
- **Baelz versus Itō.** Erwin Baelz told Itō that Western dress did not suit the Japanese body and that corsets were harmful. Itō replied (as quoted): "ベルツさん、あんたは高等政治の要求するところを、何もご存じないのだ…我が国の婦人連が日本服で姿を見せると『人間扱い』されないで、まるで玩具か飾り人形のように見られるんでね". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/昭憲皇太后
- **Prices.**
  - The Empress's full *manteau de cour*, ordered from Germany, cost 130,000 yen.
  - A countess's court dress, ordered by Countess Uesugi (wife of 上杉茂憲) from Shirokiya's Western-clothing shop at the end of 1901, cost 1,028 yen 81 sen. That equalled 2½ years of the family's clothing budget.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大礼服
- **The 婦人束髪会 (1885).** Founded in 1885 by the doctor 渡辺鼎 (Watanabe Kanae) and the economic journalist 石川暎作. Its manifesto attacked the 日本髪 as inconvenient, unhygienic and uneconomical.
  - Britannica calls it "婦女結髪改良束髪会"; Nipponica calls it "大日本婦人束髪会".
  - Pamphlets such as 『洋式婦人束髪法』 (1885, ed. 村野徳三郎) and colour prints (〈婦人束髪改良図〉) promoted it. Kunichika's print 「婦人束髪会」 dates from 1885.
  - Styles had names like 西洋上げ巻, 西洋下げ巻, イギリス巻, イタリア結び, マーガレット, 夜会巻, and later 庇髪 and 二百三高地.
  - Nipponica: the 束髪 was especially popular among schoolgirls in the Rokumeikan years.
  - Sources: https://kotobank.jp/word/束髪-89943 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/婦人束髪会-1405570 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/束髪
- **Bread and fringes.**
  - Around the 1890s, 木村屋 in Ginza sold a sweet raisin "束髪パン" rolled in the shape of the knot.
  - The 庇髪 (pompadour fringe) is said to have been started by the actress 川上貞奴 in the Meiji 30s (late 1890s–1900s). It was so common among schoolgirls that "庇髪" became slang for "schoolgirl". NKD dates that usage to late Meiji and early Taishō.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/束髪 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/庇髪-609718
- **Backlash in dress.**
  - Girls' schools: the first government girls' school (1872) had its pupils wear *men's* hakama, and this spread to the women's normal school. In 1883 the Ministry banned the outfit as "異様ノ装".
  - From 1886 Western-dress uniforms were adopted at the Women's Higher Normal School and some normal schools, in parallel with boys' uniforms. This Westernisation "ended after a short time", and schools returned to Japanese dress in the early 1890s.
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/袴-113519 (Sekai "学生服" extract); https://kotobank.jp/word/学生服-460439
- **Nipponica's version.** It says Western uniforms were worn at the Tokyo Women's Normal School in the mid-1880s. https://kotobank.jp/word/学生服-460439
- **Backlash in politics.**
  - After 1887 Inoue's fall ended the Rokumeikan era. https://kotobank.jp/word/鹿鳴館時代-414211
  - Yamakawa: the era gave way to the 国粋保存主義 of the same years. https://kotobank.jp/word/鹿鳴館時代-414211
  - 11 February 1889: Education Minister 森有礼, seen as a radical Westerniser, was stabbed by 西野文太郎 on the day the Constitution was promulgated, and died the next day. https://kotobank.jp/word/森有礼-142842
  - On the name change at Aoyama, see §4.

## 4. Girls' schools

### Government schools
- **Tokyo Jogakkō (官立女学校).**
  - The Ministry announced it in 明治4年12月 (NAOJ: 10 January – 8 February 1872). It would hire Western women teachers and admit girls "from kazoku to commoners" on payment of fees, ages 8–15.
  - Opened 明治5年2月 (NAOJ: 9 March – 7 April 1872). In November 1872 it moved to 竹平町 and was renamed 東京女学校.
  - 38 pupils in 1873, with a 6-year course.
  - The 1875 rules aimed to let girls "converse with foreigners".
  - Abolished February 1877.
  - Source: https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317595.htm
- **After 1879.** The 1879 教育令 said "凡学校ニ於テハ男女教場ヲ同クスルヲ得ス", which ended girls' admission to middle schools.
  - A March 1882 Ministry circular told prefectures to drop English, algebra, trigonometry, economics and law from girls' secondary schools and add sewing, household economy, women's etiquette and music, giving "中人以上ノ女子ニ順良適実ノ教育".
  - Source: https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317595.htm
- **Tokyo Women's Normal School (東京女子師範学校).**
  - Proposed by the Ministry adviser David Murray in November 1873. 田中不二麿 petitioned on 4 January 1874, and 木戸孝允 issued the founding notice on 13 March 1874.
  - Entrance exam 20 October 1875, with 71 admitted, aged 14–20, on a 5-year course.
  - 中村正直 became head (摂理) on 18 November.
  - Opening ceremony with the Empress present, 29 November 1875.
  - Kindergarten 16 November 1876; attached higher girls' school 10 July 1882 (Japan's first "高等女学校").
  - Merged into the Tokyo Normal School as its women's division in 1885. Became 女子高等師範学校 in 1890 and 東京女子高等師範学校 in 1908.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東京女子高等師範学校 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/昭憲皇太后
  - School song: the Empress's poem 「みがかずば玉も鏡も何かせむ まなびの道もかくこそありけれ」, bestowed in December 1875 and set to gagaku-style music by 東儀季煕. ja.wiki calls it Japan's first school song. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/お茶の水女子大学

### Atomi and the Peeresses' School
- **Atomi Kakei 跡見花蹊.** Real name 瀧野. Born 天保11年4月9日 (10 May 1840) in Settsu; died 10 January 1926.
  - She took over her father's school in Osaka in 1859, moved to Kyoto in 1866 and to Tokyo in 1870.
  - Opened the 跡見女学校 at 神田中猿楽町 in 1875 (明治8). It stressed classical culture: Chinese learning, calligraphy, tea and 体操.
  - Painted before the Emperor in 1872 and 1893.
  - On 9 March 1887 the 『東京日日新聞』 wrongly printed her obituary. Condolence callers and funeral offerings arrived while she went on teaching.
  - Successor: her pupil and adopted daughter 跡見李子 (1868–1956), daughter of 万里小路通房, became 2nd principal and the first 理事長.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/跡見花蹊
- **Founding of the 華族女学校 (Peeresses' School).** The girls' section of the Gakushūin was abolished and the school was created in 1885 under the Imperial Household Ministry, at 四谷区尾張町.
  - NKD: "皇后の命により".
  - Moved to 永田町 in 1889, when commoners' daughters were also admitted.
  - Became 学習院女学部 in 1906 and 女子学習院 in 1918.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学習院女子中・高等科 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/華族女学校-463143
- **Principals.** All held the post concurrently: 谷干城 (14 September – 22 December 1885), 大鳥圭介 (April 1887 – July 1888), 西村茂樹 (1888–1893), 細川潤次郎 (1893–1906). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学習院女子中・高等科
- **Shimoda Utako 下田歌子.** Born 平尾鉐, 嘉永7/安政元年8月8日 (29 September 1854).
  - Entered court service in October 1872. The Empress gave her the name "歌".
  - Married the swordsman 下田猛雄, who died in May 1884.
  - In 1882 she opened the 桃夭学校 for peers' daughters, with Tsuda Umeko teaching English there from March 1884.
  - In 1885 she became 幹事兼教授 of the Kazoku Jogakkō. Nearly half its first intake came from her school, and she kept a boarding house (桃夭塾) for girls living away from home.
  - 学監 from February 1886.
  - Sent to study European royal girls' education 1893–95; audience with Queen Victoria on 8 May 1895, wearing 袿袴.
  - Founded the 帝国婦人協会 (1898) and the 実践女学校 (1899).
  - Removed as head of the girls' department in November 1907. That year the socialist 『平民新聞』 ran a serial titled "妖婦下田歌子".
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/下田歌子
- **Tsuda Umeko 津田梅子.** Taught at the Peeresses' School from 1885 to 1900. After her return from Bryn Mawr in 1892 her salary was 800 yen, "the highest position available to women". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsuda_Umeko ; staff list at https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学習院女子中・高等科
- **Chiba Sana 千葉さな子 as dormitory mistress (舎監).** She was dormitory mistress of the Gakushūin girls' section / Kazoku Jogakkō from September 1882 to February 1888.
  - Born 天保9年3月6日 (31 March 1838). Second daughter of 千葉定吉 of the Hokushin Ittō-ryū.
  - Licensed in the short sword (小太刀免許皆伝) and a naginata instructor (長刀師範). Called "千葉の鬼小町".
  - In 1856, aged 19, she taught swordsmanship to the Date princesses and beat the future lord 伊達宗徳 in a bout.
  - She was Sakamoto Ryōma's fiancée. After 1888 she ran a moxa clinic in Senju, and died 15 October 1896.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/千葉さな子 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学習院女子中・高等科

### Mission and Christian schools (founding dates and founders)
- **Ferris (フェリス).** Mary Kidder (Reformed Church in America) opened an English school for girls in Hepburn's clinic in Yokohama on 21 September 1870 (NAOJ: 明治3年8月26日). It was co-educational at first and girls-only from September 1871.
  - School and dormitory at 山手178 completed 1 June 1875 and named Ferris Seminary.
  - Eugene Booth became 2nd principal in December 1881.
  - Renamed フェリス和英女学校 in 1889. In the 1890s "nationalist currents made enrolment collapse" and the higher course was abolished.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/フェリス女学院
- **Joshi Gakuin (女子学院).** Formed by merging three schools, completed in 1890:
  - the A六番女学校 (1870, Julia Carrothers, at No. 6 Tsukiji settlement);
  - the B六番女学校 (1874, Misses Youngman and Park);
  - the 櫻井女学校 (1876, 櫻井ちか, in Kōjimachi).
  - At Sakurai, Mary True took over management and 矢嶋楫子 acted as 校主. Yajima became the first 院長 of the merged 女子学院 in 1890.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/女子学院中学校・高等学校
- **Kobe Jogakuin (神戸女学院).** Grew from a private school started in 1873 by Eliza Talcott and Julia Dudley (American Board). It opened as 「女學校」 ("Kobe Home") on 12 October 1875.
  - 1879: 神戸英和女学校. 1885: college course. 1894: Kobe College.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/神戸女学院大学
- **Doshisha girls' school.** In 1876 A. J. Starkweather and 新島八重 opened a girls' class in J. D. Davis's house in the Kyoto Gosho grounds.
  - Renamed 同志社分校女紅場 in April 1877 under principal 新島襄, and 同志社女学校 in September 1877.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/同志社女子大学
- **Rikkyo Jogakkō (立教女学校).** Opened September 1877 by Bishop C. M. Williams with the Blanchets, in the 九鬼邸 at Yushima.
  - Foreigners could not be 校主 outside the settlement, so the nominal owner was a Japanese man (近藤勝敏).
  - 1887: 57 pupils.
  - 1899: new Conder-designed building and dormitory at Tsukiji settlement No. 38.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学校法人立教女学院
- **Kwassui (活水).** Founded December 1879 at Nagasaki by Elizabeth Russell. Her motto: 「知恵と命の泉、主イエス・キリストにむすべよ。女子に最高水準の教育を」. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/活水女子大学
- **Aoyama.** Dora E. Schoonmaker opened a 女子小学校 at 麻布新堀町 on 16 November 1874.
  - Renamed 救世学校 (1875) and 海岸女学校 (1877).
  - In 1888 its upper girls moved to the Aoyama campus as 東京英和女学校, renamed 青山女学院 in 1895.
  - ja.wiki: the "英和" name was "attractive in the 1880s Westernisation era" but drew a nationalist reaction, hence "青山学院" (1894).
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学校法人青山学院
- **Tōyō Eiwa (東洋英和女学校).**
  - Martha Cartmell (Canadian Methodist Woman's Missionary Society) landed in Yokohama on 27 December 1882.
  - The WMS bought 382 tsubo at 麻布東鳥居坂町.
  - Approved by the Tokyo governor on 25 September 1884. It was registered in the name of the 「東洋英和女学校会社」, headed by four Japanese Methodist ministers, with 小林光泰 as 校主 and Cartmell as 校長.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学校法人東洋英和女学院
- **Meiji Jogakkō (明治女学校).**
  - Opened 15 October 1885 at Kudanshita by 木村熊二, a pastor just back from America, and his wife 木村鐙子 (取締役). Backers included 島田三郎, 田口卯吉, 植村正久 and 巌本善治.
  - It was a Protestant school founded "without mission money". Tōko died in 1886.
  - Iwamoto became 教頭 in 1887 and principal in 1892.
  - At its peak in 1892, at 下六番町, it had about 300 pupils.
  - Teachers included 北村透谷, 島崎藤村, 津田梅子, 若松賤子, 幸田延子, 荻野吟子 and 内村鑑三.
  - No religious services were held. Students sat on the governing council, and **the dormitory was self-governing (寄宿舎は自治制)**.
  - Fire destroyed most buildings in February 1896. The school closed in 1908 or 1909 (sources differ; see the conflicts section).
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明治女学校 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/明治女学校-141081
- **Others.** The Catholic Sisters of Saint Maur (arrived 1872) opened the 高等仏和女学校 near Tsukiji church in 1888; it was the forerunner of 雙葉. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本のキリスト教史

### Numbers (government statistics)
- **Girls' higher schools.**
  - 1882: 5 public schools, 286 pupils. 1885: 8 public schools, 504 pupils. Private schools were not yet recognised as 高等女学校. https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317596.htm
  - 1894: 14 schools. 1900: 52 schools, 11,984 pupils. 1905: 100 schools, 31,918 pupils. 1910: 193 schools, 56,239 pupils.
  - 実科高等女学校: 49 in 1911.
  - Source: https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317631.htm
- **Enrolment rate.** Under 5% of girls went on to a 高等女学校 in 1905, 9% in 1920 and nearly 15% in 1925. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/高等女学校
- **Boys' middle schools, for comparison.**
  - 1887: 48 schools, 10,177 pupils, after Mori's one-per-prefecture limit.
  - 1905: 271 schools, 104,968 pupils. 1910: 311 schools, 122,345 pupils.
  - Source: https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317631.htm
- **Before 1880.** Girls-only middle and foreign-language schools numbered 7 in 1876 and 15 in 1878. Girls in such schools, including co-ed ones, numbered 436 in 1874 and 1,497 in 1876, and 2,748 girls were in middle schools alone in 1879. https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317596.htm

### The law
- **Legal steps.**
  - 14 December 1891: the amended Middle School Order made the 高等女学校 a kind of 尋常中学校 for "女子ニ須要ナル高等普通教育".
  - 29 January 1895: 高等女学校規程, with a 6-year course after 4-year primary school.
  - 1899: 高等女学校令 (勅令第31号). Every prefecture had to build one. 4-year course, entry at 12 or older. Private schools allowed. Public schools must charge fees.
  - Source: https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317627.htm ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/高等女学校令
- **Purpose.** Education Minister 樺山資紀, July 1899: the purpose was "賢母良妻タラシムルノ素養ヲ為スニ在リ" for women of the middle class and above. https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317627.htm

### The schoolgirl look
- **Hakama: the 女袴.**
  - Sekai: the women's hakama was created on the basis of the Empress's 1887 思召書 and first adopted as the Kazoku Jogakkō uniform. It is a lantern-style (行灯) hakama without a crotch gusset, usually of cashmere.
  - Colours: 海老茶, purple, 紫紺 and green. Ebicha was the Meiji fashion, green in early Shōwa.
  - Sources: https://kotobank.jp/word/袴-113519 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/女袴-456091
  - ja.wiki credits Shimoda Utako with designing it from court hakama. 福永操 dated the start of schoolgirls' hakama-wearing to the late 1890s on photographic evidence. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/袴 ; also https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utako_Shimoda
- **"Ebicha Shikibu" 海老茶式部.** Schoolgirls avoided noble purple and chose maroon (海老茶) hakama, so they were nicknamed 海老茶式部, a pun on 紫式部. https://kotobank.jp/word/海老茶-192669
- **The usual later look.** Hakama over kimono for school, a 束髪 or 庇髪 hairstyle, ribbons (see the "ome" ritual below), and in Makaze koikaze a bicycle. https://kotobank.jp/word/庇髪-609718 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/魔風恋風
- **The sailor suit** came later. Its spread belongs to the Taishō period and is outside this file's range.

### Dormitory life, same-sex attachments, and the press panic
- ***Makaze koikaze* 『魔風恋風』 by 小杉天外.** Serialised in the 『読売新聞』 from 25 February to 16 September 1903.
  - Heroine 萩原初野 is a pupil of the fictional "帝国女子学院". The story opens with her bicycle accident.
  - She loves 夏本東吾, a Tokyo Imperial University law student who is the *adopted son and son-in-law-designate* of a viscount and engaged to her intimate friend 芳江, the viscount's daughter, who calls Hatsuno "義姉さん".
  - Hatsuno dies of beriberi heart failure (脚気衝心), joining the two lovers' hands.
  - Published in three volumes by 春陽堂 (1903–04). Staged at the 東京座 in March 1905.
  - Its lyrics entered popular song, and newspapers took the unusual step of reprinting it.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/魔風恋風 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/魔風恋風-633376 (NKD, Sekai, kabuki 外題辞典)
- **Self-governing dormitories.** Meiji Jogakkō's dormitory was self-governing, and its girls were "orderly in dress" even fleeing the 1896 night fire. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明治女学校
  - Tōyō Eiwa had a resident dormitory mistress (舎監), 加茂令子, from 1896 to 1936. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学校法人東洋英和女学院
- **Girls' magazines.** 『少女界』 (1902), 『少女世界』 (1906), 『少女の友』 (1908), 『姉妹』 (1909), 『少女画報』 (1912). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/少女小説
- **"Ome" and "S".**
  - Before "S" (from *sister*) spread in the 1910s, schoolgirls called passionate pair-bonds お目 (ome), the term in elite Tokyo girls' schools, or おでや (odeya), used in other regions.
  - The bonds were between girls, or between a girl and a woman teacher.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/エス_(文化) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_S_(culture), citing 赤枝香奈子 (Akaeda Kanako) 2002 and 2011.
- **The October 1910 *Murasaki* account (as quoted on en.wiki).**
  - An upper-form girl who fancies a new first-year consults her "clique" (五人連中, 4–6 classmates). If no one else claims the girl, they act as go-betweens.
  - She hands them a ribbon of "deep purple" or "burning passion red". They give it secretly to the younger girl.
  - If the girl silently accepts it and ties it in her hair, the proposal is accepted, and they call each other "ome-san".
  - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_S_(culture). Cited there to Akaeda 2011, without a page number.
- **Peer rules (en.wiki and ja.wiki).** One partner in the form above and one in the form below. Breaking the rules brought peer sanctions. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/エス_(文化) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_S_(culture)
- **Panic, 1911.**
  - Double suicides of women had been reported in newspapers since the 19th century.
  - A double suicide of two girls'-school graduates in **Niigata, July 1911** made "同性愛" a mainstream press topic, including in the weekly 『婦女新聞』.
  - Educators then split "temporary, spiritual" schoolgirl love (to be "guided") from "true", pathologised same-sex love.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/エス_(文化) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_S_(culture) (cited to Akaeda 2002, 『京都社会学年報』 10:83–100)

## 5. Boys' elite schools and student culture

- **Gakushūin (学習院).**
  - Origins: 学習所 at the Kyoto palace in 1847, given the 勅額 「学習院」 in 1849.
  - Revived in 1868: on 明治元年3月12日 (4 April 1868), with classes to begin on the 19th (11 April), then renamed 大学寮代 on 4月15日 (7 May). Wiki gives both dates.
  - Tokyo school: opened 17 October 1877 at 神田錦町 as a private 華族学校 run by the Kazoku Kaikan, with the Emperor present and naming it 学習院.
  - Became a government school under the Imperial Household Ministry on 17 April 1884.
  - Locations: 1888, the former 工部大学校 site at 麹町区三年町; 1890, 四谷尾張町 (the old Kazoku Jogakkō site); 1908, Mejiro.
  - Under the 1884 華族就学規則, peers' children were *obliged* to attend.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学習院
- **Commoner pupils at Gakushūin.** Admitted by a hard exam that needed a peer's introduction and guarantee. They paid fees (50 sen a month, later 1 yen), which peers were spared. A November 1879 rule set the commoner quota at one third of the current peer enrolment. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学習院
- **First Japanese school uniform (1879).** Nipponica: uniforms were fixed for Gakushūin children from 1879, "said to be the first school uniform in Japan". https://kotobank.jp/word/学習院-43633
  - The current uniform is navy-blue, hook-fastened, with braid (紺のホック留め蛇腹海軍型), modelled on the naval officer's dress. ja.wiki's school article misprints the date as "1872". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学習院中・高等科
  - ja.wiki 学生服 also gives 1879 for Gakushūin. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学生服
- **Earlier stand-collar uniforms.** The 工部省工学寮 and Sapporo Agricultural College had stand-collar uniforms around 1873.
  - The Kōgakuryō's was issued by the government: navy wool, Norfolk-jacket-like, with a Glengarry cap.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学生服
- **Imperial University (1886).**
  - The 帝国大学令 is 勅令第3号 dated 1 March 1886. MEXT says promulgated 2 March. Its aim was "国家ノ須要ニ応スル学術技芸ヲ教授シ及其蘊奥ヲ攻究スル".
  - It had five colleges: law, medicine, engineering, letters and science.
  - Renamed Tokyo Imperial University on 22 June 1897, when Kyoto was founded.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/帝国大学令 ; https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317632.htm
- **The gakuran.**
  - The model for later student uniforms is the Imperial University uniform of 1886. The same year a Ministry notice adopted it at the Higher Normal School, and normal, middle and higher middle schools followed. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学生服
  - Sekai: adopted first at the Higher Normal School and Imperial University in 1886 and spread to normal and middle schools in 1887–88. It was introduced as dress for military drill (兵式体操) and copied the army NCO's black, stand-collar, gold-button fatigue tunic. A navy-blue, hook-fastened naval-officer type also appeared. The look never changed when the army went khaki.
  - Nipponica gives "1885年ごろ" for the Imperial University's gold-button stand-collar.
  - Source for the Sekai and Nipponica points: https://kotobank.jp/word/学生服-460439
  - In practice most Meiji students wore kimono, geta and a student cap. 石橋湛山 recalled that few even at Tokyo Imperial University owned both summer and winter uniforms. At the July graduation, attended by the Emperor, they sweated in black winter wool and joked "忠義とはあついものだ". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学生服
- **Higher middle schools / First Higher School (一高).**
  - First Higher Middle School founded 1886; renamed 第一高等学校 on 11 September 1894.
  - Moved to 本郷向ヶ岡 in 1889.
  - **1890: principal 木下廣次 authorised student self-government and opened the 自治寮 (self-governing dormitory)**. The first 紀念祭 (anniversary festival) was held in 1891. It became fully residential (皆寄宿制) after 1901.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/第一高等学校_(旧制) ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/木下広次
  - Kinoshita, a law professor, was 教頭 from August 1888 and 校長 from May 1889 to June 1893. He later founded Kyoto Imperial University's self-run dormitory (the forerunner of 吉田寮). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/木下広次
- **"籠城主義".** The 一高 spirit of a "castle under siege", walled off from the world. Principal 新渡戸稲造 later warned that it bred arrogance and exclusiveness.
  - Other dormitory customs: ストーム (storms), デカンショ節, decorated rooms for the 紀念祭, and 鉄拳制裁 (fist punishment).
  - Dormitory songs: 「嗚呼玉杯に花うけて」 was first sung at the 12th 紀念祭, 1 March 1902.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/第一高等学校_(旧制)
- **Storms.** Nighttime mass rowdiness, sometimes smashing windows. Varieties included welcome storms, street storms, fire storms and "sermon" storms.
  - In the early 1880s, upper-floor boarders stamped on the floor and the ones below poked the ceiling with brooms.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ストーム_(学生生活)
- **Bankara (蛮殻) and 弊衣破帽.** Worn-out uniform, cape and cap, high geta, a towel at the hip, long hair. It began with the 旧制高校, chiefly 一高, as the deliberate antithesis of ハイカラ. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/バンカラ ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学生服
  - "硬派/軟派": NKD's first citation of the student sense of 硬派 is 森鴎外 『ヰタ・セクスアリス』 (1909): "その頃の生徒仲間には軟派と硬派とがあった". Sekai defines 硬派 as the fist-swinging type and 軟派 as the type given to the opposite sex and fine clothes. https://kotobank.jp/word/硬派-497079
- **Student violence.**
  - 17 May 1890, the **Imbrie incident**: during a baseball game against 明治学院, Ichikō students threw stones at the missionary-teacher William Imbrie, injuring him, after he climbed the fence to cheer.
  - 9 January 1891: the Uchimura affair (§7).
  - 22 May 1903: 藤村操 drowned himself at Kegon Falls, leaving 「巌頭の感」.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/第一高等学校_(旧制)
- **Ichikō fencing club (撃剣部).** Under 山里忠徳 it taught kendo, naginata (長刀) and the 宝蔵院流高田派 spear, preserving that school from extinction. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/第一高等学校_(旧制)
- **Shosei (書生).**
  - After 1872, young men from the provinces lodged with relatives or patrons, doing chores in return for room and board while studying. Patrons gained status and future connections. The type declined from the 1890s as dormitories spread. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/書生
  - Tsubouchi Shōyō's 『当世書生気質』 (1885–86) begins by claiming Tokyo's most numerous people were rickshaw-men and students, "60,000 each" by a count seven years old. Sekai notes that later Meiji "書生" became faintly derogatory (書生芝居, 書生論).
  - Nipponica: the shosei's long haori and 朴歯 high geta spread to the general public.
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/書生-534735
- **Duels.** I found no source for formal student duels in the European sense. Recorded student violence is collective: storms, 鉄拳制裁, the Imbrie stoning. ja.wiki's バンカラ page links "メンズーア" (German Mensur) only as a related-topics comparison. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/バンカラ

## 6. Private school governance

- **校主 and 校長.**
  - 校主 = the owner/operator of a (usually private) school. NKD's first citation is 坪内逍遥 1886. https://kotobank.jp/word/校主-495546
  - Joshi Gakuin's history glosses 校主 as "現在の校長と理事長を兼ねた職". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/女子学院中学校・高等学校
  - Mission schools outside the settlements needed a Japanese 校主 because foreigners could not hold the title there:
    - Rikkyo Jogakkō had a nominal 校主 (近藤勝敏) while the Blanchets/Pitman ran it. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学校法人立教女学院
    - Tōyō Eiwa was owned by a "company" whose 校主 was 小林光泰, with Cartmell as 校長. In 1891 Jessie Munro created an 8-member council of 4 foreigners and 4 Japanese, the 商議員会. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学校法人東洋英和女学院
- **Private School Order (私立学校令).** 勅令第359号, promulgated 3 August 1899, in force from 4 August.
  - Private schools came under the prefectural governor. Founding, closure and change of founder had to be reported.
  - It set disqualifications for principals and teachers and gave the authorities power to close schools.
  - **It was first conceived to curb mission schools.**
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/私立学校令
- **Ministry Instruction No. 12 (文部省訓令第12号), issued the same day.** It banned religious instruction and worship, inside or outside the curriculum, in all government-recognised schools except miscellaneous schools (各種学校).
  - Christian schools had to choose between becoming recognised schools without religion, or keeping religion and losing draft deferment and the right to send graduates on to higher schools.
  - Aoyama chose religion and saw pupils drop out and transfer.
  - Ferris answered by adding an English teachers' course in 1903, which reestablished its standing.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/私立学校令 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/フェリス女学院
- **1911 amendment (明治44年勅令第218号).** Founders of private middle schools and colleges now had to form a **財団法人 (foundation)**, and the authorities could dismiss principals and teachers. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/私立学校令
- **What a 財団法人 was under the Civil Code.**
  - Allowed only for public-interest purposes (民法34条).
  - Created by a founder's endowment plus a 寄付行為 (charter), with ministry permission.
  - Run by 理事 (directors), checked by 監事 (auditors). It has no members' assembly.
  - Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/財団法人-68067 (Sekai, Britannica). The Civil Code took effect in 1898; that date is standard knowledge and was not checked on a page in this file.
- **Waseda as a worked example.**
  - 東京専門学校 opened 21 October 1882. Its first 校長 (1882–86 per the infobox; he resigned in August 1887 per the text) was **大隈英麿, Ōkuma Shigenobu's mukoyoshi**.
    - Born 安政3年9月11日 (9 October 1856), second son of the Nanbu lord of Morioka. Studied at Dartmouth and Princeton (astronomy, mathematics).
    - Married Ōkuma's eldest daughter 熊子 and became heir. He read the "開校の詞" proclaiming 「学の独立」.
    - Later divorced out of the Ōkuma house.
    - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大隈英麿
  - November 1898: Tokyo granted the school status as a **社団法人**. 1907: the 校長/学監 posts were replaced by 総長 (president) and 学長, and it became a 財団法人, run by a 維持員会 (board of trustees). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東京専門学校
  - Ōkuma's *second* mukoyoshi, 大隈信常 (born 松浦常, 明治4年8月16日 = 30 September 1871, son of the Hirado lord 松浦詮), married Ōkuma's adopted daughter 光子 in 1902. She was in fact Ōkuma's child by a maidservant.
    - He became principal of 早稲田中学, succeeding 大隈英麿. A pupil recalled him turning up in casual kimono, sometimes smelling of drink.
    - Later 侯爵 and honorary president (名誉総長) of Waseda (1923).
    - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大隈信常
- **Juntendō and the Women's Art School.** 佐藤志津 (born 嘉永4年12月16日 = 7 January 1852) was the daughter of 佐藤尚中, himself adopted into the Satō house of the Sakura 順天堂.
  - In 1867 she took the Juntendō student 高和東之助 as husband-heir (婿); he became 佐藤進.
  - In 1901 she became 学校主 of the struggling 私立女子美術学校 (founded 1900), and in 1904 its 2nd principal.
  - As a girl she had learned **穴沢流薙刀** from a Mito-domain martial-arts instructor.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/佐藤志津
- **Adoptive succession at Atomi.** Founder Atomi Kakei passed the school to her adopted daughter and pupil 跡見李子, 2nd principal and later first 理事長. Her nephew's son was then adopted as Momoko's heir (養孫 跡見純弘, later chairman). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/跡見花蹊
- **Waseda's later governance crisis.** A 1917 governance crisis (the gate occupation was on the night of 11 September 1917), the "Waseda disturbance", pitted 維持員 (trustees), 学長 and students against one another. It ended with 5 trustees resigning and students occupying the gate. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東京専門学校 (post-1912; included for structure only)

## 7. Christianity in Meiji society

- **End of the ban.** The anti-Christian edict boards of 1868 (五榜の掲示, 慶応4年3月15日 = 7 April 1868) were removed in **1873**, after the Iwakura Mission learned that toleration was a condition for treaty revision. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本のキリスト教史
- **Adherent numbers (only partial figures found; see the uncertainty section).**
  - Catholics, 1884: 30,230 believers, 54 priests, 252 catechists, 84 churches. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本のキリスト教史
  - Protestants, end of 1888: 249 churches, **15,514** members (sic; probably 25,514), 451 missionaries, 142 Japanese ministers, 14 seminaries with 287 students, about 7,000 baptisms a year. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本のキリスト教史
  - Orthodox, same period: about 17,000. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本のキリスト教史
  - Catholics, 1895: 50,302 believers, 4 bishops, 88 MEP priests, 27 Marianists, 20 Japanese priests. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本のキリスト教史
  - Orthodox, 1911: 265 churches, 31,984 believers. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本のキリスト教史
  - **I could not open a source for the Protestant figures for 1882, 1890 and 1900 that were asked for.**
- **The fashionable years.** ja.wiki describes the Westernising early-to-mid Meiji as a time when "上流階級がキリスト教に殺到した" and Fukuzawa argued for Christianity as a state religion.
  - Revival of 1883: at the 3rd 全国基督教信徒大親睦会 (8–12 May 1883), a revival began, and 4,000 attended the 14 May mass meeting.
  - At the 1884 Doshisha revival, 200 students professed faith at the 17 March prayer meeting.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本のキリスト教史
- **Bible translation (明治元訳).**
  - Joint translation agreed at the missionaries' meeting in Hepburn's dispensary, 20–25 September 1872 (NAOJ: 明治5年8月18日–23日). The committee first met in March 1874.
  - Gospels and epistles appeared from 1875. The NT 『新約全書』 was completed in **1880**, celebrated in April 1880 at 新栄橋教会.
  - The OT was finished in **1887**, celebrated on **3 February 1888** at 新栄教会. Hepburn ceremonially laid the NT on top of the OT.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明治元訳聖書
  - A dispute over translating βαπτίζω produced 16 votes for 洗礼 against 30 for バプテスマ (1875), and N. Brown left to make his own version. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明治元訳聖書
- **Christian women's networks.**
  - **『女学雑誌』**, founded July 1885 (edited by 近藤賢三, then 巌本善治), ran to No. 526 in February 1904. Sekai calls it "日本初の本格的女性誌". It backed temperance, the abolition of licensed prostitution and monogamy petitions, and nurtured 若松賤子 (her 『小公子』 translation), 岸田俊子 and the 『文学界』 circle. https://kotobank.jp/word/女学雑誌-80055
  - **東京婦人矯風会**, founded in 1886 by 矢島楫子 on the American WCTU model, campaigned for peace, purity and temperance, punishing adultery for both sexes, and against prostitution. In 1893 it became the national 日本基督教婦人矯風会. Iwamoto was an active backer. https://kotobank.jp/word/東京婦人矯風会-1375823
  - The 1884 Rokumeikan bazaar ladies are listed in §1.
- **Backlash, 1889–1893.**
  - Mori Arinori's assassination (11 February 1889) is covered in §3.
  - The Imperial Rescript on Education was issued on 30 October 1890.
  - **9 January 1891, Uchimura Kanzō 内村鑑三 lèse-majesté affair.** At the First Higher Middle School's ceremony for the Rescript, Uchimura (a contract teacher and dormitory master) bowed but not deeply enough before the imperial signature. Colleagues and students denounced him and he resigned in February. A colleague who defended him was also forced out.
  - 植村正久 called bowing to the Rescript no more legally binding than "a sports-day agreement".
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/内村鑑三不敬事件 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/内村鑑三不敬事件-1148288
  - Afterwards anti-Christian opinion rose, and 井上哲次郎 started the "教育ト宗教ノ衝突" (Education and Religion in Conflict) controversy. https://kotobank.jp/word/内村鑑三不敬事件-1148288 . The 1893 book itself was not opened.
  - Mission girls' schools emptied: Ferris's enrolment "激減" in the 1890s. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/フェリス女学院 ; Aoyama's "英和" name was felt to be a liability. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学校法人青山学院
  - The 1899 Instruction 12 is covered in §6.
- **Image of Christian girls' schools in the press.** Not directly sourced beyond these items:
  - the 平民新聞 attacks on Shimoda Utako, who was not Christian (§4);
  - Christian girls being prominent among "new women" educated at Meiji Jogakkō.
- **1912.** At the 三教会同 the government treated Christianity "on a par" with Shinto and Buddhism in order to enlist it for moral education. https://kotobank.jp/word/三教会同-70519

## 8. Swordsmanship and fencing in Meiji

- **Gekiken kōgyō (撃剣興行), April 1873.** 榊原鍵吉, former 講武所 sword master, organised commercial sword exhibitions on the sumo model, with the support of Tokyo governor 大久保一翁.
  - The first ran 10 days from **11 April 1873** (Sekai) at 浅草左衛門河岸. Sekai calls it the 「官許撃剣会」.
  - Arrangements: a sumo-like raised floor, fighters in showy costume divided East and West, callers and referees, best-of-three bouts, conch shells and drums.
  - **Young women with naginata were included to draw crowds**, and also foreigners.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/撃剣興行 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/撃剣興行-1163203 (Sekai, Nipponica)
  - Copycat troupes multiplied to 37 sites in Tokyo and spread to Nagoya, Kurume and Osaka. Some prefectures banned the shows because they were used to gather crowds for political speeches.
  - When the police hired swordsmen from 1879, the troupes turned into touring groups that practised at local police stations. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/撃剣興行
- **Sakakibara Kenkichi 榊原鍵吉.** 文政13年11月5日 (19 December 1830) – 11 September 1894.
  - Swung a 6-shaku (180 cm), 3-kan (11 kg) training bar 2,000 times; his upper arm measured 55 cm.
  - After the 1876 Sword Abolition Edict he carried a hooked wooden "倭杖" and a wooden "頑固扇", and never cut his topknot.
  - Umpired the imperial bouts at Ueno in 1878.
  - **11 November 1887, at the 伏見宮 residence before the Emperor (天覧兜割り):** he split a Myōchin helmet with the sword 同田貫, a cut 3 sun 5 bu long and 5 bu deep, after two police swordsmen had failed. He wore white, ready to commit seppuku if he failed.
  - Foreign pupils at his Kurumazaka dojo included Thomas McClatchie, Heinrich von Siebold, Baelz, and the Toyama School's French fencing masters "ウイラレー及びキール".
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/榊原鍵吉
- **Police swordsmanship.**
  - The 抜刀隊 of picked sword-skilled policemen was used at Tabaruzaka (1877). 川路利良 wrote 『撃剣再興論』.
  - 1879: a dojo at the police training school, with 上田馬之助, 梶川義正 and 逸見宗助 as the first 撃剣世話掛.
  - By 1883 more than 200 policemen were good enough to head a dojo. The 警視流 forms and grades followed.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/剣道 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/撃剣興行
  - The song "抜刀隊" (1885) is covered in §1. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/抜刀隊_(軍歌)
- **Toyama Army School and French fencing.**
  - The school began as 陸軍兵学寮戸山出張所 on 22 June 1873 and was renamed 陸軍戸山学校 on 4 February 1874. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/陸軍戸山学校
  - 1874: French infantry sergeant "ジュクロー" taught "French fencing" on the side while teaching gymnastics; it did not take root. Officers kept practising Japanese kenjutsu, even after the sabre became regulation in 1875.
  - **1884:** France sent infantry lieutenant **ド・ラビレー (de Villaret)** and artillery sergeant **キエール (Kiehl)**. They taught 正剣術 (foil), 軍刀術 (sabre) and French bayonet. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/片手軍刀術
  - **November 1884:** Army Minister 西郷従道 ordered selection of instructor candidates at Toyama for "fencing". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/フェンシング
  - de Villaret recommended abolishing Japanese sword and spear training, and the army came to look down on them. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/片手軍刀術
  - May 1886: the first course for sword-instructor students opened at Toyama. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/陸軍戸山学校
  - The French left in 1887. The 1889 『陸軍剣術教範』 was a translation of the French manual; Nakamura Tamio says the word 防具 first appears here.
  - In June 1890 the new principal 大久保春野 decided to drop the French style.
  - In 1892 the swordsman 津田教修 (津田一伝流) became head of gymnastics.
  - **1894:** a Japanese one-handed sabre method, 片手軍刀術, was adopted, using a 94 cm, 370 g bamboo sword and kendo armour. Revised in 1907. The two-handed method came only in 1915.
  - Source for this sequence: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/片手軍刀術
- **Dai Nippon Butokukai (大日本武徳会).** Founded 17 April 1895 in Kyoto, with Prince 小松宮彰仁親王 as president. First 武徳祭大演武会 26–28 October 1895. Martial-arts teachers' school from 1 October 1905; renamed 武術専門学校 in 1911. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/剣道
- **Kendo in schools.**
  - Debated from 1883 but rejected as hard to teach and harmful.
  - Petitions led by 星野仙蔵 and 小沢愛次郎 passed the Lower House in 1907.
  - **In 1911 (明治44) kenjutsu ("撃剣") became *permissible* as part of gymnastics in middle schools (中学校で撃剣を正科として採用可能となった).** ja.wiki elsewhere says "実施されるようになった".
  - The word "剣道" first appears in law at this point.
  - Source: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/剣道
- **Naginata and women.**
  - Naginata became a women's martial art in the Edo period, the 「女薙刀」. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/なぎなた
  - Naginata as compulsory girls' school PE came only in 1936. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/なぎなた
- **Sonobe Hideo 園部秀雄 (a female fighter of the Meiji era).** Born たりた, 明治3年3月18日 (18 April 1870), sixth daughter of a Sendai retainer. Her father named her "たりた" because "女はもう足りた".
  - **1886:** she ran away with 佐竹鑑柳斎's touring gekiken troupe, where she was billed as a "美人剣士".
  - 1888: naginata licence, renamed 秀雄. 1891: married the swordsman 吉岡五三郎; widowed, she gave her daughter for adoption.
  - 1896: head (宗家) of the 直心影流薙刀術. Remarried 園部正利 the same year.
  - **May 1899, 4th 武徳祭 (Kyoto):** the only woman competitor. At Prince Komatsu's request she fought the 61-year-old 渡辺昇, a former 練兵館 head, in a mixed-weapons bout, and he abandoned the match.
  - **1901:** reportedly beaten by his 19-year-old pupil 堀田捨次郎. In 1955 she told the Yomiuri this was untrue.
  - Taught at the 姫路師範 and 大阪女子師範 schools, and from 1918 at the 女子学習院.
  - Reputedly lost only twice in "hundreds" of mixed-weapons bouts against swordsmen and spearmen.
  - Her son-in-law 園部繁八 founded the school's present body, the 秀徳会.
  - Sources: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/園部秀雄 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/直心影流薙刀術
- **Chiba Sana** (sword and naginata; dormitory mistress at the Peeresses' School 1882–88) is covered in §4. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/千葉さな子
- **Shimoda Utako** married a swordsman, and en.wiki says she was "an early colleague and training partner of Jigoro Kano". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/下田歌子 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utako_Shimoda
- **Western fencing by Japanese women.** I found no evidence of Japanese women doing Western-style fencing in Meiji. Organised fencing beyond the army began only with the 日本フェンシング倶楽部 (1935). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/フェンシング

## Telling details

- **The Rokumeikan's name and the hostess's past.** The man who named the Rokumeikan, 中井弘, was the first husband of the hostess of its opening night, Inoue's wife Takeko. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokumeikan
- **Couples invited together.** The 1,200 invitations for 28 November 1883 were addressed to husbands and wives jointly, a deliberate break with Japanese custom. https://kotobank.jp/word/鹿鳴館-152691
- **Opening night was Inoue's birthday.** https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- **Cloakrooms by sex and rank.** On ball nights the ground floor had separate cloakrooms (帽衣預所) by sex *and* rank. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- **English-only club.** The Tokyo Club inside the Rokumeikan banned the Japanese language. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- **Arisugawa palace versus Rokumeikan.** The Conder palace of Prince Arisugawa (finished in July 1884) cost about 470,000 yen, against the Rokumeikan's 180,000. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/有栖川宮
- **The Empress's court dress.** Her German-made *manteau de cour* cost 130,000 yen, nearly three quarters of the cost of the Rokumeikan itself. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大礼服
- **A school visit as the first public appearance.** The Empress chose a *girls' school* visit (Kazoku Jogakkō, 30 July 1886) for her first public appearance in Western dress. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/昭憲皇太后
- **Dress as patriotism.** The Empress's 1887 memorandum argued that Western dress (upper garment plus skirt) was a *return* to ancient Japanese dress. Western clothes became patriotic, not foreign. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/昭憲皇太后
- **Itō on dolls.** Itō to Baelz: in kimono, Japanese ladies were treated "like toys or ornamental dolls" rather than as human beings. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/昭憲皇太后
- **The fancy-dress ball.**
  - Inoue Kaoru, the architect of the whole policy, came dressed as a 三河萬歳 street entertainer. Itō's daughter came as an Italian peasant girl. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
  - It was secretly a British legation party held in the Prime Minister's house. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- **Sakakibara's helmet cut.** In November 1887, under the Emperor's eyes at Prince Fushimi's residence, the last great swordsman of the shogunate split a helmet in white robes, ready to cut his belly if he failed. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/榊原鍵吉
- **French fencing masters at an old dojo.** The Toyama School's French fencing masters went to learn at Sakakibara's dojo. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/榊原鍵吉
- **A swordswoman as dormitory mistress.** Chiba Sana, a licensed swordswoman and naginata teacher who once beat the heir of Uwajima in a bout, was dormitory mistress (舎監) of the peers' daughters from 1882 to 1888. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/千葉さな子
- **Sonobe Hideo's name and the 1899 bout.** She was named "たりた" ("enough girls already") by her father and billed as the "美人剣士" of a touring sword show. In 1899 she made a 61-year-old famous swordsman quit a public bout chosen for her by an imperial prince. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/園部秀雄
- **Women in the first sword show.** The first gekiken show of 1873 already put young women with naginata in the ring to draw crowds. https://kotobank.jp/word/撃剣興行-1163203
- **The 1910 ribbon ritual.** An older girl's proposal went through her clique of 4–6 friends. She sent a ribbon of deep purple or "burning red", and acceptance was signalled by silently tying it in one's hair. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_S_(culture)
- **The schoolgirl's nickname.** 海老茶式部: schoolgirls avoided noble purple for their hakama and became "Lady Maroon", a pun on Murasaki ("purple") Shikibu. https://kotobank.jp/word/海老茶-192669
- **The sweet bun.** Ginza's Kimuraya sold a 束髪パン shaped like the new hairstyle. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/束髪
- **Atomi's false obituary.** She was declared dead by a newspaper in March 1887 and taught her classes as usual while condolence callers queued. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/跡見花蹊
- **Meiji Jogakkō's student self-rule.** Its students sat on the governing council and ran their own dormitory, years before Ichikō's famous 自治寮 (1890). Its girls fled the 1896 fire "with their dress in order". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明治女学校
- **Uchimura's role at Ichikō.** He was the dormitory master, third in the school's hierarchy, when he failed to bow deeply enough. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/第一高等学校_(旧制)
- **Ichikō's slogan.** It called its ethos 籠城主義, "the doctrine of the besieged castle". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/第一高等学校_(旧制)
- **Uniform jokes.** Tokyo Imperial University graduates sweating in black winter wool before the Emperor in July joked "忠義とはあついものだ" ("loyalty is a hot/thick/fervent thing"). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/学生服
- **Waseda's mukoyoshi.** Waseda's first principal was the founder's mukoyoshi, a Princeton-trained astronomer and lord's son. He later left the family by divorce. A second mukoyoshi later ran Waseda Middle School. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大隈英麿 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大隈信常
- **Makaze koikaze.** The 1903 bestseller about a schoolgirl begins with a bicycle crash. Its male lead is a viscount's adopted son, engaged to the heroine's best friend, who calls the heroine "elder sister". https://kotobank.jp/word/魔風恋風-633376 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/魔風恋風
- **Christians in the old Rokumeikan.** In 1912 Christian leaders were received as equals of Shinto and Buddhist leaders at the 華族会館, the old Rokumeikan. https://kotobank.jp/word/三教会同-70519
- **Saionji's dare at court.** He wagered his life that court dress would be Western within a year. He later raised a geisha's daughter to speak French from the age of two and married her to a Mōri prince adopted as his heir. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/西園寺公望
- **Hepburn's Bible ceremony.** At the February 1888 Bible celebration, Hepburn held the New Testament in one hand and the Old in the other and "reverently" laid one upon the other. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明治元訳聖書

## Uncertain / conflicting

- **Rokumeikan construction start:** 1880 (ja.wiki) vs January 1881 (Nipponica) vs 1881 (Sekai, en.wiki).
- **Rokumeikan floor area:** 467 tsubo (ja.wiki 鹿鳴館) vs 466 tsubo (ja.wiki 条約改正) vs 1,450 m² (Nipponica).
- **Rokumeikan style:** Jacobian/colonial vs neo-Renaissance vs neo-Baroque vs French Renaissance.
- **Rokumeikan opening and Dance Day:** the opening fête is consistently 28 November 1883, but ja.wiki notes Japan's ballroom federation keeps "Dance Day" on 29 November as the "opening day". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- **Rokumeikan demolition:** March–June 1940 (ja.wiki, which quotes 東京日日新聞 of 9 March 1940) vs 1941 (Daijisen, Sekai, Nipponica, en.wiki). NKD says it was "burned in 1945", which probably confuses the building with its gate. The 1940 date has the most specific evidence.
- **Kazoku Kaikan use of the Rokumeikan building:** 1890–1927 (ja.wiki) vs 1890–1933 (Sekai, Nipponica).
- **Loti and *Japoneries d'automne*:** en.wiki says Loti "arrived in Japan in 1886"; ja.wiki and fr.wiki put him there in 1885, which is correct for the Rokumeikan visit. *Japoneries d'automne* is dated 1889 (fr.wiki, en.wiki) vs 1888 (ja.wiki 鹿鳴館).
- **Bigot's monkey-mirror cartoon:** often given a French title and dated to *Tôbaé* 1887, but **I could not verify the exact title, issue or date.**
- **1884 peerage numbers:** 504 houses (公11/侯24/伯73/子322/男74, ja.wiki 華族) vs 509 peers (11/24/76/324/74, en.wiki; ja.wiki 華族令 gives 509 without a breakdown). Sekai: 566 created in 1884–87.
- **Death of Arisugawa Taruhito:** ja.wiki 有栖川宮 says he died "in 1894… during the Sino-Japanese War" as Chief of Staff. From general knowledge (not checked on a page here) he died in January 1895; verify before use.
- **Date in ja.wiki 有栖川宮:** "明治8年（1873年）" for the Imperial Household's purchase of the Shiba villa is internally inconsistent (Meiji 8 = 1875).
- **Women's court dress edict:** the 大礼服 article gives 23 June 1886, the day the Empress's permission was granted (昭憲皇太后 article). The documentary form is a 宮内省内達; I did not see the text itself.
- **Fujin Sokuhatsukai's name:** 婦人束髪会 (Sekai, Kunichika print title) vs 大日本婦人束髪会 (Nipponica) vs 婦女結髪改良束髪会 (Britannica). The founder's given name appears as 渡辺鼎 / 渡部鼎.
- **Who designed the 女袴:** Sekai says it was created on the basis of the Empress's 1887 memorandum and first adopted as the Kazoku Jogakkō uniform; ja.wiki and en.wiki credit Shimoda Utako's design. These are compatible but not proven. 福永操 dates general schoolgirl hakama to the late 1890s.
- **Meiji Jogakkō closure:** 1908 (Daijisen, Sekai "1908年暮れ") vs 1909 (ja.wiki) vs 1913 (Britannica).
- **Ferris founding:** the school counts 21 September 1870 (Gregorian; NAOJ = 明治3年8月26日). Co-education ended September 1871.
- **Gakushūin uniform date:** 1879 (Nipponica, ja.wiki 学生服) vs "1872" in ja.wiki 学習院中・高等科, which is impossible since the school opened in 1877.
- **Imperial University uniform date:** 1886 (Sekai, ja.wiki) vs "around 1885" (Nipponica).
- **Imperial University Order:** dated 1 March 1886 (勅令第3号) vs "promulgated 2 March" (MEXT).
- **Girls' Higher School Order:** 7 February 1899 (ja.wiki) vs 8 February 1899 (MEXT).
- **Boys' middle-school enrolment for 1900:** MEXT's text reads "七万八、一二一五人", a garbled figure (probably 78,315). Not used above.
- **Private School Order date in Sekai:** the old Sekai cross-reference gives "私立学校令(1889)", a misprint for 1899.
- **Christian statistics:** the 1888 Protestant figure "15,514" in ja.wiki 日本のキリスト教史 may be a typo; other literature I recall, but did not open here, gives about 25,000 for 1888. **No opened source gave Protestant totals for 1882, 1890 or 1900, or Catholic totals for 1890 or 1900.** These need a check against Otis Cary, *A History of Christianity in Japan* (1909), or the *Christian Movement in Japan* yearbooks.
- **1911 kendo rule:** whether kenjutsu became a *permitted* (採用可能) or *regular* (正科) part of middle-school gymnastics in 1911. ja.wiki 剣道 uses both phrasings. The text of the 1911 amendment to the 中学校令施行規則 was not seen.
- **The 1911 Niigata double suicide:** known here only from Wikipedia citing Akaeda Kanako (2002). Names, schools and exact date are not established.
- **The October 1910 *Murasaki* "ome" passage:** known here only as quoted, in English translation, by en.wiki citing Akaeda (2011), without a page number.
- **Students' formal duels:** no source found. Do not assume European-style student duelling existed in Meiji Japan; recorded violence is collective (storms, 鉄拳制裁, the Imbrie stoning).
- **Founding dates of Kazoku Jogakkō:** "1885年9月創設" (Shimoda article) and principal appointment 14 September 1885. The opening ceremony date (often given as 13 November 1885) was **not** confirmed on any page I opened.
- **Gregorian equivalent of the 学制 promulgation (明治5年8月3日):** NAOJ gives 5 September 1872; many books give 4 September. Not used above, but flagged in case it is needed.
