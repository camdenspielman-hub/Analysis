# Tokyo and Yokohama, April 1885 – April 1887: a dated calendar for a newspaper reader

Real history only. Every bullet has a source URL that I opened. Where a source is weak or two sources disagree, the bullet says so, and the item is repeated in the last section.

## How to use this file, and the main source

- **The newspaper chronicle.** Many items come from 『新聞集成明治編年史』 vol. 6, 「歐化政治期」 (林泉社, 1936), which reprints dated newspaper articles from 1885 to 1887. NDL Digital Collections pid 1920367. Frame N is at `https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/N`, and I read it through the NDL Lab OCR text. Below it is cited as **SSMH6 fr. N** with the reprinted paper and its date, e.g. 〔朝野 1885-09-18〕.
  - The date is the **issue date** of the paper. "Yesterday" or "the day before yesterday" in an article is counted back from it.
  - The volume's own index says its month headings follow publication dates, not event dates (fr. 6).
  - OCR caveat: numerals sometimes break. 「一一」 (11) can come out as 「二」, so "〔二·二〇〕" on a November page means 11-20. I corrected these only where the page header settles the month, and say so.
- **Year pages.** ja.wikipedia's year pages, raw wikitext: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/1885年 , https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/1886年 , https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/1887年
- **Calendar background.** The Gregorian calendar was in force (from 1873).
  - National holidays in these years (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/祝祭日):
    - 1 Jan (customary); 新年宴会 5 Jan; 元始祭 3 Jan; 孝明天皇祭 30 Jan; 紀元節 11 Feb
    - 春季皇霊祭 (vernal equinox, about 21 Mar); 神武天皇祭 3 Apr
    - 秋季皇霊祭 (about 23 Sept); 神嘗祭 17 Oct
    - 天長節 3 Nov; 新嘗祭 23 Nov
  - Weekdays I computed for anchoring: 19 Apr 1885 and 3 May 1885 were Sundays (both confirmed by the newspapers); 13 Nov 1885 a Friday; 3 Nov 1886 a Wednesday; 20 Apr 1887 a Wednesday; 26 Apr 1887 a Tuesday.

## 1. Politics and diplomacy

- **18 Apr 1885: Tianjin Convention.** Itō Hirobumi and Li Hongzhang signed the 天津条約 settling the Kapsin coup of Dec 1884. Both armies were to leave Korea within four months, and each side would notify the other before sending troops again.
  - https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/天津条約_(1885年4月)
  - The government announced it in a 官報号外 of 27 May 1885 (same page).
  - Itō sailed from Tianjin on 19 Apr and was back in Japan on 28 Apr. Tokyo Nichinichi of 29 Apr 1885 reported "伊藤特命全權大使一行歸朝". SSMH6 fr. 66 and fr. 135: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/135
- **15 Apr 1885: Port Hamilton.** The British navy occupied Kŏmundo (Port Hamilton) off Korea. Tokyo papers were already speculating on 14 Apr 「英國、朝鮮巨文島を占領か」 (改進新聞, SSMH6 fr. 61). The British withdrew on **27 Feb 1887**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/巨文島事件 ; https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/61
- **Emperor's Sanyō tour, summer 1885.** The emperor toured Yamaguchi, Hiroshima and Okayama, the last of the "six great tours".
  - Tokyo Nichinichi reported his arrival at Okayama on 5 Aug 1885 and his return to Tokyo on 12 Aug (13 Aug issue). SSMH6 fr. 92, 95: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/95 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明治天皇
  - Departure date: see Uncertain.
- **15 Jun 1885: new Tokyo governor.** 渡邊洪基 replaced 芳川顯正 as governor of Tokyo-fu (Jiji, 15 Jun 1885). https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/78
- **Late 1885: the Osaka Incident (大阪事件).** 大井憲太郎, 景山英子 (later 福田英子), 小林樟雄, 磯山清兵衛 and others of the old Jiyūtō left wing planned to go to Korea and overthrow the pro-Qing government. Isoyama defected, the plot leaked, and **139** were arrested in Osaka and Nagasaki.
  - ja.wikipedia's 1885 page gives **23 Nov 1885**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/1885年 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/大阪事件-39191 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大阪事件
  - In the same month 馬場辰猪 and 大石正巳 were arrested in a separate explosives case. Tokyo papers linked it to the Osaka affair. They were **acquitted on 2 Jun 1886** (Chōya 3 Jun 1886) and Baba then left for America. SSMH6 index fr. 7; fr. 172: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/172
  - Ōi and the other ringleaders were sentenced in Sept 1887 (index fr. 8) and amnestied in Feb 1889.
- **22 Dec 1885: the cabinet system.** The Dajōkan was abolished and the 内閣 created, with **Itō Hirobumi** as first prime minister. He also held the post of Imperial Household Minister.
  - Ministers: Foreign, 井上馨; Home, 山縣有朋; Finance, 松方正義; Army, 大山巖; Navy, 西郷従道; Justice, 山田顯義; Education, 森有禮; Agriculture & Commerce, 谷干城; Communications, 榎本武揚.
  - https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/第1次伊藤内閣
  - Tokyo Nichinichi of 23 Dec 1885 printed 三條實美's memorial asking for the Dajōkan to be abolished. SSMH6 fr. 131: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/131
- **1886 legislation.**
  - Hokkaidō-chō created 26 Jan.
  - 帝国大学令 2 Mar.
  - 小学校令, 中学校令 and 師範学校令 10 Apr.
  - Japan joined the Geneva Convention 5 Jun.
  - Standard-time ordinance 13 Jul (Akashi meridian, effective 1 Jan 1888).
  - 登記法 13 Aug.
  - https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/1886年
- **1 May 1886: treaty revision conference opens.** 井上馨 and 青木周蔵 met delegates of 12 powers at the Foreign Ministry.
  - On **15 Jun 1886** (6th session) Britain and Germany tabled the joint draft, which the conference then worked from.
  - It ended at the **26th session on 22 Apr 1887**. Its foreign-judges clause later brought Inoue down (resignation Sept 1887).
  - https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/条約改正
  - Tokyo Nichinichi called the opening "我が國權を世界に伸張すべき條約改正の今日ぞ切つて落さる" (1 May 1886). It printed nothing on the first session until 5 May. SSMH6 fr. 166–167: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/166
- **3 Jun 1886: the Shizuoka Incident (静岡事件).** A plot by former Jiyūtō men to attack the opening of the Hakone detached palace and kill officials was uncovered, and arrests followed. 25 were sentenced in July 1887, to at most 15 years. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/静岡事件 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/1886年
- **13 and 15 Aug 1886: the Nagasaki Incident.** The Beiyang ships 定遠, 鎮遠, 済遠 and 威遠 entered Nagasaki on 1 Aug.
  - On 13 Aug drunken sailors fought in the pleasure quarter.
  - On 15 Aug some 300 sailors came ashore and beat police. Rickshaw-men joined in, and police and sailors fought with swords. Japanese dead included police. One Chinese officer was killed.
  - Settlement: each side punished its own nationals; compensation ¥52,500 from Japan and ¥15,500 from China.
  - https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/長崎事件
- **24 Oct 1886: the Normanton Incident.**
  - The British freighter *Normanton* (240 t) sank off Kii on 24 Oct. All 25 Japanese passengers drowned, while Captain J. W. Drake and the crew took to the boats.
  - 1 Nov: consular maritime hearing in Kobe. 5 Nov: the crew were acquitted.
  - 13–14 Nov: on Inoue's order, the Hyōgo governor charged Drake with murder.
  - **8 Dec 1886**: the British consular court in Yokohama (Judge Hannen) gave Drake **three months' imprisonment**.
  - A 「ノルマントン号沈没の歌」 of 36 verses, later 59, was sung to the tune of 「抜刀隊」. A plan to stage the wreck was stopped.
  - https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ノルマントン号事件
  - Tokyo Nichinichi took eleven days to print details ("悠々半月目に委細判明", 7 Nov 1886). SSMH6 fr. 206: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/206
- **1887 to April.**
  - The Empress's dress memorandum, 17 Jan (see §12).
  - Imperial visit to Kyoto: 25 Jan to 24 Feb, back to Yokohama aboard the *Naniwa*. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/昭憲皇太后
  - **14 Mar 1887**: rescript granting ¥300,000 from the privy purse for coastal defence. On 23 Mar Itō called the prefectural governors to the Rokumeikan to raise patriotic donations. SSMH6 fr. 248, 250: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/248
  - 所得税法 23 Mar. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/1887年

## 2. Disasters and disease

- **Cholera, national totals** (内務省衛生局『衛生局年報』, in MHLW White Paper 2014, fig. 1-1-1 back data): https://www.mhlw.go.jp/wp/hakusyo/kousei/14/backdata/1-1-1-01.html

  | 1885 | 13,824 | 9,329 |
  | 1886 | 155,923 | 108,405 |
  | 1887 | 1,228 | 654 |

- **1885 epidemic.**
  - It broke out in mid-August at Nagasaki, spread to Hizen, Higo, Satsuma and Ōsumi, and was over by early November. Quarantine was lifted in December. Tokyo saw only sporadic cases (Tokyo Nichinichi year review, Jan 1886). SSMH6 fr. 137: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/137
  - One secondary paper says the 1885 outbreak began in Nagasaki's foreign settlement, wintered over, and spread nationwide in 1886. https://www.bgu.ac.jp/library/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/08/fsell2007_311-324.pdf
  - Tokyo reactions in 1885: "長崎港コレラ猖獗" (Tokyo Nichinichi 4 Sept). Honjo and Fukagawa bathhouses debated cutting the bath fee to 1 sen so the poor would keep washing (Chōya 13 Sept). SSMH6 fr. 100, 104: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/104
- **1886 epidemic.**
  - Sporadic cases appeared from January. Wakayama and Tokushima showed signs in April. In May it spread to Hyōgo, Osaka, Kyoto, Okayama and Ehime, worst in Osaka. A quarantine station at Nagaura failed. It entered Yokohama and then Tokyo, "近年に稀なる激烈の病勢" (Tokyo Nichinichi year review, Jan 1887). SSMH6 fr. 222: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/222
  - **Tokyo-fu, from the outbreak to 6 Dec 1886: 12,034 cases, 8,259 deaths.** Japan in the same period: 153,930 cases, 100,492 deaths. Kanagawa: 5,952 cases, 3,743 deaths. Osaka: 20,538 cases, 15,821 deaths.
    - Source: U.S. Minister Hubbard to Bayard, Tokio, 10 Dec 1886, translating the Official Gazette (*FRUS* 1887, doc. 409). https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1887/d409
  - Tokyo episodes in the papers:
    - 6 Jun: the 大日本私立衛生会 (佐野常民, 長与専斎, 高木兼寛) met 300 strong at the Kōseikan, Kobikichō, on cholera (燈新聞 8 Jun).
    - Early June: Tokyo had dredged 480 ri of street ditches in half a month (Tokyo Nichinichi 3 Jun).
    - July: restaurants empty, "不景氣の揚句が官員の節儉、お負にコレラの手傳" (Tokyo Nichinichi 3 Jul); soldiers forbidden to eat or drink outside barracks (Chōya 17 Jul).
    - Kanda Mikawa-chō's day-labourers (立ちん坊), drinking 5-rin cups of ice water, were blamed as a source (Chōya 31 Jul).
    - From 1 Aug the Shitaya ward office handed out free bath tickets in the slums of 萬年町 and nearby (毎日 3 Aug).
    - In Yokohama people fled to dry Nogeyama, where a 50-sen back-tenement rose to over a yen (Chōya 1 Aug).
    - SSMH6 fr. 173, 177, 179, 183: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/183
  - The 毎日 article of 3 Aug 1886 「虎列剌の蔓延」 describes a patient carried off to the isolation hospital as feeling he was leaving this world. By day such patients went under a yellow flag, at night with a cholera-marked lantern. https://www.bgu.ac.jp/library/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/08/fsell2007_311-324.pdf
  - Kyoto suspended all shows from 1 Jun 1886 and reopened in late July with restricted hours (Chōya 30 Jul). SSMH6 fr. 182–183.
- **Beriberi (脚気), Aug–Sept 1886.** It struck Tokyo out of season, on the heights and in the low town alike (Chōya, June 1887, looking back). SSMH6 fr. 274: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/274
- **Floods, 1885.**
  - Kinai: rains from mid-June 1885 breached the Yodo tributaries. A typhoon on 1–2 Jul flooded Osaka again: 72,509 households flooded, 1,749 houses swept away, 81 dead or missing, 304,199 affected. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明治十八年の淀川洪水
  - **Tokyo, 1–2 Jul 1885.**
    - The Sumida rose from 6 pm on 2 Jul. By 10 pm the riverside road stood under more than 4 shaku of water.
    - About 11 pm **千住大橋 broke in the middle**. It had stood since the Edo period and never been carried away before. Four firemen rode the wreck downstream shouting warnings, and it struck and took out **吾妻橋** (Tokyo Nichinichi 3 Jul, 「三大橋流失」).
    - Tokyo Nichinichi of 4 Jul called it Tokyo's worst flood since Kōka 3 (1846), after two months of rain.
    - SSMH6 fr. 83: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/83
    - Fire Department chronology: "7.2 東京に暴風雨、吾妻橋が流失（1887年に鉄橋で完成）". https://www.tfd.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/content/000106217.pdf ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/千住大橋
    - A temporary Azumabashi was open by Sept 1885 (fr. 104). The iron Azumabashi, the Sumida's first iron bridge, opened **9 Dec 1887**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/吾妻橋
- **Fires.**
  - The year review for 1885 says Tokyo's fires were becoming fewer thanks to police, an organised fire brigade, wider streets and building rules. The great fire of 1885 was in **March** at 日本橋區坂本町, about **1,300 houses**, just before this period. SSMH6 fr. 137: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/137
  - **16 Feb 1886, about 5:20 pm**: the Gakushūin at Kanda Nishikichō burned, beginning in a second-floor classroom after classes had left (Tokyo Nichinichi 18 Feb). SSMH6 fr. 148: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/148
  - Outside Tokyo: Toyama, 5,500 houses (31 May 1885; Tokyo Nichinichi 2 Jun); Toyama again, about 1,000 houses (Apr 1887). SSMH6 fr. 75, 256.
  - I found no Tokyo fire of over 1,000 houses between April 1885 and April 1887 (see Uncertain).
- **Hardship.** The Matsukata deflation ran through 1884–85. Papers spoke of famine: "草根木皮をかじり死馬の肉を食ふ" (Chōya 15 May 1885), and in 1886 of straw cakes (藁餅) eaten in place of rice. SSMH6 fr. 71, 147, 163: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/71
- **Ashio, first press notice.** From 6–7 Aug 1885 the sweetfish in the Watarase died. Chōya's headline asked 「渡良瀨川の鮎斃死 足尾の鑛毒の爲か」. SSMH6 fr. 94: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/94

## 3. Weather and natural events

- **Spring–summer 1885.** The season was unsettled. Tokyo Nichinichi reported that the emperor worried the bad weather foretold famine (14 Jun 1885). The great rains of June–July followed (§2). SSMH6 fr. 78.
- **3 Mar 1886.** Heavy snow fell on the night of the Tokyo governor's Rokumeikan ball: "折あしき大雪にて來賓の難義一方ならず" (Tokyo Nichinichi 4 Mar). SSMH6 fr. 151: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/151
- **15 Jan 1887, earthquake felt in Tokyo.**
  - 関谷清景, made the first professor of seismology in 1886, built a wire model of an earth-particle's motion during 72 seconds of the shaking, magnified 50×. It is now in the Science Museum, London. https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co53914/model-showing-the-motion-of-an-earth-particle-during-the-earthquake-in-japan-on-15-january-1887
  - Magnitude about 6.2, epicentre SE Sagami/Musashi: from a JAEA table seen only in a search summary, not opened (see Uncertain).
- **1 Jan 1887.** The 東京気象台 was renamed **中央気象台**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/気象庁
- **January 1887.** Snow stopped trains between Naoetsu and Sekiyama for months (Tokyo Nichinichi 23 Jan 1887). SSMH6 fr. 232.

## 4. Festivals and seasonal customs

- **Sanja matsuri, Asakusa: 17–18 May** every year since 1872. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/三社祭
- **Kanda matsuri.**
  - Held in **September** (the 15th, its Edo date) until 1892, when typhoons and other reasons moved it to May.
  - Kanda Myōjin's own history: 46 floats came out in Meiji 17 (1884) and 40 in **Meiji 20 (1887)**. The festival was then declining because of hard times and overhead wires.
  - https://kandamatsuri.com/about/ ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/神田祭
- **Sannō (Hie) matsuri.** Held in June, in even-numbered years by the Edo alternation with Kanda, so a main festival would fall in **1886**. Floats last appeared in 1889. Yoshitoshi's print 「月百姿 神事残月」 (1886) shows the old procession. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/山王祭_(千代田区)
- **Ryōgoku kawabiraki (fireworks).** The firm record nearest the period is **28 Jun 1887**: opening of the river, 100 shells, 20 set-pieces. From 1873 the date had been put back a month. No 1885 or 1886 date found. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/隅田川花火大会
- **Cherry season 1885.**
  - Sunday **19 Apr** was the first fine Sunday in a long while. The Sumida bank, Ueno, Asakusa and Dangozaka were packed, and Okada's 惣菜料理 on Benten-yama was full (今日新聞 20 Apr).
  - The same day 300-odd workers of the Koishikawa arsenal went to **Asukayama** in matching lined kimono, towels and geta, having hired all 25 Ushigome geisha for the day (Yomiuri 19 Apr).
  - 21 Apr: a 5-sen-subscription cherry party before the Ueno museum (自由燈 20 Apr).
  - **Sunday 3 May**: about 220 officials rode to **Koganei** for the late blossoms in seven coloured teams. Matsukata led the purple, Nogi Maresuke one of the others; Princes Komatsu, Fushimi, Kitashirakawa and Arisugawa joined (Tokyo Nichinichi 5 May).
  - 6 May: Shibusawa Eiichi's cherry party for Matsukata at his Asukayama villa.
  - SSMH6 fr. 62–63, 68–69: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/63 ; https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/68
- **Cherry season 1886.**
  - A Western restaurant opened on the Sumida bank for the first time (Chōya 7 Apr).
  - Yūbin Hōchi (8 Apr) called Tokyo "a city buried in dust", since watering was neglected and the blossoms on the bank were pitiful.
  - 8 Apr, the Buddha's birthday: crowds went for sweet tea at the Ekō-in in the rain (Yomiuri 9 Apr).
  - SSMH6 fr. 160–161: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/160
- **Cherry season 1887.** Rents for the tea-stalls on the Sumida bank rose with the number of bidders (Chōya, mid-March 1887). SSMH6 fr. 245.
- **Summer.**
  - Bon *yabuiri*, 16 Jul 1885: maids on holiday in muslin obi and Awa-chijimi single kimono, singing the old Bon songs (Chōya 17 Jul). SSMH6 fr. 88.
  - The Toranomon Konpira fair on the 10th of each month.
  - 23 Jun 1886: the giant revolving lantern at Shitaya's Satake-ga-hara was lit, with kagura (Chōya 25 Jun). SSMH6 fr. 94, 175.
  - Sept 1885: after the new soy-sauce tax, Kikkoman cost ¥1.40 per 9-shō keg, about 10 sen up on average (Jiji 26 Sept 1885). fr. 108.
- **Autumn and winter.**
  - Dangozaka chrysanthemum figures; a Nichiren show by the Dangozaka gardener 楠田梅次郎 (Nov 1885).
  - Yoshiwara saying: a year with three Tori-no-ichi brings a fire, and 1885 had three (日本たいむす, Nov 1885).
  - 8 Nov: smiths' bellows festival (鞴祭). In 1885 the smiths "in civilised style" did not scatter mandarins.
  - SSMH6 fr. 119, 120: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/120
- **New Year 1886.** New Yoshiwara counted 4,575 visitors on 1 Jan, 6,291 on 2 Jan and 5,300 on 3 Jan, with 1,470 prostitutes (Yomiuri 5 Jan 1886). Year-end 1885 had almost no officials' 忘年会, because the new government preached thrift. SSMH6 fr. 131, 137.

## 5. The Rokumeikan and society

- **Monday dance practice.** From **27 Oct 1884** the Rokumeikan held a 舞踏練習会 every Monday evening, with the German J. L. Janson as dancing master. It was limited to officials, diplomats, foreign employees and their wives and daughters, and lasted until about 1887. 村山茂代「帝国ホテルの舞踏会」(日本女子体育大学紀要 36): https://jwcpe.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/819/files/nichijo_kiyo36-05.pdf
- **29 Jun 1885.** The society (舞樂會) closed its first year with a ceremony.
  - Over 70 members came, including the wife of Duke (公爵) Iwakura, the Nabeshima and Maeda couples, and Countesses Itō, Inoue and Sasaki. Following Mori Arinori's advice, the ladies dressed simply.
  - Secretary-general 鍋島直大 thanked Janson and the music teacher "ローレ令孃". Janson replied that dancing would raise women's place in society (Jiji 1 Jul; Tokyo Nichinichi 30 Jun).
  - SSMH6 fr. 81–82: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/81
- **Outside view.** An English visitor said the Japanese danced well, but "in Europe old women do not dance" (Yūbin Hōchi 3 Jul 1885). fr. 86.
- **The Ladies' Charitable Association (婦人慈善会).**
  - Founded May 1884 for the 有志共立東京病院, with 13 committee ladies: 伊藤梅子, 井上武子, 松方満佐子, 大山捨松, 西郷清子 and others. President: Princess Arisugawa Tadako. https://ir.jikei.ac.jp/record/506/files/118-3-171.pdf
  - **First bazaar**: Rokumeikan, 12–14 Jun 1884. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
  - **Second bazaar.** On 7 Feb 1885 the committee planned a three-day bazaar for mid-April 1885, with proceeds to the hospital and the 楽善会訓盲唖院 and stalls allotted by lot (Tokyo Nichinichi 19 Feb 1885, fr. 50). It actually took place in **November 1885**. The **Empress Dowager and the Empress** visited "yesterday" (Tokyo Nichinichi 20 Nov 1885, so 19 Nov). They toured stalls 1–10 upstairs, bought heavily, and visited the tea stalls below. SSMH6 fr. 122: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/122
  - The two bazaars raised **¥15,000** between them, mostly spent on the nurses' school. https://ir.jikei.ac.jp/record/506/files/118-3-171.pdf
  - **30 Jun 1886**: Itō Umeko and 29 others petitioned the Empress to become patron. Accepted 26 Oct 1886. From **1 Apr 1887** the hospital became the 東京慈恵医院 and the association turned into the 東京慈恵医院会 (same paper). Chōya reported the association dissolved, May 1887 (fr. 257).
- **3 Nov 1885, Tenchōsetsu ball.** Pierre Loti's 「Un bal à Yeddo」 describes the emperor's-birthday ball at the Rokumeikan.
  - The invitation came from "le ministre des affaires étrangères et la comtesse Sodeska" (that is, Inoue), with "On dansera" and a special train back from "Shibachi" (Shinbashi) at 1 a.m.
  - In print Loti dated it "l'an de grâce 1886", but he was in Japan only in 1885 (see Uncertain).
  - https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Japoneries_d%E2%80%99automne/2 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ピエール・ロティ
- **Late February–March 1886: a week of entertainments** (Tokyo Nichinichi 27 Feb 1886):
  - 1 Mar: farewell for Foreign Ministry secretary Inoue (Katsunosuke)
  - 2 Mar: ladies' dance
  - 3 Mar: Tokyo governor's ball
  - 4 Mar: Navy Minister Saigō's banquet
  - 5 Mar: Nagaoka and Ōmura families
  - 6 Mar: Tani Tateki's farewell before going to Europe
  - A gas lamp "equal to 1,000 candles" was put up in the garden.
  - For the governor's ball, ladies could come in Western dress or in crested kimono with white collars, because no women's court dress had yet been fixed (Yūbin Hōchi 28 Feb).
  - SSMH6 fr. 150–151: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/150
- **Mid-March 1886.** 吉田要作 replaced 井上勝之助 as manager of the Rokumeikan (Jiji 15 Mar). fr. 155.
- **18 Mar 1886.** The Itōs gave a ball at the **Enryōkan** (Hama palace), hung with chrysanthemum-crest curtains. Dancing began at 9:30, and the paper praised the Countess's poise (Tokyo Nichinichi 20 Mar). SSMH6 fr. 156: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/156
- **26 Mar 1886.** The dance society began testing members and giving certificates to proficient dancers (Tokyo Nichinichi 26 Mar). fr. 157.
- **27 Apr 1886.** A 女子交際会 was founded at Kōjimachi by 吉田京佐, to widen women's knowledge and acquaintance (Chōya 27 Apr). fr. 165.
- **1886 social fads.** 男女交際会 spread to the provinces (Oct 1886). ## 6. Schools and girls' education

## 9. Exhibitions and novelties

- **Asakusa.**
  - **Asakusa aquarium (浅草水族館), October 1885.** Building began in summer. Tokyo Nichinichi of **14 Oct** says it was finished and would open to the public on **17–18 Oct**. ja.wikipedia's 1885 page gives 14 Oct as the opening, probably the article's date (see Uncertain). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/1885年 ; https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/112
  - **25–27 Dec 1885**: the brick **Nakamise** was completed. Units of 9 shaku × 2 ken rented at **¥3.80 a month**, with 7 months in advance (Chōya 27 Dec). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/浅草寺 ; fr. 132
  - **Opening of Asakusa Park, from 20 May 1886**:
    - Yoshiwara geisha did 手古舞 and 木遣り. The second day was rained off and held on 22 May.
    - Crowds came by horse-tram, 円太郎 omnibus, rickshaw and river steamer. A temporary Kaminarimon was put up.
    - It turned cold and the ice-sellers lost out.
    - At night **an electric light was moved from the Fukuzumi bath-house in Asakusa-tanbo to the Kaminarimon**, putting the brick shops' lanterns to shame (Jiji 24 May).
    - Over three days: Hanayashiki had **27,000** visitors, the 500 Rakan hall 4,894, the aquarium over 5,000 (Tokyo Nichinichi 9 Jun).
    - SSMH6 fr. 167, 170, 173: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/170
- **Electric light.**
  - May 1885: Fujioka Ichisuke tested incandescent lamps at his house on two evenings ("日本家屋にも適合"); the 福住温泉 in Asakusa-tanbo installed a 9-hp generator. fr. 69
  - 5 Jul 1886: Tokyo Electric Light (東京電燈) began business. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東京電燈
  - Nov 1886: the official cost was given as 5 rin 1 mō per candle-hour. fr. 210
  - Feb 1887: lights in the Kobikichō telegraph office (fr. 235); a rush of demand on Tokyo Electric Light (fr. 237).
  - Mar 1887: new plant at Kyōbashi because of demand along Ginza (fr. 246).
  - 20 Apr 1887: an electric lamp at the fancy-dress ball (§5).
  - The first public supply from the 茅場町 station was in **Nov 1887** (29 Nov per ja.wikipedia). ja.wikipedia's 1887 page dates a start of service to **22 Jan 1887** (see Uncertain).
- **Chiarini's Royal Italian Circus.**
  - Came to Japan in 1886 (ja.wikipedia 1886: 1 Sept).
  - **Opened at Soto-Kanda Akiba-ga-hara on 1 Sept 1886** in tents. The menagerie had an elephant, tigers, lions, a great snake and monkeys. Seats for 3–4,000, with shows nightly from 7 pm.
  - Acts: Chiarini in gold lace leading eight riders; the boy acrobats "Hesley and Walter"; a Burmese horse; a monkey riding a horse (Jiji 3 Sept).

  - SSMH6 fr. 189, 191: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/189

  - The emperor watched in **November 1886**, his first circus, and is said to have given $5,000 in gold. A print by Utagawa Kunimasa IV is registered 9 Nov 1886. https://pages.uoregon.edu/jsmacollections/home/artists/utagawa-kunimasa-iv-1848-1920/chiarini-s-astounding-circus.html

  - Students' bicycle club (Mar 1886).
  - **Thomas Stevens**, the American riding round the world, reached Japan (Jiji 4 Dec 1886). fr. 212

- **Kokkuri.** 「狐狗狸」 table-turning spread from Kyoto and Osaka to Tokyo in autumn 1886. Three sticks of bamboo were tied in a tripod with a tray on top; three people held it, and it answered questions (Jiji, Nov 1886). SSMH6 fr. 203: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/203

- **Ice cream.** Fūgetsudō (米津松造, 京橋区南鍋町1丁目) advertised ice cream "as every year", in gift buckets from 50 sen (Tokyo Nichinichi 19 May 1886). In Dec 1885 it had begun selling Western liquor by the glass and à la carte dishes. SSMH6 fr. 129, 169: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/169

## 12. Fashion and hair

- **婦人束髪会 (Women's Bundled-Hair Society).**
  - Founded **July 1885** by army surgeon 渡辺(渡邊)鼎 and 石川暎作 of the 東京経済雑誌. It attacked the Japanese coiffure as inconvenient, unhygienic and costly, and offered four Western styles: 西洋上げ巻, 西洋下げ巻, 英吉利結び and "まがれいと" (Margaret).
  - It had over **2,000 members by Nov 1885** (Yomiuri 7 Nov 1885).
  - Watanabe's appeal ran in the Yomiuri on 18, 19, 21, 25 and 29 Aug 1885.
  - It held a lecture at 梅窓院, Aoyama, on 1 Sept 1885 and a talk on bundled hair at a popular hygiene meeting on 13 Sept.
  - Source: 飯田未希, 政策科学 23-3 (2016): https://ritsumei.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/5098/files/P23-3_13_iida.pdf
- **Its cost argument.** A Tokyo woman paid 10 sen a hairdo (top grade), 5–8 sen (middle) or 3–4 sen (low), about six times a month: **at 5 sen, ¥3.60 a year**, plus ¥1 for oil and paper cords (same source). A press piece of 3 Jul 1885 (東京横濱毎日, on the society) reckoned 20 sen a month × about 9 million women aged 17–50 = ¥21 million a year (SSMH6 fr. 86).
- **The craze, 1885.**
  - 「婦人の束髪—今や燎原の火」 (Chōya 3 Sept).
  - Downtown women hairdressers met to discuss it (Tokyo Nichinichi 4 Sept).
  - Haberdashers prospered (6 Sept).
  - The leading female hairdressers, **more than 4,000**, proposed bundled-hair guilds (Chōya 17 Sept).
  - By 15 Sept there were **22 bundled-hair salons**: Nihonbashi 5, Kyōbashi 7, Asakusa 5, Shiba 1, and 4 in a ward the OCR loses.
  - A count at **Nihonbashi** from 2–3 pm on 15 Sept found **52 Western bundles, 37 Japanese-style bundles, 404 old mage**, about one in five (Chōya 18 Sept).
  - Flower hairpins for bundled hair went on sale (24 Sept).
  - A Senju courtesan went bundled-haired, Western-dressed, shod, and shook hands (Yūbin Hōchi 1 Oct).
  - Westerners praised Japanese hair (12 Oct).
  - A bundled-hair debating meeting was held at 栖岸院, Kōjimachi, on 24 Nov.
  - SSMH6 fr. 100–107, 109, 112, 125: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/106
- **Western dress at court.**
  - At the palace dinner of 19 May 1886 the Empress was still in Japanese dress.
  - The emperor consented on **23 Jun 1886**.
  - First public Western dress: **30 Jul 1886** at the Peeresses' School (§6).
  - The Empress's 思召書 of **17 Jan 1887** argued that Western upper garment and skirt matched ancient Japanese dress.
  - https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/昭憲皇太后
  - Army officers then agreed their wives would wear Western dress at ceremonies and banquets (Tokyo Nichinichi 10 Feb 1887). fr. 237
  - The new court dress was so dear that few had it made (Mar 1887). fr. 241
- **Western clothes boom.**
  - Dec 1885: "洋服全盛の時代". The old-clothes dealers of 日蔭町 (Shiba) did well and tailors were overwhelmed (Chōya 3 and 5 Dec).
  - Oct 1886: rush of winter orders.
  - Nov 1886: a shop advertising women's Western clothes "patronised by the imperial family".
  - Dec 1886: waistcoats of Japanese damask and coats of Kai silk.
  - SSMH6 fr. 126, 200, 207, 211.
- **Smaller items.**
  - A new yukata pattern, 切継形 (Jiji 7 Aug 1885).
  - The 白牡丹 shop in Owari-chō sold handkerchiefs ready-powdered with face powder (Aug 1885).
  - Chōya (25 Aug 1885) complained that noblewomen were copying geisha and kept women in dress and hairstyle.
  - SSMH6 fr. 91, 93, 98.

## Telling details

1. **Mondays at the Rokumeikan.** Social dancing in Japan began on 27 Oct 1884 as weekly lessons. At the year-end ceremony on 29 Jun 1885 the German dancing master Janson told the ladies that dancing would "raise woman's position in society". Mori Arinori had asked them to dress plainly for it. (Murayama; SSMH6 fr. 81–82)
2. **The bridge that floated.** At 11 pm on 2 Jul 1885 the Senju Ōhashi, unbroken since Edo, broke in two. Four firemen rode the wreck down the black Sumida shouting to the men at Azumabashi, and the wreck then took Azumabashi too. (Tokyo Nichinichi 3 Jul 1885)
3. **Counting heads on Nihonbashi.** For one hour on 15 Sept 1885 an amateur statistician counted women's hair on Nihonbashi: 52 Western bundles, 37 Japanese bundles, 404 traditional mage. Tokyo had 22 bundled-hair salons within weeks of the society's launch. (Chōya 18 Sept 1885)
4. **4,000 hairdressers in a panic.** The trade feared ruin, met ward by ward, and decided to join the fashion. (Chōya 17 Sept 1885)
5. **A girl's own marriage advertisement.** On 24 Mar 1887 the Tokyo Nichinichi printed a letter from a Koishikawa girl "aged sixteen years and two months". She had home schooling, some commercial training and a little English, and at her mother's urging sought a suitable husband; suitors were to apply at the paper's front desk. A man's notice in January 1887 had asked for a bride of 15–25 who could ride, or would dare to ride after marriage. (SSMH6 fr. 248; fr. 224)
6. **The Empress's dress debut was a schoolgirls' graduation.** On 30 Jul 1886 the Empress wore Western dress in public for the first time, at the first (elementary-course) graduation of the Peeresses' School. A girl named Nomura Hisako read the thanks. (Tokyo Nichinichi 31 Jul–1 Aug 1886)
7. **"Schoolgirls in decline."** In February 1887 the authorities announced strict rules for boarders at private girls' schools, citing "unseemly conduct" that harmed women's morals. (Chōya 25 Feb 1887)
8. **Suggesting the students dance together.** A high personage proposed in October 1886 to cure stiffness between the sexes by mixed balls of Imperial University men and higher-girls'-school pupils. The paper thought this "skipped a few steps". (Chōya 29 Oct 1886)
9. **Ice-cream "as every year."** Fūgetsudō's May 1886 advertisement implies ice cream was already an annual summer treat in Ginza, sold in gift buckets from 50 sen. (Tokyo Nichinichi 19 May 1886)
10. **Electric light upstages lanterns.** At the Asakusa Park opening (May 1886) an arc light carried to the Kaminarimon made the new brick shops' paper lanterns look like "a reed boat stealing a thousand-koku ship's wind". (Jiji 24 May 1886)
11. **Children for hire.** In early 1887 the poor rented other people's children for 5–8 sen a day to beg with, because a child made passers-by give. (Tokyo Nichinichi 17 Feb 1887)
12. **Kokkuri comes to Tokyo.** Autumn 1886: three bamboo sticks, a tray and three people's hands, "answering everything, and never wrong". It had come up from Kyoto and Osaka. (Jiji, Nov 1886)
13. **A circus in Akiba-ga-hara.** Chiarini's tents seated 3–4,000, with an elephant, tigers, lions and a horse-riding monkey. Two boy acrobats somersaulted from a pole. The run was extended to 30 Sept 1886, and the emperor saw his first circus in November. (Jiji 3 Sept; Tokyo Nichinichi 16 Sept 1886)
14. **The Yoshiwara keeps count.** New Year 1886: 4,575 visitors on the 1st, 6,291 on the 2nd, 5,300 on the 3rd, with 1,470 prostitutes. Yoshiwara superstition held that a year with three Tori-no-ichi would bring fire, and 1885 had three. (Yomiuri 5 Jan 1886; 日本たいむす Nov 1885; SSMH6 fr. 120, 137)
15. **Bath tickets against cholera.** In August 1886 the Shitaya ward office gave free bath tickets to the slums of Mannen-chō. In Yokohama, people fleeing to dry Nogeyama doubled back-street rents from 50 sen to over a yen. (毎日 3 Aug; Chōya 1 Aug 1886)
16. **The headmistress who died in the epidemic.** Kimura Tōko, co-founder of the Meiji Jogakkō, died of cholera on 18 Aug 1886, ten months after the school opened. Uemura Masahisa wrote the hymn for her coffin. (ja.wikipedia 木村鐙子)
17. **A dead woman who kept teaching.** The Tokyo Nichinichi published Atomi Kakei's death notice on 9 Mar 1887. She was alive, and the paper had to print that she was "生存仕候". (SSMH6 fr. 242–243)
18. **"In Europe old women don't dance."** An English visitor in 1885 judged the Rokumeikan's dancing good but its dancers ill-matched. (Yūbin Hōchi 3 Jul 1885)
19. **The snow ball.** On 3 Mar 1886 men's tailcoats could stand the snow, but ladies in carriages were soaked through the cracks. (Tokyo Nichinichi 4 Mar 1886)
20. **"Three little maids from school."** *The Mikado* (London, 14 Mar 1885) cast its three short sopranos as Japanese schoolgirls, coached by a tea-girl from the Knightsbridge Japanese Village. That village burned on 2 May 1885, killing a Japanese woodcarver. (en.wikipedia)
21. **The emperor looks for the strings.** On 26 Apr 1887, during Kikugorō's puppet-style 「操三番叟」, the emperor kept looking up at the ceiling to see whether there really were strings. He said the kabuki was "easier to follow than nō". (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/天覧歌舞伎)
22. **The Osaka plotter and the Rokumeikan gate.** A newspaper profile of Kageyama (Fukuda) Hideko, reprinted on a January 1887 page of SSMH6 (fr. 227; I did not identify the paper in the OCR), says the following.
    - She had studied English at a Tsukiji girls' school alongside "the famous heroine Tomii".
    - She wanted to found a girls' school "free of men's control".
    - She vowed to emulate the heroines of France and Russia.
    - Once, passing the Rokumeikan gate during a ball, she was moved to anger at social inequality.
    - She played the yueqin, wrote poems, was "fair and plump", and unlike ordinary schoolgirls did not give up adornment.

    In July 1887 the geisha of Osaka's 北新地 held a "flower-world lecture meeting" for her fund (Chōya 7 Jul 1887, fr. 271). https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1920367/1/227
23. **Peeresses with commoners.** The first applicants to the Peeresses' School included 52 shizoku and 20 commoners' daughters as well as peers' daughters. (Tokyo Nichinichi 21 Sept 1885)
24. **First Ashio warning.** On 6–7 Aug 1885 the sweetfish in the Watarase died, and Chōya already asked whether the Ashio mine was to blame.

## Uncertain / conflicting

- **Peeresses' School "opening".** The brief says October 1885. The record shows: established September 1885 (ja.wikipedia 下田歌子; Shimoda's own speech "去年の九月"); classes began 5 Oct 1885 (Chōya 7 Oct); formal opening ceremony with the Empress 13 Nov 1885 (ja.wikipedia 1885年 and 昭憲皇太后, citing 打越孝明 2012). I could not open the Meiji Memorial Picture Gallery's own caption.
- **Applicant total, 1885.** The OCR reads 「百七…」 (107?), but 22 + 35 + 52 + 20 = 129.
- **Meiji Jogakkō opening.** 30 Sept 1885 (ja.wikipedia 木村鐙子) or 15 Oct 1885 (ja.wikipedia 明治女学校, citing 手塚竜麿 1970). Tokyo Nichinichi (21 Sept) names 津田うめ among the promoters. Some names in that article are OCR-garbled, e.g. 「木村くら」, which may be 木村とう.
- **Second charity bazaar.** It was planned (7 Feb 1885) for mid-April 1885. The Empresses' visit appears on a November 1885 page dated 「〔二·二〇、東京日日〕」, which I read as 11-20, so the visit was on 19 Nov. Matsuda's Jikei paper says the second bazaar was in 1885 without a date. Treat 19 Nov 1885 as likely, not certain.
- **First bazaar takings.** ¥16,000 (秋山ひさ, via ja.wikipedia 大山捨松); about ¥8,000 (Yūbin Hōchi, cited in a search summary I did not open); ¥15,000 for both bazaars together (Matsuda, Jikei).
- **Jikei nursing school.** Founded Oct 1885 (Jikei 130-year history), Oct 1884 (the site monument, after Mrs Reade began teaching on 17 Oct 1884), or Oct 1886 (ja.wikipedia 大山捨松).
- **Loti's ball.** He prints "1886", but he was in Japan only in 1885 (Nagasaki July–August, Tokyo in autumn), so the ball he describes must be the Tenchōsetsu ball of **3 Nov 1885**. en.wikipedia 'Rokumeikan' wrongly says he arrived in 1886.
- **Fancy-dress ball guest count.** 350 (ja.wikipedia 鹿鳴館) or 400 (ja.wikipedia 明治天皇).
- **Electric light, 1887.** ja.wikipedia 1887年 says Tokyo Electric Light began distributing in the city on **22 Jan 1887**, and en.wikipedia "1887 in Japan" says it "begins selling electric lights" that day. The widely repeated story that this was a Rokumeikan lighting I could **not** confirm from any page I opened. ja.wikipedia 東京電燈 puts the first public supply in **Nov 1887** (29 Nov, 第二電燈局, 200 V DC). Heibonsha (via Kotobank) says a small plant and supply began in **1886**, and elsewhere that first generation was in **1887** (30 hp boiler, 25 kW, 210 V). Tokyo Nichinichi in Feb–Mar 1887 already reports demand and a Kyōbashi plant (SSMH6 fr. 237, 246).
- **Tokyo cholera totals, 1886.** I have 12,034 cases and 8,259 deaths only to 6 Dec 1886, from the U.S. legation's translation of the Gazette. Its table has obvious misprints in other rows (deaths exceeding cases for Aichi, Akita and Nemuro). A school worksheet's "nearly 10,000 dead in Tokyo" is unsourced. The final 衛生局年報 Tokyo figure was not found.
- **Earthquake of 15 Jan 1887.** M6.2, SE Sagami/Musashi, comes from a JAEA table I saw only as a search snippet. No damage report found.
- **Imperial tour 1885.** The departure date is not confirmed. SSMH6 shows an announcement for the "26th" in July, and his return on 12 Aug (13 Aug paper).
- **Festivals.** For Sanja I have only the fixed date (17–18 May) and no year-specific report. For Kanda, the official site names Meiji 17 (1884) and Meiji 20 (1887) as big years. That breaks the Edo odd/even alternation, so whether 1885 and 1886 had main or minor festivals is unknown. For Sannō, "even years" is the Edo rule and the modern practice; I did not confirm the 1886 date. For the Ryōgoku fireworks, no 1885 or 1886 date was found; the cholera summer of 1886 may have affected it, but that is unverified.
- **Tokyo fires.** I found no Tokyo fire of over 1,000 houses between April 1885 and April 1887 in the chronicle or the Fire Department chronology, but I did not search systematically. The 1,300-house Sakamoto-chō fire was in **March 1885**, just before the period.
- **Kyōfūkai.** 6 Dec 1886 (ja.wikipedia) versus "formed in November" (Chōya 13 Dec 1886).
- **Asakusa aquarium.** Opened 14 Oct 1885 (ja.wikipedia 1885年), or opened to the public on 17–18 Oct 1885 (Tokyo Nichinichi 14 Oct 1885, which announces it).
- **共立女子職業学校.** 22 Mar 1886 (ja.wikipedia 1886年). Tokyo Nichinichi and Chōya in Aug 1886 still describe it as being set up.
- **Danjūrō's Kazan play, May 1886.** The paper does not name the play. Linking it to 『夢物語盧生容画』 rests only on an 1886 picture-book of that title (U-Tokyo catalogue, via search) and on general knowledge. Unverified.
- **Ferris and Yokohama Kyōritsu.** No dated 1885–87 events found on the pages I read.
- **『将来之日本』 publisher.** Nipponica says 経済新報社. The book is usually given as published by 経済雑誌社 (田口卯吉), and I did not open a title page.
- **OCR.** All SSMH6 quotations come from machine OCR of a two-column, five-tier vertical page. Names and numerals should be checked against the page image (frame URLs given) before being quoted exactly.
- **Not opened, from earlier research files.** LTES wage data (day labourer 16 sen in 1885; maid ¥0.75 a month) are in research file 14 but were not re-opened here, so they are left out of §13.
