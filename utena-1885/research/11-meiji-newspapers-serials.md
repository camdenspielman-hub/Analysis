## 1. Koshinbun and oshinbun; founding dates; circulations

### Definitions
- **大新聞 ōshinbun** ("big papers"): roughly blanket size; editorial and political argument; kanbun-style prose; written mainly by ex-shogunal samurai; read by the middle and upper classes; subscription **50–70 sen/month** paid in advance. **小新聞 koshinbun** ("small papers"): about half the size (tabloid); no editorials; police news, gossip of the pleasure quarters (花柳), theatre; **every character given furigana** (総ルビ) and colloquial style; written by gesaku authors of commoner origin; read by the "lower" classes; **about 20 sen/month**; many illustrations. The two types were "clearly distinct from about 1874". Sekai (Yamamoto Taketoshi) in Kotobank 「大新聞・小新聞」: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A4%A7%E6%96%B0%E8%81%9E%E5%B0%8F%E6%96%B0%E8%81%9E-1150916 ; table form in ja.wikipedia 「大新聞と小新聞」: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大新聞と小新聞
- Contemporary self-descriptions (both quoted in Sekai, same URL): 東京日日新聞 1878-02-13: large papers with lofty argument are read by "中等以上ノ人民", while "仮名付小新聞" are read on average by "下等社会"; a reader's letter in 読売新聞 1878-10-23 said the small papers' news (雑報) was *more* careful than the big papers', because big-paper writers put editorials first.
- Nipponica (Haruhara Akihiko) says koshinbun readers were "婦女子、芸人", writers were nativist scholars, gesaku authors and kyōka poets, and copies were "normally sold by street hawkers" (街頭で読み売り). Same Kotobank page.
- "Ōshinbun" is a retronym, coined after koshinbun appeared (ja.wikipedia 大新聞と小新聞, citing Nipponica). Tsuchiya: when the Yomiuri began the word did not yet exist; people said 仮名書新聞, 平かな新聞, 傍訓(ふりかな)新聞; "小新聞" appears from about late 1875, pushed by the *Kanayomi Shinbun* to distinguish itself from its sister *Yokohama Mainichi*; it became a term of contempt as the gap narrowed; the formal distinction was emptied by the *Yūbin Hōchi* reform of 1886 but the prejudice lasted to about the Sino-Japanese War (Tsuchiya summary, ch.2).
- Convergence: from about 1885 big papers adopted reporting and plain prose, small papers added plain editorials and political news; the result is called **中新聞** "middle papers". Sekai, URL above. Tsuchiya: the 1886–87 collapse of the two-tier system is tied to the *Yamato Shinbun*'s success with serialized storytelling, after which "big and small papers alike" ran novels.
- Specimen of koshinbun style (Yomiuri, 1874-12-14, quoted in ja.wikipedia): 「君(あなた)はまだ読売新聞(よみうりしんぶん)を御存知(ごぞんじ)ないと見(み)えるそれこそ太政大臣(だじゃうだいじん)より下(しも)は挽車(くるまひき)に至(いた)るまで苦(く)なしに読(よめ)て…」 ("from the Chancellor of the Realm down to rickshaw men, anyone can read it without trouble"). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大新聞と小新聞

### Founding dates and title lineages (all verified)
- **横浜毎日新聞** (first daily): 明治3年12月8日 = **1871-01-28**. **東京日日新聞**: 明治5年2月21日 = **1872-03-29**. **郵便報知新聞**: 明治5年6月10日 = **1872-07-15**. 朝野新聞 1874-09-24; 東京曙新聞 1875-06-02. ja.wikipedia 大新聞と小新聞 (table); NDL guide (post_700041).
- **読売新聞**: first issue **1874-11-02**, published by 日就社 (Koyasu Takashi 子安峻, Motono Morimichi 本野盛亨, Shibata Masakichi 柴田昌吉); **every other day**, two pages (front and back), sheet **26 × 35 cm**; first print run **about 200 copies, fewer than half sold**; became daily **1875-05-01** (Nipponica) and reached **17,000 copies by the end of 1875**. ja.wikipedia 読売新聞 (citing 『読売新聞』 2009-11-02 retrospective): https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/読売新聞 ; Nipponica in Kotobank: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%AA%AD%E5%A3%B2%E6%96%B0%E8%81%9E-146486 ; sheet size from 『読売新聞百年史 別冊』 as cited by NDL reference case: https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?page=ref_view&id=1000336310 (sheet 31 × 23 cm from 1876-01-04; 39 × 28 from 1881-01-04; 47 × 35 from 1884-01-04; 49 × 37 from 1886-01-04; 53 × 37 from 1894-08-16).
- **東京絵入新聞** lineage: 平仮名絵入新聞 **1875-04-17** (every other day) → 東京平仮名絵入新聞 1875-09-02 → 東京絵入新聞 **1876-03-02** → 東西新聞 1889-03-22 → ceased 1890-06. NDL guide. Founded by gesaku writer **Takabatake Ransen 高畠藍泉** (editor) and ukiyo-e artist **Ochiai Yoshiiku 落合芳幾** (owner), Britannica-J and Sekai in Kotobank: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9D%B1%E4%BA%AC%E7%B5%B5%E5%85%A5%E6%96%B0%E8%81%9E-103318 . It was "loved by townspeople and women and children (町人や婦女子) who had known kusazōshi since Edo".
- **仮名読新聞** (Kanayomi): founded **1875-11-01** in Yokohama by **Kanagaki Robun 仮名垣魯文**, illustrations by **Kawanabe Kyōsai 河鍋暁斎**, published by the Yokohama Mainichi company; every other day, daily from 1876-08; moved to Tokyo 1877-03; retitled 『かなよみ』; last issue **1880-10-29, no. 1,400**. Nipponica: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BB%AE%E5%90%8D%E8%AA%AD%E6%96%B0%E8%81%9E-45814 ; ja.wikipedia: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/仮名読新聞 . Yomiuri, Hiragana eiri and Kanayomi were called the "three great koshinbun" (ja.wikipedia 大新聞と小新聞 citing Kokushi Daijiten).
- **朝日新聞** (Osaka): first issue **1879-01-25**, Edobori, Osaka; **1 sen per copy, 4 pages**; sheet **32 × 23 cm, 3 columns**; owner Murayama Ryōhei 村山龍平, money from Kimura Heihachi 木村平八, president his son Kimura Noboru 木村騰, editor Tsuda Tei 津田貞. ja.wikipedia 朝日新聞: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/朝日新聞 ; Asahi's own format table: https://xsearch.asahi.com/help/pdf/meizi_taisyo_01.pdf ; en.wikipedia (staff of 20, first circulation "approximately 3,000"): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Asahi_Shimbun . Renamed 大阪朝日新聞 **1889-01-03** (ja.wikipedia 大阪朝日新聞). From 1882 the Asahi secretly received money from the government and Mitsui Bank (ja.wikipedia 朝日新聞, citing the company history; en.wikipedia agrees).
- **絵入自由新聞**: founded **1882-09-01** (as 東京絵入自由新聞) by Liberal Party members Takeuchi Meitarō 竹内明太郎 and Kuno Hatsutarō 久野初太郎, as the popular edition of the party's 自由新聞 (founded 1882-06-25); tabloid, illustrated, furigana, but with Liberal-party political novels and columns. Peak circulation "just under 10,000", "more than three times" the *Jiyū Shinbun*. Writers: Sakurada Momoe, Sakazaki Shiran, Miyazaki Muryū, Kuroiwa Ruikō (editor-in-chief from 1884), Watanabe Katei; illustrators Takeuchi Denchō and Tsukioka Yoshitoshi. ja.wikipedia 絵入自由新聞: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/絵入自由新聞
- **今日新聞** (Konnichi): founded **1884-09-25** by Konishi Yoshitaka 小西義敬 as an **evening paper**, editor-in-chief **Kanagaki Robun**; strong on theatre, yose and the pleasure quarters; said to have started at **10,000 copies** (ja.wikipedia 都新聞, an article tagged "sources needed"). Renamed **みやこ新聞 1888-11-16**, switched to a morning paper, then **都新聞 1889-02-01** (NDL guide). Bought in 1888 by Watanabe Osamu 渡辺治 (ex-Jiji Shinpō) and Hara Ryōzaburō 原亮三郎, founder of the textbook house Kinkōdō. Under editor Kuroiwa Ruikō (from the E-iri Jiyū) circulation "jumped at once to **30,000**". ja.wikipedia 都新聞: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/都新聞 ; Kotobank 今日新聞: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BB%8A%E6%97%A5%E6%96%B0%E8%81%9E-709795
- **自由燈 (Jiyū no tomoshibi)** → **燈新聞 (Tomoshibi shinbun)** → **めさまし新聞** → **東京朝日新聞**. Verified lineage with exact dates (NDL guide, microfilm holdings): 自由燈 **1884-05-11** → 燈新聞 **1886-01-14** → めさまし新聞 **1887-04-01** → 東京朝日新聞 **1888-07-10**. Founded by **Hoshi Tōru 星亨** as the Liberal Party's small paper; first spelled 自由之灯. Sekai (Yamamoto): it "for a time boasted the largest circulation in Tokyo"; renamed under repression; as *Mezamashi* it reported **Prime Minister Itō Hirobumi's scandal at the Rokumeikan** and was suspended for **8 weeks** right after the renaming, and in 1887 was suspended for **133 days** in all (treaty revision, three-great-petitions movement). When Hoshi was expelled from Tokyo under the **Peace Preservation Ordinance (保安条例)** at the end of 1887 the paper fell into crisis and was bought by the Osaka Asahi in July 1888. Kotobank めさまし新聞: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E3%82%81%E3%81%95%E3%81%BE%E3%81%97%E6%96%B0%E8%81%9E-395973 ; Kotobank 自由灯: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%87%AA%E7%94%B1%E7%81%AF-2096888 ; ja.wikipedia 東京朝日新聞: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東京朝日新聞 . The first *Tokyo Asahi* issue was numbered **No. 1,076**, continuing the three predecessors' numbering (en.wikipedia The Asahi Shimbun). Tokyo Asahi first sheet **50 × 36.5 cm, 4 pages, 6 columns** (Asahi format PDF above).
- **改進新聞** (Kaishin): Progressive (Kaishintō) small paper, sister to the *Yūbin Hōchi*. 有喜世新聞 founded **1878-01**; after a government suspension restarted as 開花新聞 **1883-03-10**; renamed 改進新聞 **1884-08-01**; reverted to 開花新聞 **1894-08-01**; ended c.1895. In **1888 it hired the French artist Georges Bigot** for political cartoons; Sudō Nansui 須藤南翠 serialized political novels in it. NDL guide; Britannica-J and Daijisen Plus in Kotobank: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%94%B9%E9%80%B2%E6%96%B0%E8%81%9E-42541
- **やまと新聞** (Yamato): predecessor 警察新報 **1884-10-04** (a sober police-news paper with furigana but no serials or pictures, secretly run by the *Tokyo Nichinichi*'s company); relaunched as やまと新聞 **1886-10-07** under Jōno Saigiku (Denpei) 條野採菊; serials by rakugo master **San'yūtei Enchō 三遊亭圓朝** taken down in shorthand (a first for a newspaper), illustrations by **Yoshitoshi** and **Mizuno Toshikata**; occasional large colour nishiki-e supplements by Yoshitoshi (「近世人物誌」). Renamed 日出国新聞 1900-11-01, back to やまと新聞 1904-12-10. ja.wikipedia やまと新聞: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/やまと新聞 ; NDL guide. Tsuchiya: with Enchō's dictations and Yoshitoshi's pictures it "rose rapidly to the top of the Tokyo papers" (Tsuchiya summary, ch.11).
- Other lineages, for completeness: 絵入朝野新聞 1883-01-22 → 江戸新聞 1889 → 東京中新聞 1890 → 中央新聞 1891-08-16; いろは新聞 1879-12-04 (Robun) → 1884 勉強新聞 …; 萬朝報 1892-11-01 (Kuroiwa); 二六新報 1893-10-26; 國民新聞 1890-02-01 (Tokutomi Sohō). NDL guide; ja.wikipedia 萬朝報, 國民新聞.

### Prices
- c.1875–85: big papers 50–70 sen/month prepaid, small papers ~20 sen (Sekai). Asahi 1879: 1 sen a copy (ja.wikipedia 朝日新聞).
- 1905: larger journals **2 sen a copy, 35–50 sen a month**; smaller **1.5 sen a copy, 20–30 sen a month** (Chamberlain).
- Monthly subscriptions 1901–1912 (Ariyama Table 1, p.30): Yomiuri 35 sen throughout; Hōchi 35; Tokyo Nichinichi 40; Tokyo Asahi **33** (1901–03) then **37** (1904–); Jiji 50; Kokumin 35 (38 from 1909); Miyako 35; Yorozu **24** (1901–05) then **27** (1906–12) — the cheapest.

## 2. Serial fiction (tsuzukimono to the modern newspaper novel)

### Origins: the tsuzukimono 続き物
- The newspaper novel grew out of the koshinbun's **雑報 (zappō, "talk" column)**: newsworthy cases were rewritten as story-like narratives and, if popular, run as **続き物 "continued pieces"**, then padded with invention until they became novels. Nipponica (Takahashi Haruo) and Sekai (Asai Kiyoshi), 新聞小説: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%96%B0%E8%81%9E%E5%B0%8F%E8%AA%AC-82525 . Mypedia: tsuzukimono were "社会記事（雑報）を脚色して挿絵を入れて連載化した" (dramatized news with illustrations).
- **First newspaper "novel"**: Maeda Kōsetsu 前田香雪, 「岩田八十八の話」, in 平仮名絵入新聞, **three days**; it was a court record retold in gesaku manner. Dates conflict: Kokugo says from **1875-10-28**; Britannica-J says **1875-11-28** (see Uncertain). Kōsetsu's 「金之助の話」 ran **16 installments** from **1878-08** (Britannica-J, 東京絵入新聞 entry).
- Tokyo Eiri's writers Takabatake Ransen, Somezaki Nobufusa 染崎延房 (Tamenaga Shunsui II), and Maeda Kōsetsu "turned news items one after another into gesaku-style continued pieces"; Sekai says this "opened a new face for the koshinbun's news". Kotobank 東京絵入新聞.
- **Torioi Omatsu 鳥追お松**: Kubota Hikosaku 久保田彦作, 「鳥追お松の伝」, serialized in 『かなよみ』 from **December 1877** (Nipponica; NDL "Portraits" page: https://www.ndl.go.jp/portrait/pickup/030 ); a shamisen-playing street singer (tori-oi) who ruins a conscript, a draper's clerk and a gambler, and dies mad. Readers believed she was real (said to be a 小屋者's daughter from Yanaka). Staged **April 1878** (Kawatake Shinshichi III, 春木座). Sekai (Koike Shōtarō) notes the plot's resemblance to Mérimée's *Carmen*. Kotobank: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%B3%A5%E8%BF%BD%E3%81%8A%E6%9D%BE-1095616 . Kubota (1846–98) was a kabuki playwright trained under Onoe Kikugorō V and Mokuami; editor of 『歌舞伎新報』 1879: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B9%85%E4%BF%9D%E7%94%B0%E5%BD%A6%E4%BD%9C-1072138
- **Takahashi Oden 高橋お伝**: beheaded **1879-01-31** (sentence that day; 朝野新聞 1879-02-01 printed the court's judgment). The next day the koshinbun (Kanayomi, Ukiyo Shinbun) all ran Oden stories in gesaku style. Serials: 「毒婦高橋お伝」 in 東京曙新聞 **1879-02-01 to 02-07**; 「毒婦お伝のはなし」 in 東京絵入新聞 **1879-02-01 to 02-21**. Kabuki: Mokuami, 『綴合於伝仮名書』, 新富座 1879, Kikugorō V. Robun later (1881) arranged her grave at Yanaka paid by actors and Enchō. ja.wikipedia 高橋お伝: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/高橋お伝
- 毒婦物 "poison-woman stories" as a genre: Nipponica (Asai Kiyoshi) defines them as Meiji-10s works in kusazōshi/gōkan form, especially 『鳥追阿松海上新話』 (1878) and 『高橋阿伝夜刃譚』 (1879), which joined real scandals to the kusazōshi world. https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%AF%92%E5%A9%A6%E7%89%A9-1189682

### How the daily installment worked
- Typical **modern** newspaper novel (Sekai, Asai Kiyoshi): "usually illustrated, **1,200 to 1,600 characters per installment**, running **100 to 300 installments**"; each installment needs its own high point while keeping the whole together; topicality and season mattered: the karuta scene in *Konjiki Yasha* was timed to be read during the New Year (松の内); *Gubijinsō* hinged on the Ueno industrial exhibition that had just closed. Kotobank 新聞小説. (This describes the mature form; I found no open source giving character counts for 1870s tsuzukimono.)
- Installments were numbered **回 (kai)**. A worked example from 1888: the political serial 「戀の革命」 in 絵入自由新聞 ran from **1888-02-05 to 1888-04-01** in **44 installments** (numbered to 45 because no. 29 was misprinted as 30), appearing on most days (e.g., Feb 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 14, 16, 17…). Every installment carried an illustration by **芳宗 (Yoshimune)** except no. 9, for which the author forgot to order one. Author: the pseudonym **冥々道史**. 汲田美砂, 「自由民権運動と政治小説『戀の革命』」, 高知市立自由民権記念館紀要, pp.2–4 (read from page images; installment-date table on p.3): https://www.city.kochi.kochi.jp/uploaded/life/193359_842736_misc.pdf . Same source: illustrated continued pieces were "expected to encourage readers to keep subscribing" (citing Yamada Shunji).
- Typical newspaper novel lengths of the 1900s: *Makaze koikaze* 190 installments (Yomiuri 1903-02-25 to 09-16); *Gubijinsō* 127 installments (Asahi 1907-06-23 to 10-29); *Baien* 127 installments (Tokyo Asahi 1909-01-01 to 05-16). ja.wikipedia 魔風恋風, 虞美人草, 煤煙 (小説).

### Illustrators and how pictures were printed
- **Ochiai Yoshiiku 落合芳幾** (1833–1904), Kuniyoshi pupil, joined the founding of the *Tokyo Nichinichi* (1872) and the *Hiragana eiri* (1875) and "brought illustrations into the newspaper"; as owner of the Tokyo Eiri he **carved the blocks himself** (みずから彫刻して). Kotobank 落合芳幾: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%90%BD%E5%90%88%E8%8A%B3%E5%B9%BE-1063557 ; 東京絵入新聞 entry. Sekai: the illustrated papers inherited kusazōshi, "pictures primary, text secondary".
- **Tsukioka Yoshitoshi 月岡芳年** (1839–92): newspaper illustrations for the *Yūbin Hōchi*, *E-iri Jiyū*, *Yamato*. Nipponica: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9C%88%E5%B2%A1%E8%8A%B3%E5%B9%B4-19147 . In **1884** he got into trouble with the E-iri Jiyū for supplying pictures to the rival *Jiyū no tomoshibi* (ja.wikipedia 絵入自由新聞).
- **Yōshū Chikanobu 楊洲周延**: illustrated the book version of *Torioi Omatsu*, 『鳥追阿松海上新話』 (Kubota Hikosaku, revised by Kanagaki Robun, 陽州斎/楊洲周延 画; 錦栄堂, 1878, 2nd part 1878-01) and Kubota's other gōkan. University of Tokyo Library record: https://da.dl.itc.u-tokyo.ac.jp/portal/en/assets/cb3342bd-64c3-55f9-9733-dad9aa3f2597
- **Mizuno Toshikata 水野年方** (1866–1908), Yoshitoshi's pupil: drew for the *Yamato Shinbun* from about 1887; also illustrated Ichiyō's 「うもれ木」 and Fukuchi Ōchi. https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B0%B4%E9%87%8E%E5%B9%B4%E6%96%B9-16844
- **Kaburaki Kiyokata 鏑木清方** (b. 1878-08-31 per Nipponica), son of Jōno Saigiku (founder of the *Tokyo Nichinichi* and *Yamato*); entered Toshikata's studio at 13; drew newspaper novel illustrations for the *Jinmin Shinbun* and *Yomiuri* from about age 17, and frontispieces for Izumi Kyōka, Shimazaki Tōson. https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%8F%91%E6%9C%A8%E6%B8%85%E6%96%B9-15829
- **Utagawa Kunimatsu 歌川国松**: Yokohama prints in early Meiji, later "many newspaper illustrations"; illustrated Okamoto Kisen's 『恨瀬戸恋神奈川』 and Shōyō's 『京わらんべ』. https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%AD%8C%E5%B7%9D%E5%9B%BD%E6%9D%BE-1639714
- **Printing**: in Meiji "newspaper and magazine illustrations were reproductive **woodblocks** cut by block-cutters (彫師) from the artist's drawing"; the switch from woodblock to photo-engraving came around the **1923 earthquake**. Sekai, quoted in Kotobank 挿絵 (under 伊上凡骨 and イラストレーション): https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%8C%BF%E7%B5%B5-68981 . (Woodblocks were type-high and locked into the letterpress forme with the type; I did not find an open page stating this explicitly for Meiji papers.)
- Nishiki-e shinbun (the colour-print forerunner): first issued c. **July–August 1874** by the publisher Gusokuya, retelling *Tokyo Nichinichi* stories with Yoshiiku's pictures; Yoshitoshi did a *Yūbin Hōchi* series; about **40 titles** appeared in Tokyo, Osaka and Kyoto, mostly 1874–1881, killed off by the cheaper illustrated koshinbun. ja.wikipedia 錦絵新聞: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/錦絵新聞 . Price **1.6 to 2 sen** a sheet (Lavenberg essay).

### Pay, pen names, reporter-writers
- **Kanagaki Robun 仮名垣魯文** (文政12年1月6日 = **1829-02-09** – 1894-11-08; real name 野崎文蔵; pen names 鈍亭, 猫々道人, and "Kanagaki Robun" from 1873). 1873: clerk at the Kanagawa prefectural office for **20 yen a month**; 1874 news reporter for the Yokohama Mainichi; founded the Kanayomi 1875; salary as its editor **40 yen a month**; as editor of the Konnichi Shinbun **70 yen a month**. His gossip columns 「猫々奇聞」 about geisha (he coined 「猫」 for geisha) were popular; first theatre reviews in a newspaper. ja.wikipedia 仮名垣魯文 (citing Itō Sei): https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/仮名垣魯文 ; Nipponica (Maeda Ai): https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BB%AE%E5%90%8D%E5%9E%A3%E9%AD%AF%E6%96%87-45707
- Newspaper novelists were divided into two schools: Robun's **仮名垣派** and Takabatake Ransen's **柳亭派** (ja.wikipedia 仮名垣魯文; 高畠藍泉).
- **Takabatake Ransen 高畠藍泉** (天保9年5月12日 = **1838-07-03** – 1885-11-18), "the first literary-world novelist of Meiji", took the name 柳亭種彦 (counted as the third) in 1882; one of his pen names was **一葉舎**. Reporter at the *Tokyo Nichinichi* 1872; editor of *Hiragana eiri* 1875, left in December after quarrelling with the owner and joined the Yomiuri, where he discovered the 22-year-old proofreader **Aeba Kōson 饗庭篁村**; founded the **東京毎夕新聞 1877-11-12**, "Japan's first evening paper", which failed within months. ja.wikipedia 高畠藍泉: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/高畠藍泉
- **Kubota Hikosaku** (above); **Okamoto Kisen 岡本起泉** wrote the rival Oden book 『其名も高橋毒婦の小伝 東京奇聞』 (1879) (ja.wikipedia 高橋お伝).
- **Ozaki Kōyō 尾崎紅葉** joined the Yomiuri in **December 1889** while still at the Imperial University (ja.wikipedia 尾崎紅葉; the Bunkyō city page also says 1889); first big Yomiuri serial 「伽羅枕」 from 1890-07-05; 「三人妻」 1892, 「多情多恨」 1896, **「金色夜叉」 1897-01-01 to 1902-05-11, intermittent, unfinished** (he died 1903-10-30). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/尾崎紅葉 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/金色夜叉 . The Yomiuri, under owner Motono Morimichi from 1890 with Takata Sanae and Tsubouchi Shōyō, became "the literary newspaper", with Kōda Rohan and Kōyō as staff (Nipponica, 読売新聞). Pay: a personal website (not scholarly) says Kōyō's Yomiuri salary was **30 yen a month on entry**, **100 yen** by 1901, with no separate manuscript fees; Kawakami Bizan c.1908 got about **4 yen per newspaper installment**; Nagai Kafū at the *Yamato* (1901) **12 yen a month**: https://www.asahi-net.or.jp/~bh3h-smjy/shunyo/shunyo008.htm (low reliability; see Uncertain).
- **Tokutomi Roka 徳冨蘆花, 『不如帰』**: 國民新聞 **1898-11-29 to 1899-05-24**; revised and published by **民友社 1900-01-15**; first printing of **1,000 sold out in a month**; **10,000 in two years**; by Roka's death (1927) **190 printings, 500,000 copies** (ja.wikipedia 不如帰 (小説), citing Kandatsu Haruki 1991 and Dou Xinguang 2014): https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/不如帰_(小説)
- **Natsume Sōseki 夏目漱石** left all teaching posts and joined the Asahi in **1907** (decision February; formally 1 April) at **200 yen a month**; both the Yomiuri and the Osaka Asahi had courted him; he was the only writer whose novels ran **simultaneously in the Tokyo and Osaka Asahi**. ja.wikipedia 夏目漱石: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/夏目漱石 ; en.wikipedia Asahi (1 April). His first serial, 『虞美人草』, ran **1907-06-23 to 10-29**, 127 installments, **with full furigana**; book by **Shun'yōdō 1908-01-01, 1 yen 50 sen**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/虞美人草 . Hasegawa (Futabatei) had joined the Asahi in 1904 (Nipponica 朝日新聞).

## 3. Serial-to-book

- **Gōkan reissues of serials (to c.1886)**: the 毒婦物 serials were reissued as illustrated **gōkan** (kusazōshi format, woodblock pictures on every page). Robun's 『高橋阿伝夜叉譚』: publisher **金松堂**, **Meiji 12–13 (1879–80)**, **24 volumes**, 18 cm (NDL record: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/en/books/R100000039-I883903 ). 『鳥追阿松海上新話』: 錦栄堂 1878, pictures by Chikanobu (UTokyo record above). Nipponica says Robun's book "turned newspaper tsuzukimono into kusazōshi" and revived gesaku.
- **Typeset (katsuji) reissue**: the same Oden story reappeared as a **145-page, 18 cm typeset book**, 「仮名垣魯文 補綴」, from **自由閣, April 1886** (NDL record: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/en/books/R100000002-I000000508414 ); a compilation 『新編明治毒婦伝』 (鈴木金次郎編, 金泉堂, **1887-11**) followed (NDL: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/en/books/R100000039-I881370 ). Kotobank 貸本屋 (Sekai): lending libraries declined "from about **1883**, as cheap typeset books spread": https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%B2%B8%E6%9C%AC%E5%B1%8B-462164 . Late gōkan: Takagi Gen distinguishes reprints of Edo gōkan, continuations, Meiji-made gōkan and Meiji kusazōshi (ja.wikipedia 合巻: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/合巻 ); Britannica-J says gōkan flourished "to the middle of Meiji": https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%90%88%E5%B7%BB-61653
- **Part-issue booklets**: Tsubouchi Shōyō's 『一読三歎 当世書生気質』 came out in **17 numbered booklets** (和装活版分冊雑誌, Japanese-bound, typeset) from **晩青堂**, no.1 **1885-06-24**, no.17 **January 1886**; illustrations by 梅蝶楼国峰, 葛飾正久, 長原止水, 武内桂舟; then reissued in 1886 as two Japanese-bound volumes and as one Western-bound volume. 『小説神髄』 likewise came out in **9 part-booklets** from 松月堂, **1885-09 to 1886-04** (paused after no.3). ja.wikipedia 当世書生気質: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/当世書生気質 ; 小説神髄: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/小説神髄
- **Kuchi-e 口絵 (colour woodblock frontispieces)**: early example **1884**, Utagawa Kunimatsu, 「南海紀聞誉音信」; **1889** Shun'yōdō's series 『新作十二番』; from its first issue in **January 1895** Hakubunkan's 『文芸倶楽部』 carried a full-colour woodblock kuchi-e in every issue until mid-**1914**: **295** in all, of which **193 were bijinga unrelated to any story**; from **1902** it used independent bijinga. Size about A4 folded three or four times, or a kikuban single page. "In the Meiji 30s (1897–1906) it was said that magazines and books without a kuchi-e would not sell." ja.wikipedia 口絵: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/口絵 . Shun'yōdō's series were "half-paper, woodblock printed, coloured covers, with kuchi-e, famous for beautiful binding" (Nipponica, 春陽堂).
- **Konjiki Yasha** as a book: **Shun'yōdō bought the manuscripts outright** and published five volumes (前編 to 続々編) **1898–1903**, then one-volume, pocket and bunko editions; NDL lists it as a Meiji best-seller. NDL 「本の万華鏡」: https://www.ndl.go.jp/kaleido/entry/4/1.html . First volume (July 1898) kuchi-e 「熱海の景」 by **武内桂舟** (ja.wikipedia 金色夜叉, image caption).
- **Royalties**: the same NDL page says the royalty (印税) system became established only with the enpon boom of the late 1920s; before that, outright purchase and royalties co-existed. The personal site above says Shun'yōdō paid Mori Ōgai a 25 % royalty in 1892 when he insisted on one.
- **Best-seller numbers I could verify**: Fukuzawa's 『学問のすすめ』 first part "not under 200,000 copies, plus perhaps a hundred-odd thousand pirated" (Fukuzawa's own 1880 preface, NDL Kaleidoscope); *Hototogisu* as above; *Setchūbai*: publisher **博文堂** (Hakubundō, not Hakubunkan), vol.1 1886-08, vol.2 1886-11, 2nd printing 1886-12, 3rd 1887-09; revised one-volume edition by 青木嵩山堂 1890-05 reached **20 printings** (ja.wikipedia 雪中梅: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/雪中梅 ). Kuroiwa Ruikō's detective translations from 1889 (e.g., 『法庭の美人』, 1889-05) "became best-sellers as soon as they were published as books" (NDL Kaleidoscope).
- **Publishers**: **春陽堂 Shun'yōdō**, founded **1878** by Wada Tokutarō 和田篤太郎 (1857–99), first a hawker of picture books; published fiction from about 1882; 『新小説』 1889 (first series closed 1890; second series 1896, edited by Kōda Rohan); near-exclusive publisher of Kōyō, most of Sōseki, Tōson's poems, Ōgai, Kyōka; 『小説百家選』 15 vols 1894. Nipponica and Sekai: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%98%A5%E9%99%BD%E5%A0%82-1174409 . **博文館 Hakubunkan**, founded **1887** in Hongō by Ōhashi Sahei 大橋佐平 with the reprint magazine 『日本大家論集』; magazines 『太陽』 (1895), 『文芸倶楽部』 (1895), 『少年世界』, 『女学世界』 (1901), 『少女世界』 (1906), 『文章世界』 (1906); book series 『帝国文庫』 100 vols (1893–1902), 『帝国百科全書』 200 vols (1898–1909); the 博文館日記 diaries were its cash cow. Sekai and Mypedia: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%8D%9A%E6%96%87%E9%A4%A8-860515
- Ichiyō dealt with Hakubunkan through **Ōhashi Otowa 大橋乙羽**; in 1895 she asked his wife for an **advance on her fee** to pay for her father's seventh-anniversary memorial service, delivering the unfinished 『にごりえ』 in late July and the rest on 2 August. ja.wikipedia 樋口一葉: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/樋口一葉

## 4. Reading practices

- **Newspaper reading rooms (新聞縦覧所)**: first set up free by private benefactors in **1872** (Kokugo); later most charged a fee and served tea; by mid-Meiji some "put women at the door to pull in customers" (Kokugo). Some were run by the state, some by Liberal-party groups as propaganda points, some for profit; a few lasted into early Taishō (図書館情報学用語辞典). Kotobank: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%96%B0%E8%81%9E%E7%B8%A6%E8%A6%A7%E6%89%80-1344735 . ja.wikipedia: spreading from c.1870, private rooms spreading in Tokyo, Yokohama and elsewhere from Meiji 5/8, **peak around 1877**; a public one stood in Ueno Park; in late Meiji some were fronts for unlicensed prostitution: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新聞縦覧所 . The **milk hall** (ミルクホール) inherited the function, letting milk drinkers read papers free (Sekai, quoted in the same Kotobank page).
- **Robun's wife ran a reading room** in Yokohama while he worked at the prefecture (ja.wikipedia 仮名垣魯文); the Yomiuri of **1876-06-22** announced that Robun was opening a "風流な粋な" reading room called 「窟螻蟻」 from the 2nd of the next month (Kokugo citation in Kotobank 新聞縦覧所).
- **新聞解話会 (newspaper explanation meetings)**: ja.wikipedia 「日本の新聞」 says the early Meiji government set up free reading rooms and "新聞解話会 where newspapers were read aloud to people", and bought newspapers with public money and favoured them in the post: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本の新聞 . I found no open primary or scholarly page with dates or places for kaiwakai (see Uncertain).
- **Reading aloud**: Maeda Ai's essay 「音読から黙読へ――近代読者の成立」 (English: "From Communal Performance to Solitary Reading," in *Text and the City*, Duke UP 2004, p.223) argues that Meiji readers commonly read aloud in families and groups before silent solitary reading took over: https://www.dukeupress.edu/text-and-the-city . I could not open the essay itself; I found no open page documenting bathhouse reading specifically.
- **Literacy and who read koshinbun** (Tsuchiya, ch.3): Tsuchiya posits three layers — illiterate (nishiki-e shinbun), kana-literate but weak in kanji (koshinbun), kanji-literate (ōshinbun). Surveys of the period show **female literacy about half or less of male**, with big regional gaps; in farm villages perhaps **2 %** could read a big paper. But a **1876 Tokyo-fu survey** counted **over 80 % of men and over 60 % of women** as "able to read books and write" (書ヲ読ミ字ヲ書キ得ル者). She estimates big-paper readers at about **10 %** and small-paper readers about **20 %** of Tokyo's literate population; about **70 % of kazoku/shizoku** read a big paper, while small papers took the rest of them plus about 20 % of commoners.
- **Female readership**: Tokyo Eiri "loved by townspeople and women and children" (Sekai); Nipponica: koshinbun aimed at "婦女子、芸人". Tsuchiya (ch.7): in libel cases against koshinbun 1875–80, the persons libelled were ordinary townspeople, **about 30 % of them women** (adultery, quarrels between adopted children and parents, theft).
- **Readers' letters (投書)**: in the Yomiuri, Tokyo Eiri and Kanayomi letters peaked in **1876**, sometimes **over 300 a month** for the three; Tsuchiya identified **2,650** contributors to the Yomiuri, 688 to the Tokyo Eiri, 539 to the Kanayomi, **3,721** individuals in all; **89 regulars (2.4 %)** wrote **over 40 %** of the letters; **over 70 %** lived in Tokyo; of 59 identifiable contributors the great majority were men, half of Edo birth and samurai or townsman stock. Letter-writers used jōruri, hauta, senryū, kyōka and kanshi parodies (Tsuchiya ch.6). In Osaka, the *Asahi* won by recruiting the city's star letter-writers (投書家) (ch.10). By the time of the *Iroha Shinbun* (1879–) the letters column had almost disappeared — "the days when the letters column was a lively liberated zone were over" (ch.8).
- **School enrolment by sex** (学齢児童の就学率; my calculation from MEXT, 『学制百年史』 資料編, 第1表 学齢児童数および就学児童数, read from the table image: https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1318190.htm ):

- **Lending libraries (貸本屋)**: Edo had **656** in 1808; revived after the Restoration but declined from about **1883** as cheap typeset books spread; new kinds appeared, e.g. **いろは屋貸本店** in Kanda (**1886–c.1913**) for scholarly books, translations and new fiction; 池清 in Ushigome survived from Edo to about 1931. Sekai in Kotobank 貸本屋 (URL above). In the early 20th century some lenders rented **home-made books of pasted-together newspaper serials** for **3 sen**, against **6 sen** for a printed book, and some made rounds of **boarding houses** (下宿), changing books every 3–10 days (ja.wikipedia 貸本, citing 下川耿史『明治・大正家庭史年表』 p.357: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/貸本 ). The *Tokyo Nichinichi* was co-founded by a lending-library clerk, 西田伝助, with Jōno Saigiku and Yoshiiku (NDL Portraits page).

## 5. Political novels, 1880–1890

- Overview: the first is Toda Kindō 戸田欽堂, 『民権演義 情海波瀾』 (1880), an allegory in which the geisha **阿権 (O-Ken, "Rights")** is courted by 和国屋民次 ("People") and 国府正文 ("Government"); the real season began when the Liberal Party founded the *Jiyū Shinbun* and *E-iri Jiyū* (1882) and the Progressives used the *Hōchi*; the *Jiyū no tomoshibi* and *Kaishin* were major venues. The genre lost vigour around 1890 but raised the social standing of fiction. Sekai (Maeda Ai) and Nipponica (Kamei Hideo), 政治小説: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%94%BF%E6%B2%BB%E5%B0%8F%E8%AA%AC-85886
- **Sakurada Momoe 桜田百衛** (1859–1883-01-18), Liberal journalist, "the Hugo of the East": 『仏国革命起源 西洋血潮小暴風 (にしのうみちしおのさあらし)』, serialized in the *Jiyū Shinbun* from **1882**, adapted from Dumas's *Joseph Balsamo* (Sekai says *Mémoires d'un médecin*), adding fiery people's-rights speeches not in the original; unfinished at his death aged 25. He also helped found the rickshaw-men's union **車会党** (1882-10-04 meeting of 300+ pullers at Kanda Myōjin). ja.wikipedia 桜田百衛: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/桜田百衛 ; Kotobank: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%A1%9C%E7%94%B0%E7%99%BE%E8%A1%9B-1077959

## 7. Women's and girls' magazines

- **『女学雑誌』 Jogaku zasshi**: first issue **1885-07-20**, editor Kondō Kenzō; after his sudden death in May 1886 **Iwamoto Yoshiharu 巌本善治** took over (Kokugo/Sekai: from no.30; Daijisen: from no.24); published first by 万春堂, from no.11 by the 女学雑誌社; frequency changed from twice a month to three times, weekly, fortnightly, monthly; **526 numbers / 548 volumes** to **1904-02** (last editor Aoyagi Yūbi from no.524). Christian, aimed at raising women's status: anti-prostitution, monogamy petitions, the Women's Christian Temperance Union. In November 1890 Iwamoto assigned **Shimizu Toyoko as 主筆**, Wakamatsu and Tanabe for fiction, **Ogino Ginko** (first licensed woman doctor) for hygiene. ## 8. Scandal, readers' letters, controversies

- **Press law**: 新聞紙条例 **1875-06-28** (Dajōkan decree no.111) with the libel law **讒謗律** the same day; administrative bans for "national security" added **1876-07-05**; for **"corruption of morals" (風俗壊乱) 1880-10-12**; full revision **1883-04-16** (security deposits, power of prefectural governors to suspend); **1887-12-29** (or 12-28) licensing replaced by registration; **1897-03** abolition of the Home Minister's power to suspend; replaced by the **新聞紙法 1909-05-06**. Sekai (Uchikawa Yoshimi) and Nipponica, Kotobank: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%96%B0%E8%81%9E%E7%B4%99%E6%9D%A1%E4%BE%8B-82522 . First suspension of a daily: *Chōya Shinbun* printed the assassins' manifesto after Ōkubo's murder (**1878-05-15**) and was suspended 7 days (ja.wikipedia 日本の新聞). Chamberlain (1905): fines 5–500 yen, prison 1 month–2 years, security deposit **1,000 yen in Tokyo**.
- **Koshinbun scandal-mongering and libel** (Tsuchiya ch.7): 1875–1880, big and small papers had about the same number of press-law cases, but **over 98 %** of small-paper cases were **libel (讒謗律)**, none for sedition; **over 70 %** ended in a **5-yen fine**; small papers libelled ordinary people (about **30 % women**) over adultery, family quarrels, theft — "preaching reward-good-punish-evil to the townspeople while calling for civilization". By the early 1880s even the slang words 「猫」 (geisha) and 「鯰公」 (officials, for their whiskers) were dangerous to print (ch.8).
- ***Mezamashi Shinbun* and Itō Hirobumi (1887)**: reported the Prime Minister's scandal at the Rokumeikan and was suspended for 8 weeks; 133 days of suspension in 1887. Sekai, Kotobank めさまし新聞 (URL above).
- ***Yorozu Chōhō*'s 「蓄妾実例」 ("actual cases of kept mistresses")**: ran from **July 1898** for almost three months, naming mistresses (with ages, fathers' names and jobs) of peers, officials and shopkeepers but not the imperial family; there was reportedly a complaint, "Why haven't you printed *my* mistress?"; the paper used pink paper for a time, hence 「赤新聞」, and gave the word 「三面記事」 for sensational page-three news. ja.wikipedia 萬朝報.
- **Banned serial-turned-book**: Miyazaki Muryū's 『鬼啾啾』 (serial 1884–85, book October 1885 banned, trial March 1886, light imprisonment). Sources in §5.
- **Readers taking fiction as fact**: *Hototogisu* — "because it became a best-seller, people believed the novel was true, and the real models suffered baseless rumours" (ja.wikipedia 不如帰 (小説)); *Torioi Omatsu* was believed real (Sekai).
- ***Baien* (1909)**: Morita Sōhei's account of his 1908 attempted love-suicide at Shiobara with the schoolgirl-turned-writer **Hiratsuka Haruko (Raichō)**, serialized in the Tokyo Asahi on Sōseki's suggestion with her family's consent, **1909-01-01 to 05-16**; Raichō published a rebuttal in 『女学世界』 (May 1909) the moment it ended. ja.wikipedia 煤煙 (小説): https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/煤煙_(小説)
- **Readers' letters**: see §4 (Tsuchiya). Iwamoto's *Jogaku zasshi* and the girls' magazines institutionalized letters columns; Shōjo sekai's editor told girls that the purpose of contributing was "practice", not fame (嵯峨, 少女世界 paper).
- I found **no verified case** of a Meiji newspaper serial halted mid-run by police order or by readers' protests (see Uncertain).

## Telling details

- **The schoolbook picture is a novel.** The much-reproduced image of a policeman stopping a people's-rights speech (E-iri Jiyū Shinbun, 1888-03-14) illustrates installment 32 of the serial 「戀の革命」 ("Revolution of Love"), whose heroine is a 19-year-old noblewoman, Kajiwara Yoshiko, who wants to be "the people's ally and women's good friend". The serial ran 44 numbered days; the artist 芳宗 drew every one except no. 9, which the author forgot to commission (汲田, Kōchi bulletin).
- **"Love ten million, not one."** In Muryū's *Kishūshū* (Jiyū no tomoshibi, from Dec 1884) Sophia Perovskaya falls in love, then renounces it to love "ten million compatriots" instead; the novel ends with her letter to her mother; its title comes from Du Fu's wailing ghosts (Zhan, Sinica PDF).
- Robun's wife ran a newspaper reading room in Yokohama; in 1876 the Yomiuri announced Robun's own stylish reading room 「窟螻蟻」 (ja.wikipedia 仮名垣魯文; Kokugo in Kotobank 新聞縦覧所).
- Robun coined 「猫」 as slang for geisha, and as editor of the *Kanayomi* earned 40 yen a month, as editor of the *Konnichi* 70 (ja.wikipedia 仮名垣魯文).
- The *Tokyo Nichinichi* was founded by a gesaku writer (Jōno Saigiku), a **lending-library clerk** (Nishida Densuke) and an ukiyo-e artist (Yoshiiku) (NDL Portraits). Saigiku's son became the illustrator Kaburaki Kiyokata.
- Yoshiiku cut his own newspaper blocks (Sekai, 東京絵入新聞).
- The first *Tokyo Asahi* was numbered **No. 1,076** because it continued the Liberal Party's *Jiyū no tomoshibi* (en.wikipedia).
- *Mezamashi Shinbun* ("Wake-Up News") was suspended for eight weeks for reporting the Prime Minister's Rokumeikan scandal; Hoshi Tōru's expulsion from Tokyo under the Peace Preservation Ordinance is what put the paper up for sale to the Osaka Asahi (Sekai).
- Sixteen Tokyo papers tried, and failed, to organize a newsagents' boycott of the upstart Tokyo Asahi in March 1890 (ja.wikipedia 東京朝日新聞). In 1890 the Asahi bought Japan's first Marinoni rotary press, **6,300 yen**, able to print 30,000 eight-page papers (Hamada).
- The Tokyo Asahi used **carrier pigeons** to send copy on 1897-02-01 for the Hachiōji fire (ja.wikipedia 東京朝日新聞).
- Tokyo Asahi's real 1907 sale was about **91,000**, while the trade survey credited it with 200,000 (Ariyama).
- *Kanayomi Shinbun* lasted exactly **1,400 issues** (Nipponica).
- *Torioi Omatsu* reached the kabuki stage within months of its serial (April 1878); critics see Carmen in it (Sekai).
- Lending libraries rented **scrapbooks of clipped newspaper serials at 3 sen** (half the price of a book) and carried them round student boarding houses (ja.wikipedia 貸本).
- Ichiyō's first fee was 11 yen 50 sen; Kaho's first was 33 yen 20 sen, spent on her brother's memorial (ja.wikipedia 樋口一葉; 藪の鶯). Ichiyō signed newspaper pieces with joke names, 浅香のぬま子 and 春日野しか子.
- Kishida Toshiko was arrested in 1883 after a speech called **"The Daughter in the Box" (箱入娘)** (ja.wikipedia 中島湘煙).
- Wakamatsu Shizuko's pen name means "servant [of God]" from Wakamatsu; at her 1889 wedding she gave her husband Alice Cary's poem "The Bridal Veil", which assumes equality (ja.wikipedia 若松賤子).
- *Setchūbai* (1886) opens in **Meiji 173 (2040)**, when a landslide uncovers a monument to a husband-and-wife pair who fought for the Diet (ja.wikipedia 雪中梅). *Kajin no kigu*'s heroines are a Spanish and an Irish revolutionary exile (Kotobank).
- When *Gubijinsō* began (1907), Mitsukoshi sold **Gubijinsō yukata**, the jeweller 玉宝堂 sold **Gubijinsō rings**, and station newsboys hawked the Asahi crying "Sōseki's Gubijinsō!" (Komiya Toyotaka, quoted in ja.wikipedia 虞美人草).
- *Hototogisu* made the waved handkerchief the prop of farewell (ja.wikipedia 不如帰).
- Kosugi Tengai's *Makaze koikaze* (Yomiuri 1903, 190 installments): a brilliant, beautiful schoolgirl, Hatsuno, hurt in a **bicycle accident**, whose closest friend, a viscount's daughter, is her "sworn sister" and rival; the papers had to be **reprinted** to meet demand (ja.wikipedia 魔風恋風).
- The *Jogaku zasshi* came in **white** (literary) and **red** (household) covers in 1892–93 (ja.wikipedia 女学雑誌).
- In Bungei kurabu's 20 years of colour frontispieces, two-thirds (193 of 295) were pin-up bijinga with no story attached (ja.wikipedia 口絵).
- Shōjo sekai's letters page was laid out as a building, 「少女会館」, with a "conversation room" where girls wrote to one another (嵯峨).
- Yoshitoshi was in trouble in 1884 for drawing for a rival paper (ja.wikipedia 絵入自由新聞). Wikipedia also says the E-iri Jiyū's proofreader-turned-reporter Tomii Oto 富井於菟 was "Japan's first woman newspaper reporter" (Kotobank's entry on her, https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%AF%8C%E4%BA%95%E6%96%BC%E8%8F%9F-1094948 , does not mention journalism; she died in December 1885 aged 20).
- The *Asahi*'s name: Ueno Riichi said Tsuda Tei proposed it as "the thing people get first every morning"; the "rising sun, all things bright" motto was added later by Tsuda's friends (ja.wikipedia 大阪朝日新聞).

## Uncertain / conflicting

- **First newspaper novel date**: 「岩田八十八の話」 began 1875-10-28 (Kokugo) or 1875-11-28 (Britannica-J, 新聞小説 entry); Britannica-J's 東京絵入新聞 entry just says "November 1875, three days". Kotobank 新聞小説.
- **Kanayomi renaming**: 『かなよみ』 from 1877-03-17 (NDL guide) vs from the 1877-05-03 issue (Nipponica; ja.wikipedia).
- **Tokyo Eiri's end**: renamed 東西新聞 1889-03-22, ended 1890-06 (NDL; Britannica-J says renamed 1889, ended 1890); Mypedia says renamed in 1890.
- **E-iri Jiyū's end**: ja.wikipedia 絵入自由新聞 says it ended **1890-11-15**, merged with 『かみなり新聞』 into 『雷新聞』; NDL says *Yorozu Chōhō* merged with *E-iri Jiyū* in **June 1893**, and ja.wikipedia 萬朝報 also says 1893 (while its infobox gives 1890). The ja.wikipedia table in 大新聞と小新聞 says "1893-06 廃刊". The 1890 date may be a muddle.
- **Who bought what, and when, for the Tokyo Asahi**: Hamada says the Asahi bought *Mezamashi* in **June 1888**; Sekai says July 1888; Daijisen Plus says the Asahi bought the 「灯新聞」 (the paper's 1886–87 name). Kokugo says Murayama personally bought it. First Tokyo Asahi issue: 1888-07-10 (all sources). NDL microfilm of 燈新聞 runs only to 1887-02-15 (gap to 1887-04-01 unexplained).
- **First evening paper**: ja.wikipedia 都新聞 calls the *Konnichi Shinbun* (1884) "Japan's first real evening paper"; ja.wikipedia 高畠藍泉 says Ransen's 東京毎夕新聞 (1877-11-12) was the first, and Tsuchiya calls the *Konnichi* "the second attempt". The *Konnichi*'s 10,000 starting circulation is from an unsourced Wikipedia passage.
- **Asahi founding circulation**: 2,586 (Hamada, from the company history) vs "approximately 3,000" (en.wikipedia).
- **Asahi founding year in Daihyakka**: one Kotobank entry (旺文社 or Yamakawa) says the Asahi "entered Tokyo in 1889"; all others say 1888.
- **Circulation figures generally**: early Tokyo numbers come from Tokyo-fu and police statistics (annual totals, sometimes July–June years: Yamamoto's 1875–76 figures run July to June). Claimed figures (Chamberlain, Dentsū 1907, 1911–12 estimates) run far above internal figures (Tokyo Asahi 1907: 200,000 claimed vs 91,389 actual). I could not open Yamamoto's, the Yomiuri 80-year history's or 『明治世相編年辞典』's tables, so **1880, 1885 and 1890 figures for the Yomiuri, Tokyo Eiri, Kaishin, Yamato, Miyako and E-iri Jiyū are missing**; only the Asahi series is complete.
- **Kishūshū serial dates**: 1884-12-10 to 1885-04-03 comes from a jumbled OCR of Miura's chronology; Zhan gives "from December 1884". Muryū's conviction is said to be under the Publication Ordinance (出版条例) for the book; whether the newspaper serial itself was interrupted is unknown.
- **Muryū's Sophia quotation**: I have it only in Zhan's Chinese translation; the Japanese wording is in 『日本近代文学大系2』 p.126, which I did not see.
- **Robun and Oden**: ja.wikipedia 仮名垣魯文 says Robun serialized 「高橋お伝のはなし」 in the *Kanayomi* and then published the book; Kotobank entries say the book "turned newspaper tsuzukimono into kusazōshi" without naming a Robun serial. The authorship of the Tokyo Eiri serial 「毒婦お伝のはなし」 (1879-02-01–21) is not given in my sources.
- **Ozaki Kōyō's entry into the Yomiuri**: December 1889 (ja.wikipedia; Bunkyō city) vs 1892 (personal website). The salary figures (30 → 100 yen), Bizan's 4 yen per installment, Kafū's 12 yen at the *Yamato* all come from that personal website without citations; treat as indicative only.
- **Sōseki's start date**: ja.wikipedia says he resigned and joined in February 1907 (chronology: April); en.wikipedia says 1 April 1907.
- **Kaburaki Kiyokata's birth**: 1878-08-31 (Nipponica, Daijisen) vs "1882–1972" in Sekai; 1878 is almost certainly right.
- **Shimizu Shikin's birth**: 慶応4年1月11日 (= 1868-02-04 by my conversion; 20世紀日本人名事典) vs "late 12th month of Keiō 3, registered January 1868" (ja.wikipedia). Daijisen-type entries give "1868-01-11", which treats the lunar date as Gregorian.
- **Kishida Toshiko's death**: 1901-05-25 (ja.wikipedia) vs 1901-05-12 (Nipponica). Birth: 1863 lunar = 1864-01-13 Gregorian, so dictionaries give both 1863 and 1864.
- **Jogaku zasshi**: Iwamoto took over from no.24 (Daijisen) or no.30 (Kokugo, Sekai); totals 526 numbers (Daijisen) vs 548 volumes (Nipponica) — ja.wikipedia reconciles as "526 numbers, 548 volumes".
- **Shōnen sekai founding**: Sekai's Hakubunkan entry says 1898; the 少女世界 article and general knowledge say 1895 (Shōnen sekai began January 1895). Probably an error in Sekai.
- **Press-law revision dates**: 1887-12-29 (Sekai) vs 12-28 (Nipponica); 1897-03-24 (Sekai) vs 03-19 (Nipponica).
- **Uchida Roan's 『破垣』 ban**: ja.wikipedia 文芸倶楽部 dates it 1902-01; I believe it was 1901 but did not verify.
- **Keishū shōsetsu issues**: contents of the December 1895 issue beyond 「十三夜」 and 「萩桔梗」 are from a search-result snippet of a reprint listing (Ichiyō, Nakajima Utako, Miyake Kaho, Ōtsuka Kusuoko, (Kitada) Usurai, Wakamatsu Shizuko) that I could not open; whether Inabune's 「しろばら」 and Kimiko's work were in it is unverified. The famous detail that the issue printed **photographs** of the women writers is something I recall but could not verify.
- **新聞解話会** and reading aloud at bathhouses/barbershops: only a general Wikipedia statement (kaiwakai) and Maeda Ai's thesis (family reading aloud); no dated examples found.
- **Serial halted by police or readers**: no verified Meiji newspaper example found in open sources; the closest are the *Kishūshū* book ban, administrative suspensions of whole papers (*Mezamashi* 1887), and confiscation of a magazine issue (*Jogaku zasshi*, 1900).
- **Tomii Oto as "first woman reporter"**: Wikipedia only (no citation); Kotobank's biography is silent on it.
- **Jokan editor**: 三輪田眞佐子's role is inferred from a paper title in search results; not verified. Its end date (1909?) is from library holdings, not a stated closure.
- **Utagawa Kunimatsu's 1884 kuchi-e** and the Shun'yōdō 1889 series as "early" kuchi-e come from ja.wikipedia 口絵 (no inline citations).
