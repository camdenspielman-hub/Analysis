## 1. The Freedom and People's Rights movement (自由民権運動), 1874–1890

### Petition and first organisations
- **1874年（明治7）1月12日**: 板垣退助, 後藤象二郎, 江藤新平 and 副島種臣 (the four councillors who resigned in the 1873 征韓論 split) found the **愛国公党**. Five days later, on **1月17日**, eight men submit the **民撰議院設立建白書** (Memorial on the Establishment of a Popularly Elected Assembly) to the 左院. The eight are 板垣, 後藤, 江藤, 副島, 由利公正 (former Tokyo governor), 岡本健三郎, 古沢滋 (古沢迂郎, the drafter) and 小室信夫. The memorial attacks 有司専制 (rule by officials). It argues from 天賦人権 (rights bestowed by Heaven): "天の斯民を生ずるや之に附与するに一定動かすべからざるの通義権理を以てす". It also argues that "人民政府に対して租税を払ふの義務ある者は、乃其政府の事を与知可否するの権理を有す", that is, whoever pays taxes has the right to a say in government. It asks for the vote for 士族 and 豪農商. It was published in J. R. Black's newspaper 『日新真事誌』. https://kotobank.jp/word/民撰議院設立建白書-640646 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/民撰議院設立建白書
- The 明六社 intellectuals, led by 加藤弘之 (in print on 2月3日), opposed the memorial as premature. 大井憲太郎 replied, and the debate ran in the press. In 『明六雑誌』 no. 4, 加藤's "民撰議院不可立の論" set out the gradualist position. 西村茂樹, 津田真道 and 福沢 sided with establishing an assembly. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/民撰議院設立建白書 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明六雑誌
- **愛国社**: founded at a congress in Osaka on **1875年2月22日** by about 40 men from 立志社 (Tosa), 自助社 (Awa) and groups in Kaga, Chikuzen, Buzen and Higo, with headquarters to be in Tokyo. It soon lapsed. It was revived by 立志社 in **1878**, holding a congress in Osaka from 9月11日 to 10月1日. At the **third congress in November 1879**, delegates from 18 prefectures and 21 societies attended. https://kotobank.jp/word/愛国社-421235
- **国会期成同盟** (League for Establishing a National Assembly): created at the fourth 愛国社 congress in Osaka, which opened on **1880年3月15日**; the League was formed on **3月17日** (Nipponica). 片岡健吉 (立志社) and 河野広中 (石陽社, Fukushima) presented 「国会を開設するの允可を上願する書」 to both the 太政官 and the 元老院, and both refused to accept it. The second congress (Tokyo, November 1880) represented about 130,000–135,000 signatories and agreed that each society would bring a draft constitution (憲法見込案) to the 1881 meeting. The 1881 congress merged into the founding of the 自由党. https://kotobank.jp/word/国会期成同盟-502355
- Scale: from 1874 to 1881 there were **130 petitions and memorials** for a national assembly (90 to the 元老院 and 40 to the emperor or 太政官), carrying more than **319,311 signatures**. https://kotobank.jp/word/自由民権運動-77425
- Repression: 讒謗律, the revised 新聞紙条例 and the revised 出版条例 (all 1875), the 集会条例 (**1880年4月5日**) and its 1882 extension. https://kotobank.jp/word/自由民権運動-77425
  - The 集会条例 required police permission for political meetings and societies and let attending police dissolve meetings. It banned soldiers, teachers and students from political activity, and banned outdoor meetings and links between societies.
  - In 1882 there were 86 violations and **351 dissolutions** of meetings and societies.
  - Its line of descent runs to the 集会及政社法 (1890年7月25日) and the 治安警察法 (1900). https://kotobank.jp/word/集会条例-76769
- **Women in the movement, 1878**: 楠瀬喜多 (Kusunose Kita, 天保7年9月9日〈1836年10月18日〉 – 1920年10月18日) was a widow and female household head (女戸主) in Kōchi who paid her taxes. Refused a vote in the 1878 ward-assembly election, she stopped paying tax. She told the prefecture that if she could not vote she would not pay ("権利と義務は両立するはず"), and petitioned the 内務省. After the 区町村会法 of **1880年9月20日** let local assemblies write their own election rules, the 上町 assembly in Kōchi became the first in Japan to give the vote to female household heads, and the neighbouring 小高坂村 followed. The government took that power back in **1884**. She was known as 「民権ばあさん」. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/楠瀬喜多 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/楠瀬喜多-830723 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/女性参政権-895295

### The crisis of 1881 and the promise of a Diet
- The **開拓使官有物払下げ事件** (Hokkaido Colonization Office scandal): 黒田清隆 arranged to sell assets on which the state had spent **14 million yen** to the Satsuma merchant 五代友厚 for **380,000 yen**, payable over 30 years. In March 1881 大隈重信 had privately submitted a memorial calling for party cabinets, an English-style constitution within the year and a Diet within two years. https://kotobank.jp/word/明治十四年の政変-3172769 (Britannica section)
- **1881年10月11日**: a council before the emperor dismisses 大隈 and his followers and cancels the sale. **10月12日**: the 国会開設の勅諭 (Rescript on the Establishment of a National Assembly) promises a Diet "by 1890" (明治23年を期して) and a constitution granted by the emperor. 井上毅 drafted it, and 太政大臣 三条実美 countersigned. The coup waited until the emperor had returned from his tour of Tōhoku and Hokkaido. https://kotobank.jp/word/明治十四年の政変-3172769 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/国会開設の詔 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/国会開設の勅諭-2129950

### Parties
- **自由党** (Liberal Party, 1881–84): formally launched on **1881年10月29日** with 総理 板垣退助 and 副総理 中島信行 (Nipponica). It had **101 members at founding and 2,224 in May 1884**. Its organ, 『自由新聞』, began in June 1882. https://kotobank.jp/word/自由党-77250 (Britannica gives 10月18日; see "Uncertain")
- **立憲改進党** (Constitutional Reform Party): founded **1882年4月16日** with 大隈 as 総理. Its programme was 王室の尊栄と人民の幸福, internal reform, local self-government, gradual extension of the vote, trade, and a sound metallic currency, aiming at an English-style cabinet system. It had **116 members at founding, 1,272 in February 1883 and 1,729 in April 1884**. 大隈 and others left the party in December 1884. https://kotobank.jp/word/立憲改進党-148941
- **1882年4月6日**, Gifu: 相原尚褧 stabbed 板垣 with a 9-寸 (about 27 cm) dagger, leaving 7 wounds. The *Osaka Asahi* of 4月11日 already reported the cry "板垣は死すとも自由は亡びませぬぞ". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/板垣退助岐阜遭難事件

### The "incidents of radicalisation" (激化事件)
- **Fukushima (福島事件), 1882**: Governor 三島通庸 (posted there in February 1882) forced through the 三方道路 (roads from Aizu-Wakamatsu to Yamagata, Tochigi and Niigata). Residents of Aizu aged **15–60, men and women alike**, owed one day of labour a month for two years, or a commutation fee of **15 銭 a day for men and 10 銭 for women**. The prefectural assembly under 河野広中 rejected every one of the governor's bills (May 1882). On **11月28日** several thousand farmers gathered at 弾正ヶ原 and marched on the 喜多方 police station (喜多方事件). About 2,000 people were arrested. In **September 1883** 河野 received 軽禁獄7年 and five others 6 years. https://kotobank.jp/word/福島事件-124004
- **Gunma (群馬事件), May 1884** and **Kabasan (加波山事件), September 1884**: **16** radicals from Fukushima, Tochigi and Ibaraki rose on 加波山 on **9月23日**. Their plans to kill 三島 and senior officials had failed, and a bomb had exploded while 鯉沼九八郎 was making it. They raised banners reading 「自由之魁」「圧制政府転覆」「一死以報国」, attacked the 町屋 police substation, and were all captured. In July 1886 they were tried as common robbers, not political offenders: **7 death sentences, 7 life terms**. The government then issued the 爆発物取締罰則 (December 1884). https://kotobank.jp/word/加波山事件-46064
- **Dissolution of the 自由党, 1884年10月29日**, at a congress in **Osaka**. Funding from wealthy farmers had dried up under the Matsukata deflation. 星亨, writing from prison, telegraphed against dissolution, and 板垣 is said to have wired back 「バカイフナ」 ("don't talk rubbish"). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/自由党_(日本_1881-1884) ; https://kotobank.jp/word/自由党-77250
- **Chichibu (秩父事件), 31 October – 9 November 1884**, two days after the dissolution:
  - The background was the collapse of silk prices plus debt to moneylenders. The 秩父困民党 demanded a 10-year moratorium on debts with repayment over 40 years, cuts to school and village levies, and (per Mypedia) a three-year closure of primary schools.
  - 風布村 rose on 10月31日. On 11月1日 the rebels mustered at 椋神社 in 下吉田. They were organised as an army with 総理 田代栄助, 副総理 加藤織平 and 会計長 井上伝蔵, in two battalions with village platoons under strict military rules.
  - On 11月2日 **3,000** of them occupied 大宮郷. Gendarmes and 東京鎮台 troops were engaged at 皆野 and at 金屋村.
  - 菊池貫平's column fought on into Nagano and was destroyed at 東馬流 on **11月9日** (14 killed).
  - Seven rebels were executed under the 兇徒聚衆罪. Figures for those charged or punished vary (see "Uncertain").
  https://kotobank.jp/word/秩父事件-96257
- **Osaka (大阪事件), 1885**: 大井憲太郎, 小林樟雄, 磯山清兵衛 and others planned a coup in Korea that would spark war with Qing and a rising at home. The plot was discovered in November 1885; about 130 people were arrested, and 38 were convicted of 外患罪. The arrest of the woman activist **景山英子** (later 福田英子) drew national attention. https://kotobank.jp/word/大阪事件-39191
- **Women orators**: 岸田俊子 (号 湘烟; born 文久3年12月5日 = **1864年1月13日**; died 1901):
  - She served at court as tutor in Chinese learning to the Empress in 1879 (other accounts give 1878 or 1880) and then left.
  - In April 1882 she spoke on 「婦女の道」 in Osaka. Her speaking tour of Okayama converted the young 景山英子.
  - On **1883年10月12日** she gave the speech 「箱入娘」 ("Daughters in boxes") in 大津, was arrested under the 集会条例 and fined.
  - In 1884 she published 「同胞姉妹に告ぐ」 in 『自由燈』, in 10 instalments.
  - In August 1885 she married 中島信行 (former 自由党 副総理 and later the first Speaker of the lower house).
  https://kotobank.jp/word/岸田俊子-155442 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/中島湘煙

### Daidō danketsu (大同団結) and the expulsions of 1887
- **1886年10月24日**: 星亨 and 中江兆民 hold a 全国有志大懇親会 in Tokyo, calling for "小異を捨て大同につく" (set aside small differences and unite). In 1887 外相 井上馨's treaty-revision draft leaked: it allowed foreign judges and gave foreigners the right to reside anywhere in Japan (内地雑居). Agitation followed, and 井上 resigned on 9月17日. https://kotobank.jp/word/大同団結運動-91686 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/三大事件建白運動-514110
- **三大事件建白運動** (1887): petitions demanding (1) freedom of speech and assembly, (2) a lower land tax, and (3) "recovery of diplomacy" (an equal treaty). 片岡健吉's Kōchi delegation submitted first, in October. On 12月15日 delegates from 2 府 and 18 県 submitted theirs, and the movement spread to 3 府 and 35 県. 後藤象二郎 founded the 丁亥倶楽部 on **1887年10月3日**. https://kotobank.jp/word/三大事件建白運動-514110 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/大同団結運動-91686
- **保安条例** (Peace Preservation Ordinance), promulgated and in force **1887年12月25日**, 7 articles. Article 4 let the authorities banish anyone judged to be plotting or inciting disorder to beyond **3 里** (about 12 km) of the palace for up to three years. Within days **570** activists were ordered out of Tokyo, among them 星亨, 中島信行, 尾崎行雄 and 中江兆民, each followed by police. 片岡健吉 and 10 others refused and were jailed. The ordinance was abolished in **June 1898**. https://kotobank.jp/word/保安条例-131568 . 中島湘煙 (岸田俊子) followed her husband into exile in Yokohama. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/中島湘煙
- End of the movement: in **March 1889** 後藤 joined the 黒田 cabinet as 逓信大臣. The movement split into the 大同倶楽部 and the 大同協和会, both formed on 1889年5月10日 per Britannica. Nipponica nonetheless credits it with laying the foundation of the opposition "民党" coalition in the early Diets. https://kotobank.jp/word/大同団結運動-91686

## 2. The Constitution and the first Diet

### Drafting
- 伊藤博文 studied in Europe under Gneist (Berlin) and Stein (Vienna). From **c. 1887年6月4日** he worked with 井上毅, 伊東巳代治 and 金子堅太郎 at his villa on 夏島 (now Natsushima-chō, Yokosuka) and at first at the 東屋 inn in 金沢.
  - 井上毅 produced a first draft in **March 1887** and the 甲・乙 systematic drafts in **May 1887**. The 甲 draft had 7 chapters and 72 articles. 井上 is believed to have discussed them with Roesler and Mosse.
  - The 夏島草案 was revised into a final draft of **7 chapters and 76 articles**.
  - In **April 1888** the commentaries 『憲法義解』 and 『皇室典範義解』 were completed.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/伊東巳代治
- **Karl Friedrich Hermann Roesler (ロエスレル)**, 1834年12月18日 – 1894年12月2日, came to Japan in October 1878 as a Foreign Ministry adviser, recommended by 青木周蔵. He later became legal and then cabinet adviser. Wikipedia credits 井上毅 with the draft but says much of it came from Roesler's discussion and guidance; Roesler submitted his own 「日本帝国憲法草案」 in 1887. His name was spelled so many ways (ロエスレル, レースラー, ロイスレル…) that a joke verse ran 「ロエスレルとは俺れのことかとレースラー言い」. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ヘルマン・ロエスレル
- **枢密院** (Privy Council), founded **1888年4月30日** (勅令第22号) to review the draft, with 伊藤 as its first president. It met in the 赤坂仮御所別殿, which was later preserved as the 憲法記念館 (today's 明治記念館). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/枢密院_(日本) . During the Council's review, 森有礼 proposed replacing 「臣民ノ権利」 with 「臣民ノ分際」 ("the station of subjects"). 伊藤 replied that if a constitution did not list subjects' rights, but only their duties, there was no point in having one. https://kotobank.jp/word/大日本帝国憲法-91835 (Sekai daihyakka excerpt)
- The draft was kept entirely secret from the public until promulgation. https://kotobank.jp/word/大日本帝国憲法-91835

### Promulgation, 11 February 1889
- **大日本帝国憲法**: promulgated **明治22年（1889）2月11日**; in force **1890年11月29日** (the day the Diet opened); 7 chapters, 76 articles. It was signed by Prime Minister 黒田清隆 and by 伊藤 (as Privy Council president), 大隈 (foreign minister), 西郷従道, 井上馨, 山田顕義, 松方正義 and others. Article 1 reads 「大日本帝国ハ万世一系ノ天皇之ヲ統治ス」. Article 28 granted freedom of religion only "insofar as it does not prejudice peace and order or conflict with the duties of subjects" (安寧秩序ヲ妨ケス及臣民タルノ義務ニ背カサル限ニ於テ). https://kotobank.jp/word/大日本帝国憲法-91835 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/国家神道
- The 皇室典範 (Imperial House Law) was enacted the same day. It was treated as the imperial house's own law and was not published in the 官報 until the 1907 supplement. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/皇室典範_(1889年)
- On the morning of promulgation day, Education Minister **森有礼** was stabbed by 西野文太郎. He died the next day, **2月12日**. https://kotobank.jp/word/森有礼-142842
- An amnesty at promulgation freed those convicted in the 大阪事件, including 景山英子. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/福田英子

### Franchise and the first election (1 July 1890)
- The **衆議院議員選挙法** (明治22年法律3号) was promulgated with the constitution on 1889年2月11日.
  - Voters: men aged **25 or over** who had paid at least **15 円** a year in direct national tax (land tax, or income tax paid for 3 years) and had lived in the prefecture at least one year.
  - Candidates: men aged 30 or over meeting the same tax test.
  - Single-member districts as the rule, with voting by signed and sealed ballot (記名捺印).
  - Seats: 300.
  https://kotobank.jp/word/衆議院議員選挙法-76825
- Electorate: **450,872, out of a total population of 39,933,478 (about 1.13%)**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/第1回衆議院議員総選挙 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1890_Japanese_general_election . Yamakawa rounds this to "約45万人、内地人口の1.1％強". https://kotobank.jp/word/衆議院議員選挙法-76825
  - The highest share of voters was in Shiga, at **2.30%** (15,456 voters in a population of 671,788).
  - The lowest was **Tokyo-fu at 0.35%** (5,715 voters out of 1,628,551).
  - Hokkaido, Okinawa and the Ogasawara Islands had no election at all.
  - There were 214 single-member and 43 two-member districts, and 1,243 men stood.
  - Rural landlords qualified in large numbers, so a rural seat could take hundreds or thousands of votes while some urban seats were won with a few dozen.
  - 191 of the 300 winners were commoners (平民); farming was the most common occupation.
  - Some men acquired the tax qualification through adoption into a rich family, or by having property re-registered in their names.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/第1回衆議院議員総選挙
- Votes cast: **422,594**, a turnout of **93.73%** according to en.wikipedia, citing the Statistics Bureau. Results: Liberals 130, 大成会 79, 立憲改進党 41, 国民自由党 5, independents 45. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1890_Japanese_general_election
- Women were also excluded from voting for city, town and village assemblies under the **市制町村制 of 1888**. https://kotobank.jp/word/集会及政社法-1335370

### The first Diet (第1回帝国議会)
- Convened on **1890年11月25日**; the opening ceremony, attended by the emperor, was held in the House of Peers chamber on **11月29日**. https://kotobank.jp/word/帝国議会-100022
  - On the first day 290 of the 300 members attended and elected **中島信行** Speaker and **津田真道** Vice-Speaker.
  - The opposition parties (民党) held **171** seats.
  - 中江兆民's labels 「民党」 and 「吏党」 (government-side party) became catchwords.
  - On 12月6日 Prime Minister 山県有朋 made his 「主権線・利益線」 speech, which named Korea as Japan's "line of interest".
  - The 民党 demanded "政費節減・民力休養" (cut government spending, relieve the people) and a 10% cut to the budget. The government peeled off the 土佐派 of the 立憲自由党, and an amended budget passed **by 2 votes**.
  - The session closed on **1891年3月7日**.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/第1回帝国議会
- **The temporary Diet building burned down before dawn on 1891年1月20日**. The lower house moved to the 東京女学館 (the former 工部大学校 building), and the Peers moved to the 華族会館 (the former 鹿鳴館) and then the Imperial Hotel. The clerk 曾禰荒助 blamed an electrical short circuit in the 官報. Customers rushed to cancel electric lighting, oil companies ran advertisements attacking electricity, and the whole board of 東京電燈 resigned after losing a libel suit. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/第1回帝国議会

### House of Peers (貴族院)
- In existence from 1890年11月29日 to 1947年5月3日; total membership ranged between about 250 and 400.
  - Adult male imperial princes, and 公爵 and 侯爵 aged 25 or over, sat automatically, unpaid.
  - 伯爵, 子爵 and 男爵 elected members from among their own rank for 7-year terms; the first such election was held on **1890年7月10日**. In the first Diet there were 14 counts, 70 viscounts and 20 barons.
  - **61** members were appointed by the emperor (勅選) at the start: 27 from the 元老院, 10 officials, 9 private persons, 6 from the Imperial University, 6 court councillors and 3 from the Legislation Bureau.
  - **45** members represented the largest taxpayers: one per prefecture, chosen by and from the top 15 taxpayers, with none from Hokkaido or Okinawa.
  - 金子堅太郎 drafted the House's rules. He at first called it a 「元老院」; 伊藤 renamed it 「貴族院」.
  - 伊藤 took a seat as an elected count in order to serve as the first President for the first session, then resigned after little more than a year.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/貴族院_(日本)
- The two houses had nearly equal powers; the lower house's only advantage was the right to consider the budget first. Under Article 71, if the Diet failed to pass a budget the government could carry on with the previous year's budget. https://kotobank.jp/word/帝国議会-100022

## 3. Ideology

### The family-state (家族国家観)
- Sekai daihyakka (in its 天皇制 entry): by the late Meiji period a family-state view had taken shape in which the imperial house was the head family (宗家) of the nation, the emperor its father, and the people his children (天皇の赤子). It was joined to the doctrine that loyalty to the lord and filial piety are one and the same (忠孝一致). https://kotobank.jp/word/家族国家観-1290065
- **穂積八束** published 「民法出デテ忠孝亡ブ」 ("When the Civil Code appears, loyalty and filial piety perish") in 『法学新報』 no. 5 (1891). He defended the patriarchal family as a "beautiful custom" against 旧民法, the French-modelled Civil Code drafted by Boissonade and others and promulgated on 1890年4月21日 and 10月7日. His brother 穂積陳重 later wrote that the slogan exerted a "great power over crowd psychology" (群集心理を支配するに偉大なる効力). https://kotobank.jp/word/民法出でて忠孝亡ぶ-1424426 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/民法_(日本)
- The **Meiji Civil Code** came in two laws:
  - 明治29年法律第89号 (1896): general provisions, property and obligations.
  - 明治31年法律第9号 (**1898年6月21日**): family (親族) and inheritance (相続), in force **1898年7月16日**. These books set up the 家 system with household heads (戸主) and succession of the headship (家督相続); both were abolished in 1947.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/民法_(日本)
- **Concubines in law**:
  - The criminal code 新律綱領 (明治3年12月20日 = **1871年2月9日**) ranked wife and concubine alike as relatives of the second degree (二等親).
  - The concubine provisions disappeared from the 1880 Penal Code (promulgated 1880年7月, in force 1882年1月). The 内務省 ruled, however, that concubines already entered in the family register should be treated as before.
  - The word 妾 was removed from the family register by the **1898 戸籍法**.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新律綱領 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/妾
- **Adultery law (姦通罪)**:
  - 1880 Penal Code art. 353: "有夫ノ婦姦通シタル者ハ六月以上二年以下ノ重禁錮", that is, a married woman who commits adultery gets 6 months to 2 years' heavy confinement, and so does her partner.
  - 1907 Penal Code art. 183: up to 2 years' penal servitude.
  - Prosecution only on the husband's complaint; a wife could not bring one. Only the wife's adultery was a crime.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/姦通罪

### The Imperial Rescript on Education (教育ニ関スル勅語), 30 October 1890
- Background: the 1879 「教学聖旨」 and 元田永孚's 『幼学綱要』 (1882) tried to restore Confucian moral teaching. In February 1890 a conference of prefectural governors asked for a rescript on moral education. Prime Minister 山県 wanted something for schools like the 1882 Rescript to Soldiers and Sailors (軍人勅諭).
  - A first draft by 中村正直 was rejected by 法制局長官 **井上毅** as too philosophical and religious.
  - 井上 wrote the base text, with **元田永孚** revising it.
  - It was issued just before the first Diet opened.
  https://kotobank.jp/word/教育勅語-52408
- Form: **315 characters**, in three sections.
  - The first places the source of education in the 国体, founded by the imperial ancestors.
  - The second lists **14 virtues**, from filial piety to obeying the law, ending with 「一旦緩急アレハ義勇公ニ奉シ以テ天壌無窮ノ皇運ヲ扶翼スヘシ」: should an emergency arise, offer yourselves courageously to the state and so support the imperial fortune.
  - The third declares these the teachings of the imperial ancestors, true at all times and places.
  - It deliberately carried **no ministerial countersignature**, to set it apart from ordinary political edicts.
  https://kotobank.jp/word/教育勅語-52408
- Ritual:
  - On **10月31日** the Ministry of Education sent copies (謄本) to every school, and on 12月25日 copies bearing the emperor's own signature went to schools directly under the ministry. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/教育ニ関スル勅語
  - In 1891 the 小学校祝日大祭日儀式規程 (rules for school ceremonies on national holidays) prescribed bowing to the 御真影, reading the Rescript, and singing 「君が代」. A principal's chief duties became reading the Rescript correctly, in its special intonation, and saving the copy and the portrait from fire. https://kotobank.jp/word/教育勅語-52408
  - On **1891年11月17日** a ministerial instruction required the portraits and copies to be kept "with the utmost reverence" (最モ尊重ニ奉置) in a fixed place. This began the 奉安庫 and 奉安殿 (separate shrine-like storehouses), which spread from the Taishō era onward. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/御真影 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/奉安殿-627003
- **The 内村鑑三 lèse-majesté incident**: on **1891年1月9日**, at the ceremony at the 第一高等中学校, the Christian lecturer 内村鑑三 did not give the deepest bow (最敬礼) before the Rescript bearing the emperor's signature. He made only an ordinary bow. Colleagues and students attacked him and he lost his post. 井上哲次郎 attacked Christianity over the affair, and 植村正久 defended Uchimura. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/内村鑑三不敬事件
- **井上哲次郎** (安政2年12月25日 = **1856年2月1日** – 1944): in Germany 1884–90, then professor at the Imperial University.
  - His 『勅語衍義』 (**September 1891**) was the semi-official commentary on the Rescript, read in draft by 中村正直 with a preface by Education Minister 芳川顕正.
  - Later works: 『教育ト宗教ノ衝突』 (1893), which attacked Christianity as contrary to the 国体, and 『国民道徳概論』 (1912).
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/井上哲次郎 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/教育ニ関スル勅語 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/勅語衍義-1368529

### Building State Shinto
- In **1868** the government proclaimed the unity of rites and government (祭政一致), revived the 神祇官 and ordered Shinto and Buddhism separated (神仏判然令). In **1871** shrines were declared 「国家の宗祀」 (the nation's rites), ranked in a new system of shrine grades, and hereditary priesthoods were abolished.
- In **1882** shrine priests were separated from religious preaching (神官教導職分離), on the doctrine that 「神社は宗教にあらず」 (shrines are not religion).
- In **1900** the 内務省 set up a separate 神社局 (Shrine Bureau) apart from its 宗教局 (Religions Bureau). This is usually taken as the point at which "State Shinto" was established.
- Because shrines were officially "not religion", shrine priests were forbidden to preach or conduct funerals.
https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/国家神道

### 西村茂樹 and 加藤弘之
- **西村茂樹** (文政11年3月13日 = **1828年4月26日** – 1902): a founder of the 明六社 and leader of the moral-education society later called the 日本弘道会. In December 1886 he lectured for three days at the university in 一ツ橋, and in spring **1887** printed the lectures as 『日本道徳論』. Minister 森 approved of it, but **伊藤 was furious**, reading it as slander of the new government, and rebuked 森. 森 had passages marked for revision; 西村 preferred to withdraw the book, but pirated copies were sold. https://kotobank.jp/word/日本道徳論-110245 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/西村茂樹
- **加藤弘之** (天保7年6月23日, which I compute as about 5 August 1836 – 1916; Nipponica gives only the lunar date):
  - 1861: 『鄰草』, the first introduction of constitutional ideas in Japan. Later works: 『真政大意』 (1870) and 『国体新論』 (1875).
  - 1877: first head (綜理) of the new University of Tokyo.
  - 1882: 『人権新説』, which repudiated natural-rights theory on Social Darwinist grounds, denounced at the time as a 「転向」 (apostasy).
  - Later he held that "忠君愛国" was an inborn property of the human "cells" making up the state organism (『自然と倫理』, 1912).
  https://kotobank.jp/word/加藤弘之-45577

### The Meirokusha (明六社) debates on women and marriage
- The 明六社 was founded in **1873** on 森有礼's initiative, with 西村, 津田真道, 西周, 中村正直, 加藤弘之, 箕作秋坪, 福沢諭吉, 杉亨二 and 箕作麟祥; 森 was its president.
  - 『明六雑誌』: first issue dated March 1874 but actually out **4月2日**; 43 issues; about 3,200 copies an issue (3,205 according to 森; 2,840 according to the 内務省).
  - It closed with **no. 43 on 1875年11月14日** because of the 讒謗律 and the new 新聞紙条例 (both 1875年6月28日), on a proposal by 福沢 and 箕作秋坪.
  https://kotobank.jp/word/明六社-141193 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/明六雑誌-141192 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明六雑誌
- **森有礼 「妻妾論」 (On Wives and Concubines)**, in five parts:
  - no. 8 (published 1874年5月31日), no. 11 (7月4日), no. 15 (9月7日), no. 20 (11月29日) and no. 27 (**1875年2月13日**).
  - It took monogamy as natural and condemned keeping concubines and having wife and concubine live together. Husband and wife should be equal, and the wife's roles as household manager and as educating mother should be respected.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明六雑誌
- The replies:
  - 津田真道 「夫婦有別論」 (no. 22, 1874年12月19日) and 「夫婦同権弁」 (no. 35, 1875年5月14日), the latter rejecting votes for women.
  - 加藤弘之 「夫婦同権の流弊論」 (no. 31, 1875年3月15日), which mocked Western "ladies first" as equal rights carried too far. 加藤 counted the exclusion of women from the vote, along with minors, the insane, criminals and the very poor, as 「正理」 (right reason).
  - 福沢 「男女同数論」 (no. 31) argued that since men and women are equal in number, one man with several women 「十露盤の勘定合はぬ」, does not add up on the abacus.
  - 阪谷素 「妾説の疑」 (no. 32).
  - 中村正直 「善良なる母を造る説」 (no. 33, 1875年4月6日): improve the people by educating girls to become wise mothers.
- 森 backed away in a note to no. 32: 「余曩きに妻妾論を著し夫妻の間は同等にして尊卑の差なきことを述べたれども、同権に至ては絶て之を論ぜしことなし」. That is, he had said husband and wife were equal with no distinction of rank, but had never argued for equal rights.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明六雑誌
- **森's contract marriage**: on **明治8年（1875）2月6日**, with **福沢諭吉 as witness**, 森 and 広瀬常 (daughter of the former shogunal retainer 広瀬秀雄; born 1855; educated at the 開拓使女学校) signed a three-article marriage contract:
  - each would be husband and wife to the other;
  - they would respect and love each other unless the contract were dissolved;
  - common property would not be lent, borrowed or sold without both parties' consent.
  - It is described as the first contract marriage, and the first marriage registration (婚姻届), in Japan.
  - They had three children. The couple divorced in **1886**, which Wikipedia calls the first divorce registration.
  - One rumour attributed the divorce to a blue-eyed daughter. The writer 森本貞子 instead connects it to Tsune's adoptive brother 広瀬重雄, a leader of the 静岡事件 plot to kill 伊藤.
  - In 1887 森 married 岩倉具視's daughter 寛子.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/森有礼 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/森有礼-142842

### 福沢諭吉 on women
- 「**日本婦人論**」 appeared as editorials in 『時事新報』 on **1885年6月4日–6月12日**, with a continuation on **7月7日–7月17日**. The 後編 was published as a book in August; the first part was not published as a book at the time. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/福澤諭吉
  - Related works of 1885–86: 『品行論』, 『男女交際論』, 『日本男子論』. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/福澤諭吉
  - Keio University's summary: he urged readers to imagine the sexes' positions reversed, used the image "Mustard is spicy and sugar is sweet, regardless of sex", and mocked a male-dominated society that expected women to endure what men could not. https://www.keio.ac.jp/en/keiotimes/index-164
- 「**女大学評論**」 and 「**新女大学**」: a critique of the Edo-period conduct book 『女大学』, together with his own replacement. Kotobank (女大学 entry) dates both to **1899**. https://kotobank.jp/word/女大学-42009 . Keio's English page says the book appeared in 1900, collecting earlier 『時事新報』 articles. https://www.keio.ac.jp/en/keiotimes/index-164 . 福沢 died on 明治34年（1901）2月3日. https://kotobank.jp/word/福沢諭吉-15113
- He condemned polygamy and concubinage and admired J. S. Mill. He was also scornful of English schooling for poor rural girls ("狂気の沙汰", madness). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/福澤諭吉

## 4. Economy

### The Matsukata deflation (松方デフレ / 松方財政), 1881–85
- **松方正義** became 大蔵卿 after the October 1881 political crisis. He retired government paper money and built up specie reserves out of budget surpluses. He also sold off state enterprises, raised taxes on sake and tobacco (and created new ones such as a patent-medicine stamp tax), and founded a central bank.
  - Paper money in circulation fell from **170 million yen (January 1880) to 125 million yen (May 1884)**.
  - The Convertible Bank Note Ordinance (兌換銀行券条例) followed in May 1884, and from **May 1885** the Bank of Japan issued silver-convertible notes, establishing the silver standard.
  - The 1882 Korean crisis (壬午軍乱) launched an 8-year naval and army build-up, paid for with more taxes in the middle of a slump.
  https://kotobank.jp/word/松方財政-170474
- Reserve ratio: in fiscal 1881, 0.1 億円 of silver backed 1.5 億円 of notes (8%); in fiscal 1885, 0.45 億円 backed 1.2 億円 (37%). Historian 中村隆英 likened the policy to "the operation succeeded, but the patient died". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/松方デフレ
- Rural distress:
  - Rice and cocoon prices collapsed. Farmers who could not pay taxes and debts lost their land and fell into tenancy.
  - Debtor disturbances erupted across the country in **1883–84**, especially in silk districts. In 1884 bankruptcies (身代限り) multiplied there, and debt relief became the main demand.
  - On **1884年8月10日** several thousand debtor farmers rallied at 御殿峠, and more than 200 were taken to the Hachiōji police station.
  https://kotobank.jp/word/松方財政-170474 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/困民党-506760
- Tenancy: tenant households numbered **950,000 (20.6% of farm households) in 1888 and 1.49 million (27.6%) in 1908**. https://kotobank.jp/word/小作農-500855 . Tenanted land reached **45%** by 1908 (旺文社) or "the 1900s" (Britannica). https://kotobank.jp/word/寄生地主制-50610
- Low interest after 1886 set off the first company boom (企業勃興). https://kotobank.jp/word/松方財政-170474

### Bank of Japan, silver and the gold standard
- The **日本銀行**, modelled on the Belgian national bank, opened for business on **1882年10月10日**. Its capital was **10 million yen**, half from the government. Its governor was appointed by the emperor, and the 大蔵卿 could veto its business. The first governor was 吉原重俊, serving from 明治15年10月6日. https://kotobank.jp/word/日本銀行-6942
- **貨幣法** (Coinage Act, 明治30年法律16号): promulgated **March 1897**, in force **October 1897**. It defined the yen as **750 mg of pure gold** (2 分). Gold coins became unlimited legal tender, and bank notes became convertible into gold rather than silver. It rested on the Chinese indemnity. https://kotobank.jp/word/貨幣法-46386
- The 1871 新貨条例 had already nominally set 1 yen at 1.5 g of gold (4 分), but trade silver kept Japan in practice on a bimetallic or silver basis. https://kotobank.jp/word/金本位制-248529

### Silk and tea
- **Raw silk**:
  - From 1884 the United States was the largest buyer of Japanese raw silk.
  - Japanese raw-silk exports passed Italy's total silk output in 1905 and China's silk exports in **1909**, making Japan the world's largest exporter.
  - Two-thirds to three-quarters of Japanese raw silk was exported each year, almost all through Yokohama until 1923.
  - By 1879 there were already **666** machine-reeling mills, mostly in Nagano, Yamanashi and Gifu.
  - Machine reeling overtook hand reeling (座繰) in output in **1894**.
  - In 1909 the Katakura firm (片倉組) alone employed **4,282** reeling women.
  https://kotobank.jp/word/生糸-49853
- **Share of exports**:
  - Silk and cocoons were 86% of exports in 1862. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomioka_Silk_Mill
  - Raw silk was the leading export from 1850 to 1930, at "20%–40% of Japan's total exports". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk
  - Silk goods were always above 20% of exports from 1912 to 1935, and 49% in 1922. https://kotobank.jp/word/蚕糸業-513554
  - **I could not get a sourced figure for 1880, 1890 or 1900 itself**; see "Uncertain".
- **Tea**: until the end of Meiji, more than 60% of Japanese tea output was exported, chiefly to Britain and the US. Refiring plants were monopolised by Westerners until the mid-Meiji period. A US ban on adulterated tea took effect in 1883 (明治16年) and exports dropped sharply; a US tariff in 1908 caused another crisis. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本茶

### Tomioka and the reeling girls
- The government recruitment notice for women workers went out on 明治5年2月12日 = **1872年3月20日**. Few came, because of the rumour that 「工女になると西洋人に生き血を飲まれる」 (foreigners would drink the girls' blood; really the red wine the French staff drank).
  - To scotch the rumour, 尾高惇忠 sent his own daughter 勇 (Yū) in as the first worker.
  - The mill opened on 明治5年10月4日 = **1872年11月4日**, with about 210 women at first and **404** by January 1873, mostly daughters of former samurai.
  - It had 300 reeling basins, then among the largest mills in the world.
  - Conditions: Sundays off, 10-day holidays at New Year and in summer, roughly an 8-hour day, free food, lodging and medical care, and **uniforms issued**. Workers were graded in 8 ranks from 1873.
  - Silk reeled by first-rank women including 尾高勇 won a prize at the Vienna Exposition in May 1873.
  - The mill was sold to the 三井 family in 1893.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/富岡製糸場

### Cotton spinning and the factory workforce
- **大阪紡績会社**: founded in **May 1882** on 渋沢栄一's initiative, mainly with money from former court nobles and daimyo (華族), with a capital of 250,000 yen. It opened in **1883** with English-trained 山辺丈夫 as works manager, **10,500 spindles**, steam power and **fewer than 300 workers**. It was then the largest mill in Japan, and ran **two shifts, day and night, under electric light**, which started the spinning boom of the late 1880s. https://kotobank.jp/word/大阪紡績会社-822075
- Spinning employed more than **50,000** workers by 1897, and in 1900 accounted for **16%** of all workers in factories with 10 or more employees. Ring spindles, night shifts of young women and very low wages pushed yarn exports above yarn imports in **1897**. https://kotobank.jp/word/紡績業-869003
- From 1886 to 1897 the value of yarn output rose from 12 million to 176 million yen. In 1886, 62% of the yarn used in Japan was imported. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_era
- **Female share of the factory workforce**: Nipponica (伍賀一道) says women made up **more than 60%** of all factory workers in the period of the industrial revolution (from the Sino-Japanese War to the Russo-Japanese War). Textile workers were 64.4% of all factory workers in 1894 and 64.7% in 1913. Hours ran 13–14, and up to 17–18. Men outnumbered women among factory workers only from **1933**. https://kotobank.jp/word/女性労働-159973
- 『**職工事情**』 (published by the Ministry of Agriculture and Commerce, **March 1903**, 5 vols.): the investigation was set up in 1900 under 窪田静太郎, with 横山源之助 among the contributors. It covered spinning, reeling and weaving plus 13 other industries. It describes Suwa reeling hours in 1901 as 「決シテ十三四時間ヲ降ルコトナク長キハ十七八時間ニ達セル」 (never less than 13–14 hours, and as long as 17–18). Its appendices record recruitment, kidnapping and abuse of factory girls. https://kotobank.jp/word/職工事情-80501 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/生糸-49853
- The **工場法** (Factory Act) was passed in 1911 and took effect in 1916. It applied to factories with 15 or more workers, banned work under age 12, and limited women and under-15s to 12 hours. Night work by shift crews was allowed for 15 more years. https://kotobank.jp/word/女性労働-159973

### Zaibatsu
- In **1880** the 工場払下概則 (rules for selling off government factories) was issued. Loss-making state enterprises were sold, very cheaply, to favoured "political merchants" (政商). Through these sales **三井 got the 三池炭鉱 coal mine** and **三菱 got the 高島炭鉱 mine and the 長崎造船所 shipyard**. https://kotobank.jp/word/官営事業払下げ-2129904 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/財閥-68169
- From the mid-Meiji period people stopped calling these families "政商" and called them "富豪" (millionaires). They became "財閥" only once they set up holding companies:
  - 三井合名会社 (1909–11), owned by the 11 Mitsui family houses;
  - 三菱 (1917–20); 住友 (1921).
  - Each family had a house constitution (家憲) restricting ownership to the family.
  https://kotobank.jp/word/財閥-68169

- **東京市** (Tokyo City, created 1889年5月1日 from the 15 wards), resident population at 31 December of each year:
  - 1889: **1,389,684**
  - 1890: **1,155,290**
  - 1898: 1,440,121
  - 1903: **1,818,655**
  - 1908: **2,186,079**
  - 1913: 2,050,126
  - 1920 census: 2,173,201
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/国勢調査以前の日本の人口統計
- The city's population passed 2 million for the first time in 1906, peaked at about 2.19 million in 1908, and fell to about 2.05 million in 1913 as growth shifted to the suburbs. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東京市

## 5. Wars and empire

### Sino-Japanese War (日清戦争) and Taiwan
- Fighting from **1894年7月25日** (the naval battle at 豊島沖); war formally declared **8月1日**; peace treaty signed **1895年4月17日** at 春帆楼, Shimonoseki.
  - Japan gained Korea's "independence" from Qing, Taiwan, the Penghu Islands and the Liaodong Peninsula, plus an indemnity of **2億両** (200 million taels; 1 tael = 37 g of silver).
  - The war cost **250 million yen**, mostly paid with bonds. The indemnity actually received, **364 million yen**, went mainly to arms.
  - 李鴻章 was shot by a Japanese assailant on 3月24日 during the negotiations.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日清戦争
- **Triple Intervention (三国干渉)**: on **1895年4月23日** the Russian, German and French ministers called at the Foreign Ministry and "advised" Japan to return Liaodong. 外相 陸奥 was ill at Maiko, so vice-minister 林董 received them. The retrocession treaty was signed in Beijing on **11月8日**, for **30 million taels (46.65 million yen)**. The press began writing of 臥薪嘗胆 ("sleeping on brushwood and tasting gall", nursing a grievance for revenge). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/三国干渉
- **Conquest of Taiwan (台湾征服戦争 / 乙未戦争)**, **1895年5月29日 – 11月18日**.
  - Japan lost **333 killed in action and 6,903 dead of disease**; Taiwanese casualties were about **14,000**.
  - 北白川宮能久親王, an imperial prince, commanded the Imperial Guards Division and **died of illness at Tainan on 10月28日**; a commemorative stamp with his portrait was issued on 1896年8月1日.
  - The 台湾総督府 inauguration ceremony was held in Taipei on 6月17日.
  - The 台湾民主国 leader 劉永福 fled on 10月19日.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/台湾征服戦争

### Russo-Japanese War (日露戦争) and the Hibiya riot
- The first fighting came on **1904年2月8日** at Chemulpo and Port Arthur; war was declared on **2月10日**. Peace was signed at Portsmouth on **1905年9月5日**.
  - Japan mobilised about **1.08 million** men, 490,000 of them in the field.
  - Japanese dead: **88,429**, of whom 55,655 were killed in action or died of wounds.
  - Official war expenditure: **1,826.29 million yen**.
  - 高橋是清 raised foreign loans in six issues from 1904 to 1907, totalling **£130 million** (just under 1.3 billion yen).
  - On 3月15日 1904, 松方 and 井上馨 founded the 帝国軍人援護会 (Imperial Soldiers' Relief Association).
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日露戦争
- **日比谷焼打事件** (Hibiya incendiary riot), **1905年9月5日–7日**.
  - It began at a rally against the Portsmouth treaty in Hibiya Park, chaired by **河野広中**, the Fukushima defendant of 1882.
  - The public had expected an indemnity of about 5 billion yen and all of Sakhalin. Japan got no indemnity and only southern Sakhalin.
  - Rioters attacked the Home Minister's residence, the government newspaper 国民新聞 and police boxes.
  - Martial law was declared on **9月6日**, and the Home Minister was given power to suspend newspapers.
  - Casualties: **17 dead**. Injured: about 500 (ja.wikipedia); over 450 police and 48 firemen plus civilians (en.wikipedia).
  - More than 2,000 people were arrested and **87 convicted**. About 70% of Tokyo's police boxes were destroyed.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日比谷焼打事件 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibiya_incendiary_incident
- **Annexation of Korea**: the treaty was signed in Seoul on **1910年8月22日** by Resident-General 寺内正毅 and Prime Minister 李完用, and promulgated and in force on **8月29日**. The 朝鮮総督府 (Government-General of Korea) replaced the Residency-General. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/韓国併合

### War and gender
- **Red Cross**: 佐野常民 and 大給恒 founded the 博愛社 during the Satsuma Rebellion; Nipponica-based Wikipedia gives **1877年5月1日** as the founding date, and permission was formally notified on 6月18日. Japan joined the Geneva Convention in 1886, and on **1887年5月20日** the society was renamed the 日本赤十字社.
  - It sent its first wartime relief teams in the Sino-Japanese War, about **1,400** people.
  - In the Russo-Japanese War it sent **152 teams, about 5,170 relief workers**, to the front, hospital ships and military hospitals.
  - The 1901 日本赤十字社条例 (an imperial ordinance) authorised it to assist the army's and navy's wartime medical services, within limits set by the two ministers.
  - The army bandsman 加藤義清 wrote the song 『婦人従軍歌』 ("Women Go to War") in one night, after seeing Red Cross nurses leave for the front.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本赤十字社
  - By the start of the Russo-Japanese War it was the largest Red Cross society in the world, with over a million members. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Red_Cross_Society
  - In June 1884 aristocratic wives held Japan's first charity bazaar (婦人慈善会) at the 鹿鳴館, selling 3,000 items; the proceeds founded Japan's first nurse-training school in 1885. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- **愛国婦人会** (Patriotic Women's Association): founded on **1901年2月24日** (Wikipedia flags this date as needing a source) by **奥村五百子**. She had visited the front during the Boxer War with a Higashi Honganji mission, and the association was backed by 近衛篤麿 and the army and navy.
  - Its statutory purpose was relief for the families of the war dead and for disabled soldiers (戦死者遺族・廃兵の救護).
  - First president: 岩倉久子. Princess 閑院宮載仁親王妃智恵子 became patron (総裁) in 1903.
  - Membership rose to **460,000 in 1905** (when it opened to ordinary women), **700,000 in 1907** and 3.11 million in 1937. It is described as Japan's first nationwide women's organisation.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/愛国婦人会
  - 奥村五百子 (弘化2年5月3日 = **1845年6月7日** – 1907年2月7日) was the daughter of a Higashi Honganji priest. In **1862 she carried a secret message to Chōshū dressed as a man (男装)**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/奥村五百子
- **Women's anti-war voices**: 与謝野晶子 published 『君死にたまふことなかれ』 ("Brother, do not give your life") in 『明星』 in **September 1904**, and argued over it with 大町桂月. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/与謝野晶子 . 大塚楠緒子 published 「お百度詣」, a wife's prayer for her husband at the front, in 『太陽』 in January 1905. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大塚楠緒子
- Sekai daihyakka: the wars of 1894–95, 1900 and 1904–05 came about every five years, and each pressed women harder to serve the state. Good-wife-wise-mother schooling confined women to the home and so tended to make them indifferent to the nation. From around the Russo-Japanese War it was therefore reinforced from a nationalist and militarist angle. https://kotobank.jp/word/良妻賢母-149966

## 6. Socialism and anarchism

- **平民社** (Society of Commoners): founded on **1903年10月27日** by 幸徳秋水 and 堺利彦 after the 『萬朝報』 newspaper switched to supporting war with Russia.
  - Its weekly 『**平民新聞**』 ran from **no. 1 (1903年11月15日) to no. 64 (1905年1月29日)**. Its manifesto proclaimed 平民主義・社会主義・平和主義.
  - On **1904年3月13日** it printed the address 「与露国社会党書」 to the Russian socialists.
  - It had **1,400** direct subscribers in July 1904.
  - **No. 53 (1904年11月13日)** carried the first Japanese translation of the *Communist Manifesto*, made by 堺 and 幸徳 from the English.
  - The society dissolved on **1905年10月9日**. It was revived to publish the daily 『日刊平民新聞』 of the 日本社会党 from 1907年1月15日 to 4月14日.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/平民社 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/平民新聞-129042
- **堺為子** (born 延岡為子, 明治5年5月19日; I compute about 24 June 1872, ±1 day – 1959): she joined the 平民社 household in 1904 to do the cooking and chores, married 堺利彦 in 1905, and took part in the petition campaign to amend Article 5 of the 治安警察法. https://kotobank.jp/word/堺為子-1077216
- **西川文子** (1882年4月2日 – 1960): first married 松岡荒村, who died in 1904. With 今井歌子 and 川村春子 of the 平民社 she worked on the petitions to amend Article 5, then married 西川光二郎 in 1905. In 1913 she founded the 新真婦人会. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/西川文子
- **The legal bar**:
  - The 1890 集会及政社法 banned women from attending or organising political meetings and from joining political associations.
  - The **治安警察法** of **1900年3月10日**, Article 5, listed 「女子」 among those barred from political associations, and barred women and minors from attending or organising political meetings.
  - Women of the 平民社 led petitions to amend it in late Meiji. Only the meeting ban was lifted, in **1922**.
  https://kotobank.jp/word/集会及政社法-1335370 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/治安警察法-95575 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/女性参政権-895295
- **福田英子** (旧姓 景山; 慶応元年10月5日 = **1865年11月22日** – 1927):
  - Converted to women's rights by 岸田俊子's 1882 Okayama speech, she opened the private girls' school 蒸紅学舎 with her mother in 1883; it was closed under the 集会条例.
  - She was jailed for the 1885 大阪事件. After her release she had a child with 大井憲太郎.
  - Her autobiography 『妾の半生涯』 appeared in October 1904.
  - She founded 『**世界婦人**』 in **1907**, with contributions from 安部磯雄, 木下尚江 and 幸徳 and reports on foreign suffrage movements. It ended with **no. 38 in July 1909**.
  - When 内村鑑三 barred her from his Bible class, she answered in no. 6 (1907年3月15日).
  - She was called 「東洋のジャンヌ・ダルク」, the Joan of Arc of the East.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/福田英子 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/福田英子-124086 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/世界婦人-1351000
- **赤旗事件** (Red Flag Incident), **1908年6月22日**: after a welcome party at the 錦輝館 in Kanda for 山口孤剣 (義三) on his release from prison, 大杉栄, 荒畑寒村 and others waved red flags with 「無政府共産」 and 「無政府」 sewn on in white. **14 people were arrested, including 管野スガ and three other women**, as well as 堺 and 山川均, who had been trying to calm things down. Sentences: 大杉 2 years 6 months; 堺 and 山川 2 years; 荒畑 1 year 6 months. The 西園寺 cabinet fell in July. https://kotobank.jp/word/赤旗事件-24401
- **大逆事件** (High Treason Incident), also called 幸徳事件:
  - 宮下太吉 made bombs at Akashina in Nagano and tested one in November 1909. 管野, 新村忠雄 and 古河力作 joined his plan to kill the emperor; 幸徳 was lukewarm.
  - Arrests: 宮下 on **1910年5月25日**, 幸徳 on **6月1日** at Yugawara. Hundreds of socialists nationwide were questioned, and **26** were indicted.
  - The trial was a closed, single-instance hearing before the Supreme Court (大審院) from **12月10日**, with no witnesses called.
  - Sentence on **1911年1月18日**: **24 death sentences**, 2 prison terms. Next day 12 were commuted to life by imperial grace.
  - **11** were hanged on **1月24日** and **管野** alone on **1月25日**.
  - 管野's prison memoir says the case was 「検事の手によって作られた陰謀」, a plot made by the prosecutors.
  - The following years were called the 「冬の時代」 ("winter years") of socialism.
  https://kotobank.jp/word/大逆事件-90983
- **管野スガ** (明治14年6月7日, 1881 – 1911年1月25日), the only woman among the defendants: journalist at the 牟婁新報 and the 『毎日電報』; married 荒畑寒村, later lived with 幸徳. She was acquitted after the Red Flag Incident. https://kotobank.jp/word/管野すが-471184

## 7. The Meiji emperor's image

- **Birth and marriage**: 睦仁 was born on 嘉永5年9月22日 = **1852年11月3日** at the 中山 residence in Kyoto, to the 権典侍 (junior lady-in-waiting) 中山慶子. He married 一条美子 (later 昭憲皇太后) on 明治元年12月28日 = **1869年2月9日**. He died on **1912年7月30日** at 0:43 a.m. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明治天皇
- **The father image**: see the 家族国家観 entry in section 3 (emperor as father, subjects as 赤子). Sekai daihyakka (御真影 entry, quoted under 巡幸): in early Meiji the imperial tours (巡幸) "exposed the living emperor to people's eyes to strengthen power"; the portrait later replaced the tours. https://kotobank.jp/word/巡幸-529834 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/家族国家観-1290065
- **The portrait (御真影)**:
  - A photograph of the emperor by 内田九一 dates from 1873. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Meiji
  - In 1873 the Nara governor 四条隆平 asked for a portrait to hang in his prefectural office, and other prefectures followed. Private copying led to bans on selling the photographs (April 1874, February 1875).
  - In **September 1887** the first portrait for a prefectural school went to the Okinawa Normal School. On **1889年12月19日** distribution was extended to higher primary schools.
  - Sales of imperial portrait paintings were permitted from 1891, and of imperial photographs from 1898. The page writes this year as 「1898年(明治32年)」, but 1898 is 明治31; the year is uncertain.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/御真影
  - In **1888**, because the emperor refused to be photographed again, the grand chamberlain 徳大寺実則 had Edoardo Chiossone sketch him from behind a screen. Chiossone drew two conté portraits (one in uniform, one in civilian dress). These were photographed and became the official image, often mistaken for a photograph. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edoardo_Chiossone
  - In **1898**, after fire destroyed the portrait at the 上田 higher primary school in Nagano, the principal **久米由太郎** (father of the novelist 久米正雄) committed seppuku. One historian has argued that local enmity was the real cause. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/御真影
- **The white horse 金華山**: the ja.wikipedia article on the 南部馬 (Nanbu horse) carries an image captioned 「御料馬金華山」, implying Kinkazan was a Nanbu-breed imperial horse. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/南部馬 . Nothing else about the horse could be verified this session; see "Uncertain".
- **Concubines and the Taishō emperor's birth**:
  - The emperor had **15 children** by five ladies-in-waiting: 葉室光子 (1853–73), 橋本夏子 (1856–73), **柳原愛子** (1855–1943), 千種任子 (1855–1944) and 園祥子 (1867–1947).
  - 葉室光子 bore the first son on 明治6年9月18日; he died the same day and she died four days later. 橋本夏子 died after bearing the first daughter.
  - Of the 15, only one son and four daughters grew up.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明治天皇
  - **柳原愛子** (安政2年4月16日 = 1855年5月31日, or 4月17日 = 6月1日, depending on the source) entered court service in 1870. She became 権典侍 in 1873 under the court name 早蕨典侍. Her daughter 薫子 (born 1875) and son 敬仁 (born 1877) died young. She bore **嘉仁親王 (the future Taishō emperor) on 明治12年（1879）8月31日**, became 典侍 in 1902, and was later styled 二位局. https://kotobank.jp/word/柳原愛子-22037 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/柳原愛子 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/大正天皇-18607
  - Officially, the emperor's own birth mother 中山慶子 was subordinate to his father's consort, who was called his 「嫡母」 (legal mother). In the same way the childless Empress 美子 was treated as Yoshihito's legal mother. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明治天皇 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/昭憲皇太后
- **The Crown Prince's wedding, 1900年5月10日**:
  - 嘉仁 married **九条節子** (later 貞明皇后; born 1884; fourth daughter of Prince 九条道孝) in the palace sanctuary (宮中賢所大前 / 宮中三殿). It was the first imperial wedding performed this way.
  - The press coverage made people want a "wedding before the gods". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/神前式
  - The 神宮奉斎会 at 日比谷大神宮 (Hibiya Daijingū, today's 東京大神宮) built a 神前結婚 ritual on the imperial model and staged a **model wedding on 1901年3月3日**. In 1902 it held what it called the first wedding of private persons by the new rite. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東京大神宮
  - Britannica: the 神前結婚 imitates the imperial wedding in the 賢所 and became general only after Meiji. https://kotobank.jp/word/神前結婚-82093
  - Both 嘉仁 and 節子 were children of concubines. They kept **no concubines**, following the monogamy of European royal houses. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/側室
- **Empress 美子 (昭憲皇太后)**:
  - Her first public appearance in Western dress was at the **華族女学校 (Peeresses' School) on 1886年7月30日**. Wikipedia says she thereafter wore only Western clothes, except to sleep.
  - On **1887年1月17日** she issued a memorandum (思召書) urging Western dress for women. It called present-day kimono a bad legacy of the civil wars and said Western dress was closer to ancient Japanese women's clothing. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/昭憲皇太后 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_Shōken
  - Wikipedia also attributes to her the view that a garment not divided into upper and lower parts restricts women's movement. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/昭憲皇太后
  - From November 1886 she accompanied the emperor to naval and army displays. She was the patron of the Red Cross. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_Shōken ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本赤十字社

## 8. Cultural debates

### The Rokumeikan (鹿鳴館) and the backlash against it
- The building was designed by Josiah Conder on 8,532 坪 of the former Satsuma residence at 内山下町. Construction began in 1880 and it was completed in July 1883, with 467 坪 of floor space.
  - **1883年11月28日**: the opening banquet for 1,200 guests, held on 井上馨's birthday.
  - **1886年11月3日**: the emperor's-birthday ball, the high point of the "鹿鳴館時代".
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- **The fancy-dress ball of 1887年4月20日** was held at the **Prime Minister's residence**, not the Rokumeikan. Its 350 guests included:
  - 伊藤 and his wife as Venetian nobles; their daughter 生子 as an Italian peasant girl;
  - 井上馨 as a 三河萬歳 street performer; 山県 as a 奇兵隊 soldier;
  - 鍋島直大 and his wife as 18th-century French aristocrats;
  - Albert Mosse as a Turk; 渋沢栄一 as a kabuki character.
  The ball went out in the name of Mrs 伊藤 (Umeko) but was really organised by the British minister Plunkett. Nationalists called it 「亡国の兆し」, a sign of national ruin, and 勝海舟 submitted a 21-article memorial on the evils of the time. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- The 華族令 (Peerage Ordinance) of 1884年7月7日 created the new peerage that was to carry out Western-style social life. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館
- The building became the 華族会館 from 1890 and was used as the House of Peers chamber after the 1891 Diet fire. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鹿鳴館 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/第1回帝国議会

### The "national essence" (国粋) reaction of 1888
- **政教社** was founded on **1888年4月3日** (the 神武天皇祭 holiday) by 11 men: 三宅雪嶺, 井上円了, 島地黙雷 and others from the 哲学館 circle, and 志賀重昂 and others from the 東京英語学校 circle, joined soon after by 杉浦重剛. They launched the journal 『**日本人**』 the same month; it ran from April 1888 to December 1906, then became 『日本及日本人』.
  - 志賀's doctrine of 「国粋保存」 (preserving the national essence) meant keeping the nation's religion, morals, arts, politics and productive systems, while digesting Western civilisation before adopting it. It was not rejection of everything foreign.
  - The group's first campaign exposed conditions in the 高島炭坑 coal mine.
  - The journal was frequently banned, and a substitute, 『亜細亜』, was published in its place.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/政教社 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本人_(雑誌)
- 陸羯南's newspaper 『**日本**』 first appeared on **1889年2月11日**, the day the constitution was promulgated, and ran until 1914年12月31日. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本_(新聞)

### The "women's question" in the press, 1885–1890
- 『**女学雑誌**』 ran from **1885年7月20日 to 1904年2月15日**: 526 numbers in 548 volumes, described as Japan's first real women's magazine.
  - Its founding aim was 「日本の婦女をしてその至るべきに至らしめんことを希図す」, to bring Japanese women to what they should become.
  - Price 5–10 銭; about 2,500 copies (December 1885). Many readers were men.
  - 近藤賢三 edited it until his sudden death in May 1886; then **巌本善治** took over.
  - Contributors included 中島湘煙 (岸田俊子), 若松賤子 (whose translation 『小公子』 ran in it), 清水豊子, 北村透谷 and 津田梅子.
  - From November 1890 清水豊子 was editor-in-chief and 荻野吟子 covered hygiene.
  - In March 1900 an issue was confiscated for printing 田中正造's 「鉱毒文学」.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/女学雑誌
- **清水紫琴** (清水豊子; born late 慶応3年12月, registered as January 1868 – 1933):
  - She married the people's-rights lawyer 岡崎晴正 in 1885 and left him in 1889 because he kept his former lover as a concubine.
  - She met 植木枝盛, a supporter of equal rights for women, in 1888. She became a reporter for 『女学雑誌』 in 1890 (Kotobank: editor-in-chief).
  - Her story 「こわれ指環」 ("The Broken Ring") appeared in 1891.
  - She bore 大井憲太郎's child out of wedlock; her brother adopted it.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/清水紫琴 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/清水紫琴-1081149
- Women were excluded from the vote by the 1888 local-government code and the 1889 election law, and from political meetings and associations by the 1890 law. Nipponica: after 1890, apart from **watching the Diet and petitioning**, women had no political rights. https://kotobank.jp/word/集会及政社法-1335370 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/女性参政権-895295 . 楠瀬喜多 watched the House of Representatives four times through 河野広中's connections. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/楠瀬喜多

### The turn to "good wife, wise mother" (良妻賢母) in the 1890s
- Sekai daihyakka (千野陽一):
  - During the civilisation-and-enlightenment years, equal personhood of the sexes and common general education were preached.
  - From the 明治10年代 (1877–86), demands for gender-specific education rose.
  - By the 明治20年代 (1887–96), the Confucian 『女大学』 view of women predominated.
  - The Rescript on Education (1890) and the patriarchal family books of the Civil Code (1898) made good-wife-wise-mother education the settled policy.
  https://kotobank.jp/word/良妻賢母-149966
- The earliest dictionary example of the phrase is from 徳富蘆花's 『思出の記』 (1900–01). https://kotobank.jp/word/良妻賢母-149966
- The **高等女学校令** (Girls' High School Order, 明治32年勅令第31号) was issued on **1899年2月7日**, printed in the 官報 on 2月8日, and took effect on **4月1日**. Girls' high schools were to have 4-year courses (3 or 5 allowed), taking girls from age 12. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/高等女学校令 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/高等女学校令-2129938

## Telling details

- **Ōkuma's defeat paid for the Diet.** The Diet was promised on 1881年10月12日, the day after 大隈 was purged. The coup waited until the emperor came home from his Tōhoku–Hokkaido tour. https://kotobank.jp/word/明治十四年の政変-3172769
- **Unequal labour levies, 1882.** In Aizu, women aged 15–60 owed the same road labour as men, but could buy themselves off for 10 銭 a day against 15 銭 for men. https://kotobank.jp/word/福島事件-124004
- **The Kabasan banners** read 「自由之魁」 ("vanguard of liberty"), 「圧制政府転覆」 ("overthrow the tyrannical government") and 「一死以報国」 ("repay the nation with one death"). The rebels were hanged as robbers, not as political offenders. https://kotobank.jp/word/加波山事件-46064
- **The Chichibu rebels organised themselves as an army,** with a 総理, battalions, platoons and military rules. Their last column was destroyed on the slopes of Yatsugatake on 1884年11月9日. https://kotobank.jp/word/秩父事件-96257
- **"バカイフナ".** 板垣's alleged telegram to the jailed 星亨, who had protested the party's dissolution. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/自由党_(日本_1881-1884)
- **"Daughters in boxes".** 岸田俊子 was arrested in Ōtsu on 1883年10月12日 for a speech titled 「箱入娘」, treated by the police as a political speech. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/中島湘煙 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/岸田俊子-155442
- **The feminist's husband ran the first Diet.** The first Speaker of the House of Representatives (1890) was 中島信行, husband of the feminist orator 岸田俊子. The Vice-Speaker was 津田真道, who in 1875 had written against equal rights for husband and wife and against votes for women. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/第1回帝国議会 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明六雑誌
- **Women voted in Kōchi before anyone in Japan.** The 上町 assembly gave female household heads the vote after September 1880, until Tokyo took the power back in 1884. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/楠瀬喜多
- **The draft constitution was stolen.** On 1887年8月6日, while 伊藤's team was in Yokohama, thieves took the bag holding the draft from the 東屋 inn. It turned up in a nearby field with only the valuables gone, and the work moved to the 夏島 villa. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/伊東巳代治

- **Mori backed away.** "I said husband and wife were equal; I never argued equal rights." https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明六雑誌

- **The empress out of kimono.** She appeared in Western dress at a girls' school (the Peeresses' School, 30 July 1886) before most of the court did, and in 1887 issued a memorandum condemning kimono for women. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/昭憲皇太后

23. **The Bank of Japan, silver notes and the gold standard.** **CONFIRMED.**

      - 吉原重俊 appointed governor on 10月6日.
      - Licence granted on 10月9日, with a capital of 10 million yen, half from the government.

## Additional facts found while verifying

- **The Meiji emperor's time of death.** The official time is 1912年7月30日 at 0:43 a.m. According to the 宮内書記官 栗原広太, he actually died at 22:43 on 7月29日. The time was moved two hours later so that the 践祚 could take place on the official day of death, since only about an hour of 7月29日 remained. Sekai daihyakka and 日本人名大辞典 give 7月29日. Section 7's "died 1912年7月30日 at 0:43 a.m." is the official time, not the actual one. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明治天皇 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/明治天皇
- **Concubines in law after 1882.** The two sources describe the change differently, but they are compatible:
  - Sekai daihyakka (Kotobank 妾): the old Penal Code, in force from 1882, ended concubines' standing as spouses, and their registration was abolished soon afterwards.
  - ja.wiki 妾: concubines registered before 1882 kept their status, and the word 妾 left the registers only with the 1898 戸籍法.
  - Read together: no new concubines were entered as kin after 1882, but existing entries lasted until 1898.
  - The old Penal Code itself was 1880年7月17日 太政官布告第36号.

  https://kotobank.jp/word/妾-78787 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/妾
- **Motive for 森有礼's murder.** en.wiki says 西野 was outraged by Mori's alleged disrespect at Ise Shrine two years earlier: not removing his shoes, and lifting a sacred curtain with his stick. These are allegations, and only en.wiki gives them, but this partly answers the "Rumours not checked" item. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mori_Arinori
- **Signatures in 1880.** ja.wiki 国会期成同盟 gives about 87,000 signatures from 114 delegates of 2府22県 at the March congress. The second congress (Tokyo, 1880年11月10日) had 64 delegates and about 130,000 signatures. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/国会期成同盟
- **The 1884 revision of the 区町村会法.** Voters had to be men aged 20 or over, resident in the locality and paying land tax there; candidates had to be men aged 25 or over (Arts. 9–10). Local rules were now set by the governor. https://ja.wikisource.org/wiki/區町村會法
- **The Hokkaido sale scandal, timeline.** The cabinet request went in on 7月21日 1881. The 『東京横浜毎日新聞』 exposed it on 7月26日, the emperor sanctioned it on 7月30日, the day he left on his tour, and it was made public on 8月1日. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/明治十四年の政変
- **立憲改進党 deputy leader.** The 副総理 was **河野敏鎌**, not 河野広中. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/立憲改進党
- **自由党 in 1884.** At its congress of 1884年3月13日 the party gave 板垣 special powers to act on his own, and set up the 文武館 (later named 有一館). Its first 常議員 were 馬場辰猪, 末広重恭, 後藤象二郎 and 竹内綱. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/自由党_(日本_1881-1884)
- **The 保安条例 after 1887.** It was used again on 1891年1月13日, during the first Diet, against 54 壮士 (political toughs), and on 2月11日 against 8 more. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/保安条例
- **Tomioka's workforce.** By 1893 its women workers had come from 3府37県 and from Hokkaido. At the start, four French women instructors taught the work (Nipponica). https://kotobank.jp/word/富岡製糸場-105820
- **Gold content of the yen.** It was 1.5 g of gold under the 1871 新貨条例 and 2分 (0.75 g) under the 1897 貨幣法, so the gold content was halved, and old gold coins circulated at twice face value. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/貨幣法
- **Chichibu casualties.** Five police officers were killed in the rising. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/秩父事件
