## 1. Rabbit bubble (兎バブル), omoto boom, and a possible rose bubble

**What is true**
- **Timeline.** The rabbit craze began c. 明治4年 (1871) and took off in spring 明治5年 (1872), when imported long-eared rabbits came into fashion. It peaked in 明治6年 (1873).
  - Owners met at "兎会", held at teahouses, to compete on price and coat colour.
  - The craze came in a sequence: pigs (1870–71), then sheep (1872, mainly Kyoto), then rabbits. The *North-China Herald* covered all three.
- **Prices quoted in sources I opened:**
  - 明治6年3–4月 rumour-book (『諸聞集』): spotted bucks 15両, does 50–60両, pregnant does 70–80両.
  - A 明治5年 press item reports a 600円 rabbit. Another reads "今年ハマタ兎ノ価一疋五十円ニ騰レリ".
  - April 1873 (『新聞雑誌』): a son killed his father after the father refused 150円 for a rabbit and held out for 200円; the rabbit then died overnight.
  - *North-China Herald*, 17 May 1873: $180 asked for a pair. *The Times*, Aug. 1873: $150.
  - 赤田1997, via ja.wp: "更紗兎" stud bucks sold for 200–600円, with stud fees of 2–3円.
  - For scale: brown rice in Tokyo in 1873 cost 4円80銭 per 石.
- **Regulation was by Tokyo Prefecture (東京府), not the national government.**
  - Osaka-fu banned rabbit markets and meetings in 明治5年7月 (July 1872).
  - 東京府達第4号, 明治6年1月18日 (18 Jan 1873), banned 兎会 auctions.
  - **The rabbit tax was 東京府達第144号 of 明治6年12月7日 (7 Dec 1873):**
    - owners register with the 区扱所;
    - **1円 per rabbit per month**;
    - 2円 fine per unregistered rabbit;
    - mass auctions stay banned.
    - The covering report says the revenue would fund the new 養育院 (the Tokyo poorhouse).
  - Revised by 甲第25号, 明治9年5月9日 (in force 15 May 1876): concealed rabbits confiscated, back-tax charged, and informers paid half of the fine.
  - Abolished from 30 June 1879 (明治12年6月4日 布達).
- **The crash.** Prices fell at once after the 7 Dec 1873 notice.
  - 『郵便報知新聞』, 18 Dec 1873, ran a piece headed "兎のいましめ 一朝にして大下落": kits (コロ) at **2銭**, pregnant does at **5銭**.
  - Owners killed rabbits, threw them in rivers, hid them under floors, or sold them outside Tokyo.
  - A contemporary dialogue-pamphlet has a ruined owner complain that prices fell from 100両 to 4–5両.
  - The craze faded by Feb–Mar 1880.
- **Omoto (万年青).** A Meiji boom is real. The evidence:
  - A cluster of omoto books, 1877–1885: 『新撰萬年青圖解』 1877; 『萬年青見競鑑』 1881.2; 『小萬年青見立鏡』 1882; 『萬年青培養法』 1883; 『萬年青名鑑』 1884; 篠常五郎『萬年青培養秘録』『万年青図譜』 1885.
  - Fukui newspaper items: 「万年青（おもと） 大造値上がり」 (『福井新聞』297号, 5 Nov 1882, p.3) and 「萬年青の流行」 (6 Dec 1882). A 14 Mar 1884 item contrasts songbird-keeping with "兎や万年青の様に激しき価格".
  - ja.wp claims a Kyoto-centred boom around 明治10年 with "一鉢千円". It gives no source, so that detail is UNVERIFIED.
  - I found no Meiji ban on omoto. The bans were in the Edo period, under the Tenpō reforms.
- **Roses: no evidence of a Meiji rose fad or price bubble.** No article, edict or catalogue I opened suggests one. There is steady elite and horticultural interest instead (see item 2). The 1882–84 Fukui index has omoto and rabbits as the bywords for speculative prices, not roses.

**Verdicts**
- Rabbit bubble 1872–73: **TRUE**, with corrections:
  - it started in 1871;
  - the tax was a 東京府 measure of 7 Dec 1873, at 1円 per head per month;
  - the crash followed the tax at once;
  - the tax was abolished in 1879.
- Omoto bubble in Meiji: **PARTLY** (a boom c.1877–85 is attested; ja.wp's "Kyoto, 1,000 yen a pot" is unsourced).
- Rose fad or bubble: **UNVERIFIED / no evidence found.** Treat it as invented if used.

## 2. Western roses in Meiji

**What is true**
- **Before Meiji.** Chinese roses (コウシンバラ *R. chinensis*, モッコウバラ) were already grown in Edo gardens. Native species include ノイバラ, テリハノイバラ and ハマナス.
- **Arrival of Western roses and rose literature.**
  - The earliest Japanese rose manuals are two translations dated **明治8年 (1875)**:
    - 『ヘンデルソン氏薔薇培養法』, after Peter Henderson, tr. 水品梅処, 開物社, 1875.6 / 明8.7;
    - 『薔薇栽培法 図入』, by "Samuel Panson" (almost certainly Samuel B. Parsons of *Parsons on the Rose*), tr. 安井真八郎, 共由社, 明8.9.
  - The hybrid tea 'La France' (1867) was known in Meiji–Taishō Japan as **天地開**.
  - ja.wp says the government grew it at "青山官制農園（現東大農学部）", but gives no source. The real early Aoyama site was the 開拓使青山試験場 (Hokkaido Development Commission farm), whose greenhouse dates from 明治4年 (per the Shinjuku Gyoen history page). Treat the La France detail as UNVERIFIED.
- **Rose nurseries.** Firm evidence is the **variety catalogues of 1889–90**:
  - 『薔薇種類』, 長春園, 1889.4;
  - 『薔薇名集』, 開花園, 1889.5;
  - 『薔薇名一覧』, ばら新, 1890.3;
  - 『長春園薔薇類集』, 朝倉豊義, [1890].
  - So by 1889 there were named nurseries (長春園, 開花園, ばら新) selling roses by variety name. Their locations were not checked.
  - Later manuals: 賀集久太郎『薔薇栽培新書』 (朝陽園, 明35.7/1902); 井関十二郎『最新薔薇栽培法』 (明36/1903); 柚木梶雄『薔薇の栽培』 (1905); 野村買笑『薔薇栽培法』 (1908).
  - ja.wp names 安行 (Kawaguchi) and 宝塚・山本 as grafting centres. No date is given.
- **First rose society and first rose show: UNVERIFIED for the Meiji period.**
  - The only dated show in my sources is postwar: a 1948 rose exhibition in Ginza, followed by Yokohama in 1949.
  - I found no Meiji 薔薇会 or 品評会 record.
- **Shinjuku.**
  - 内藤新宿試験場 was founded in 明治5年 (1872). It became 新宿植物御苑 under the Imperial Household Ministry in 明治12年 (1879). It reopened as **新宿御苑** in May 明治39年 (1906), after Henri Martinet's redesign (commissioned in 1900, works 1901–06).
  - The official history says **Western ornamental floriculture had "hardly developed" up to the Meiji 10s**. Four greenhouses for orchids and similar plants came in 1897.
  - It does not mention roses. A Meiji-era rose collection at Shinjuku is therefore UNVERIFIED.
  - The French formal garden, which has rose beds today, dates from 1906; I could not date when roses were planted in it.

## 3. 『新体詩抄』 (Shintaishi-shō)

**What is true**
- **Compilers:** 外山正一 (as ヽ山仙士), 矢田部良吉 (尚今居士), 井上哲次郎 (巽軒居士).
- **Publication:** 丸屋善七 (now Maruzen), 初編.
  - Licence July 1882. The title page reads "明治十五年七月刊行".
  - It actually came out in **August 1882 (明治15年8月)**: the publisher's advertisement gives 8月16日, and the colophon says 8月.
  - Second printing December 1884. The planned second volume never appeared.
- **Contents:**
  - three prefaces (by Inoue, Yatabe and Toyama), plus 凡例;
  - 19 poems: **14 translations and 5 original poems**;
  - an afterword by 久米幹文 (水屋主人).
- **Translations** include:
  - Gray's Elegy (「グレー氏墳上感懐の詩」, Yatabe);
  - Tennyson's "Charge of the Light Brigade" (Toyama);
  - Longfellow's "Psalm of Life", rendered twice: Toyama's version, and Inoue's 「玉の緒の歌（一名人生の詩）」, which is his only piece;
  - Campbell, Kingsley, Charles d'Orléans;
  - Shakespeare: Wolsey, from *Henry VIII*; *Henry IV*; *Hamlet* soliloquies by both Yatabe and Toyama.
- **The five originals:** 「抜刀隊」 (Toyama), 「勧学の歌」, 「鎌倉の大仏に詣でゝ感あり」, 「春夏秋冬」 (all Yatabe), and **「社会学の原理に題す」 (Toyama)**.
- **Darwinism.**
  - 「社会学の原理に題す」 is a versification of Spencer's *Principles of Sociology*: the universe and society governed by 規律 (law) and progressive development.
  - The OCR of the 1882 edition shows "スペンセル", "アリストートル、ニウトン", a fragmented "ダ…ウ井ン", and 猿 in the poem. It is a poem on Spencerian evolution that names Darwin.
  - Yatabe's 大仏 poem also uses 「規律に由りて進化」.
  - "A poem on Darwinism" is therefore fair, but the poem is about Spencer.
- **Reception.** 「抜刀隊」 became the march-song "我は官軍我が敵は" (Kunikida Doppo, preface to 『独歩吟』). Popular imitation followed: 竹内節編『新体詩歌』 from 10 Oct 1882, five parts.

**Sources opened**
- https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新体詩抄 (cites 西田直敏『『新体詩抄』研究と資料』1994)
- NDL digital copy https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/876377 (1882 edition), OCR via https://lab.ndl.go.jp/dl/api/book/fulltext-json/876377

**Verdict: TRUE.** Date: August 1882 (title page says July). Darwin is present, framed through Spencer.

## 4. Darwinism in Japan: Morse 1877, Katō 1882, reception

**What is true**
- **Morse.**
  - Arrived 18 June 1877 (明治10年6月18日).
  - Became professor of zoology and physiology at Tokyo University in July 1877.
  - ja.wp: began teaching 12 Sept; **lectured on evolution at the university on 24 Sept 1877**; gave public lectures on it in October 1877.
  - In his stay he gave serial lectures 『進化論』 (4 lectures), 『動物変進論』 (3) and 『動物変遷論』 (9), and was a regular at 江木学校講談会.
  - en.wp (citing Shimao, "Darwinism in Japan 1877–1927", *Annals of Science* 1981) calls him the first to bring Darwin's theory to Japan.
- **『動物進化論』.**
  - Translated by 石川千代松 from students' notes of Morse's lectures "in the 東京大学講義堂".
  - Preface by 矢田部良吉; Ishikawa's 例言 dated 明治16年4月.
  - Licensed 明治16年2月8日; **published 明治16年4月28日 (28 Apr 1883)** by 万巻書房 (OCR-checked colophon).
  - ja.wp's 石川千代松 article dates the lectures to 1879, which conflicts with 1877. The 1883 book says only "往年".
- **Katō Hiroyuki, 『人権新説』.**
  - Licensed **明治15年9月13日**; **published October 1882 (明治15年10月)**; 谷山楼藏版 (OCR-checked).
  - He had already attacked natural-rights theory in a lecture at 青松寺 in November 1879.
  - The book argues from Darwin (spelled 達賓) and Haeckel that rights arise from struggle. It coins the **「優勝劣敗ノ定規」** ("the law that the superior win and the inferior lose").
  - Exact wording (OCR-checked): "余ハ此一大定規ヲ稱シテ優勝劣敗ノ定規ト云ハント欲ス盖シ宇宙ハ宛カモ一大修羅場ナリ".
  - Chapter 1 is titled 「天賦人權ノ妄想ニ出ル所以ヲ論ス」.
- **Reception: an immediate pamphlet war.**
  - 矢野文雄（竜渓）『人権新説駁論』 (報知社, Dec 1882)
  - 石川正美編『人権新説駁撃新論』 (Dec 1882)
  - 梶木甚三郎編『人権新説駁論集』 (Dec 1882)
  - 植木枝盛『天賦人権弁』 (Jan 1883)
  - 馬場辰猪『天賦人権論』 (Jan 1883)
  - 中村尚樹編『人権新説駁論集』 (Jan 1883)
  - By the 1880s, 進化 / 進化主義 / 優勝劣敗 / 生存競争 / 自然淘汰 were all in print. ja.wp's 社会進化論 article notes that the doctrine was read as "progressive" and had some resonance even inside the 民権 movement.

**Sources opened**
- https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/エドワード・S・モース , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_S._Morse , https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/石川千代松
- https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/832826 (動物進化論, OCR)
- https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/加藤弘之 , https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/798590 (人権新説, OCR)
- https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/社会進化論

**Verdict: TRUE.** Morse lectured on evolution in autumn 1877; Katō's book is October 1882; reception was immediate.

## 5. Tsubouchi Shōyō: 『小説神髄』 and 『当世書生気質』

**What is true**
- **『小説神髄』.**
  - Issued in **9 instalment fascicles by 松月堂, from September 1885 (明治18年9月) to April 1886 (明治19年4月)**.
  - Three fascicles appeared by the end of Sept 1885. It then paused, resumed in March 1886, and was complete in April 1886.
  - Rebound as two volumes (上下) in May 1886.
  - Planned at 7 fascicles, then advertised as 10. The type had been set by the failed 東京稗史出版社, whose name stays in the page margins.
  - An abstract, 「小説文体」, had appeared in 『明治協会雑誌』 25–28 (Sept–Oct 1883).
- **Key claims (OCR-checked against NDL pid 871694):**
  - **「小説の主脳は人情なり、世態風俗これに次ぐ」**, followed by 「人情とは人間の情慾にて、所謂百八煩悩是れなり」.
  - Against Bakin: the eight heroes of 『八犬伝』 are embodiments of the eight virtues 「仁義八行の化物にて、決して人間とはいひ難かり」.
  - It distinguishes 摸写小説 (mimetic) from 勧懲小説 (didactic, "encourage good, punish evil"). The novel is a fine art (美術) whose 主眼 is 人情 (human emotion and psychology).
  - Also: style (雅/俗/雅俗折衷), plotting, protagonist, narration.
- **『一読三歎 当世書生気質』.**
  - Published as 春のやおぼろ, by 晩青堂.
  - Begun 9 Apr 1885. **No.1 came out 24 June 1885; it ran 17 numbers to No.17 in January 1886 (明治19年1月).**
  - Nos 1–8 are the 前編 and 9–17 the 後編. Reissued in book form in 1886.
  - Illustrations by 歌川国峰, 葛飾正久, 長原止水 and 武内桂舟.
  - The cram-school setting is modelled on Tokyo University.

**Sources opened**
- https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/小説神髄 (cites 柳田泉 1960/66)
- https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/当世書生気質
- https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/坪内逍遥
- NDL OCR https://lab.ndl.go.jp/dl/api/book/fulltext-json/871694 (1886 合本, https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/871694)

**Verdict: TRUE**, with exact dates as above. "Realism against Bakin's didacticism" is precisely the 摸写 vs 勧懲 argument.

## 6. Yōshū Chikanobu (楊洲周延)

**What is true**
- **Life.** Born 天保9年8月8日 (26 Sept 1838). Died 大正元年 (1912): 29 Sept per ja.wp; the *Miyako Shinbun* obituary says the night of 28 Sept. Real name 橋本直義, called 作太郎.
- **Samurai background.**
  - Son of a low-ranking Edo-resident retainer of **高田藩 (榊原家)**. ja.wp says the common "shogunal gokenin" claim is wrong.
  - Joined the 神木隊, formed by Takada's Edo retainers, which joined the **彰義隊 at Ueno in 慶応4年5月 (1868)**.
  - Escaped by sea in August. Fought under Enomoto at Hakodate.
  - Badly wounded aboard the *Kaiten* at the **Battle of Miyako Bay (March 1869)**.
  - Surrendered, was sent to Takada, and received 50 days' confinement.
- **Training.** Pupil of 国芳 (as 芳鶴, from 1852), then 三代豊国, then 豊原国周, from whom he took the name 周延.
- **Rokumeikan-era Western-dress prints:**
  - 「世界第一チャリネ大曲馬之図」 (1886);
  - 「鬘附束髪図会」 (1887);
  - 「女官洋服裁縫之図」;
  - 「貴顕舞踏の略図」 (Kobe City Museum; c.1888, exact date unverified);
  - 「欧洲管弦楽合奏之図」 (1889);
  - **「幻燈写心競」 (1890, publisher 横山良八)**. This series frames vignettes as magic-lantern projections; sheets include 「女史演説」 (a woman orating), 「学校試験」 and 「洋行」.
- **『千代田の大奥』.** NDL record: **明治27–29 (1894–96), publisher 福田初次郎 (具足屋; some sheets 福田熊次郎)**. Thirty-nine triptychs, one pentaptych, one single sheet. Dated sheets run from 1894 to Dec 1896.
  - ja.wp says 明治28–30; en.wp says 1895. NDL's 1894–96 is the better figure.
  - Companion series 『千代田之御表』, 1897.
  - Source text: 永島今四郎・太田義雄『千代田城大奥』 (朝野新聞社, 1892).
- **Newspaper work.**
  - The *Miyako Shinbun* obituary (No. 8847, 2 Oct 1912, as quoted and translated on en.wp) says he came to Tokyo in 明治8年 and "**became an artist for the 改進新聞**", producing nishiki-e on the side.
  - NDL records show 改進新聞 was published by 三益社 as the successor of 開花新聞 (1883–84). Issue no. 424 is dated 1 Aug 1884, and it runs into the 1890s.
  - So Chikanobu's newspaper illustration falls within c.1884–1890s. His exact years are not stated.

## 7. Mizuno Toshikata (水野年方)

**What is true**
- **Life.** Born **慶応2年1月20日 (6 Mar 1866)** in 神田東紺屋町, son of a plasterer foreman. Died 7 Apr 1908.

## 9. Kage-e (影絵) prints, toys and Meiji silhouette entertainment

**What is true**
- **Edo books and prints:**
  - 山東京伝作・鳥居清長画『児訓影絵喩』 (寛政10年, 1798).
  - 十返舎一九作・喜多川月麿画『紅毛影絵 於都里伎』 (文化7年, 1810). A joke manual of 19 near-impossible hand and body shadows (hairpin, brazier, lantern, dragon, clock...), each shown on one page with the "how-to" overleaf. It was played as a party game at drinking parties.
  - **歌川広重『即興かげぼしづくし』 (1839–42)**: toy prints (玩具絵), 竪中判 two-to-a-sheet, showing people making shadow figures. This is the verified Utagawa kage-e series.
  - **Kuniyoshi kage-e: UNVERIFIED.** I could not confirm a specific Kuniyoshi kage-e title in any source I opened. Don't name a title without checking.
- **Performance (Edo to Meiji):**
  - 写し絵 (utsushi-e): magic-lantern projection on a paper screen. It began in **1803**, when 都屋都楽 adapted a Dutch "エキマン鏡" show for the yose. ja.wp's 幻灯機 article cites 大久保遼 2017. In Kansai it was called 錦影絵.
  - Shadow and toy devices: 走馬灯 (revolving lanterns); cut-paper shadow puppets on sticks; hand shadows (手影絵).
  - **Meiji:**
    - Touring shows are documented in the Fukui newspaper index: 「東京初下り菊本文蝶・菊本万蔵等の一座 大道具入錦大影絵芝居」 (8 Nov 1884); 錦影絵 at 偕楽亭 (2 Dec 1884) on a mixed bill with storytelling, conjuring and niwaka; a 影絵芝居 troupe (29 Apr 1891).
    - The magic lantern (幻燈) spread in Meiji, with a **幻燈 boom in the Meiji 20s (late 1880s–90s)** and use in schools.
    - Chikanobu's 「幻燈写心競」 (1890) shows the lantern as a fashionable frame for bijin prints.

**Sources opened**
- https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/影絵
- https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/於都里伎
- https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/歌川広重 (works list)
- https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/幻灯機
- https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/歌川国芳
- NDL records: 『児訓影絵喩』 1798; Fukui newspaper items 1884-08-19, 1884-11-08, 1884-12-02, 1891-04-29; 「幻燈写心競」 1890

**Verdict: PARTLY.** The genre, toys, party game and Meiji shows are real. The Kuniyoshi attribution is unverified; Hiroshige's series is the safe example.

## 10. Sadayakko (川上貞奴) in Paris, 1900, and her actress school

**What is true**
- **Life dates.** 明治4年7月18日 (2 Sept 1871) – 7 Dec 1946.
- **Paris venue.** After London, the Kawakami troupe played the **Théâtre de la Loïe Fuller at the Exposition Universelle, opening 4 July 1900** (ja.wp; Downer).
- **Titles in French.** Gide's own words: "Elle n'a guère joué que deux pièces: **«la Geisha et le Chevalier»**, puis **«Kesa»**."
  - Downer (cited on en.wp) gives the Paris repertoire as *The Geisha and the Knight*, *Kesa*, *Takanori* and *Zingoro*.
  - French forms of those last two are UNVERIFIED (likely "Takanori", "Zingoro").
- **Who saw her.**
  - **André Gide: verified, primary.** "Lettres à Angèle" VIII, collected in *Prétextes* (1903). He went often to the Loïe Fuller theatre. He describes the Dōjōji-based climax of *la Geisha et le Chevalier*. Quotations:
    - "ce fut beau comme de l'Eschyle"
    - "Sada Yacco ne cesse jamais d'être belle… elle ne l'est jamais plus que dans sa mort"
    - his moral: "l'œuvre d'art ne s'obtient que par contrainte, et par la soumission du réalisme à l'idée de beauté préconçue"
    - He calls Otojirō "Kawa Kamy" and repeats the rumour that she was the first woman on the Japanese stage.
  - **Picasso: PARTLY.** fr.wp, citing Claudia Palazzolo, "Le jeune Picasso et la danseuse Sada Yacco", *Registres* 7 (2002), says Picasso made a portrait of her, as did Cappiello. A 1900 kakemono-shaped poster by Alfredo Müller exists. That Picasso saw her perform is not established in what I read.
  - **Rodin:** the anecdote (invited on opening night; asked to sculpt her; she declined) is in ja.wp, unsourced, and in Downer. Treat it as UNVERIFIED-primary.
  - ja.wp also lists Debussy as an admirer, unsourced.
  - Other contemporary texts: Judith Gautier, *Les musiques bizarres à l'Exposition: danses de Sada-Yacco* (1900); Camille Mauclair, "Sada Yacco et Loïe Fuller" (1904).
  - Max Slevogt painted her in 1901.
  - Officier d'Académie decoration (ja.wp: awarded to Otojirō at the Élysée).
- **Actress school.** **帝国女優養成所, opened September 1908** (Heibonsha 世界大百科, via Kotobank). Downer gives the opening as 15 Sept 1908.
  - Backed by Shibusawa, Ōkura, Fukuzawa Momosuke and others.
  - Entrants aged 16–25, higher-primary education, two guarantors.
  - First class included 森律子 and 村田嘉久子.
  - It later became the 帝劇附属技芸学校.

## 11. Qiu Jin (秋瑾)

**What is true**
- **Life dates.** 1875-11-08 to 1907-07-15 (executed at Shaoxing).
- **In Japan.**
  - Sold her jewellery and left for Japan in **1904 (光緒30年; zh.wp "5月", probably lunar, i.e. early summer 1904)**. en.wp's "1903" is wrong.
  - Studied first at a Japanese-language school. ja.wp says 弘文学院速成師範班, which conflicts. en.wp, citing Ono Kazuko, says a language school in Surugadai.
  - Then entered **実践女学校, Shimoda Utako's school in Kōjimachi**. Shimoda had taken Qing women students from 1901 and set up a 清国女子速成科; nearly 100 graduated by 1911.
  - Went home briefly in early 1905. Returned to Japan **15 July 1905**. Joined the **Tongmenghui in Aug 1905** (ja.wp: Sept).
  - After the Japanese Ministry of Education's student-control regulations of **2 Nov 1905**, she led the walk-out faction and **left Japan in December 1905** (ja.wp). zh.wp says February 1906, so sources differ on whether that is the departure or the arrival date.
  - At the Zhejiang students' meeting she drove a blade into the lectern. 徐双韵 says a **倭刀 (Japanese-style blade)** drawn from her boot. Zhou Zuoren's memoir has her "sentence to death" the stay-behinds, including Lu Xun.
- **Photographs.**
  - The famous portrait in **Japanese kimono holding a short sword or dagger** is genuine. Commons files it under "Kimono in China", dated 1900–07 without precision.
  - Photos of her in **Western men's suits** also exist. zh.wp notes she wore men's clothes to a Beijing theatre in 1903.
  - ja.wp: she loved swords, especially 日本刀.
  - I did not confirm where and when the men's-suit photo was taken (Beijing 1903 vs Tokyo 1904–05).
- **『精衛石』.** An unfinished 弾詞 (tanci).
  - Table of contents on zh.wikisource: **20 chapters planned, 6 extant ("存目二十回，存文六回")**.
  - The heroine 黄鞠瑞 is later named **黄競雄**, after Qiu's own 号 競雄.
  - Chapter 5 is 「美雨歐風頓起沉疴宿疾 發聾振聵造成兒女英雄」. **Chapter 6 is 「擺脫範圍雄心遊海島」 (breaking out, sailing to the island-country, i.e. Japan).**
  - Planned later chapters cover:
    - opening schools (ch. 8);
    - a girl first hearing the "revolutionary tide" (ch. 9);
    - natural-footed girls doing military drill (ch. 13);
    - "news from the island" (ch. 14);
    - women in the uprising (ch. 16);
    - sisters funding the Red Cross (ch. 18);
    - and finally **「拍手凱歌中共欣光復 同心革弊政大建共和」** (ch. 20: restoration of Han rule and a republic).
  - Hershatter (via en.wp): five well-born women flee their families and arranged marriages to study in Tokyo and join the revolution.
  - The preface calls on Chinese women to rise "like Roland, Perovskaya, Stowe(?) and 如安 (Joan)".
  - ja.wp's claim that it was first printed in 『中国女報』 the year after her death is doubtful, since that paper had only two issues (1907). Publication history is UNVERIFIED.

- https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/精衛石 (preface plus 20-chapter table of contents)
- https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/下田歌子
- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Qiu_jin.jpg

## 12. Otake Kōkichi (尾竹紅吉 = 富本一枝, 1893–1966)

## 13. Fukuda (Kageyama) Hideko as "東洋のジャンヌ・ダルク"

**What is true**
- **Life.** Born 慶応元年10月5日 (22 Nov 1865) in Okayama. Died 2 May 1927.
- **Arrest.** Arrested in the **Osaka Incident (大阪事件) of 1885**, the Ōi Kentarō plot to carry dynamite to Korea. Released in the 1889 constitutional amnesty.
- **The 1887 biography.** Already in 1887 there was a popular biography: 独善狂夫（清水太吉）編『景山英女之伝 自由の犠牲 女権の拡張』 (明治20年7月 and following, several publishers).

- **The epithet.**
  - "東洋のジャンヌ・ダルク" is cited in ja.wp to 村上信彦『明治女性史』中巻前編 (1969) p.101.
  - en.wp (citing Horimoto 1999) says "the media popularized her as 'Japan's Joan of Arc'".
  - I could not open any 1880s newspaper using the phrase. The 1887 biography is access-restricted in NDL's full text.
  - The katakana ジャンヌ・ダルク is modern. Meiji texts write ジヤンダーク (Fukuda), ジャンダーク, or 如安 (as in Qiu Jin's preface).
