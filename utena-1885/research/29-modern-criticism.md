# 29 — Japanese literary and cultural criticism, 1900–2026 (real history only)

Scope: this file covers real schools, venues, critics and key texts, with dates, so that an invented critic of any decade can be placed accurately. It contains no alternate history. Every bullet names the page it came from. "ja.wp" means the Japanese Wikipedia article (raw wikitext read via `action=raw`). ## 1. 1900s–1920s: naturalism, Shirakaba, Seitō, culturalism, proletarian criticism, shinkankaku-ha, Kobayashi Hideo

### 1a. The inherited machinery: the review column (before 1900)

- The **bungei jihyō 文芸時評** (monthly review of new writing) grew out of mid-Meiji *getsutan* 月旦 columns. The column that made readers take it seriously was **「三人冗語」** in the magazine **『めさまし草』 (1896)**, written by **森鷗外 Mori Ōgai, 幸田露伴 Kōda Rohan and 斎藤緑雨 Saitō Ryokuu**; it helped launch **樋口一葉 Higuchi Ichiyō** and **田山花袋 Tayama Katai**. Later practitioners were 近松秋江, 正宗白鳥, 佐藤春夫 and 広津和郎. **川端康成 Kawabata Yasunari wrote bungei jihyō for 20 years from 1922**. **十返肇 Togaeri Hajime (1914–63)** was the leading monthly reviewer for about 30 years from c.1933. **小林秀雄 Kobayashi Hideo (from 1930)** and **中村光夫 Nakamura Mitsuo (from 1935)** stood for "principled" criticism, and **吉本隆明 Yoshimoto Takaaki's** bungei jihyō of **1980–82** is singled out as a high point (Sekai Daihyakka, written by 紀田順一郎). https://kotobank.jp/word/文芸時評
- **没理想論争 (the "submerged ideals" debate), 1891–92**: 坪内逍遥 Tsubouchi Shōyō argued for *botsu-risō* (the author's ideals kept out of the work); Ōgai answered that there is no literature without ideals (Daijisen). It was fought partly in the first 『早稲田文学』. https://kotobank.jp/word/没理想論争
- **『早稲田文学』 Waseda bungaku**: founded **October 1891** as the organ of the 東京専門学校 literature course, edited by Tsubouchi. Suspended **October 1898**. Revived **January 1906** by **島村抱月 Shimamura Hōgetsu** and became "the stronghold of naturalism", with 相馬御風, 片上天弦, 正宗白鳥, 小川未明, 秋田雨雀 and 近松秋江 inside, and 田山花袋, 岩野泡鳴, 島崎藤村 and 徳田秋声 contributing from outside (Nikkoku, Daijisen). https://kotobank.jp/word/早稲田文学
- **『新潮』 Shinchō** was founded in **May 1904** by 佐藤義亮 Satō Giryō as the successor to his reader-contributor magazine 『新声』 (1896). It sat alongside Waseda bungaku as a power in the bundan; its 国木田独歩 memorial issue, edited by 中村武羅夫, was much praised (Nipponica, Sekai Daihyakka). https://kotobank.jp/word/新潮
- **『中央公論』 Chūō kōron** began in **1887** as 『反省会雑誌』, the temperance organ of a society at the Nishi Honganji school in Kyoto. It was renamed 『反省雑誌』 in 1892 and 『中央公論』 in **1899**. By late Meiji it was the bundan's "gateway", and under editor **滝田樗陰 Takita Chōin** in the Taishō period it was the leading liberal general magazine (Nikkoku, Mypedia). https://kotobank.jp/word/中央公論
- **高山樗牛 Takayama Chogyū (1871–1902)**, editor of 『太陽』, moved from 日本主義 (Japanism) to praise of Nietzsche. His **「美的生活を論ず」 appeared in 『太陽』 in 1901**; at the end of his life he turned to Nichiren (Daijisen). https://kotobank.jp/word/高山樗牛 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/美的生活を論ず

### 1b. Naturalism and its theorists

- **島村抱月 Shimamura Hōgetsu (28 Feb 1871 – 5 Nov 1918)**: studied at Tōkyō Senmon Gakkō under Tsubouchi and 大西祝, then in Oxford and Berlin from 1902 to 1905. He revived and ran the second Waseda bungaku. Key essays: **「囚はれたる文芸」 (Waseda bungaku, January 1906)** and **「文芸上の自然主義」 (Waseda bungaku, January 1908)**. In 1906 he co-founded the 文芸協会 (Literary Arts Society) with Tsubouchi; its inaugural meeting was held on **17 Feb 1906 at the Kōyōkan in Shiba**. His affair with the actress **松井須磨子 Matsui Sumako** became a scandal in 1913, and in **September 1913** the two founded the troupe **芸術座 Geijutsuza**. Their **1914 『復活』 (Resurrection)** and its song 「カチューシャの唄」 became a record hit. He died of Spanish flu with pneumonia at the Geijutsu Club in Ushigome on **5 Nov 1918**, and Sumako later killed herself. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/島村抱月
- **長谷川天渓 Hasegawa Tenkei (26 Nov 1876 – 30 Aug 1940)**, an editor at Hakubunkan's 『太陽』: **「幻滅時代の芸術」 (Taiyō, October 1906)** and **「現実暴露の悲哀」 (Taiyō, January 1908)**, collected in the book **『自然主義』 (Hakubunkan, July 1908)**. Senuma Shigeki judged that "his arguments were crude and bold, but he had the knack of coining buzzwords" such as 幻滅時代 ("age of disillusion"), 論理的遊戯 ("logical play") and 現実暴露の悲哀 ("the sorrow of reality laid bare"). He later turned publisher-editor (chief editor of 『太陽』 1923–27) and translated Freud (1930s). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/長谷川天渓
- The core naturalist texts were **『破戒』 (Shimazaki Tōson, 1906)** and **『蒲団』 (Tayama Katai, 1907)**, the latter known for its "露骨なる描写" (frank description). After Futon, naturalism came to be read as "depicting reality nakedly", which fed into the **私小説** (I-novel), seen by some as a "dwarfing" of the movement. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/自然主義文学

### 1c. Critics of naturalism and the anti-naturalists

- The **anti-naturalist** camp comprised the **耽美派** (aestheticists: 永井荷風 Nagai Kafū, back from Europe), the **Shirakaba** group, **余裕派** (the "leisure school": 夏目漱石 Natsume Sōseki), the **高踏派** (森鷗外 Mori Ōgai) and the **新現実主義** of 芥川龍之介 Akutagawa Ryūnosuke. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/自然主義文学
- **石川啄木 Ishikawa Takuboku, 「時代閉塞の現状」 ("The impasse of our age"), 1910**, subtitled 「強権、純粋自然主義の最後および明日の考察」. It was written as a rebuttal to **魚住折蘆 Uozumi Setsuro's 「自己主張の思想としての自然主義」** (1910) and showed his interest in socialism. Written for the Asahi, it **was not printed in his lifetime** and appeared only after his death (Daijisen). https://kotobank.jp/word/時代閉塞の現状
- Taishō "anti-naturalist" venues, as listed by Mypedia: the aesthete magazines 『スバル』 and 『三田文学』 (the Mita school) and the intellectualist 『新思潮』, alongside Shirakaba. https://kotobank.jp/word/白樺派

### 1d. Shirakaba (1910)

- **『白樺』** ran from **April 1910 to August 1923** (Sekai Daihyakka and Mypedia). Its writers were 武者小路実篤 Mushanokōji Saneatsu, 志賀直哉 Shiga Naoya, 有島武郎 Arishima Takeo, 有島生馬, 里見弴, 木下利玄, 郡虎彦, 長与善郎 and 柳宗悦, nearly all Gakushūin men. Painters around it included 岸田劉生 and 高村光太郎. The group championed humanism, individualism and idealism against naturalism. https://kotobank.jp/word/白樺派
- Origins: Mushanokōji and Shiga discussed the magazine at the **旅館東屋 inn at Kugenuma from 18 Oct 1907** (Shiga's diary). About a dozen Gakushūin acquaintances paid **2 yen a month from 1908** to fund it. At Gakushūin itself the magazine was **banned** as the work of "遊惰の徒" (idlers). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/白樺派

### 1e. Seitō (1911) and women's criticism

- **『青鞜』 Seitō, September 1911 – February 1916, 52 issues**, edited first by 平塚らいてう Hiratsuka Raichō and from January 1915 by 伊藤野枝 Itō Noe. **1,000 copies of the first issue** went out nationwide. The cover was by 長沼智恵子 (later 高村智恵子), and it opened with **与謝野晶子 Yosano Akiko's poem 「そぞろごと」** ("山の動く日来る"). Raichō's manifesto **「元始女性は太陽であった」** was the first appearance of the pen-name らいてう. The name Seitō ("Bluestocking") was chosen by 生田長江 Ikuta Chōkō. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/青鞜
- Key events: the **January 1912** issue carried an Ibsen 「附録ノラ」 supplement. 尾竹紅吉 Otake Kōkichi (19) joined in January 1912, and her "五色の酒" and Yoshiwara-visit stories, together with her published love for Raichō, made the press mock the "新しい女" (New Woman). The **April 1912 issue was banned** for 荒木郁's adultery story 「手紙」. 津田梅子 forbade her students to join. In **October 1913** the group's aim was changed from "女流文学の発達を計り" ("to develop women's literature") to "女子の覚醒を促し" ("to awaken women"). Under Itō Noe the magazine hosted debates on **chastity, abortion and licensed prostitution**. The **June 1915 issue was banned** for 原田皐月 Harada Satsuki's abortion essay. The magazine stopped after Itō left for 大杉栄 Ōsugi Sakae in April 1916. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/青鞜
- **The Maternity-Protection Debate 母性保護論争, March 1918 – June 1919**, was fought in 『婦人公論』 and 『太陽』 between **与謝野晶子** (women must be economically independent and should not ask the state for maternity support), **平塚らいてう** (who used Ellen Key to argue for state support of mothers), **山川菊栄 Yamakawa Kikue** and **山田わか Yamada Waka**. It began with Yosano's 「紫影録」 in the March 1918 issue of Fujin kōron (Nipponica, Mypedia). https://kotobank.jp/word/母性保護論争
- **『婦人公論』 Fujin kōron was founded in 1916**, after Chūō kōron's coverage of women's issues proved popular; its first editor was 嶋中雄作 (Daijisen). https://kotobank.jp/word/婦人公論
- **『女人芸術』 Nyonin geijutsu, July 1928 – June 1932, 48 issues**, was run by the playwright 長谷川時雨 Hasegawa Shigure and serialized 林芙美子's 『放浪記』 (Daijisen). https://kotobank.jp/word/女人芸術

### 1f. Taishō culturalism and kyōyō

- Sekai Daihyakka sets the Taishō idea of **教養 kyōyō** (inner, spiritual, non- or anti-political, personalist cultivation) against the Meiji idea of **修養 shūyō** (practical, careerist, political self-improvement). From late Meiji into Taishō a **教養主義** of reading Western philosophy and literature, inward-turning and apolitical, became dominant among students (Sekai Daihyakka, entries 教養 and 読書). https://kotobank.jp/word/教養主義
- **文化主義 bunka-shugi** ("culturalism", the claim that realizing cultural values is life's highest end) was shaped by German neo-Kantianism in late Taishō. Nikkoku's citation is **左右田喜一郎 Sōda Kiichirō, 「文化主義の論理」 (1919)**. https://kotobank.jp/word/文化主義
- **阿部次郎 Abe Jirō, 『三太郎の日記』 ("Santarō's Diary"), 1914–18**, was the idealist student-generation bible: "the inward search of a cultivated man who loves truth and suffers the contradiction of ideal and reality" (Nikkoku). https://kotobank.jp/word/三太郎の日記
- The general magazine **『改造』 Kaizō** was founded in **April 1919** by 山本実彦 Yamamoto Sanehiko. Its first issues printed **30,000 copies at 35 sen**, and many came back unsold until "social reconstruction" specials from the 4th issue took off. It hosted Russell and Einstein, gave proletarian literature space, and launched the **円本** (one-yen book) boom (Sekai Daihyakka). https://kotobank.jp/word/改造
- **『文藝春秋』 Bungei shunjū** was founded in **January 1923** by **菊池寛 Kikuchi Kan**, with 芥川龍之介's 「侏儒の言葉」 on its first page, and became a general magazine from the **September 1926** issue (Nipponica, Nikkoku). https://kotobank.jp/word/文芸春秋

### 1g. The proletarian movement and Marxist criticism

- **青野季吉 Aono Suekichi**: published the first Japanese translation of Ropshin's 『蒼ざめた馬』 (1919); 「階級闘争と芸術運動」 (1922); member of the 『種蒔く人』 group. His key essays were **「『調べた』芸術」 (Bungei sensen, July 1925)** and **「自然生長と目的意識」 ("Natural growth and purposive consciousness", Bungei sensen, September 1926)**. His first essay collection was 『解放の芸術』 (April 1926). In the 1927 split he joined the 労農芸術家連盟 (the 『文藝戦線』 faction, against the 『戦旗』 faction). He was arrested in the 1938 Popular Front incident, and after the war was vice-president of PEN Japan from 1948. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/青野季吉
- What "自然生長と目的意識" argued: an application of Lenin's *What Is to Be Done?* to the literary movement, in which spontaneous proletarian expression must be raised to class-conscious purpose. Kurahara's **「プロレタリア・レアリズムへの道」 (1928)** sought to plant "the Party's point of view" at the centre of proletarian realism (Sekai Daihyakka, under プロレタリア文学). https://kotobank.jp/word/自然生長と目的意識
- **『種蒔く人』** was killed by the 1923 Kantō earthquake and restarted in 1924 as **『文芸戦線』**. 葉山嘉樹's 『海に生くる人々』 (1926) is called the first monument of Japanese proletarian literature (Sekai Daihyakka). https://kotobank.jp/word/自然生長と目的意識
- **蔵原惟人 Kurahara Korehito** returned from the USSR in November 1926. In 1928 he brought the three feuding groups into the short-lived 日本左翼文芸家総連合 and helped found **NAPF** and its magazine 『戦旗』 (Kobayashi Takiji, Tokunaga Sunao). He wrote 「プロレタリア・レアリズムへの道」 in 1928 and joined the JCP in 1929. In 1930 he went secretly to the USSR as an interpreter at the Profintern congress. His 「芸術的方法についての感想」 and the organizational line led to **KOPF (日本プロレタリア文化連盟)** in autumn 1931. He was arrested in 1932 and sentenced to 7 years under the Peace Preservation Law. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/蔵原惟人
- **NAPF 全日本無産者芸術連盟** (Esperanto Nippona Artista Proleta Federacio) was formed in **1928** after the **3.15 incident** (mass arrests of Communists, 15 March 1928), from the merger of プロ芸 and 前芸. Its magazine 『ナップ』 ran **September 1930 – November 1931**. It was renamed 全日本無産者芸術団体協議会 in December 1930 with sections NALP, PROT, Prokino, YAP and PM, and was dissolved into KOPF in 1931. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/全日本無産者芸術連盟

### 1h. Shinkankaku-ha (1924)

- **『文藝時代』 was founded in October 1924** (横光利一 Yokomitsu Riichi, 川端康成, 片岡鉄兵, 中河与一, 今東光, 岸田国士) and ended with the **May 1927** issue. The journalist **千葉亀雄 Chiba Kameo** named the school in **「新感覚派の誕生」, 『世紀』, November 1924**, picking out the first issue's 「頭ならびに腹」, which opens: "真昼である。特別急行列車は満員のまま全速力で馳けてゐた。沿線の小駅は石のやうに黙殺された。" ("It is high noon. The express ran full speed, packed full. The little stations along the line were ignored like stones.") The group took Dada and German Expressionism as models. They made the avant-garde film 『狂った一頁』 (1926), and a **"formalism debate" with the proletarian side ran from 1927 to early 1929**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新感覚派

### 1i. Kobayashi Hideo's debut (1929)

- **「様々なる意匠」 ("Multiple Designs")** was published in 『改造』, **September 1929**, as the **second-prize entry in Kaizō's prize essay contest**. **First prize went to 宮本顕治 Miyamoto Kenji's 「『敗北』の文学」** (on Akutagawa). Kobayashi then wrote a monthly bungei jihyō in 『文藝春秋』 from April 1930 for a year, attacking both the abstraction of proletarian literature and the frailty of the I-novel (Sekai Daihyakka, Mypedia). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/小林秀雄_(批評家) ; https://kotobank.jp/word/小林秀雄
- He co-founded **『文學界』 in October 1933** (Bunka Kōronsha) with 宇野浩二, 武田麟太郎, 林房雄 and 川端康成, and took charge of it in January 1935 (serializing 『ドストエフスキイの生活』). He wrote **「私小説論」 in 1935**. In January 1936 he ousted the older members (里見弴, 宇野浩二, 豊島与志雄, 広津和郎) and brought in 村山知義, 島木健作, 舟橋聖一, 阿部知二 and 河上徹太郎; Kobayashi himself delivered the news to them. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/小林秀雄_(批評家)

## 2. 1930s–1945: censorship, tenkō, the Japan Romantic School, "Overcoming Modernity", the patriotic literary associations

### 2a. Censorship, repression and tenkō

- Control of the press and of books rested on the Meiji-era **出版法 (Publication Law)** and **新聞紙法 (Newspaper Law)**, which Ishikawa Tatsuzō was prosecuted under in 1938 (see below). Political dissent was handled under the **治安維持法 (Peace Preservation Law)**, under which Kurahara (1932) and others were jailed. https://kotobank.jp/word/治安維持法 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/蔵原惟人
- **Tenkō literature 転向文学**: novels about renouncing communism under state pressure. Daijisen's examples: 中野重治's 「村の家」, 村山知義's 「白夜」, 島木健作's 「癩」 and 高見順's 「故旧忘れ得べき」. https://kotobank.jp/word/転向文学
- **石川達三 Ishikawa Tatsuzō, 「生きてゐる兵隊」, 『中央公論』 March 1938**: based on a month or more spent with the army in central China from late 1937 as a Chūō kōron correspondent. It was banned under the **Newspaper Law**; author and editors were prosecuted, and on the prosecutor's appeal he received **4 months' imprisonment suspended for 3 years**. It was "the test case for the limits of depicting the war in literature" and was published only in **December 1945** (Nipponica, 久保田正文). https://kotobank.jp/word/生きてゐる兵隊
- The **情報局 Information Bureau** (内閣情報局) existed **1940–45** for propaganda and speech control (Daijisen). https://kotobank.jp/word/情報局
- **横浜事件 the Yokohama Incident (from 1942)**: the Kanagawa Special Higher Police treated an article by 細川嘉六 in 『改造』 as communist and arrested him, his associates and publishing staff. Several died under torture, and **『改造』 and 『中央公論』 were forced to close** (Daijisen). Kaizō stopped with its **June 1944** issue (Sekai Daihyakka), and Chūō kōron was dissolved in 1944 and revived in 1946. https://kotobank.jp/word/横浜事件 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/改造 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/中央公論
- The **Akutagawa and Naoki Prizes** were founded in **1935** at Kikuchi Kan's suggestion. The first Akutagawa went to Ishikawa Tatsuzō's 『蒼氓』, with Dazai Osamu and Takami Jun among the candidates. The original prize was **500 yen** (now ¥1,000,000). The prizes were suspended 1945–48 and revived in 1949 (Nipponica, Daijisen). https://kotobank.jp/word/芥川賞

### 2b. The Japan Romantic School 日本浪曼派 (1935) and the aesthetics of defeat

- Magazine **『日本浪曼派』, March 1935 – March 1938 (29 issues)** according to Nipponica and ja.wp. Daijisen, Nikkoku, Sekai Daihyakka, Britannica and Yamakawa give **August 1938** for the end (see conflicts). The six founders were **保田與重郎 Yasuda Yojūrō, 亀井勝一郎 Kamei Katsuichirō, 神保光太郎, 中島栄次郎, 中谷孝雄 and 緒方隆士**. Later members included **伊東静雄, 太宰治 Dazai Osamu, 檀一雄, 芳賀檀 and 山岸外史**, and there were over 50 members by the end. Yasuda's founding **"広告" (advertisement) in 『コギト』** attacked naturalist slice-of-life fiction as "平俗低徊の文学" (vulgar dawdling literature) and called for the "高邁" (lofty) over the "卑近" (near-at-hand), the "不易" (unchanging) over the "流行" (fashionable), and for an **"irony that exists as the handmaid of truth and sincerity"** (Nipponica). Its quarrel was with the realists of 『人民文庫』 (武田麟太郎 Takeda Rintarō, 高見順; 1936–38). https://kotobank.jp/word/日本浪曼派 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本浪曼派
- **保田與重郎 Yasuda Yojūrō (15 Apr 1910 – 4 Oct 1981)**, from Sakurai in Nara, studied aesthetics at Tokyo Imperial University. 『コギト』 (1932) declared "私らはこの国の省みられぬ古典を愛する" ("we love this country's neglected classics"). His works: **『日本の橋』 (1936, first 池谷信三郎賞)**, 『英雄と詩人』 (1936), **『戴冠詩人の御一人者』 (1938, 透谷文学賞)** on Yamato Takeru, 『後鳥羽院』 (1939) and 『近代の終焉』 (1941). **Sekai Daihyakka (大久保典夫): he "grafted the German Romantic method of 'irony' onto the classical culture he had known since childhood, and in his own obscure style sang an 〈敗北の美学〉 (aesthetics of defeat)" that captivated young men in the nihilistic years after the proletarian movement's collapse. After the war he was condemned as "the singer of the aesthetics of the battlefield"** and was **purged in 1948**. He founded the magazine 『祖国』 in 1949, returned to public life with 『現代畸人伝』 (1963/64), and from 1958 lived at the villa 身余堂 in Narutaki, Kyoto. His collected works run to 40 volumes plus 5. https://kotobank.jp/word/保田与重郎 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/保田與重郎
- Later critics: **橋川文三 Hashikawa Bunzō, 『日本浪曼派批判序説』 (Miraisha, 1960)** analysed the school's base. Mishima Yukio recalled that the Roman-ha-line 『文藝文化』 was "a fortress protecting the fragile classical beauty of Japan" amid wartime utilitarianism (「私の遍歴時代」, 1963). Kevin Doak's 『日本浪曼派とナショナリズム』 (Japanese translation, 1997) is a later study. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本浪曼派

### 2c. "Overcoming Modernity" 近代の超克 (1942)

- The symposium was billed as an "知的協力会議" (intellectual cooperation conference) and was held over **two days, eight hours, in late July 1942**, chaired by **河上徹太郎 Kawakami Tetsutarō**. It was printed in **『文學界』, September and October 1942** and published as a book by **Sōgensha in July 1943**. There were **13 participants**: Kyoto School philosophers (西谷啓治, 鈴木成高, 下村寅太郎), the Catholic 吉満義彦, the physicist 菊池正士, the composer 諸井三郎, the film critic 津村秀夫, and Bungakukai and ex-Roman-ha writers 小林秀雄, 亀井勝一郎, 林房雄 (「勤王の心」), 三好達治, 中村光夫 (「『近代』への疑惑」) and Kawakami. It was later condemned as ideological support for the war. **竹内好 Takeuchi Yoshimi's essay 「近代の超克」 (1959, 『近代日本思想史講座』 vol. 7)** reopened it as a problem of Japanese intellectual history. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/近代の超克

### 2d. Wartime literary associations

- The precursor was the **"pen squadron" (ペン部隊)**: in August 1938 the Cabinet Information Division asked writers to accompany the Hankou campaign (an army squad of 14, a navy squad of 8). Other groups followed: the 農民文学懇話会, 経国文芸の会, 国防文芸聯盟, 大陸開拓文芸懇話会 and others. At the **文学者愛国大会 of 24 Dec 1941**, about **350** writers met with Kikuchi Kan in the chair. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本文学報国会
- **日本文学報国会 (Patriotic Association for Japanese Literature)**, a corporation founded **26 May 1942** as a de facto outer body of the Information Bureau, existed "to make national policy known". Its **president was 徳富蘇峰 Tokutomi Sohō** (幸田露伴 declined, citing health), with **久米正雄 Kume Masao** as executive director and secretary-general. It began with **eight sections** (fiction, drama, criticism-and-essays, poetry, tanka, haiku, Japanese literature, foreign literature) and added kanshi/kanbun in 1943. Kawakami Tetsutarō headed the criticism section and the review department. Nearly all writers joined, including ex-proletarians such as 宮本百合子, 蔵原惟人 and 中野重治; **中里介山 and 内田百閒 refused**, and **永井荷風 complained in his diary of being enrolled without consent**. It ran the **Greater East Asia Writers' Congresses (November 1942, August 1943, November 1944 in Nanjing)**. Of its commissioned novels on the Greater East Asia Declaration's "five principles", only **Dazai's 『惜別』 and 森本薫's 『女の一生』** were finished. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本文学報国会

### 2e. A data point on 1930s girls' fiction and its authorship (relevant to criticism later)

- **『乙女の港』**, credited to Kawabata Yasunari, ran in **『少女の友』 from June 1937 to March 1938 (10 installments)** with illustrations by **中原淳一 Nakahara Jun'ichi**. The book appeared from Jitsugyō no Nihonsha on 1 Apr 1938 and reached **47 printings in five years**. Letters printed in the new Kawabata Complete Works (**補巻2, 1984**) show that it was drafted by his protégée **中里恒子 Nakazato Tsuneko** and revised by Kawabata. From his letter of 14 Sept 1937: "乙女の港はだんだん文章が粗くなり…" ("the writing of Otome no minato is getting rougher…"). Later critics split: 内田静江 says it is not ghostwriting, 小谷野敦 says it should be credited to Nakazato, and 中嶋展子 (2010) compared the drafts. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/乙女の港

## 3. 1945–1970: Kindai bungaku, war responsibility, Maruyama, the school canon, Yoshimoto, Etō

### 3a. Kindai bungaku and the shutaisei and politics-and-literature debates

- **『近代文学』**, founded by **7 coterie members: 荒正人 Ara Masahito, 平野謙 Hirano Ken, 本多秋五 Honda Shūgo, 埴谷雄高 Haniya Yutaka, 山室静, 佐々木基一 and 小田切秀雄 Odagiri Hideo** (end of 1945, first issue dated January 1946). It expanded in July 1947 (花田清輝, 大西巨人, 野間宏, 福永武彦, 加藤周一, 中村真一郎, among others) and **ended in 1964**. Its line was the autonomy of literature, held by men who had stood at the fringe of the proletarian movement. The first two issues featured Kobayashi Hideo and Kurahara Korehito. It serialized 埴谷雄高's 『死靈』 (1946–49) and printed 安部公房's 「壁」 (1951, Akutagawa Prize). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/近代文学_(雑誌)
- **Kobayashi Hideo at the 12 Jan 1946 Kindai bungaku round-table** (「コメディ・リテレール」): "僕は政治的には無智な一国民として事変に処した。黙って処した。…僕は無智だから反省なぞしない。利巧な奴はたんと反省してみるがいいじゃないか。" ("As a politically ignorant citizen I went through the war in silence… I am ignorant, so I won't repent. Let the clever ones repent all they like.") https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/近代文学_(雑誌)
- **主体性論争 (shutaisei / subjectivity debate), 1946–49**: raised by Ara Masahito and the Kindai bungaku group and carried into philosophy and history. It asked whether Marxist historical materialism leaves room for individual subjectivity (Sekai Daihyakka, under マルクス主義; ja.wp dates it from the late 1940s to the late 1950s). https://kotobank.jp/word/主体性論争 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/主体性論争
- **政治と文学論争 (politics-and-literature debate)**: Hirano and Ara on one side, **中野重治 Nakano Shigeharu's 「批評の人間性」** on the other. Hirano and Ara argued that writers' war responsibility, the merits and faults of the proletarian movement and tenkō were inseparable, and that literature must become independent of politics. Hirano's 「島崎藤村―〈新生〉覚え書」 (1946) championed "宿命の特権化" (privileging one's fate) against the contempt for the person in "the primacy of politics" theory. Ara wrote 「第二の青春」. 福田恆存, 加藤周一 and 小田切秀雄 joined in. Kindai bungaku emerged as the axis of postwar literature (Mypedia, Sekai Daihyakka). The **新日本文学会** had been founded on **30 Dec 1945**, with 9 founders including 秋田雨雀, 蔵原惟人, 中野重治 and 宮本百合子, and its magazine 『新日本文学』 began in March 1946. https://kotobank.jp/word/政治と文学論争
- **War-responsibility lists**: in **January 1946** Ara, Odagiri and Sasaki ran a column 「文学検察」 ("literary prosecution") in the magazine **『文学時評』**, naming **about 40 writers**. In March 1946 Odagiri had the 新日本文学会 adopt the clarification of war responsibility as an official task. Odagiri's list included **菊池寛, 火野葦平, 横光利一, 高村光太郎, 小林秀雄, 亀井勝一郎, 保田與重郎, 佐藤春夫 and 林房雄**, and he called for their removal from public literary office. A **second war-responsibility debate came in 1956**, between the 新日本文学会 and New-Left sympathizers around the poetry journal **『荒地』** (Simone Müller, "Le « champ littéraire » japonais en lutte", *Ebisu* 54, December 2017). https://journals.openedition.org/ebisu/2038

### 3b. Maruyama Masao on the emperor system (1946)

- **丸山眞男 Maruyama Masao (22 Mar 1914 – 15 Aug 1996)**, whose 「超国家主義の論理と心理」 ("Logic and Psychology of Ultranationalism", 1946, in 『世界』) analysed Japanese ultranationalism. Nihon jinmei daijiten summarizes it as identifying "the **system of irresponsibility (無責任体系)** of Japanese-style fascism and the emperor state". Mypedia lists it with 「軍国支配者の精神形態」 as the essays that dissected "the inner structure of emperor-system fascism". His afterword line "大日本帝国の'実在'よりも戦後民主主義の'虚妄'の方に賭ける" ("I bet on the 'illusion' of postwar democracy rather than the 'reality' of the Empire", 増補版 『現代政治の思想と行動』, 1964) became emblematic (Nipponica). https://kotobank.jp/word/丸山真男
- Müller (*Ebisu* 2017) places the essay in 1946 and paraphrases it: the state was at once political and moral authority, centred on the tennō. https://journals.openedition.org/ebisu/2038
- **『日本の思想』 (Iwanami shinsho, 1961)** had sold **1.02 million copies by May 2005**. Its 「『である』ことと『する』こと」 became a high-school textbook staple. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/丸山眞男
- Postwar venues: **『世界』 (Iwanami, January 1946**; first editor 吉野源三郎) argued for an all-party peace treaty in 1951 and against renewal of the Security Treaty in 1960. **『思想の科学』 (May 1946 – March 1996, 536 issues**; 鶴見俊輔, 丸山, 都留重人, 武谷三男, 鶴見和子) studied popular fiction, songs, film and performance. **『群像』 (Kōdansha, October 1946)** began its 「創作合評」 round-table reviews in April 1947 and set up the **Gunzō New Writers' Prize in 1958**, which produced the critics 秋山駿, 磯田光一 and 柄谷行人. https://kotobank.jp/word/世界 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/思想の科学 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/群像

### 3c. The postwar textbook canon

- **System chronology** (Kotobank Nipponica 教科書, by 朝倉充彦 and 宮﨑秀一; ja.wp 国定教科書, after 山住正己's 『教科書』): free publication under the 1872 学制; banned lists in 1880; notification (開申) in 1881; approval (認可) in 1883; **authorization (検定) from 1886** (Mori Arinori's 小学校令 and 中学校令; the 教科用図書検定条例, replaced in 1887 by the 検定規則). The **1902 textbook bribery scandal** reached 1 dō, 3 fu and 35 prefectures, with about 200 people arrested, including governors and school inspectors. **State-authored (国定) textbooks for elementary schools ran from 1903/04 to 1945.** Under the occupation came "墨塗り" (blacked-out) textbooks. The **1947 School Education Law** ended the state system; the **1948 教科用図書検定規則** and the 教科書の発行に関する臨時措置法 followed, then the **1949 教科用図書検定基準**, and **new authorized textbooks were in use from 1949**. In 1955 the Democratic Party pamphlet 『うれうべき教科書の問題』 attacked "biased" social-studies books, and a 1956 textbook bill failed. In **1958** the course of study (学習指導要領) was made legally binding. https://kotobank.jp/word/教科書 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/国定教科書
- **How the high-school literary canon formed (the 定番教材 "staple texts")**:
  - **「羅生門」** (Teikoku bungaku, November 1915) never appeared in a textbook for about 40 years. It first appeared in **1956** in three books: 明治書院 『高等学校総合2』 (the only one for the compulsory 国語甲, placed in the 2nd year), 数研出版 『日本現代文学選』 and 有朋堂 『国文現代編』 (both for the elective 国語乙). It **first went into the 1st year** in a **Sanseidō book the next year (1957)**. Its rate was **83 %** when 「国語Ⅰ」 began (1982), and **100 %** when 「国語総合」 began in **2003**. Kōda Kunihiro attributes the last step to market homogenization as the textbook market shrank. Shōji Tatsuya (2021) states that **「こころ」 and 「舞姫」 were first adopted in the same period**. Source: 坂東智子, 「定番文学教材指導の方向性に関する一考察―『羅生門』を中心に―」, 山口大学教育学部附属教育実践総合センター研究紀要 53 (March 2022), citing 庄司達也 in 『教科書と近代文学』 (秀明大学出版会, 2021) and 幸田国広, 『国語教育は文学をどう扱ってきたのか』 (大修館, 2021), p.151. https://petit.lib.yamaguchi-u.ac.jp/28948/files/165692
  - **「舞姫」** entered high-school textbooks in the **Shōwa 30s (from c.1955)**. Before the war, Ōgai's historical fiction and verse had been used in middle-school and girls'-school readers, but not Maihime. According to 高橋修, it carried the Kindai bungaku reading of "**the frustration of the modern self (近代的自我の挫折)**" and the national-literature school's reading of a uniquely Japanese modernization, so both camps could endorse it. The **1960 course of study** created the subject **「現代国語」** (from the 1963 first-year cohort), explicitly including Meiji works in **文語** (classical style), which pushed adoption up. A **1973 teacher's manual allotted 6 class hours; 2006 manuals allotted 10–11**. **佐藤泉 Satō Izumi** argued that 「こころ」 was adopted to turn potentially critical students' attention inward to the individual. Source: 伊藤誠子, 「『舞姫』と教科書―定番教材『舞姫』を学校現場で読み続けるために」 (data from 阿武泉's 戦後高等学校国語教科書データベース). https://petit.lib.yamaguchi-u.ac.jp/8208/files/164355
  - **「山月記」** was adopted in a high-school textbook in **Shōwa 26 (1951)**, per 堀誠, 「中島敦と馮夢龍の眼」, 早稲田大学大学院教職研究科紀要 10. https://waseda.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/40890/files/KyoshokuKenkyukaKiyo_10_4.pdf
  - Itō also dates the rise of **作品論** (reading the work cut off from the author's biography) to the **Shōwa 40s (1965–74)**, with Maihime the ideal test case, and cites **前田愛's** reading of its Berlin urban space. https://petit.lib.yamaguchi-u.ac.jp/8208/files/164355

### 3d. 1950s debates and generations

- **国民文学論 (national literature debate), early 1950s**: 竹内好, 伊藤整 and 野間宏 asked "what is literature for the nation?" after the San Francisco peace treaty and the Korean War. Takeuchi's 『国民文学論』 appeared in 1954 (Daijisen). https://kotobank.jp/word/国民文学論
- **第三の新人 ("third new generation")**: the writers who followed the postwar school in the late Shōwa 20s (1950–54): 安岡章太郎, 吉行淳之介, 遠藤周作 (Daijisen). https://kotobank.jp/word/第三の新人
- **中村光夫 Nakamura Mitsuo, 『風俗小説論』 (1950)** traced the Japanese I-novel to Futon and attacked it (Daijisen). https://kotobank.jp/word/風俗小説論
- **純文学論争 (pure-literature debate), 1960s**, set off by remarks of Hirano Ken as the readership expanded with economic growth (Sekai Daihyakka, under 戦後文学). https://kotobank.jp/word/純文学論争
- **小田切秀雄 Odagiri Hideo** was a JCP member 1946–64 (later expelled or broken with the party) and founded the 日本近代文学研究所 in 1953; he left Kindai bungaku in 1947 after the politics-and-literature debate (20 seiki jinmei jiten, Mypedia). https://kotobank.jp/word/小田切秀雄

### 3e. Yoshimoto Takaaki

- **吉本隆明 Yoshimoto Takaaki (25 Nov 1924 – 16 Mar 2012)**, a chemistry graduate of Tokyo Tech who was pushed out of companies for union work. His poetry came first: **『固有時との対話』 (1952)** and **『転位のための十篇』 (1953)**. Then **「マチウ書試論」 (1954)**; **『文学者の戦争責任』 (1956)**, which indicted both prewar and postwar Marxist writers; and **「転向論」 (1958)**, which held that tenkō was not just surrender to state force but **the isolation of intellectuals from the masses**. He backed the Zengakuren in 1960 and founded **『試行』** in 1961 with 谷川雁 and 村上一郎 (a one-man magazine from 1964). His theory of "**大衆の原像**" (the original image of the masses) led to **『言語にとって美とは何か』 (1965)** and **『共同幻想論』 (1968)**, which defined the state as a communal illusion and was huge with the Zenkyōtō generation; it was serialized in 『文藝』 1966–67 (Nipponica, Nihon jinmei daijiten, Daijisen). https://kotobank.jp/word/吉本隆明 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/共同幻想論

### 3f. Etō Jun, 『成熟と喪失』 (1967)

- **江藤淳 Etō Jun** (real name 江頭淳夫; born 25 Dec 1932, though Nipponica gives 1933; died 21 July 1999 by suicide after his wife's death). He debuted with **「夏目漱石」 in 『三田文学』 (1955–56)**, followed by 『奴隷の思想を排す』 (1958), 『小林秀雄』 (1960–61), 『アメリカと私』 (1965) after two years in the US from 1962, and 『漱石とその時代』 (1970–). https://kotobank.jp/word/江藤淳
- **『成熟と喪失 ―"母"の崩壊―』 (Kawade Shobō Shinsha, June 1967)** was begun in **summer 1966**, 15 months after his return from Princeton. It reads five "third generation" novels: 安岡章太郎's 「海辺の光景」, 小島信夫's 「抱擁家族」, 遠藤周作's 「沈黙」, 吉行淳之介's 「星と月は天の穴」 and 庄野潤三's 「夕べの雲」. The thesis: in a Japanese culture of tight mother–child bonding there is **no "maturity" without "the collapse of the mother"**, and "成熟するとは、喪失感の中に湧いてくるこの「悪」を引き受けることである" ("to mature is to take on this 'evil' that wells up from the sense of loss"). The Kōdansha Bungei Bunko edition (October 1993) carries a commentary by **上野千鶴子 Ueno Chizuko**, who stresses that the subtitle says "collapse", not "loss" or "matricide". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/成熟と喪失

## 4. 1970–2000: Maeda Ai, Karatani, Hasumi, New Academism, feminist criticism, shōjo studies, manga criticism, queer studies

### 4a. Maeda Ai

- **前田愛 Maeda Ai (20 Apr 1931 per ja.wp and his collected works; 1932 per 20-seiki jinmei jiten; died 27 July 1987)** was a man; his given name is read *Yoshimi*. He trained in Edo literature (Tamenaga Shunsui) and taught at Rikkyō from 1970. His work: **『幕末・維新期の文学』 (1972)**; **『近代読者の成立』 (Yūseidō, 1973)**, whose thesis is that the reader changed from **reading aloud (音読) to silent reading (黙読)**; **『樋口一葉の世界』 (1978)**; and **『都市空間のなかの文学』 (Chikuma, 1982**; Art Encouragement Prize). He brought **text theory and semiotics** into kokubungaku. Nipponica stresses that he saw continuity of readers, not rupture, across the Edo–Meiji divide. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/前田愛_(文芸評論家) ; https://kotobank.jp/word/前田愛
- **The Takekurabe "Midori" debate**: 佐多稲子 Sata Ineko's **「『たけくらべ』解釈へのひとつの疑問」 (『群像』 40-5, May 1985)** challenged the dominant reading, held by **前田愛 among others**, that Midori's sudden change after the Tori-no-ichi festival is her **first menstruation (初潮説)**. Sata suggested a sexual initiation or *mizuage* instead, starting the **美登利変貌論争**. In 2002 山本欣司 argued that the menarche reading hides a modern paradigm that divides child from adult by sexual "maturity" (「売られる娘の物語―『たけくらべ』試論―」, 弘前大学教育学部紀要 87, March 2002). https://hirosaki.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/2210/files/AN00211590_87_11.pdf

### 4b. Karatani Kōjin, 『日本近代文学の起源』 (1980)

- **柄谷行人 Karatani Kōjin** (real name 善男; born 6 Aug 1941 per Nihon jinmei daijiten, 16 Aug per Britannica): won the **1969 Gunzō New Writers' Prize (criticism)** with 「〈意識〉と〈自然〉―漱石試論」 (judges Etō, Ōe, Noma, Yasuoka). He was a visiting professor at **Yale from September 1975 to January 1977**, where he met Paul de Man. 『マルクスその可能性の中心』 (1978, Kamei Katsuichirō Prize). He published **「風景の発見―序説」 (『季刊藝術』, summer 1978)** and **「内面の発見」 (autumn 1978)**, then **『日本近代文学の起源』 (Kōdansha, August 1980)**; it was a candidate for the 1981 Hirabayashi Taiko Prize but did not win. He co-founded 『季刊思潮』 (January 1989 – April 1990) and **『批評空間』 (April 1991–2002**, with Asada Akira). In 1991 he led writers' opposition to the Gulf War. In **2022** he became the first Asian to win the **Berggruen Prize**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/柄谷行人 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/柄谷行人
- **Argument (Duke University Press's description of the English edition, 1993, ed. Brett de Bary, preface by Fredric Jameson)**: "origin", "modern", "literature" and "the state" are ideological constructs. The strands are the "discovery" of **landscape** and its link to interiority; the "naked face" in drama; the challenge to Chinese characters (*genbun itchi*); **confession** as an outgrowth of the repression of sexuality and the body; the samurai turn to Christianity; the mythologizing of **tuberculosis** and illness; and the "discovery" of **"the child"**. https://www.dukeupress.edu/origins-of-modern-japanese-literature

### 4c. Hasumi Shigehiko

- **蓮實重彦 Hasumi Shigehiko (born 29 Apr 1936)**, a Flaubert scholar and film critic. Works: **『批評あるいは仮死の祭典』 (1974)**; **『反=日本語論』 (May 1977**; Yomiuri Prize 1978); **『夏目漱石論』 (1978)**; **『フーコー・ドゥルーズ・デリダ』 (1978)**; **『表層批評宣言』 (November 1979)**; 『物語批判序説』 (1985). He was **26th president of the University of Tokyo (1997–2001)** and won the **Mishima Prize in 2016** for the novel 『伯爵夫人』. His Rikkyō film students included 黒沢清 and 青山真治. As a Gakushūin middle-schooler he read schoolmate Mishima's 『仮面の告白』 and "despised his lack of athletic ability". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/蓮實重彦

### 4d. New Academism (1983–)

- **浅田彰 Asada Akira (born 23 Mar 1957), 『構造と力―記号論を超えて』 (Keisō Shobō, 1983)**: a single perspective on structuralism and post-structuralism (Lévi-Strauss, Lacan, Deleuze), written while he was a 26-year-old assistant at Kyoto University's Institute for Research in Humanities. Its preface first appeared in **『中央公論』, May 1981**, as an invitation to "知への漸進的横滑り" (a gradual sideways slide into knowledge) for the apathetic post-Zenkyōtō young. It sold **150,000 copies (Kotobank) or 600,000 (井狩春男)**. His **『逃走論』 (1984)** popularized "schizo/parano". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/構造と力 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/浅田彰
- The term **"ニュー・アカデミズム"** was coined in the Asahi book pages of **23 Jan 1984** for books by young researchers "challenging existing disciplines", led by Asada and **中沢新一 Nakazawa Shin'ichi's 『チベットのモーツァルト』 (1983)**. The boom pulled in 四方田犬彦, 松浦寿輝, 細川周平, 渡部直己 and 絓秀実, and re-spotlit 山口昌男, 柄谷行人, 蓮實重彦 and 栗本慎一郎 (『パンツをはいたサル』, 1981). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ニュー・アカデミズム

### 4e. Feminist criticism

- **駒尺喜美 Komashaku Kimi (18 Mar 1925 – 22 May 2007)**, a Hōsei professor. As a teenager (1940) she was an obsessive fan ("追っかけ") of the **OSK (Osaka Shōchiku girls' revue) male-role star 秋月恵美子**. Her **『魔女の論理―エロスへの渇望』 (Epona, 1978)** was called "a bible for thinking about women's liberation", and it re-read Sōseki and Genji from a feminist angle. Later books: 『魔女的文学論』 (1982), **『吉屋信子―隠れフェミニスト』 (Libroport, 1994)** and 『紫式部のメッセージ』 (1991). She lived for 50 years with 小西綾 Konishi Aya as a "友だち家族" (family of friends), opened the women's room "56番館" in Kagurazaka in 1977, and became representative of the 日本女性学会 (Women's Studies Association of Japan) in 1980. ### 5a. Otaku and subculture criticism

- **東浩紀 Azuma Hiroki (born 9 May 1971)** debuted in **『批評空間』 (April 1993)** with an essay he handed Karatani after slipping into his Hōsei lectures. - **斎藤環 Saitō Tamaki, 『戦闘美少女の精神分析』 (Ōta Shuppan, 2000; Chikuma bunko, 10 May 2006, with commentary by Azuma)**: Nausicaä, Sailor Moon and Ayanami Rei as "**phallic girls**", innocent and trauma-free fighting girls unlike Amazonian warriors, read through otaku sexuality. - **大塚英志**, in the **December 2001** issue of the light-novel magazine 『ザ・スニーカー』, broke off his writing course 『キャラクター小説の作り方』 to discuss how postwar subculture (Tezuka's manga, Tomino Yoshiyuki's anime) had taken in and depicted real war, and addressed an anti-war appeal to his teenage readers. ### 5b. Yuri studies

- **『ユリイカ』 46(15), December 2014, 「特集 百合文化の現在」 ("Yuri culture now")**: contributions by 中里一 (「解放区としての百合」), 川口晴美, **牧村朝子 (「百合レズ論争戦絵巻」, on the yuri-vs-lesbian debate)**, 高嶋リカ, **石田美紀 (「戦闘美少女と叫び、そして百合」)** and **Erica Friedman with 椎名ゆかり (「百合 境界なきジャンル」)**. https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1521980705058588032

### 5d. Trans and nonbinary criticism and its legal frame

- **The term "Xジェンダー"** (X-gender, Japan's home-grown nonbinary label) was in use by the **late 1990s** (S.P.F. Dale, *Intersections* 31, 2012). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xジェンダー

The first change of registered sex was made by the Naha Family Court on **28 Jul 2004** (male to female); the first female-to-male change was in Tokyo on 27 Aug 2004. The **Supreme Court Grand Bench held the sterility requirement unconstitutional (Article 13) on 25 Oct 2023**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/性同一性障害者の性別の取扱いの特例に関する法律

Her Meiji University lectures ran from 2012 to her **final lecture on 10 Mar 2026**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/三橋順子

### 5e. Digital humanities and stylometry (計量文献学 / 計量文体学)

- **安本美典 Yasumoto Biten, 「宇治十帖の作者―文章心理学による作者推定―」, 『心理学評論』 2(1):147–156 (1957)**: compared the Uji chapters of Genji with the other 44 (use of simile, sentence length and so on) and concluded they were unlikely to be by the same author. This was the pioneer study; see conflicts for the date. Later work disagreed: 新井 (1997); **村上征勝 Murakami Masakatsu and 今西祐一郎 (1999)** found differences in auxiliary verbs but would not claim another author; 土山玄 and 村上 (2012), using PCA and random forests, found "no positive grounds" for a different author. https://ipsj.ixsq.nii.ac.jp/record/107410/files/IPSJ-S-CH2014033.pdf ; https://ritsumei.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/2171/files/LEIS_5_6_2kitahara.pdf
- **Nichiren's writings**: 村上征勝, 岸野洋久 and 伊藤瑞叡, 「日蓮遺文の計量分析―思想の変化と文体の変化」 (『統計数理』, December 1990), and Murakami, Kishino and 古瀬順一, 「日蓮遺文と偽作の文体差に関する計量分析」 (December 1991). The popular book is Murakami's **『計量文献学の世界―シェークスピアは誰ですか？』 (Bunshun shinsho, 2004)**. https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1050282677781870464 ; https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1050282677779704576 ; https://books.bunshun.jp/ud/book/num/9784166604067
- **Commas as fingerprints**: **金明哲 Jin Mingzhe, 樺島忠夫 and 村上征勝, 「読点と書き手の個性」 (『計量国語学』, March 1993)**; Jin, 「読点の打ち方と文章の分類」 (December 1994); Jin, 「読点から現代作家のクセを検証する」 (『統計数理』, 1996). https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1520009408498432768
- **Famous Japanese ghostwriting cases**:
  - A **KAKEN project by 村上征勝 and 古瀬順一** on the "**ghostwriting suspicion around 『雪国』**" built tagged corpora of 14 Kawabata and 4 Mishima works. Comma usage suggested that Kawabata's style changed from 『山の音』, not from 『みづうみ』 as usually said, and that 『潮騒』 differs from Kawabata in comma habits; no firm conclusion. https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1040282256714201728
  - **孫昊 Sun Hao and 金明哲, 「川端康成の小説『花日記』の代筆疑惑検証」, 『情報知識学会誌』 28(1):3–14 (2018)**: using character and symbol bigrams, POS bigrams and phrase patterns with AdaBoost, HDDA, LMT, SVM and random forest, they concluded that the girls' novel 『花日記』 was **co-written by Kawabata and Nakazato Tsuneko**. (Sun Hao's 2018 Dōshisha doctoral thesis covers Kawabata ghostwriting generally.) https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390282679400709504
  - **柳燁佳 and 金明哲, 「菊池寛『受難華』の代筆問題の研究」, 『データ分析の理論と応用』 9(1):1–11 (2020)**: **Kawabata had testified** that Kikuchi Kan's serial 『受難華』 was ghostwritten by Yokomitsu Riichi. Using commas, POS bigrams and phrase patterns over 32 works by each man and 22 serial installments, with seven classifiers including SVM, RF and XGBoost, they concluded that **Kikuchi wrote it**. https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390285697608680960
- **Digital canon infrastructure**: **青空文庫 Aozora Bunko** was founded **7 Jul 1997** by 富田倫生, 野口英司, 八巻美恵 and らんむろ・さてぃ; it pioneered free online texts of out-of-copyright works and uses ｜ and 《》 markup for ruby. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/青空文庫

## 6. Western scholarship on Japanese literature, gender and girls' culture

- **Donald Keene (18 Jun 1922 – 24 Feb 2019)**: *World Within Walls* (1976, pre-modern 1600–1867); **_Dawn to the West: Japanese Literature of the Modern Era_, 2 vols (Fiction; Poetry, Drama, Criticism), Holt, Rinehart & Winston, 1984**, the 3rd and 4th parts of his *History of Japanese Literature*; *Seeds in the Heart* (1993). He taught at Columbia 1955–2011, received the **Order of Culture in 2008** (the only non-Japanese recipient as of 2025), and took **Japanese citizenship in 2012** as キーン ドナルド. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Keene
- **Edward G. Seidensticker (11 Feb 1921 – 26 Aug 2007)**: translator of Kawabata's *Snow Country* (1956) and *Thousand Cranes*, of Tanizaki and Mishima, and of **_The Tale of Genji_ (Knopf, 1976)**. He also wrote *Kafū the Scribbler* (Stanford, 1965) and the Tokyo histories *Low City, High City* (1983) and *Tokyo Rising* (1990). He won the National Book Award translation prize in 1971. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Seidensticker
- **Masao Miyoshi, _Accomplices of Silence_ (University of California Press, 1974)**, on the modern Japanese novel (Open Library record). **Jay Rubin, _Injurious to Public Morals: Writers and the Meiji State_ (University of Washington Press, 1983/84)**, on censorship. **Edward Fowler, _The Rhetoric of Confession: Shishōsetsu in Early Twentieth-Century Japanese Fiction_ (University of California Press, 1988)**. **Frederik L. Schodt, _Manga! Manga!_ (Kodansha International, 1983)**. https://openlibrary.org/search.json?title=Accomplices%20of%20Silence&author=Miyoshi
- **Kōjin Karatani, _Origins of Modern Japanese Literature_ (Duke University Press, 1993)**, translation edited by Brett de Bary, with a preface by Fredric Jameson and a new essay "The Extinction of Genre"; argument as in 4b. https://www.dukeupress.edu/origins-of-modern-japanese-literature
- **Tomi Suzuki, _Narrating the Self: Fictions of Japanese Modernity_ (Stanford, 1996)**, and **Haruo Shirane and Tomi Suzuki, eds., _Inventing the Classics: Modernity, National Identity, and Japanese Literature_ (Stanford, 2000)**, with chapters such as Shirane's "Issues in Canon Formation" and "Curriculum and Competing Canons", the basic English work on how the school and national canon was made (Crossref and Open Library records). https://doi.org/10.1515/9780804764544
- **Jennifer Robertson, _Takarazuka: Sexual Politics and Popular Culture in Modern Japan_ (University of California Press, 1998)**: the all-female revue "illuminates discourses of sexual politics, nationalism, imperialism, and popular culture" in 20th-century Japan, with special attention to wartime, and questions Eurocentric assumptions about gender performance and sexuality. https://www.ucpress.edu/books/takarazuka/paper
- **Gregory M. Pflugfelder, _Cartographies of Desire: Male-Male Sexuality in Japanese Discourse, 1600–1950_ (University of California Press, 1999 cloth; paperback March 2007)**. The book draws on law, literature, newspapers and medical tracts. In each of three eras one field dominated discourse on male–male sex: **popular culture in the Edo period, jurisprudence in Meiji, and medicine in the 20th century**. https://www.ucpress.edu/books/cartographies-of-desire/paper
- **Mark McLelland, _Male Homosexuality in Modern Japan: Cultural Myths and Social Realities_ (Curzon/Routledge, 2000)** and *Queer Japan from the Pacific War to the Internet Age* (Rowman & Littlefield, 2005). **Mark McLelland, Kazumi Nagaike, Katsuhiko Suganuma and James Welker, eds., _Boys Love Manga and Beyond_ (University Press of Mississippi, 2015)**. **James Welker, "Beautiful, Borrowed, and Bent: 'Boys' Love' as Girls' Love in Shôjo Manga", _Signs_ 31.3 (March 2006)**. https://doi.org/10.1086/498987 ; https://openlibrary.org/search.json?title=Male%20Homosexuality%20in%20Modern%20Japan&author=McLelland
- **Rebecca L. Copeland, _Lost Leaves: Women Writers of Meiji Japan_ (University of Hawai'i Press, June 2000, 304 pp.)** challenges the claim that Meiji had no women writers except Higuchi Ichiyō. It studies **Miyake Kaho (1868–1944)**, "often regarded as the first woman writer of modern Japan"; **Wakamatsu Shizuko (1864–1896)**, "the most prominent female translator of her time", who shaped modern written prose; and **Shimizu Shikin (1868–1933)**, who struggled to balance creative work with social roles. https://uhpress.hawaii.edu/title/lost-leaves-women-writers-of-meiji-japan/
- **Melanie Czarnecki, "Bad Girls from Good Families: The Degenerate Meiji Schoolgirl"**, in **Laura Miller and Jan Bardsley, eds., _Bad Girls of Japan_ (Palgrave Macmillan, 2005), pp. 49–64**. After the **1899 Girls' Higher School Order (高等女学校令)**, provincial elite daughters came to Tokyo's best girls' schools and, away from family surveillance, formed new communities and entered public spaces such as **Hibiya Park**. The schoolgirl's negotiation of these spaces "largely defined their status as 'good' or 'bad'". https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1057/9781403977120_4
- **Jan Bardsley, _The Bluestockings of Japan: New Woman Essays and Fiction from Seitō, 1911–16_ (University of Michigan Center for Japanese Studies, 2007)**: 17 translated Seitō pieces, four of which "provoked either a strong reprimand or an outright ban". Journalists ridiculed the Bluestockings as "self-indulgent, literature-loving, sake-drinking, cigarette-smoking tarts". The topics are harassment, abortion, love, motherhood and equality. https://press.umich.edu/Books/T/The-Bluestockings-of-Japan
- **Jim (James) Reichert, _In the Company of Men: Representations of Male-Male Sexuality in Meiji Literature_ (Stanford University Press, 2006; viii + 282 pp. per the Wellcome catalogue)**, on male–male sexuality in Meiji writing during the modernization drive (Goodreads blurb). The argument usually summarized is that **the "heterosexualization" of literature was a component of producing Japanese literary modernity, while male–male sexuality became a symbol for those resisting modernization**; this comes from a publisher blurb seen only in a search snippet, see below. https://wellcomecollection.org/works/gkawkfns ; https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/3423143
- **Sarah Frederick, _Turning Pages: Reading and Writing Women's Magazines in Interwar Japan_ (University of Hawai'i Press, 2006)**. **Indra Levy, _Sirens of the Western Shore: The Westernesque Femme Fatale, Translation, and Vernacular Style in Modern Japanese Literature_ (Columbia, 2006)**. **Michiko Suzuki, _Becoming Modern Women: Love and Female Identity in Prewar Japanese Literature and Culture_ (Stanford, 2010)** (Open Library records). https://openlibrary.org/search.json?title=Turning%20Pages&author=Frederick
- **Tomoko Aoyama and Barbara Hartley, eds., _Girl Reading Girl in Japan_ (Routledge, 2010; dated 2009 in some records)**. https://doi.org/10.4324/9780203869062
- **Deborah M. Shamoon, _Passionate Friendship: The Aesthetics of Girls' Culture in Japan_ (University of Hawai'i Press, March 2012, 208 pp.)** traces girls' print culture from the 1920s–30s magazines to the 1970s "revolution" in shōjo manga. In a private, school-centred girls' culture, a discourse favouring **"S relationships"** (passionate friendship between girls) over heterosexual romance emerged. These are linked to postwar boys'-love manga for girls, which share "the same preference for homogeneity" and give girls a protected space away from patriarchal pressure. https://uhpress.hawaii.edu/title/passionate-friendship-the-aesthetics-of-girls-culture-in-japan
- **J. Keith Vincent, _Two-Timing Modernity: Homosocial Narrative in Modern Japanese Fiction_ (Harvard University Asia Center, 2012)**. Vincent is also co-author of 『ゲイ・スタディーズ』 (1997) and a participant in the 1996–97 Japanese queer special issues (see 4h). https://doi.org/10.1163/9781684175284
- **Hiromi Tsuchiya Dollase, _Age of Shōjo: The Emergence, Evolution, and Power of Japanese Girls' Magazine Fiction_ (SUNY Press, 2019)**: girls' fiction from the 1900s to the 1980s. It covers Meiji adaptations of *Little Women*, the young women writers of the 1910s, the boom of the 1920s–30s, state interference in wartime, and Yoshiya Nobuko, Kitagawa Chiyo, Morita Tama, Kawabata, Himuro Saeko and Tanabe Seiko. The shōjo's "immature" marginality let writers voice resistance "without worrying about the reaction of authorities", on topics including **same-sex love**, feminism and socialism. https://sunypress.edu/Books/A/Age-of-Shojo2
- **Kathryn Hemmann, _Manga Cultures and the Female Gaze_ (Palgrave Macmillan, 2020)** (Open Library). https://openlibrary.org/search.json?title=Manga%20Cultures%20and%20the%20Female%20Gaze&author=Hemmann

## Telling details

- **The prize that the debut lost.** Kobayashi Hideo, founder of modern Japanese criticism, debuted with a *second*-prize essay; first prize in Kaizō's 1929 contest went to the young Communist Miyamoto Kenji, later JCP chairman. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/小林秀雄_(批評家)
- **Kobayashi's refusal to repent** (12 Jan 1946): "僕は無智だから反省なぞしない。利巧な奴はたんと反省してみるがいいじゃないか。" This was said to the young Kindai bungaku men who had just put him on their war-responsibility list. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/近代文学_(雑誌) ; https://journals.openedition.org/ebisu/2038
- **The essay that waited for its author's death.** Takuboku's 「時代閉塞の現状」 (1910) was written for the Asahi and never printed while he lived. https://kotobank.jp/word/時代閉塞の現状
- **Seitō's scandal mechanics.** A 19-year-old member drank a "five-coloured cocktail" at the bar Maison Kōnosu while asking for an advertisement, and wrote about it, and about visiting Yoshiwara, in the magazine. The press then turned "新しい女" into a jeer, and Tsuda Umeko forbade her students to join. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/青鞜 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/平塚らいてう
- **Seitō's first run was 1,000 copies; the "new women" changed their constitution in October 1913** from "developing women's literature" to "awakening women". That is a clean marker of criticism turning into activism. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/青鞜
- **Kaizō's first issue flopped** (30,000 printed at 35 sen, many returned) until it went all-in on "reconstruction" from issue 4. https://kotobank.jp/word/改造
- **Shirakaba was banned at its own school.** Gakushūin proscribed the magazine as the work of idlers, and its founders funded it at 2 yen a month each from 1908. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/白樺派
- **Yasuda's "aesthetics of defeat"** is the dictionary phrase (〈敗北の美学〉, Sekai Daihyakka), and the postwar verdict was "singer of the aesthetics of the battlefield". He then spent 23 years writing quietly at his Kyoto villa 身余堂 (Narutaki), which Satō Haruo praised as the "western" counterpart to the Shisendō (「東の詩仙堂」と並べて「西の身余堂」). Kawabata is reported to have said it outdid the Shisendō. https://kotobank.jp/word/保田与重郎 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/保田與重郎
- **Nagai Kafū was enrolled in the Patriotic Association without being asked**, and noted in his diary that he disliked its president Tokutomi Sohō. Only Nakazato Kaizan and Uchida Hyakken actually refused. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本文学報国会
- **Only two of the commissioned "Five Principles" novels were ever finished**: Dazai's 『惜別』 (about Lu Xun in Sendai) and Morimoto Kaoru's 『女の一生』, the latter staged for five days in April 1945. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本文学報国会
- **Kawabata's girls' novel was a correspondence course.** Kawabata wrote to Nakazato on 14 Sept 1937: "Otome no minato is getting rougher… please keep the tone of the start". On 16 Oct 1937: "I've decided to keep the war out and set it before the war." The letters surfaced in 1984 in the complete works, and in 2018 stylometry called the companion novel 『花日記』 a co-authorship. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/乙女の港 ; https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390282679400709504
- **Kawabata as accuser.** It was Kawabata's own testimony that started the theory that Yokomitsu ghostwrote Kikuchi Kan's 『受難華』; in 2020 a seven-classifier ensemble found for Kikuchi. https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390285697608680960
- **The schoolgirl slang before "S"**: a 1910 magazine described girls proposing through a "御連中" (committee of 4–6 friends). The chosen junior accepted by tying on a purple or crimson ribbon, after which the pair called each other "お目さん". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/エス_(文化)
- **Yoshiya Nobuko's 『花物語』 was first offered to 『少女の友』**, whose editor rejected it as unsuitable and wrote to persuade her to stop submitting. Shōjo gahō took it instead, and it was planned for 7 installments but ran from 1916 to 1924. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/花物語_(吉屋信子)
- **The textbook canon is younger than you think.** 「羅生門」, 「こころ」 and 「舞姫」 never appeared in a prewar textbook. Rashōmon entered in 1956 as a 2nd-year text in one compulsory book and two electives, and reached every 国語総合 textbook only in 2003. Teaching hours for Maihime nearly doubled between 1973 (6) and 2006 (10–11). https://petit.lib.yamaguchi-u.ac.jp/28948/files/165692 ; https://petit.lib.yamaguchi-u.ac.jp/8208/files/164355
- **A feminist critic who started as a revue fan.** Komashaku Kimi spent 1940–41 following the OSK male-role star Akizuki Emiko, and wartime didn't stop her. At 80 she taught a seminar pairing the girls' revue with Sōseki as forms that "transcend sex". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/駒尺喜美
- **Manga criticism as family revenge**: the leading theorist of panel-and-line analysis is Sōseki's grandson, who long resented the label "漱石の孫". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/夏目房之介
- **The New Academism was named by a newspaper**, on 23 Jan 1984, not by its members. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ニュー・アカデミズム
- **Hasumi on Mishima**: at Gakushūin middle school Hasumi read *Confessions of a Mask* and "despised its author's lack of athletic ability". He became Tōdai's president and, at 80, a Mishima Prize winner. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/蓮實重彦
- **The Yuriika "shōjo manga" issue reprinted within the month** (July 1981 special, 2nd printing 20 July 1981). In 2018–19 a yuri issue gave 『SFマガジン』 its first-ever third printing, and in 2019 『文藝』 made its first third printing since 1933. https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1971430859772352057 ; https://kai-you.net/article/65270 ; https://nlab.itmedia.co.jp/cont/articles/3293650/
- **Commas as the national fingerprint.** Japanese stylometry's signature discovery (Jin, Kabashima and Murakami 1993 onward) is that a modern writer's **読点 placement** is more individual than vocabulary. Commas have since been used on Kawabata, Kikuchi, Mishima and Uno Kōji. https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1520009408498432768
- **A library's 5,500 books removed on one person's complaint** (Sakai, 2008), the template for later BL panics. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ボーイズラブ
- **The yaoi debate was fought in a mini-comi** (CHOISIR, 1992–96), not in an academic journal. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ボーイズラブ
- **Maruyama's 1961 Iwanami shinsho sold over a million copies**; postwar political theory reached a mass readership. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/丸山眞男

## Uncertain / conflicting

- **End of 『日本浪曼派』**: March 1938 (Nipponica; ja.wp) vs **August 1938** (Daijisen, Nikkoku, Sekai Daihyakka, Britannica, Yamakawa). Both sides agree on 29 issues. https://kotobank.jp/word/日本浪曼派
- **千葉亀雄's 「新感覚派の誕生」**: ja.wp has 『世紀』 November 1924. Nipponica on Kotobank prints "1938.11", evidently a typo, since the school dates from 1924. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新感覚派 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/新感覚派
- **Etō Jun's birth year**: 1932 (Daijisen; 20-seiki jinmei jiten, 25 Dec 1932) vs 1933 (Nipponica, 25 Dec 1933). https://kotobank.jp/word/江藤淳
- **Maeda Ai's birth year**: 1931 (ja.wp, citing 『前田愛著作集』 vol. 6; Nipponica) vs 1932 (20-seiki jinmei jiten). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/前田愛_(文芸評論家)
- **Karatani's birthday**: 6 Aug 1941 (Nihon jinmei daijiten) vs 16 Aug 1941 (Britannica). https://kotobank.jp/word/柄谷行人
- **『構造と力』 sales**: 150,000 (Kotobank, cited by ja.wp) vs 600,000 (井狩春男 2002). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/構造と力
- **Hasumi's 『表層批評宣言』**: one ja.wp passage gives November 1979 and another "1985年、『表層批評宣言』刊行". The 1985 date is probably the bunko edition. 『反=日本語論』 is dated May 1977 (book) and 1978 (prize) in different places. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/蓮實重彦
- **Yasumoto's Uji-chapter study**: 1957 (『心理学評論』 2(1), per Tsuchiyama and Murakami) vs "安本(1958)" (Kitahara, Ritsumeikan). They may be two related publications.
- **「山月記」 first textbook use**: 1951 (Waseda, Hori; a Dōshisha paper per search snippet) vs 1950, Sanseidō 2nd-year text (a Hiroshima University document seen only as a search snippet, not opened).
- **中島梓 『コミュニケーション不全症候群』**: ja.wp やおい says 1995. CiNii shows the Chikuma first edition in **1991** and a 1995 edition, which is evidently the bunko. Use 1991.
- **主体性論争 dates**: Sekai Daihyakka has 1946–49; ja.wp has the late 1940s to the late 1950s.
- **Seitō's last issue**: ja.wp gives publication "until February 1916" (52 issues) but also says the indefinite suspension followed Itō Noe's departure in April 1916.
- **Kotobank 白樺派 entries** give April 1910 – August 1923 for 『白樺』; ja.wp says only "spring 1910".
- **Reichert's argument** ("heterosexualization… as a key component of literary modernity") comes from a publisher blurb quoted in a search result. Stanford University Press's site blocked automated access, and the Goodreads blurb I opened gives only the opening line. Treat it as probable, not verified.
- **溝口彰子 『BL進化論』**: the CiNii record has no date. My recollection is Ōta Shuppan, 2015; unverified.
- **Azuma's English translation**: the *Choice* review (August 2009) confirms a 2009 English edition. The publisher (University of Minnesota Press) is from memory; Crossref also shows a Yale UP chapter "Database Animals" in a later anthology.
- **Pflugfelder dates**: Open Library has a 1999 first publication (cloth); UC Press's live page shows the 2007 paperback; Crossref's online record is 2000.
- **Aoyama and Hartley**: Open Library has 2009 as first, a review says "New York: Routledge, 2009", and Crossref gives 2012 for the e-book. Use 2009/2010.
- **Kobayashi's oft-quoted line** in 「様々なる意匠」, "批評とは竟に己れの夢を懐疑的に語る事ではないのか" ("Is criticism not, in the end, to speak sceptically of one's own dream?"), is quoted from memory. A web search did not surface the text, and it was not checked against the essay. A Shinchōsha zenshu page exists but was not opened.
- **The textbook-system start date**: ja.wp 国定教科書 gives 1903 (the law) and Kotobank Mypedia "from 1903". The first state textbooks were actually used from 1904; the bullet above says "1903/04" to cover both.
- **Kotobank 丸山真男** gives 「超国家主義の論理と心理」 only as "1946". That it appeared in the **May 1946 『世界』** is my recollection plus Müller's general placement; the issue month was not verified in an opened source.
- **"Japan's first" claims** (first yuri SF anthology, the SF Magazine "first third printing") are the publishers' and the press's own claims.
