# 18 — Gender politics, girls' culture and censorship in Japan, 1912–present (real history only)

Research notes for the alternate-history project. Every bullet is a real-world fact with the page it was read from. Japanese Wikipedia (ja.wp) pages were read as raw wikitext (current revision, October 2026); Kotobank entries were read in full. "Not opened" marks a source cited second-hand. Nearly all dates here fall after 1 January 1873, so they are Gregorian; the two pre-1873 items are flagged in the Uncertain section.

Method note: the shared WebSearch budget for this run ran out partway through. After that, pages were opened only at known URLs (Wikipedia, Kotobank, government and repository pages). Some gaps below (shōjo-magazine circulations, a documented *fuseji* instance of 革命, the 1931 House of Peers vote count) are therefore left open and marked.

## 1. Taishō feminism

### Seitō 『青鞜』 and the Seitōsha 青鞜社
- *Seitō* ran from September 1911 (Meiji 44) to February 1916 (Taishō 5), 52 issues. The editors were Hiratsuka Raichō 平塚らいてう, then Itō Noe 伊藤野枝. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/青鞜
- The name translates "Bluestocking". Ikuta Chōkō 生田長江 suggested both the magazine and its name to Hiratsuka Haru 平塚明 (her real name). Five founders, all from or near Nihon Joshi Daigakkō: Hiratsuka, Yasumochi Yoshiko 保持研子, Nakano Hatsuko 中野初子, Kiuchi Teiko 木内錠子 and Mozume Kazuko 物集和子. The first issue was printed in 1,000 copies. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/青鞜
- The founding membership was 18 *sha'in* (incl. Tamura Toshiko 田村俊子, Nogami Yaeko 野上弥生子). There were 7 *sanjoin* supporters, including Yosano Akiko 与謝野晶子, Hasegawa Shigure 長谷川時雨 and Mori Shigeko 森しげ子 (Mori Ōgai's wife). Raichō's mother's money and members' dues were the base funding. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/青鞜
- First issue: Naganuma (later Takamura) Chieko 長沼智恵子 drew the cover. Yosano Akiko's poem 「そぞろごと」 opened the issue; it contains the line 「山の動く日来る」 ("the day the mountains move is coming"). Hiratsuka's manifesto began 「元始、女性は太陽であった」 ("In the beginning, woman was the sun"). It was the first time she used the pen-name "Raichō" (らいてう). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/青鞜 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/平塚らいてう ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/与謝野晶子
- The first office was Mozume Kazuko's room in her father Mozume Takami's house, Hongō Hayashi-chō 9 (now Sendagi, Bunkyō). After the April 1912 ban the group was evicted from it. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/青鞜
- Bans (*hakkin*):
  - April 1912 (vol. 2 no. 4), for Araki Iku's 荒木郁 adultery story 「手紙」.
  - June 1915, for Harada Satsuki's 原田皐月 essay on abortion.
  - Ja.wp's Seitōsha page also attributes the 1915 stoppage partly to the abortion-essay ban. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/青鞜 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/青鞜社
- October 1913: the society's rules changed from 「女流文学の発達を計り」 to 「女子の覚醒を促し」, from literature to women's awakening. The October 1914 third-anniversary issue reprinted a warning from the head of the Home Ministry's Police Bureau (警保局長). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/青鞜
- January 1915: Itō Noe took over, declaring the society "without rules, without policy" (無規則、無方針). Under her the magazine hosted debates on chastity (貞操), abortion (堕胎) and licensed prostitution (売娼). It stopped when Itō left for Ōsugi Sakae 大杉栄. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/青鞜
- Hiratsuka Raichō: born 10 February 1886 in Kōjimachi, Tokyo; died 24 May 1971. In March 1908 she and Morita Sōhei 森田草平 were found in the snow near Obana Pass, the "Shiobara/Baien incident" (塩原事件・煤煙事件), an attempted love-suicide that made her notorious. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/平塚らいてう
- Raichō lived with, and later married, the younger painter Okumura Hiroshi 奥村博 (later 博史, 1889–1964). Her essay title 「『燕』という名の起り」 concerns the coinage of *wakai tsubame*, "young swallow", for a younger male lover. Children: Akemi 曙生 (1915) and Atsufumi 敦史 (1917). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/平塚らいてう
- Itō Noe: born 21 January 1895 in Imajuku, Fukuoka. Married Tsuji Jun 辻潤 (1915–23), then partner of Ōsugi Sakae. Killed 16 September 1923, after the Great Kantō Earthquake, in the Amakasu incident. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/伊藤野枝
- Yosano Akiko: 7 December 1878 – 29 May 1942. She published 「君死にたまふことなかれ」 in *Myōjō* in September 1904. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/与謝野晶子

### The motherhood-protection debate 母性保護論争 (1918–19)
- The trigger was Yosano Akiko's piece 「女子の徹底した独立」 in *Fujin Kōron*, March 1918. It attacked Ellen Key–style state maternity protection as 「奴隷道徳」 and 「依頼主義」 (slave morality, dependency). Women without economic security, she wrote, should avoid marriage and childbirth. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/母性保護論争
- Hiratsuka replied that bearing and rearing children is 「社会的な、国家的な仕事」, a social and national task. If officials, soldiers and Diet members are paid, she argued, a mother paid by the state is no more a recipient of charity. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/母性保護論争
- Yamakawa Kikue 山川菊栄 took the socialist position: neither side touched the economic system. She also argued that housework is unpaid labour. Yamada Waka 山田わか sided with Hiratsuka but held that woman's vocation is the home. Men who joined included Shimanaka Yūzō 島中雄三 and Yamada Kakichi. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/母性保護論争
- Standard documentary source: 香内信子編『資料 母性保護論争』 (Domesu, 1984). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/母性保護論争

### Shin Fujin Kyōkai 新婦人協会 (New Woman Association)
- Founding dates:
  - Announced by Hiratsuka on **24 November 1919**, in a lecture 「婦人の団結を望む」 at the Ōsaka Asahi–sponsored All-Kansai Women's Congress (全関西婦人大会).
  - Announced in Tokyo on 19 December 1919.
  - Formal inaugural meeting on **28 March 1920** at Ueno Seiyōken. Many accounts date the founding from this meeting.
  - Dissolved at an extraordinary general meeting on **8 December 1922**.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新婦人協会
- The three directors were Hiratsuka, Ichikawa Fusae 市川房枝 and Oku Mumeo 奥むめお; Tajima Hide 田島ひで was secretary. The organ was 『女性同盟』, first issue 9 October 1920. Branches: Nagoya, Ōsaka, Kōbe, Fukuyama, Mihara, Hiroshima. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新婦人協会
- Goals: revise Article 5 of the Public Peace Police Law, and restrict marriage by men with venereal disease (花柳病男子結婚制限). Yosano mocked the second goal. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新婦人協会
- February 1920: a petition with 2,057 signatures went to the 42nd Diet. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新婦人協会
- November 1920, the "Hiroshima incident": the prefecture pressured women schoolteachers to quit the association's branches, citing a 1917 directive (訓令第11号) on teachers and politics. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新婦人協会
- Ichikawa resigned as director on 26 June 1921 and left for the United States. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新婦人協会

### Article 5, Public Peace Police Law 治安警察法 (1900) and its 1922 revision
- The law was promulgated 10 March 1900 and in force 30 March 1900. Article 5 barred several groups from joining political associations: serving soldiers, police, priests, teachers and students, **women** (女子) and minors. Para. 2 barred women and minors from attending or organising political meetings (政談集会). The law was abolished by Imperial Ordinance 638 of 21 November 1945. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新婦人協会 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/治安警察法
- 1921: the revision passed the lower house. It was defeated in the House of Peers on 26 March 1921 after a speech by Baron Fujimura Yoshirō 藤村義朗. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新婦人協会
- 1922 (45th Diet):
  - On 17 February, Sakamoto Makoto 坂本真琴 and Oku Mumeo called on Baron Fujimura at his home in Nakano and won his support.
  - 18 March: the lower house passed the bill.
  - 25 March, the last day of the session: the House of Peers passed it at **11:50 p.m.**
  - Promulgated 20 April 1922 (治安警察法 page); the Shin Fujin Kyōkai page gives 10 May as 「公布施行」.
  - Only para. 2 changed: women could now attend and organise political meetings. Joining parties (para. 1) stayed banned until 1945.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/新婦人協会 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/治安警察法 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A5%B3%E6%80%A7%E5%8F%82%E6%94%BF%E6%A8%A9-895295

### Fusen Kakutoku Dōmei 婦選獲得同盟 and Ichikawa Fusae
- Founded **13 December 1924** as 婦人参政権獲得期成同盟会, out of the political section of the Tokyo Federation of Women's Organisations (東京連合婦人会, formed October 1923 after the earthquake). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/婦選獲得同盟 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A9%A6%E4%BA%BA%E5%8F%82%E6%94%BF%E6%A8%A9%E9%81%8B%E5%8B%95-1201440
- Officers: Kubushiro Ochimi 久布白落実 (general director), Ichikawa (business director), Nakazawa Miyo (treasurer). There were 12 central committee members, including Sakamoto Makoto, Kaneko (later Yamataka) Shigeri 金子しげり, Kawasaki Natsu and Gauntlett Tsune. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/婦選獲得同盟
- 29 March 1925: the Universal Manhood Suffrage Law (men 25 and over) passed. Ichikawa wrote in her diary that she would remember the day as the one on which women's political rights were taken away. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/婦選獲得同盟
- Renamed **婦選獲得同盟 on 19 April 1925** under the slogan 「婦選なくして普選なし」. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/婦選獲得同盟
- 1925 onward: it presented three bills to every Diet, from the 50th on: women's right of association (結社権), local civic rights (公民権) and national suffrage (参政権). https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A9%A6%E4%BA%BA%E5%8F%82%E6%94%BF%E6%A8%A9%E9%81%8B%E5%8B%95-1201440
- 1927: organ 『婦選』 founded (later 『女性展望』). From 1930 it held seven All-Japan Women's Suffrage Congresses (全日本婦選大会). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/婦選獲得同盟
- Wartime:
  - 28 September 1937: joined the Japan Federation of Women's Organisations (日本婦人団体連盟).
  - 18 February 1939: the 婦人時局研究会 was formed.
  - **21 September 1940**: the league dissolved into it.
  - 20 August 1941: 『女性展望』 ceased.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/婦選獲得同盟
- Ichikawa Fusae: 15 May 1893 – 11 February 1981; member of the House of Councillors 1953–71 and 1974–81. She founded 新日本婦人同盟 on 3 November 1945. She was **purged on 24 March 1947** for having been a director of 大日本言論報国会, and de-purged 13 October 1950. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/市川房枝

### The 1930/1931 civic-rights bills (verification)
- Sekai Daihyakka: 「婦人公民権法案は1931年に衆議院を通過したが，貴族院で阻止された」. After 1933 women's suffrage bills were no longer even tabled. https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A9%A6%E4%BA%BA%E5%85%AC%E6%B0%91%E6%A8%A9%E6%B3%95%E6%A1%88-1405552
- Yamakawa Nihonshi Shōjiten and Ōbunsha Nihonshi Jiten: **1930 (Shōwa 5)** the women's civic-rights bill passed the lower house; **the next year** it was rejected (否決) by the House of Peers. https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A9%A6%E4%BA%BA%E5%8F%82%E6%94%BF%E6%A8%A9%E9%81%8B%E5%8B%95-1201440
- Ja.wp (Ichikawa) says the bill passed the lower house in 1930 and failed through the Peers' opposition. Ja.wp (女性参政権) says a conditional bill passed the lower house in **1931** and was killed by the Peers. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/市川房枝 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/女性参政権
- Reconciliation, consistent with all of the above: there were two bills.
  - 1930 (58th, special Diet): a members' civic-rights bill passed the lower house. Sharon Nolte's footnotes cite lower-house committee sessions on **9 May 1930** and House of Peers committee sessions on **12 May 1930**.
  - 1931 (59th Diet): the Hamaguchi (Minseitō) cabinet's own limited bill (Home Minister Adachi Kenzō spoke in the lower house on **11 February 1931**) passed the lower house and was voted down by the Peers.
  - Provisions of the 1931 government bill per en.wp: women 25 and over could vote in city, town and village elections, and hold local office only with their husband's consent.
  - Scholarly article: Sharon H. Nolte, "Women's Rights and Society's Needs: Japan's 1931 Suffrage Bill", *Comparative Studies in Society and History* 28:4 (Oct 1986), 690–714 (abstract and notes page opened).
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/comparative-studies-in-society-and-history/article/womens-rights-and-societys-needs-japans-1931-suffrage-bill/572523B478A48233F5A12156021B1B1E ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_suffrage_in_Japan
- The Peers' vote of **184 to 62** appears only in search snippets (Find a Grave / Tokyo Weekender); the Tokyo Weekender page would not load. **Unverified.**

### Shōjo magazines
- Founding dates:
  - 『少女界』: April 1902 (Kinkōdō), the first magazine with 少女 in its title.
  - 『少女世界』: September 1906 (Hakubunkan).
  - 『少女の友』: February 1908 issue, to June 1955 (Jitsugyō no Nihon-sha).
  - 『少女画報』: January 1912 to February 1942 (Tōkyō-sha, the sister magazine of 婦人画報).

### Yoshiya Nobuko 吉屋信子 and 『花物語』
- Yoshiya Nobuko: 12 January 1896 – 11 July 1973. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/吉屋信子
- *Hana monogatari*:
  - The first story, 「鈴蘭」, was a reader's submission accepted for *Shōjo gahō*, July 1916 issue. Editor Wada Koe 和田古江 promised a series.
  - It ran intermittently in *Shōjo gahō* 1916–1924. Three more stories appeared in *Shōjo kurabu* July 1925 – March 1926 (illustrated by Nakahara).
  - 54 stories in all; usually counted as 52, because the last two (「薊の花」「からたちの花」) were left out of most book editions.
  - Planned as 7 episodes; readers' letters kept it going.
  - Book editions: Rakuyōdō 3 vols 1920; Kōransha 1923–26; a 1939 Jitsugyō no Nihon-sha edition illustrated by Nakahara.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/花物語_(吉屋信子) ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/吉屋信子
- *Shōjo no tomo* had earlier returned a Yoshiya manuscript as unsuitable, with a letter urging her to stop submitting. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/花物語_(吉屋信子)
- Later stories added breakups, suicide and double suicide, which drew criticism from educators. Some readers objected too: the teenage Matsuda Keiko 松田瓊子 wrote in her diary that she was 「あきれもし、又なさけなくなった」. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/花物語_(吉屋信子)
- 1919: 『屋根裏の二処女』, autobiographical, set in a YWCA hostel, depicts love between women openly (book 1920). The same year 『地の果まで』 won the Ōsaka Mainichi prize. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/吉屋信子
- **Monma Chiyo 門馬千代** (b. 17 February 1899, Koishikawa; d. 25 August 1988), a mathematics teacher:
  - January 1923: met Yoshiya through Yamataka (Kaneko) Shigeri.
  - 1928: travelled with her to Europe via Manchuria and the USSR, seen off at Tokyo Station on 25 September by Yosano Akiko.
  - 1931: left teaching to run the household and act as secretary.
  - **1957: became Yoshiya's adopted daughter** (養子縁組).
  - Chiyo cut Yoshiya's trademark bob with clippers and scissors bought in Solingen on the European trip.
  - After Yoshiya's death the estate went to Kamakura city (吉屋信子記念館), and Chiyo lived in a corner of it.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/吉屋信子

### *Esu* (S, "Class S") and the double-suicide panic
- *Esu* (エス, from "sister") is the term for passionate attachments between schoolgirls, or between a girl and a woman teacher. It emerged in the 1910s and displaced older slang such as 「お目」 and 「おでや」. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/エス_(文化) (citing 赤枝香奈子『近代日本における女同士の親密な関係』2011; 今田絵里香『「少女」の社会史』2007)
- *Murasaki*, October 1910, describes the "ome" custom: an upper-year girl picks a new pupil and asks her clique to broker. If the younger girl ties the offered purple or red ribbon in her hair, the pair are 「お目さん」. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/エス_(文化)
- Unwritten rules of *esu*: one-to-one; exclusive; lasting; pure; mirror-like imitation. One senior and one junior; a senior could not have two. Relationships continued after graduation were disparaged with 「老嬢」 (old maid). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/エス_(文化)
- 1908–1910: of **501 love-suicides (情死), 18 (3.6%) were between women** (礫川全次『ゲイの民俗学』, cited by ja.wp). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/レズビアン
- **1911**: two graduates of a girls' school drowned themselves together at **Oyashirazu 親不知**, Niigata. Press coverage is said to have turned attention from male *nanshoku* to female homosexuality. From about then the "perverse sexual desire" (変態性欲) framing spread, lasting until the 1970s. Only a minority of girls' attachments were classed "true" homosexuality; most were treated as a "temporary" phase for educators to guide (善導). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/レズビアン ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/エス_(文化)
- **1933, Mt Mihara 三原山 (Izu Ōshima)**: students of Jissen Girls' School 実践女学校 jumped into the crater on **9 January and 12 February 1933**. The **same classmate** accompanied both. The coverage set off a wave: **129 people** threw themselves into the crater that year. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/三原山 (citing Ōshima town's history page)
- A 1928 letter to *Shōjo gahō* (September issue) shows the *esu* idiom: a third-year girl's anguished love for "M様", her neighbour in the class order. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/エス_(文化)

### The *moga* モダンガール
- *Moga* and *mobo* were 1920s urban types (roughly Taishō 9 – Shōwa 4) defined mainly by appearance. For women: Western dress with skirts just below the knee, cloche hat, **bobbed hair (断髪)**, plucked brows, lipstick and rouge. Conservative opinion called them frivolous. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/モボ・モガ
- Kon Wajirō 今和次郎 surveyed Ginza street dress in 1925 (published in *Fujin Kōron*), the founding of "modernology" 考現学. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/考現学 (The often-quoted "1% of Ginza women in Western dress" figure was **not** found on the pages opened.)

## 3. Takarazuka, the revues and *dansō*

### Takarazuka 宝塚
- Founded by Kobayashi Ichizō 小林一三 of the Minoo-Arima Electric Railway (later Hankyū), on the model of the Mitsukoshi boys' band and the Shirokiya girls' band. **July 1913**: 宝塚唱歌隊; renamed 宝塚少女歌劇養成会 in December 1913. The girls, mostly with only elementary schooling, were paid as much as university graduates. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宝塚歌劇団
- **First performance 1 April 1914**, in the Paradise Theatre (a converted indoor pool of the Takarazuka hot-spring resort), running to 31 May. The programme was 『ドンブラコ』 (a Momotarō opera), 『浮れ達磨』, the dance 『胡蝶』, orchestral pieces and choruses. Average audience that year was **1,100 a day** (free with spa admission). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宝塚歌劇団
- First Tokyo performance 26 May 1918 at the Imperial Theatre; the magazine 『歌劇』 founded the same year. In January 1919 the 宝塚音楽歌劇学校 was founded and the company renamed 宝塚少女歌劇団. In 1919 eight male students (incl. Shirai Tetsuzō and Kishida Tatsuya) were admitted to a short-lived men's section, disbanded after 10 months. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宝塚歌劇団
- Revues:
  - **1927**: Kishida Tatsuya's 『モン・パリ』, Japan's first revue (from 1 September; 16 scenes, 250 performers).
  - **August 1930**: Shirai Tetsuzō's 『パリゼット』, with 「すみれの花咲く頃」 (a German song, "Wenn der weiße Flieder wieder blüht", heard by Shirai as a French chanson).
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宝塚歌劇団
- **Otokoyaku**:
  - From 1924, with the move to the Grand Theatre and revue format, players began to dress as men. At first they pinned up their long hair under oversized hats.
  - Short hair came first at the rival Shōchiku (below). At Takarazuka, Kasugano Yachiyo 春日野八千代 was the first to use false eyelashes and permed hair.
  - In the 1930s Ashihara Kuniko 葦原邦子 and Sayo Fukuko 小夜福子 formed the star pairing.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/男役 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宝塚歌劇団
- The Tokyo Takarazuka Theatre (Yūrakuchō) opened **1 January 1934**. By 1933 the company had about 300 members. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東京宝塚劇場 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宝塚歌劇団
- Until 1939 members were legally "students in the research course" of the school, hence the in-house year count 「研○」. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宝塚歌劇団

### Shōchiku revue, Mizunoe Takiko 水の江瀧子 and the 1933 strike
- Mizunoe Takiko, "Tākī" (ターキー/タアキイ): born Miura Umeko 三浦ウメ子, 20 February 1915, Otaru; died 16 November 2009. She joined the Tokyo Shōchiku Gakugekibu (later SKD) in its first intake, 1928. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/水の江瀧子
- First "dansō no reijin" of the revue stage:
  - SKD's official history: she cut her hair short for 『松竹オン・パレード』 (September 1930), in silk hat and tuxedo.
  - Nakayama Chinatsu's research: that was only a fashionable bob. The truly mannish cut that caused a sensation came on **31 May 1931** in 『先生様はお人好し』.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/男役 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/水の江瀧子
- The nickname came from a 1931 revue in which, as a cowboy, she declared 「俺はミズノーエ・ターキーだぁ!」. Her fan club 水の江会 (from autumn 1931) reached **about 20,000 members**, including in Korea and Taiwan, with imperial-family and aristocratic fans. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/水の江瀧子
- **Momoiro sōgi 桃色争議 (1933)**, the "Tākī strike":
  - Shōchiku announced dismissals of musicians and wage cuts.
  - On 14 June 1933 the girls declared opposition and made the 18-year-old Mizunoe strike-committee chair.
  - On 15 June about **230** of them barricaded themselves in an inn at Yugawara hot springs.
  - In Ōsaka, 70-odd members led by Asuka Akiko (incl. the future Kasagi Shizuko) occupied a temple on **Kōyasan** on 28 June, under banners against the "trust".
  - Shōchiku fired Mizunoe, calling her a 「賊女」.
  - Settlement: Ōsaka on 8 July, Tokyo on 15 July. The strikers won a weekly day off and a minimum wage. Mizunoe was suspended.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/桃色争議
- Wartime: on her return from New York (around 1940), the authorities had **issued a ban on *dansō***, so she appeared in women's roles (per her 1988 memoir as cited by ja.wp). In December 1942 she founded her own troupe 「たんぽぽ」 and left Shōchiku in January 1943. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/水の江瀧子

### The 1935 Saijō Eriko / Masuda Fumiko incident
- Saijō Eriko 西条エリ子: real name Yokoo Eiko 横尾永子, born November 1911 in Asakusa. She entered Tokyo Shōchiku's 2nd intake in 1929 and was a **musume-yaku** (female-role) star, "the most beautiful revue dancer ever" according to Noguchi Fujio. She **did not join the 1933 strike and was expelled from the union**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/西条エリ子 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/桃色争議
- **1935**: she and the cross-dressing heiress **Masuda Fumiko 増田富美子** (a "男装の麗人") attempted a love-suicide. It was reported as a lesbian elopement. Contemporary pieces reprinted in 『戦前期同性愛関連文献集成』 vol. 3 (Fuji Shuppan 2006): 中野栄太郎「男装の麗人と西条エリ子 渦中に居合はして 同性愛死未遂のいきさつ」 (pp. 313–14) and 西条エリ子「男装の麗人・増田富美子の死を選ぶまで」 (pp. 315–17). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/西条エリ子
- Details from Jennifer Robertson, "Dying to Tell: Sexuality and Suicide in Imperial Japan", *Signs* 25:1 (1999), 1–35 (article itself **not opened**; read via a blog summary):
  - Robertson gives Masuda's chosen name as "Yasumare".
  - The two took sleeping pills together in a hotel after their families separated them; press intrusion brought help and both survived.
  - The press cast Saijō as victim of an obsessive fan and Masuda as transgressing an upper-class woman's duty to be 良妻賢母.
  https://alpennia.com/node/5746 ; DOI page https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/495412
- Saijō then went into film (Nikkatsu, 1935–36: 『うら街の交響楽』, 『緑の地平線』). In 1938 she married Kanematsu Renkichi 兼松廉吉, head of publicity at the Shōchiku girls' revue, and retired. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/西条エリ子

### Kawashima Yoshiko 川島芳子
- Born **24 May 1907** (Guangxu 33/4/12) in Beijing as Aisin-Gioro Xianyu 愛新覺羅顯㺭, 14th daughter of Prince Su (Shanqi 善耆). Adopted at 8 by Kawashima Naniwa 川島浪速 and educated in Japan (Matsumoto). Executed by firing squad as a traitor (漢奸) at Beiping No. 1 Prison on **25 March 1948**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/川島芳子 (citing Kotobank 20世紀日本人名事典)
- After a pistol suicide attempt at 17 she cut her hair, wore men's clothes and published a renunciation of womanhood, earning the label **「男装の麗人」**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/川島芳子
- **1932**: Muramatsu Shōfū's 村松梢風 novel 『男装の麗人』, modelled on her, was serialised in *Fujin Kōron*. **22 February 1933**: the *Asahi* reported her appointment as commander of the Jehol self-defence force 熱河自警団 (「男装の麗人川島芳子嬢…総司令に推さる」). She also wrote the words of a 1933 record 『キャラバンの鈴』 and a memoir piece 「僕は祖國を愛す」 for *Fujin Kōron*. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/川島芳子

### Wartime Takarazuka
- In 1934 titles began to reflect the war (『太平洋行進曲』, later 『揚子江』 and 『満州より北支へ』). In 1938 the Star Troupe was abolished, and in December school and company were separated. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宝塚歌劇団
- Foreign tours:
  - **2 October 1938 – 4 March 1939**: a "Cultural Mission to Germany and Italy" of 30 performers (56 people in all), which also played Poland.
  - **5 April – 4 July 1939**: a US mission of 40 performers.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宝塚歌劇団
- **1940**:
  - A Takarazuka branch of the 大日本国防婦人会 was set up, and **every student had to join**.
  - Troupes visited munitions plants and military hospitals.
  - Foreign-language titles disappeared.
  - In **October** 宝塚少女歌劇団 became 宝塚歌劇団 (the word *shōjo* dropped).
  - The two in-house magazines were suspended in 1940.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宝塚歌劇団
- **1944**: closure orders under the 決戦非常措置要綱.
  - The Tokyo theatre closed **1 March 1944** and was leased to the Army's accounts bureau.
  - The Grand Theatre's last show was on **4 March 1944**, 『翼の決戦』. Crowds queued across the bridge to Takarazuka-minamiguchi station, and police controlled them **with drawn swords** (宝塚五十年史).
  - The Grand Theatre then became barracks for the Navy (宝塚海軍航空隊, in fact training crews for *Kaiten* suicide torpedoes).
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東京宝塚劇場 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宝塚歌劇団
- Post-war:
  - The Grand Theatre reopened **22 April 1946** with 『カルメン』 and 『春のをどり』.
  - The Tokyo theatre was requisitioned by GHQ on 24 December 1945. It became the **Ernie Pyle Theatre** (8th Army) on 24 February 1946, closed to Japanese spectators, until **27 January 1955**.
  - Tokyo shows resumed 1 April 1947 at the Nichigeki, Imperial Theatre and other houses.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東京宝塚劇場 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宝塚歌劇団

## 4. Militarist-era censorship

### Peace Preservation Law 治安維持法
- **Law No. 46, promulgated 22 April 1925**, in force from May 1925 (ja.wp: 「4月公布、5月施行」; 12 May in standard references). Passed together with manhood suffrage, the "carrot and stick". It punished organising or knowingly joining an association aimed at **changing the *kokutai* (国体) or denying private property**, with up to 10 years. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/治安維持法
- **1928 revision** by emergency Imperial Ordinance No. 129 of **29 June 1928**, after the bill had died in the Diet. It split "kokutai change" from "denial of private property" and raised the maximum for organisers and leaders of kokutai-changing associations to **death**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/治安維持法
- 1941 full revision (Law No. 54, 10 March 1941; 7 → 65 articles) added "preparatory associations" and preventive detention. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/治安維持法
- Uses:
  - First applied in Korea (Korean Communist Party, from late November 1925). In the home islands, conventionally first in the Kyoto Gakuren case (Jan–Apr 1926, 38 arrested).
  - 3.15 mass arrest of communists, 15 March 1928.
  - 2nd Ōmoto incident, 8 December 1935: first use against a religion.
  - Yokohama incident (1942): about 60 editors arrested and 4 dead.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/治安維持法
- A victims' association counts **194 deaths from torture** and 1,503 deaths in prison from illness (incl. Kobayashi Takiji). Abolished **15 October 1945** (Ordinance 575) after the SCAP civil-liberties directive of 4 October. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/治安維持法

### Home Ministry censorship and *fuseji* 伏字
- The Home Minister censored newspapers, magazines and books under the Newspaper Law 新聞紙法 and Publication Law 出版法; the work was done by the Police Bureau's Book Section 警保局図書課. The two grounds were **安寧秩序紊乱** (disturbing public order: dangerous thought) and **風俗壊乱** (corrupting morals). https://gendai.media/articles/-/52783 (Tsujita Masanori)
- Penalties: a *hakkin* banned sale and free distribution, and police seized copies and printing moulds. Lesser cases got partial deletion or a warning. All Home Ministry censorship records before the 1923 earthquake were destroyed in it. https://gendai.media/articles/-/52783
- 1930 criteria for 風俗壊乱 included 「猥雑なる事項」, **「乱倫なる事項」** (sexual immorality), abortion methods, cruelty, and sensational reports of pleasure quarters. https://gendai.media/articles/-/52783
- Tsujita: after the 1931 Manchurian Incident the focus shifted from morals to order, chiefly lèse-majesté. https://gendai.media/articles/-/52783
- Censors viewed publishers' own *fuseji* with suspicion as an incitement to guess, and banned works for them. https://gendai.media/articles/-/52783
- *Fuseji* (○, ×, *, …; 『改造』 used 「ゝ」): under the Peace Preservation Law, words such as 「社会主義」「共産主義」「無政府主義」 were commonly blanked even in ordinary fiction. Publishers did this themselves to avoid bans. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/伏字
- Standard works: Jonathan E. Abel, *Redacted: The Archives of Censorship in Transwar Japan* (UC Press 2012); 牧義之『伏字の文化史』 (森話社 2014). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/伏字
- In December 1940 the Book Section became the Censorship Section 検閲課, doubling as the Cabinet Information Bureau's 4th Department, 1st Section. The 1939 Film Law (Law No. 66, 5 April 1939) brought censorship to the script stage. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本における検閲
- A *specific documented example of 革命 printed as ×× is not verified here* (see Uncertain).

### Wartime controls on entertainment and dress, 1939–42
- **Luxury ban, the 「七・七禁令」**: the Commerce and Agriculture ministries' ordinance (商工省・農林省令第2号) under the National Mobilisation Law, issued **6 July 1940**, in force **7 July 1940**. It banned making and selling luxury goods. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/奢侈品等製造販売制限規則
- **Dance halls**: under the 1940 ban, Tokyo's dance halls closed on **31 October 1940**, all packed on the last night. The Florida in Akasaka Tameike, Tokyo's grandest, ran 1929–1940. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ダンスホール
- **Stage names**: the Film Law (1939) required registration, and the Home Ministry announced a purge of names that were frivolous or "un-Japanese". On **28 March 1940** film and record companies were called in, and the renamings followed:
  - Dick Mine ディック・ミネ → 三根耕一
  - Miss Columbia ミス・コロムビア → 松原操
  - Misu Wakana → 玉松ワカナ
  - Bucky Shirakata → 白片力
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/敵性語
- 1939–40: romantic novels such as *Tess* were removed from foreign-language textbooks as contrary to the "holy war". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/敵性語
- Before 1945, blood, gambling and kissing were cut from films. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/時代劇

### Dai Nippon Fujinkai 大日本婦人会
- Founded **2 February 1942** by merging the 愛国婦人会 (1901, Home Ministry), the 大日本連合婦人会 (1931, Education Ministry) and the 大日本国防婦人会 (1932, Army and Navy). President: 山内禎子. Aims: 「国防思想ノ普及」「家庭生活ノ整備刷新」「国防ニ必要ナル訓練」. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大日本婦人会 (citing Nipponica, Yoneda Sayoko)
- Membership: every woman except the unmarried under 20. **21.49 million members (March 1944)**. Dues ¥0.60 a year, plus subsidies. Dissolved **13 June 1945**. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大日本婦人会

## 5. The Occupation (1945–52)

### Suffrage and the 10 April 1946 election
- 10 October 1945: the Shidehara cabinet decided on women's suffrage. 11 October: MacArthur's "Five Great Reforms" included enfranchising women. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/普通選挙法 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/女性参政権
- 21 November 1945: the Public Peace Police Law was abolished, giving women freedom of association. **17 December 1945**: the revised House of Representatives Election Law was promulgated. It gave the vote to all men and women 20 and over (25 for candidacy). Women's local suffrage followed on 27 September 1946. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/女性参政権 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%A1%86%E8%AD%B0%E9%99%A2%E8%AD%B0%E5%93%A1%E9%81%B8%E6%8C%99%E6%B3%95-76825
- 22nd general election, **10 April 1946** (the last under the Meiji constitution):
  - **2,770 candidates, of whom 79 were women**.
  - **39 women elected** out of 464 seated in the first count (Asahi 選挙大観). The 468 seats less 2 for Okinawa left 466, and 2 seats went to re-elections.
  - Party breakdown of the women: Liberal 4, Progressive 6, Socialist 8, Communist 1, minor parties 11, independents 9.
  - Electorate 36,878,420, of whom 20,557,668 women.
  - Turnout 72.08% overall: **men 78.52%, women 66.97%**.
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/第22回衆議院議員総選挙
- Kyodo's glossary: about **13.8 million women voted** for the first time; 39 women = 8.4% of those elected, among them Katō Shizue 加藤シヅエ. https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A5%B3%E6%80%A7%E5%8F%82%E6%94%BF%E6%A8%A9-895295
- 39 is also the figure in Sekai Daihyakka, Mypedia and Ōbunsha. **But** the Nipponica entry (大木基子) says **36**; see Uncertain. https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A9%A6%E4%BA%BA%E5%8F%82%E6%94%BF%E6%A8%A9%E9%81%8B%E5%8B%95-1201440 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A9%A6%E4%BA%BA%E5%8F%82%E6%94%BF%E6%A8%A9-124742 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A5%B3%E6%80%A7%E5%8F%82%E6%94%BF%E6%A8%A9-895295
- Women-only parties fielded candidates: 新日本婦人党 (吉田セイ) and 日本婦人党 (本多花子). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/第22回衆議院議員総選挙

### Beate Sirota Gordon and Articles 14 and 24
- Beate Sirota (25 October 1923, Vienna – 30 December 2012) was the daughter of the pianist Leo Sirota and grew up in Tokyo (1929–39). The family maid Koshiba Miyo 小柴美代, a fishing-boss's daughter from Enoura, Shizuoka, is often credited with showing her women's low status. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ベアテ・シロタ・ゴードン
- She joined GHQ Government Section on **25 December 1945**. On **4 February 1946** Whitney ordered the secret drafting of the MacArthur draft. **Six of the drafters were women.** Aged **22**, she drafted the women's-rights and social-welfare clauses, gathering foreign constitutions from Tokyo libraries. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ベアテ・シロタ・ゴードン
- Most of her text was cut, chiefly at Charles Kades's insistence. She recalled 「痛みを伴った悔しさ…それがいつしか涙に変わっていた」. Article 24 (individual dignity and equality of the sexes in family life; marriage 「両性の合意のみに基いて成立」) survives from her draft. Her role became widely known only in the 1990s. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ベアテ・シロタ・ゴードン ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本国憲法第24条
- The MacArthur draft was finished 12 February and handed over 13 February 1946. Government and GHQ negotiated overnight on 4–5 March. The government published the 「憲法改正草案要綱」 at 17:00 on 6 March 1946. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ベアテ・シロタ・ゴードン

### The 1947 Civil Code and the end of the *ie*
- The Meiji Civil Code's *ie* system (戸主 househead with powers over members' marriage and residence; single-heir 家督相続) was in force from **16 July 1898 to 2 May 1947**, "48 years 9½ months". https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/家制度
- It was suspended from **3 May 1947** by the 「日本国憲法の施行に伴う民法の応急的措置に関する法律」 (Law No. 74 of 1947). It was abolished by the **1947 Civil Code revision (Law No. 222 of 1947)**, which rewrote the family and inheritance books, effective **1 January 1948**. The new Family Register Law (Law No. 224, 22 December 1947) made the register unit a married couple and their unmarried children. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/家制度 ; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%AE%B6%E7%9D%A3%E7%9B%B8%E7%B6%9A-45595 ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/民法_(日本)

### SCAP censorship of "feudal" content
- From **9 October 1945** Asahi, Mainichi, Yomiuri, Tokyo and Nihon Sangyō were under pre-publication censorship. On **19 November 1945, 236 films** were banned and ordered burned as ultranationalist or militarist. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本における検閲
- CIE issued **13 prohibited categories** for films (popularly the 「チャンバラ禁止令」). Sword fighting was treated as militarist, and revenge stories as stirring hostility to the US. Stars switched genres: Bandō Tsumasaburō to 『王将』, Kataoka Chiezō to the 多羅尾伴内 detective series. Arashi Kanjūrō could only do swordless 右門捕物帳 mysteries. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/時代劇
- **15 January 1948**: the Kyoto studios of Shōchiku, Tōyoko and Daiei announced they would make no more period films, since "solving problems with the sword" would mislead a democratising public. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/時代劇
- **Kabuki**: GHQ held kabuki's "feudal loyalty" to be a root of militarism. *Kanadehon Chūshingura* was banned as encouraging vendetta. The ban was lifted **July 1947**, and the play was staged in **November 1947 at the Tōkyō Gekijō** (acts to 9, with acts 2 and 8 cut). It played to full houses, and the casting was reportedly at Bowers's instruction. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/仮名手本忠臣蔵
- **Faubion Bowers** (29 January 1917 – 20 November 1999): on a 1940 visit he wandered into the Kabukiza "thinking it was a temple" and saw *Chūshingura*. He returned as MacArthur's aide and interpreter (major) and censored kabuki, and was long called 「歌舞伎を救った男」. Studies of the 2000s (James Brandon, *Asian Theatre Journal* 23:1, 2006; 浜野保樹 2008) argue he exaggerated his role over that of his superior Earle Ernst and an eight-man team. Tanigawa Takeshi defends Bowers's role in the 1947 *Chūshingura*. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/フォービアン・バワーズ
- 1946: a GHQ censor viewing the romantic comedy 『はたちの青春』 objected that it had no kiss and ordered one added. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本における検閲

### Anti-Prostitution Law 売春防止法
- Licensed prostitution was nominally ended by repeal of the 娼妓取締規則 (1946) and by Imperial Ordinance No. 9 of **15 January 1947**. The *akasen* districts carried on de facto. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/売春防止法
- The law (**Law No. 118 of 24 May 1956**) was submitted 2 May and passed 21 May 1956, with the LDP courting women's votes before the upper-house election. Penal provisions took effect **1 April 1958**. It led to bribery arrests of brothel-trade leaders who had lobbied an LDP committee (October 1957). Reformed by the 2022 law on support for women in difficulty; reformatory detention (補導処分) was ended on 1 April 2024. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/売春防止法

## 6. Postwar girls' culture

### Tezuka's 『リボンの騎士』 and Takarazuka
- *Shōjo Club* serialisation **January 1953 – January 1956**; *Nakayoshi* sequel 1958–59 (later 『双子の騎士』) and remake 1963–66. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/リボンの騎士
- Premise: the princess Sapphire is born, through the angel Tink's mischief, with both a boy's and a girl's heart. Only males can inherit the throne, so she is raised as a prince. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/リボンの騎士
- The model for Sapphire was the Takarazuka *musume-yaku* **Awashima Chikage 淡島千景**, seen by Tezuka playing a man's part (probably Portia in *The Merchant of Venice*; Tezuka on TBS 『テレビ探偵団』, 10 Jan 1988). Sapphire's TV voice, Ōta Yoshiko, was also ex-Takarazuka. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/リボンの騎士
- In **1933, aged 5**, Tezuka moved to Kawamo, Kohama village (now Takarazuka city). His mother took him to the revue. **The next-door house was the Torii family's**, home of the otokoyaku top star **Amatsu Otome 天津乙女** and her sisters Kumono Kayoko and Ikebe Tsuruko. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/手塚治虫

### The Year 24 Group 24年組
- The name covers women manga artists born around Shōwa 24 (1949) who remade shōjo manga in the 1970s: Hagio Moto 萩尾望都 (1949), Takemiya Keiko 竹宮惠子 (1950), Ōshima Yumiko 大島弓子 (1947), Yamagishi Ryōko 山岸凉子 (1947), Aoike Yasuko 青池保子 (1948), Kihara Toshie 木原敏江 (1948), Kimura Minori, Sasaya Nanae, Yamada Mineko. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/24年組

### *The Rose of Versailles* and Takarazuka (1974)
- Ikeda Riyoko 池田理代子, 『ベルサイユのばら』: *Weekly Margaret* (Shūeisha) **1972 no. 21 – 1973 no. 52**. First volume 25 October 1972. Over **20 million** copies by December 2013. History drawn largely from Stefan Zweig's *Marie Antoinette*. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ベルサイユのばら
- Takarazuka staging:

### Equal Employment Opportunity Law (1985)
- A 1985 amendment (Law No. 45) turned the 1972 Working Women's Welfare Law into the EEOL, effective **1 April 1986**. It was needed for Japan to ratify CEDAW by 1985. Protective limits on women's night work were removed at the same time. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/雇用の分野における男女の均等な機会及び待遇の確保等に関する法律

## 7. *Revolutionary Girl Utena* (1997) and its theatrical lineage

Layers are kept apart: (S) on screen, (C) creators' statements, (F) others. Sources: the project's Utena archive skill (`references/makers.md`, `music.md`, `other-versions.md`, compiled October 2026 from first-hand viewing and the Empty Movement / ohtori.nu archive of translated interviews), plus ja.wp and the ohtori.nu pages opened directly.
- **Broadcast**: TV Tokyo network, **2 April – 24 December 1997**, **39 episodes**, Wednesdays 18:00–18:30 in Kantō. Produced by TV Tokyo and Yomiko Advertising; animation J.C.Staff. Director Ikuhara Kunihiko 幾原邦彦; series composition Enokido Yōji 榎戸洋司; original concept "Be-Papas" ビーパパス; characters Saitō Chiho さいとうちほ; character designer Hasegawa Shinya 長谷川眞也; score Mitsumune Shinkichi 光宗信吉; duel choruses J.A. Seazer. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/少女革命ウテナ ; Utena archive makers.md
- "Be-Papas" means 「大人になろう」 ("let's become adults", per Oguro Yūichirō's DVD liner notes). The team aimed for anime staff to be credited as original authors. Saitō's manga in *Ciao* (from mid-1996, before broadcast) is credited 「原作ビーパパス」, a sister work rather than the source. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/少女革命ウテナ
- Ja.wp characterises the show's style as a cross between **Takarazuka and avant-garde theatre**: a cross-dressed beauty (男装の麗人), painted-flat backgrounds and shadow-play girls. It names the adoption of J.A. Seazer's underground chorus 「絶対運命黙示録」 as key to its world. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/少女革命ウテナ
- **J.A. Seazer** J・A・シーザー: real name Terahara Takaaki 寺原孝明, born **6 October 1948**, Miyazaki; raised in Shizuoka. Came to Tokyo 1969 and joined Terayama Shūji's 寺山修司 **Tenjō Sajiki 天井桟敷** as composer and director. Scored Terayama's films 『書を捨てよ町へ出よう』 (1971), 『田園に死す』 (1974) and 『草迷宮』 (1979). Founded **演劇実験室◎万有引力** (Ban'yū Inryoku) in 1983 (after Terayama's death). https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/J・A・シーザー
- (C) Ikuhara, SciFi.com chat, 8 October 2000: he first heard Seazer as a high-schooler, after the student movement had ended. Seazer, he said, had been "a charismatic idol of the student activists in the late 1960s" whose music "still carried… the scream wanting to change the world." Asked what "revolutionizing the world" means, he answered: **"It means to break the eggshell."** https://ohtori.nu/creators/a_nyaf.html
- (C) Origins of the music:
  - 「絶対運命黙示録」 ("Absolute Destiny Apocalypse") was written for Ban'yū Inryoku's play ***Kaspar Hauser* (November 1995)** (2008 CD-BOX booklet).
  - King Records producer Ōtsuki Toshimichi made the request to Seazer in Shinjuku in **December 1996**, with the ascent song and three duel songs already chosen.
  - Ikuhara said people expected "Takarazuka-style"; he wanted a **"dark Takarazuka"**, which led him to underground theatre. He used existing stage songs unchanged.
  - Sponsors opposed the choruses.
  - Sources conflict on how many duel songs were pre-existing stage pieces.
  Utena archive music.md (sources: BOX 2008; Nozomi 2011 booklet; Excite interview 16 Dec 2013, https://www.excite.co.jp/news/article/E1387123357640/)
- (C) Takarazuka and *The Rose of Versailles*, 2003 commentary on ep. 38:
  - Saitō: the world "was originally derived from *Rose of Versailles*", and "we went to go see Takarazuka together". Takarazuka underlies shōjo manga by way of Tezuka.
  - Ikuhara: "we didn't see *Rose of Versailles*!" His greatest fear was being called a parody of *Ribon no kishi* or *The Rose of Versailles*, both about "a girl dressing as a boy and using swords". He concluded the visuals had to be parody.
  https://ohtori.nu/creators/a_e38.html
- (C) In 2019 Ikuhara described the show as Takarazuka plus 1960s–70s small theatre, and named Takarazuka's *Elisabeth* as a source for Mikage. Hasegawa (1997): Saitō's drawing has a Takarazuka flavour. Other acknowledged sources: Jun'ichi Nakahara's 1950s girls'-magazine illustration (Kotani 2000) and *The Three Musketeers*. Utena archive makers.md §3.19–3.20
- (C) Ja.wp, citing the North American DVD commentary: Ikuhara planned an intimate Utena–Anthy relationship. Saitō strongly rejected it as not what girl readers wanted, and the shock gave Ikuhara **acute gastroenteritis**. In a 1998 round-table (『薔薇の黙示録』, 26 March 1998) Saitō said Be-Papas' influence had led her to affirm homoerotic material. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/少女革命ウテナ
- Stage spin-offs:
  - **17–29 December 1997**: a musical at Hakuhinkan Theatre, Tokyo, by Mitsuya Yūji, with an **all-female, Takarazuka-style cast**.
  - **26 May – 1 June 1999**: Takatori Ei's 高取英 **Gesshoku Kagekidan 月蝕歌劇団** (a Terayama-lineage troupe) staged an Utena play produced by Ikuhara, timed with the film.
  - 2018 and 2019: further musicals.
  Utena archive other-versions.md ; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/少女革命ウテナ

## 8. Women's and queer history

### The word *dōseiai* 同性愛
- Nihon Kokugo Daijiten's earliest cited use of 同性愛 is a 1925 glossary (増補改版『や、此は便利だ』). https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%90%8C%E6%80%A7%E6%84%9B-103769 . It is only a dictionary's first citation, and the word was current in sexology and the press earlier (see Uncertain).
- The anal-intercourse ban (鶏姦) of 1872–73 (鶏姦律条例; 改定律例 art. 266) was dropped from the 1880 Penal Code (in force 1 January 1882). Japan has had no sodomy law since. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/同性愛
- The Health Ministry does not classify homosexuality as illness; DSM dropped it in 1980 and ICD in 1992. https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%90%8C%E6%80%A7%E6%84%9B-103769

### Lesbian history, 1910s–1990s
- Famous female couples: Hiratsuka Raichō and Otake Kōkichi 尾竹紅吉; Miyamoto Yuriko and Yuasa Yoshiko 湯浅芳子; Yoshiya Nobuko and Monma Chiyo. In the 1890s Nakamura Kotoki 中村小時 dressed as a man and kept a mistress. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/レズビアン
- **1971: Wakakusa no kai 若草の会**, Japan's first lesbian circle. It met monthly in an apartment with 20–30 attending, had about 500 members over five years, published a bulletin and lasted about 15 years. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/レズビアン
- Mini-komi:
  - 『すばらしい女たち』 (1976)

  - 『レズビアン通信』 (1982)
  https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/レズビアン
- **1987**: れ組スタジオ・東京, the first lesbian office, opened. The same year came 『別冊宝島64 女を愛する女たちの物語』, testimony from 234 women. https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/レズビアン

## Telling details

- **The day the mountains move.** The first line of the first issue of the first Japanese women's literary magazine (September 1911) was Yosano Akiko's prophecy 「山の動く日来る」. The magazine's first office was a borrowed room in a professor's house, and its first ban (April 1912) cost it the room. (ja.wp 青鞜)
- **Ribbons as proposals.** In 1910 a girls' magazine described how an upper-year girl "proposed" to a new pupil: her friends delivered a purple or crimson ribbon, and if the younger girl tied it in her hair, the pair were "ome-san". (ja.wp エス)
- **A parliamentary midnight.** Women won the right to attend political meetings at 11:50 p.m. on the last night of the 1922 session. It came after two women walked to a baron's house in Nakano and talked him out of his opposition. (ja.wp 新婦人協会)
- **Ichikawa's diary, 29 March 1925.** On the day manhood suffrage passed she wrote that she would remember it forever as the day the vote was taken *from* women. In March 1947 she, the suffragist, was herself purged by the Occupation; a GHQ officer argued that equality meant equal penalties too. (ja.wp 婦選獲得同盟, 市川房枝)
- **Solingen clippers.** Monma Chiyo cut Yoshiya Nobuko's famous bob for decades with clippers and scissors the couple bought in Germany in 1928. The only legal tie open to them was adoption, and in 1957 Yoshiya adopted her. (ja.wp 吉屋信子)
- **Rejected by the girls' magazine.** Before *Hana monogatari* made her famous, Yoshiya had sent work to *Shōjo no tomo*, whose editor returned it with a letter asking her to stop submitting. (ja.wp 花物語)
- **The same classmate twice.** In January and February 1933 two Jissen Girls' School pupils leapt into Mt Mihara's crater with the same classmate present both times; 129 people followed that year. (ja.wp 三原山)
- **Statistics of love-death.** Of 501 love-suicides recorded in 1908–10, 18 were between women. (ja.wp レズビアン, citing 礫川全次)
- **The strike in the hot spring.** In June 1933 about 230 revue girls led by the 18-year-old male-role star Mizunoe Takiko barricaded themselves in a Yugawara inn. Their Ōsaka sisters hung strike banners on Kōyasan. They won a weekly day off and a minimum wage. (ja.wp 桃色争議)
- **The girl who didn't strike.** Saijō Eriko, a *musume-yaku* who refused to join the strike and was expelled from the union, was two years later half of the most notorious female love-suicide attempt of the decade, with the cross-dressed heiress Masuda Fumiko. In 1938 she married the revue's publicity chief, Kanematsu Renkichi, the same man ja.wp later describes as Mizunoe's manager and lover in the early 1940s. (ja.wp 西条エリ子, 水の江瀧子)
- **"I'm Mizunoe Tākī!"** The nickname came from an ad-lib in a cowboy costume. Her fan club reached Korea and Taiwan, and by her own account the authorities later forbade her to appear in men's clothes at all. (ja.wp 水の江瀧子)
- **Swords drawn at the box office.** On 4 March 1944, Takarazuka's last night, police kept order with drawn swords. The theatre then housed crews training for suicide torpedoes, and the Tokyo house became the Ernie Pyle Theatre, closed to Japanese until 1955. (ja.wp 宝塚歌劇団, 東京宝塚劇場)
- **The word *shōjo* removed.** In October 1940 the 宝塚少女歌劇団 became the 宝塚歌劇団, and every performer was enrolled in the 大日本国防婦人会 (Greater Japan National Defence Women's Association). (ja.wp 宝塚歌劇団)
- **Renamed for the war.** In 1940 "Dick Mine" became Mine Kōichi and "Miss Columbia" became Matsubara Misao; Tokyo's dance halls closed the same year, on 31 October. (ja.wp 敵性語, ダンスホール)
- **Big eyes banned.** Nakahara Jun'ichi's large-eyed girls were driven off *Shōjo no tomo*'s covers in 1940 as "decadent", and schoolgirls bought up old issues as treasures. (ja.wp 少女の友)
- **The kiss ordered by the censor.** In 1946 an American censor insisted a Japanese romance add a kiss. Two years later Kyoto's studios renounced sword films altogether. (ja.wp 日本における検閲, 時代劇)
- **A maid's lessons.** The 22-year-old who wrote Japan's guarantee of sexual equality in marriage had learned what Japanese women endured from her childhood maid, and she wept when most of her draft was struck out. (ja.wp ベアテ・シロタ・ゴードン)
- **The prince next door.** As a boy Tezuka Osamu lived next door to Takarazuka's top male-role star, Amatsu Otome. His cross-dressed princess Sapphire was modelled on an actress of women's roles whom he had seen play a man. (ja.wp 手塚治虫, リボンの騎士)
- **An onnagata directs Oscar.** The 1974 Takarazuka *Rose of Versailles* was staged by Hasegawa Kazuo, who had trained as a kabuki *onnagata*. He taught the women how to make love scenes look beautiful from the stalls. (ja.wp ベルサイユのばら (宝塚歌劇))
- **"Liberation from the toilet."** Japanese women's lib announced itself on 21 October 1970 with a hand-lettered leaflet under that title. (ja.wp 田中美津)
- **The first certificate went to an ex-otokoyaku.** Japan's first same-sex partnership certificate (Shibuya, 5 November 2015) was issued to a woman who had played men in Takarazuka's Flower Troupe as あうら真輝. (ja.wp 東小雪, 渋谷区条例)
- **"Break the eggshell."** Asked in 2000 what "revolutionizing the world" means, Utena's director answered, "It means to break the eggshell". He traced his love of Seazer's choruses to their "scream wanting to change the world", carried over from the late-1960s student movement. (ohtori.nu a_nyaf)
- **"Let's become adults."** The name of the Utena collective, Be-Papas, was glossed by its makers as 「大人になろう」. (ja.wp 少女革命ウテナ)
- **A first woman PM and the family name.** Japan's first woman prime minister (October 2025) leads the opposition to letting married couples keep separate surnames, though she has worked under her maiden name since 2004. (ja.wp 高市早苗)

## Uncertain / conflicting

1. **1930 vs 1931 civic-rights bill.** Encyclopedias disagree on wording (Sekai Daihyakka: passed the lower house in 1931, blocked by the Peers; Yamakawa and Ōbunsha: passed the lower house in 1930, rejected by the Peers the next year). The best reading is two bills: a 1930 members' bill (shelved by the Peers in May 1930) and the 1931 Hamaguchi government bill (passed the lower house, rejected by the Peers). The 1931 vote of **184–62** is **unverified** (search snippets only).
2. **Women elected in 1946: 39 or 36.** Thirty-nine is supported by the Asahi 選挙大観 table on ja.wp, Kyodo, Sekai Daihyakka, Mypedia and Ōbunsha. Nipponica's 女性参政権 article (大木基子) says **36**, apparently an error. Seventy-nine women candidates (ja.wp table).
3. **Takarazuka's first performance.** ja.wp gives 1 April 1914 (prose) but "1914年3月 上演開始" (timeline), and a photo caption says "1914年4・5月". The run was 1 April – 31 May 1914.
4. **End of *Seitō*.** The 青鞜 page: last issue February 1916, 52 issues. The 青鞜社 page: "1915年…6号をもって発刊が停止". The 青鞜 page also says Itō left in "April 1916", after the last issue.
5. **Article 5 revision dates.** Passed 25 March 1922; promulgated 20 April 1922 (治安警察法 page) vs 「5月10日公布施行」 (新婦人協会 page). Probably promulgated 20 April and in force 10 May.
6. **Peace Preservation Law in force** "May 1925": the exact day (12 May) is from standard references, not a page opened.
7. ***Hana monogatari* count**: 54 stories written, 52 usually counted. Years given as 1916–24 (*Shōjo gahō*) or 1916–25/26 (including *Shōjo kurabu*).
8. **Mizunoe's first short hair**: September 1930 (SKD's official history) vs 31 May 1931 (Nakayama Chinatsu).
9. **Saijō / Masuda incident (1935)**: the exact date, place and outcome come only via a blog summary of Robertson (Signs 1999), not opened. The heiress's name is 増田富美子 in ja.wp; Robertson reportedly uses "Yasumare". The user's "Masuda Fumiko" matches 富美子.
10. **The wartime *dansō* ban**: known here only from Mizunoe's memoir as cited by ja.wp (「当局より男装禁止が通達」, around 1940). The issuing authority, date and scope (revue stage only, or Takarazuka too) are **not verified**. Whether a specific 1935 official reaction to the Saijō incident targeted otokoyaku is likewise **not verified**.
11. ***Fuseji* for 革命**: the general practice is well attested (ja.wp 伏字: 社会主義・共産主義・無政府主義 blanked; Gendai on censors' suspicion of *fuseji*). A specific documented instance of 「革命」 printed as ×× was **not found** before the search budget ran out.
12. **Shōjo-magazine circulations** (e.g. *Shōjo kurabu*, *Shōjo no tomo*, 1930s) were **not found**. Figures that circulate online, such as about 500,000 for *Shōjo kurabu* in the late 1930s, should not be used without a source.
13. **1940 girls' school enrolment (518,584)** was read from a scanned 1949 yearbook page (table 511). In that table the 1930 row (975 schools / 368,999) evidently **includes** *jikka* schools, while 1936–40 match regular schools only. So 1940 may exclude *jikka* pupils (perhaps 25–30,000 more).

## Additional facts found while verifying

- **Ichikawa could not vote in the 1946 election.** The memorial chronology says she could not cast a ballot in the first postwar election, the first in which women voted, because her name had been left off the electoral roll (「選挙人名簿に記載もれで投票できず」). It then shows her organising the 39 new women members into a 婦人議員クラブ (25 April 1946). (Chronology PDF above.)
- **Hōrei Article 1 (20-day rule).** It explains the paired dates. Peace Preservation Law: 22 April → 12 May 1925. Article 5 revision: 20 April → 10 May 1922. Use these pairs rather than en.wp's "5 May 1922".
- **Scale of the Peace Preservation Law.** The Justice Ministry counted 67,223 arrests and 6,024 indictments under it up to April 1943 (Nipponica). The first application in the home islands was the 学連事件 of December 1925 – April 1926 (Britannica).
- **Abolition ordinance 575 (13/15 October 1945).** It also abolished the 思想犯保護観察法, the Kwantung and Korean thought-control ordinances, and Meiji 32 Imperial Ordinance 352 (Wikisource text).
- **Shin Fujin Kyōkai.** Nipponica: about 70 attended the inaugural meeting of 28 March 1920 (about 20 of them men), and there were 331 members by September 1920. en.wp: the first public meeting was on 21 February 1920 at the Kanda YMCA, with about 500 present, 70% men.
- ***Seitōsha* size.** Sekai Daihyakka describes it as a women-only group of 30. This matches 5 founders + 18 *sha'in* + 7 *sanjoin*.
- **First Takarazuka cast.** The first performance had 16 girls, including Takamine Taeko and Kumoi Namiko. Kobayashi took their stage names from the *Hyakunin isshu* (Nipponica).
- **Shōchiku revue at war's end.** In 1944 the Shōchiku girls' revue was dissolved into a 松竹芸能本部女子挺身隊 (Sekai Daihyakka, quoted under 男装の麗人).
- **Mizunoe's peak.** Her Paris-dandy role in 『タンゴ・ローザ』, after the strike, ran a record 160 performances (Nipponica; 20世紀日本人名事典). In 1942 she and her manager Kanematsu Renkichi formed 劇団たんぽぽ (20世紀日本人名事典).
- **Mt Mihara.** Nipponica says most of the 1933 jumpers were men and women aged 18–25, and that the crater ceased to be a suicide site only "from the war years into the postwar".
- **Girls' secondary schooling in the war.** It expanded sharply: 996 schools / 454,423 pupils in 1937, 1,299 / 756,955 in 1943, *jikka* included (MEXT 学制百年史).
- **Utena and Takarazuka on en.wp.** en.wp *Takarazuka Revue* says *Princess Knight* began a lineage that *The Rose of Versailles* and *Revolutionary Girl Utena* "borrow directly from". This is a secondary claim, not a creator statement.
- **Postwar male section at Takarazuka.** en.wp says Takarazuka trained male performers from 1946, and the last male department closed in 1954. This is separate from the 1919 men's section noted above.
- **Women in GHQ's drafting group.** en.wp names Eleanor Hadley as the only other woman. If the "six women" figure is used, it needs its own source: it may count clerical staff or the steering-committee note-taker Ruth Ellerman. That is unconfirmed.
