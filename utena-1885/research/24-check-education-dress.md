## 1. Education Ordinance 1879 (教育令), separation of the sexes — **PARTLY TRUE** (the article number and wording are below; the claim's paraphrase is slightly off)

- **Law:** 教育令, 明治12年太政官布告第40号, promulgated **明治12年9月29日 (29 Sept 1879)**. It abolished the 学制.
- **Article 42 (第四十二条), verbatim:**
  「凡学校ニ於テハ男女教場ヲ同クスルコトヲ得ス　但小学校ニ於テハ男女教場ヲ同クスルモ妨ケナシ」
  ("In all schools, males and females may not share the same classroom. In elementary schools, however, there is no objection to their sharing a classroom.")
  - It forbids a shared classroom (教場) in every kind of school and exempts elementary schools in a proviso. It is not literally phrased as 男女別学 or "above the elementary level", but that is its practical effect.
- **Later versions:**
  - The revised ordinance (改正教育令, 明治13年太政官布告第59号, 28 Dec 1880) keeps the same text as **Art. 42**.
  - The 1885 version (明治18年太政官布告第23号, 12 Aug 1885) moves it to **Art. 27**: 「凡学校ニ於テハ男女教室ヲ同クスルコトヲ得ス　但小学校及小学教場ニ於テハ男女教室ヲ同クスルモ妨ケナシ」. In this version 教場 becomes 教室, and 小学教場 is added to the exemption.
  - Art. 37 of 1879 (Art. 25 in 1885) says teachers may be of either sex: 「教員ハ男女ノ別ナク年齢十八年以上タルヘシ」.
- **Official interpretation (MEXT, 学制百年史):** the ordinance "「凡学校ニ於テハ男女教場ヲ同クスルヲ得ス」と定め、小学校の他は男女別学を原則とした。これ以後中学校に女子の入学が認められないこととなった。" (outside elementary schools, separate schooling became the rule, and from then on girls were not admitted to 中学校). The same page notes that in 1885 there were only 8 public 女学校 nationwide.

## 2. Western dress at Tokyo Women's Normal School and other girls' schools, c.1885–86, dropped c.1889–93 — **TRUE (dates refined below)**

### Institutional status, needed for accuracy
- 東京女子師範学校 opened in Nov 1875 (ceremony 29 Nov 1875).
- **26 Aug 1885:** it was merged into 東京師範学校 as its **女子部**.
- **29 Apr 1886:** it became 高等師範学校女子部.
- **24 Mar 1890:** it became independent again as 女子高等師範学校.
- So in the 1886–89 dress period, "Tokyo Women's Normal School" is formally the women's department of the (Higher) Normal School.

### Western dress
- Per 『女学雑誌』 (cited by Iwasaki 1993), in **明治18年 (1885)** many students at 東京女子師範学校 began wearing Western clothes, and so did teachers. 秋田 and 宇都宮 women's normal schools followed, with the whole staff in Western dress. Iwasaki: "この時点では洋服はまだ一部の流行にすぎない" (at this point Western clothing was still only a partial fashion).
- **18 Aug 1886:** a decision to change student dress to Western clothes, **effective 1 Oct 1886** (Ochanomizu University official chronology: 「生徒の服装を洋服に改める（10月1日実施）」).
- 『官報』 of 8 Oct 1886 printed 「高等師範学校女生徒用服」. The background was Minister 森有礼's policy.
- Iwasaki: the same year the Akita Normal School women's section adopted a Western uniform, and Fukushima did so in 1888.

### End of Western dress
- The school's own photo series 「東京女子高等師範学校生徒服装変遷写真」 dates the change:
  - 1885 Jul: 「鹿鳴館時代以前」 (Japanese dress).
  - 1886 Jul: 「鹿鳴館全盛時代(夏服)」.
  - **1890 Mar: 「鹿鳴館全盛時代」 (still Western).**
  - **1893 Mar: 「鹿鳴館時代以後」.**
  - **1894 Mar: 「國粹的時代」.** Archive note: 「『鹿鳴館時代』終焉ののち、生徒の服装は和装へと回帰する」.
  - 1900 Mar: 「袴着用(海老茶又ハ紫紺)」.
- So the return to Japanese dress happened **between March 1890 and March 1893**. I did not find the exact date of the order that abolished the Western uniform. The archive cites 『お茶の水女子大学百年史』 pp.49–52 and 『東京女子高等師範学校六十年史』 pp.55–57 and 63–64, neither of which I could open.
- Iwasaki's summary for normal schools (Tokyo, Fukushima, Akita): **「女子の洋装が行われたのは明治19年から26年位までのわずかな期間」** (1886 to about 1893). Around the 1890 Imperial Rescript on Education, "日本全体が国風化し、生徒の洋装も和服にもどっ[た]".

### Peeresses' School (華族女学校), from the Gakushūin centennial history
- **9 May 1887:** it was ordered that from **1 June 1887** students would wear **Western clothes only**, together with the start of gymnastics. The fabrics were limited to serge, flannel, linen and cotton, with patterns chosen from 4 school models, and lace, gold or jewels were banned (「華族女学校生徒洋服制限」).
- 20 Oct 1888: Japanese dress was allowed when a student was ill.
- **31 Jul 1889:** the rule was relaxed. Students could come in 和服着袴 (kimono with hakama) except on ceremonial days.
- **4 Jun 1890:** students could choose 和服 or 洋服 freely, even on ceremonial days.

## 3. Girls in men's hakama in the early 1870s; a ban (1883 at TWNS?); Shimoda Utako's women's hakama c.1885 — **PARTLY TRUE**

### Men's hakama for girls
- When the government girls' school (官立女学校, later 東京女学校) opened in 1872, the **太政官正院 directed that girls' dress adopt the hakama of samurai men** (「服装に士分男子の袴を取り入れることが太政官正院より指令された」, Iwasaki 1993; repeated in ja.wikipedia 東京女学校).
- 東京女子師範学校 (1875) continued this with a **government-issued navy and light-blue vertically striped cotton hakama (平袴形)**. Students wore ordinary kimono on top, hair in 唐人髷, and a kanzashi marked 「女子師範」 as the badge. The archive photo caption reads 「明治十年 本校創立時代(縞袴着用)」.

### The ban — the year is wrong
- The Ochanomizu archive says: **「明治12年1月頃より袴の着用が禁止されたという」**, i.e. hakama were **banned at TWNS from about January 1879**.
- Iwasaki, citing 『お茶の水女子大学百年史』, says 「袴も簪も明治12（1879）年ころには廃止」.
- **1883 is wrong; it was c. Jan 1879.**
- Public **criticism** of girls in men's hakama in the early 1870s: **UNVERIFIED.** None of the sources I opened document it, though it is often asserted.

### Shimoda and the women's hakama
- **3 Oct 1885**, before the Peeresses' School opened, guarantors were given a dress code (verbatim, 学習院百年史 p.365):
  - 「一、本校ノ生徒タルモノハ袴ヲ着シ靴ヲ穿クベシ」
  - 「一、表布ハ随意トス」
  - 「一、袴ハ縞ヲ除クノ外、色目、地紋随意タルベシ」
  - 「一、結髪ハ随意タリ」
  - 「一、西洋服ニテモ苦シカラズ」
- The reasons given were that dress without 裳袴 lacked decorum, especially with the Empress visiting, and was impractical on chairs.
- At first students wore **recoloured court 緋袴 or men's hakama with the 腰板 removed**. The single tie of the 緋袴 came loose, and men's gusseted (襠) hakama were awkward for small girls and rode up.
- **"その後" (afterwards), with no year given, 教授 下田歌子 designed a new hakama** combining 緋袴 and 指貫, with two ties (front and back), no gusset (襠), and more pleats. The colour was not prescribed, but most chose **海老茶**. This hakama spread to girls' schools generally.
- Iwasaki, citing 『教育時論』 592号 (1901) 「華族女學校着用袴の由來」, says the 裳袴 began when 華族女学校 opened in **Sept 1885**, worn by female teachers and students alongside Western dress. Its form derived from the court 緋袴, with the colour changed, two ties and the gusset removed, and it was "俗に行燈袴といふ".
- **So "Shimoda designed the women's hakama for the Peeresses' School" is TRUE. "Around 1885" is correct for the hakama rule, but the date of Shimoda's own design is not established. The Gakushūin history says only that it came later.**
- **"Andon-bakama" is a generic term** for gusset-less hakama worn by Meiji schoolgirls (Kotobank: 「明治時代に女学生が着用し、後には男子も着用するようになった」). It is not a name specific to Shimoda.
- I did not verify the name "Utako-bakama" anywhere.

### Rival claim and timing of spread
- 跡見女学校 (opened 1875) says it had students wear **purple hakama from its opening, at the Empress's wish** (ja.wikipedia 跡見学園女子大学, citing 『跡見学園90年』, 1965). This is an institutional claim.
- 女子高等師範学校 adopted hakama in 1897 or 1898, and Minister 外山正一 encouraged hakama for exercise in 1898 (学習院百年史 pp.368–369).
- 『風俗画報』 of 28 May 1899 shows every Peeresses' School student in 海老茶袴.
- Photographic evidence (Fukunaga Misao, via ja.wikipedia 袴) points to the late 1890s for the general schoolgirl-hakama habit. A school photo of 126 people in 1895 shows no hakama; the TWHNS class of 1900 is all in hakama.
- **Link to item 9:** the 1873 Tokyo cross-dressing article expressly exempted 「女ノ着袴スル類」.

## 4. Kazoku Jogakkō (華族女学校, Peeresses' School): founded 1885, Shimoda a leading figure, Tsuda teaching English — **TRUE**

### Founding, from 学習院百年史 第一編 pp.328–342, with the page image checked
- **Jul 1884:** Shimoda was appointed 宮内省御用掛 to prepare the school. **Sep 1884:** building started on the 皇宮付属地 at 四谷区尾張町, facing the 赤坂離宮 (then the temporary palace).
- **5 Sep 1885:** a 宮内省達 set up the school: 「今般四谷区四谷仲町皇宮附属地内ヘ華族女学校ヲ設置シ当省所轄ニ相定候条…」. The same day the girls' department of Gakushūin was abolished and the 華族女学校規則 issued.
- **14 Sep 1885:** staff regulations issued. **谷干城** (Gakushūin head) became concurrent principal. **下田歌子 became 幹事兼教授.**
- **5 Oct 1885:** classes began provisionally. There were **133 students in October**, including 38 transferred from Gakushūin and about 60 from Shimoda's 桃夭女塾. Shimoda closed the 塾 in Dec 1885.
- **13 Nov 1885:** formal **opening ceremony with Empress Haruko present**. She toured the classrooms, the ceremony was held in the rainy-day gymnasium, and she issued a 令旨.
- Tani left in **Dec 1885** to become Minister of Agriculture and Commerce, and **Shimoda acted as principal**. In **Feb 1886** she became **学監兼教授**.
- The book is internally inconsistent on when Shimoda's acting principalship ended: p.377 says Apr 1887, but p.9 says 大鳥圭介 became principal in Apr 1886.
- The school moved to 麹町区永田町 (new building May 1889, ceremony July 1889).

### Tsuda Umeko
- She was appointed **教授補** at the opening in Sept 1885. She had taught English at Shimoda's 桃夭女塾 from Feb 1884.
- ja.wikipedia 津田梅子 (citing 山崎 1988) adds: 嘱託 Feb 1886, **教授 Nov 1886** (高等官6等, ¥500 a year). She and Shimoda were the only women teachers of 高等官 rank.
- She stayed until **Jul 1900**, with study in the US from 1889 to 1892, then founded 女子英学塾.

## 5. Ferris Seminary — **TRUE**

- **1870:** Mary E. Kidder (1834–1910) began classes at **J.C. Hepburn's clinic** (居留地39番). The official Ferris history calls this 「フェリス女学院の発祥」.
- **1872:** the school moved to a Japanese-style building on **野毛山**, part of the prefectural offices, with help from Governor 大江卓 (ja.wikipedia メアリー・キダー).
- **1 Jun 1875:** a new school building and dormitory were completed at **山手178番**, and the school was named **"Ferris Seminary"** after Isaac Ferris and John M. Ferris. It was renamed フェリス和英女学校 in 1889.
- The second principal was Eugene S. Booth, from 1881. Kidder herself left for Tokyo in July 1881.
- **Wakamatsu Shizuko** graduated on **29 Jun 1882** as one of the **first graduates of the 高等科**. She then taught English there until her 1889 marriage.

## 6. Yokohama Yamate (the Bluff) — **PARTLY TRUE** (the schools and views are right; the major churches were not on the Bluff in 1885)

- **Settlement:** the 山手居留地 was laid out under the 1866 慶応約書 and established in **1867**. The English names were "the Bluff" or "Yamate Bluff". It lasted until treaty revision on **17 Jul 1899**. The lower settlement was 山下居留地.
- **Schools on the Bluff by 1885:**
  - **Ferris Seminary**, 山手178, from 1875.
  - **共立女学校 (American Mission Home)**: 山手48番 on 28 Aug 1871, then **山手212番 from Oct 1872**. It was renamed 共立女学校 around 1875.
  - **Sisters of the Infant Jesus (St Maur)**: from 1872 at **山手58番** (education of foreign children and orphans), with a convent and the orphanage 仁慈堂 at **山手83番**. The orphanage was licensed in 1875. 横浜紅蘭女学校 (forerunner of 横浜雙葉) came only in 1900, at 山手88.
- **Churches: not on the Bluff in 1885.**
  - Christ Church (Anglican) was at 居留地105, in the flat settlement (consecrated 18 Oct 1863), and moved to **山手235 only in 1901**.
  - The Catholic 横浜天主堂 (1862) was in the settlement, now 山下町, and moved to **山手44 in 1906**.
  - Chapels inside the convent and mission schools are likely but **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Other 1885 Bluff features:**
  - **Yamate Park (山手公園)** opened in June 1870, Japan's first Western-style park. Tennis was played there from 1876 and the first courts were built in 1878.
  - The foreign cemetery dates from 1854.
  - **The Gaiety Theatre (ゲーテ座) moved from 本町通り68 to 山手256–257** (former British barracks site) and **opened in April 1885**.
  - British and French troops were stationed on the bluff from 1863 until 1875. Their site, フランス山, is now 港の見える丘公園, which "横浜港を見下ろす".
- **Views over the harbour:** TRUE in general.

  - https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ゲーテ座

## 7. Boys' uniforms: Gakushūin 1879, Imperial University 1886 — **TRUE** (the 1879 collar detail is only partly sourced)

- **Gakushūin (学習院百年史 p.4 and pp.165–166):**
  - At first there was no rule and most students wore kimono.
  - 「明治十二年に渡辺洪基次長の立案によって男子の制服を定めた。すなわち**海軍士官型**のもので、これが日本の学校で制服が決められた最初であった。夏服は白、冬服は紺」.
  - At first all students had vermilion piping on the edges and cuffs. From 1881–82 this was removed for middle-division and older students.
  - The cap badge was a cherry blossom.
  - The reform statutes (学習院学制) were adopted on 23 Aug 1879 and took effect **15 Sep 1879**. From then all entrants paid a 定服料 for the uniform.
  - Anecdote: 大学予備門 students mocked the uniformed boys as 「だぼくら」. A year later the 予備門 adopted a uniform too.
  - **On "tsume-eri":** the centennial history says "naval-officer type" and does not use the word 詰襟. ja.wikipedia 詰襟 (citing Yomiuri, 13 Mar 2012) calls it the origin of the 詰襟 school uniform, introduced by 渡辺洪基 in 1879, and describes the Gakushūin type as hook-fastened 蛇腹 naval style. Treat "tsume-eri" as very probable but secondary-sourced.
  - Do not use the "1872" date on ja.wikipedia 学習院中・高等科. Gakushūin itself opened only in 1877.
- **Imperial University:** created March 1886. **In April 1886** it fixed summer and winter uniforms and caps (shape, colour, buttons, collar badges with faculty initials, worn on one side only at 森有礼's suggestion), per the UTokyo official Q&A.
  - The 角帽 (square cap) became the official cap at this time (ja.wikipedia 学生帽, citing Kotobank and Tomita). It had been tried privately from Oct 1884.
  - 渡辺洪基, who designed the Gakushūin uniform, became the Imperial University's first president in 1886.
- Sources opened:
  - 学習院百年史 第一編 (PDF pp.40, 145–147, 201–202)
  - https://www.u-tokyo.ac.jp/adm/history/09_03_01_j.html
  - ja.wikipedia 詰襟, 学生服, 学生帽, 渡辺洪基 (raw text)

## 8. The 1872 Tokyo ban on women's short hair — **PARTLY TRUE** (the enforceable text is Art. 39 of the Tokyo ordinance; the earlier "女子断髪禁止令" is a modern label with unverified wording)

- **"女子断髪禁止令" is not an official title.** ja.wikipedia 散髪脱刀令, uncited, says: 「1872年5月11日（明治5年4月5日）に東京府が『女子断髪禁止令』を出した」. It explains this as a response to the misunderstanding that the 1871 散髪脱刀令 applied to women.
  - Britannica Japan (on Kotobank) confirms only the year: 「女性の断髪禁止は翌年 [1872] に発令」.
  - **I could not open the text of this 4 May (lunar 4/5) notice. Its wording is UNVERIFIED.** It is a Tokyo-fu notice and does not appear in 法令全書 明治5年; I checked the OCR of the whole volume for 斷髮/斷髪/断髪.
- **The verified legal text is the Tokyo 違式註違條例** (法令全書 明治5年, 司法省 section, NDL コマ 739–741). It carries the note 「十一月八日東京府布達ヲ以テ来ル十三日ヨリ施行ス」, i.e. promulgated on **明治5年11月8日 (8 Dec 1872)** and in force from **11月13日 (13 Dec 1872)**.
  - In the lighter category 註違罪目: **「第三十九條　婦人ニテ謂レナク斷髮スル者」** (a woman who cuts her hair short without reason).
  - Penalty for 註違: a fine of 6銭2厘5毛 to 12銭5厘. Those unable to pay were held 1–2 days (拘留).
  - The 1873 provincial version (各地方違式註違條例, 明治6年太政官布告第256号, 19 Jul 1873) repeats the rule as **第48条** 「婦人ニテ謂レナク斷髮スル者」.
- Related: 明治6年10月8日 太政官布告第337号, rules for Japanese living in Qing China, Art. 10: 「婦人ニテ謂レナク斷髪ス可ラサル事」.

## 9. The cross-dressing article in the 違式詿違条例 — **FALSE as phrased for 1872. TRUE that it was an offence in Tokyo from 1873 until 1882. Not a national offence after the 1880 Penal Code.**

- **The original Tokyo ordinance of Nov 1872 has no cross-dressing article.** Its 54 articles (違式罪目 Arts. 6–28, 註違罪目 Arts. 29–54) were read in full in the 法令全書 OCR. They include Art. 11 tattooing, Art. 12 mixed bathhouses, Art. 22 public nudity 「裸體又ハ袒裼シ或ハ股脚ヲ露ハシ醜體ヲナス者」, Art. 39 women's short hair, and Art. 49 urinating in the street. 法令全書 notes that Arts. 55–62 were added later.
- **Cross-dressing was added as Art. 62 of the 違式罪目** (the heavier class: a fine of 75–150銭; for those unable to pay, 笞 of 10–20 strokes, per Art. 3 of the Tokyo ordinance) by **司法省第百三十一號, 明治6年8月12日 (12 Aug 1873)**. Verbatim from 法令全書 明治6年, 司法省, NDL コマ 954:
  > 「今般違式罪目左之通追加候條此旨及布達候事
  > 第六十二條　男ニシテ女粧シ女ニシテ男粧シ或ハ奇恠ノ粉飾ヲ爲シテ醜體ヲ露ス者
  > 但シ俳優歌舞妓等ハ勿論女ノ着袴スル類此限ニ非ス」
  - **The proviso exempts actors and kabuki performers and, explicitly, "women who wear hakama".**
  - 司法省第百三十八號 (27 Aug 1873) corrected 「粉飾」 to **「扮飾」**.
- The national template for the provinces (各地方違式註違條例, 太政官布告第256号, 19 Jul 1873, 90 articles) contains **no cross-dressing article** in the OCR I read. One article, 38, is illegible in the OCR. The template allowed local additions with 警保寮 approval.
- A separate 教部省 notice (明治6年7月, 第26号, 5 Jul 1873) ordered prefectures to stop 「奇怪之打粉或ハ男女姿粧ヲ易ヘ候等之儀」 at shrine and temple festivals. This is a religious-administration directive, not a penal clause.
- **After the 1880 Penal Code:** 旧刑法 (明治13年太政官布告第36号, promulgated 17 Jul 1880, **in force 1 Jan 1882**). 法令全書 annotates the Tokyo 違式註違 articles 「十三年第三十六號布告ニ依リ消滅」, meaning they lapsed with the Code.
  - The Code's petty offences (違警罪, Arts. 425–429) have **no cross-dressing clause**; I read the full text.
  - **Art. 430** allowed 「各地方ノ便宜ニヨリ定ムル所ノ違警罪」. **Whether Tokyo's local 違警罪 rules of 1882–1908 kept a cross-dressing clause is UNVERIFIED.**
- **1908 警察犯処罰令** (内務省令第16号, 29 Sep 1908, in force 1 Oct 1908): **no cross-dressing clause**; I read the full text. The nearest clauses are:
  - Art. 3(2): public nudity 「…臀部、股部ヲ露ハシ其ノ他醜態ヲ爲シタル者」.
  - Art. 2(20): unauthorised use of 法令ノ定ムル服飾、徽章.
- **Net for 1885:** a girl in men's dress in Tokyo was **not committing a statutory offence under national law** (the Penal Code). Between 1873 and 1881 she would have been, under Tokyo Art. 62, unless she was "a woman wearing hakama" or an actor. ja.wikipedia 女装 cites 三橋順子『女装と日本人』 (2008), p.129, for a Meiji 司法省 cross-dressing ban, which is consistent with the 1873 司法省 達.

## 10. Kishida Toshiko (岸田俊子, later 中島俊子 / 湘煙) — **PARTLY TRUE** (one title is wrong and the Ferris dates need correcting)

- **Birth:** 文久3年12月5日 (= **13 Jan 1864**), Kyoto, a kimono merchant's daughter. Her name was originally 俊, later 俊子.
- **Death:** **25 May 1901** at Ōiso (ja.wikipedia, Britannica Japan, Yamakawa). ニッポニカ gives 12 May.
- **Court service:** in **1879** she entered the palace as **文事御用掛** and lectured Empress Haruko on Chinese classics. Wikipedia notes alternative dates of 1878 or 1880. She resigned in the **autumn of 1881**. **TRUE** for "c. 1879–81".
- **Speeches:** **April 1882**, at a 立憲政党 meeting in Osaka (president 中島信行), on 「婦女の道」. She then toured Osaka, Okayama and elsewhere; 景山(福田)英子 heard her in Okayama. **TRUE.**
- **Ōtsu:** **12 Oct 1883** speech 「函入娘」 (also written 箱入娘). She was taken into custody by Ōtsu police and jailed under the **集会条例**, which treated it as a political speech. Yamakawa adds suspicion of 官吏侮辱. 世界大百科 says she received a **罰金刑**. **TRUE, but the fine amount is UNVERIFIED.**
- **Marriage to 中島信行:** **1884** per ニッポニカ, 世界大百科, Britannica and 旺文社. ja.wikipedia says the **marriage was registered in Aug 1885**. **TRUE (1884), with the 1885 registration noted.**
- **"Zenchū no hen": FALSE title.** Her 1887 work in 女学雑誌 is **『善悪の岐』** (ぜんあくのちまた; Britannica reads ふたみち). It was an adaptation (翻案) published **July 1887** under the pen name **粧園女史**.
  - Other works: 「同胞姉妹に告ぐ」 (10 parts in 『自由燈』, 1884) and 『山間の名花』 (都の花, Feb 1889).
- **Ferris: TRUE, but later than 1885.**
  - 世界大百科: 「87年横浜フェリス女学校教師となる」.
  - ja.wikipedia: after 信行 was expelled from Tokyo under the 保安条例 (Dec 1887) the couple moved to Yokohama, and she became **フェリス和英女学校 名誉教授 around May 1888**.
  - She was also head of Japanese and Chinese studies at 新栄女学校 in Tokyo from about Sept 1887.
  - **In 1885 she was in Tokyo, not at Ferris.** She was baptised with 信行 in 1886.

## 11. Wakamatsu Shizuko (若松賤子) — **TRUE**

- **Birth:** 元治元年3月1日 = **6 Apr 1864**, 会津若松 (阿弥陀町), eldest daughter of the Aizu retainer 松川勝次郎正義.
- **Real name:** **松川甲子 (Matsukawa Kashi)**, named after the 甲子 year of her birth. Her 通称 was 島田嘉志, and her married name 巌本嘉志子.
- **Childhood:** she was adopted by 大川甚兵衛, a Yokohama merchant, and entered Kidder's school in 1871 (per Wikipedia). She was baptised in 1877.
- **Pen name:** 若松 for her home town; 賤子 meaning "God's humble servant" (「神のしもべ」). This rests on Wikipedia only. She first used it in 女学雑誌 in 1886 (「旧き都のつと」).
- **Marriage:** to 巌本善治 in **1889** at 横浜海岸教会. ja.wikipedia 女学雑誌 gives **July 1889**. She then left Ferris and taught at 明治女学校.
- **『小公子』:** serialised in 女学雑誌 from **Aug 1890 to Jan 1892** (ja.wikipedia 小公子, citing 尾崎るみ編 1995; Kotobank gives 1890–92). The book edition's first volume came out from 女学雑誌社 in Oct 1891.
- **Death:** **10 Feb 1896** (明治29年), at 麹町区下六番町, from lung disease complicated by heart failure, five days after the 明治女学校 fire. She was 31 (満).
- **Related:** 女学雑誌 itself was founded on **20 Jul 1885**. 巌本 took over as editor in May 1886.

## Extra facts useful for a story set in 1885

- Girls' secondary schooling was tiny in 1885: **9 schools and 616 students nationwide** (MEXT table). The Peeresses' School alone had 133 students in Oct 1885.
- In mid-1885 the TWNS girls were photographed in **Japanese dress without hakama**: hakama had been banned since about Jan 1879, and the caption reads "鹿鳴館時代以前", July 1885. Western dress was only a voluntary fashion that year and became the official uniform from 1 Oct 1886.
- In Oct 1885 the Peeresses' School required **hakama and shoes**, allowed Western dress, and left hair free. The school was in Yotsuya (四谷尾張町), opposite the temporary palace.
- In Tokyo, 1873–1881, Art. 62 punished cross-dressing as a 違式 offence but **expressly exempted women wearing hakama**. From 1 Jan 1882 no national statute punished cross-dressing as such.
- 女学雑誌 began on 20 Jul 1885. Wakamatsu Shizuko was then teaching English at Ferris (Yamate 178). Kishida Toshiko was in Tokyo, registering her marriage to 中島信行 in Aug 1885.
- The Gaiety Theatre opened on the Bluff (Yamate 256–257) in April 1885. The Anglican and Catholic churches were still down in the flat settlement.
