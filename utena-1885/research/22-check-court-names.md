# 22: Court names, Meiji vocabulary, naming practice (verification)

Scope: real history only. All dates before Meiji 6 (1873) are lunisolar unless marked (G). I converted them to Gregorian with the `lunar_python` library. Before using it I checked it against 9 Japanese-Wikipedia date pairs from the articles below, and all 9 matched: 天保6.2.19=1835-03-17, 慶応4.2.9=1868-03-02, 万延元.8.22=1860-10-06, 文久2.1.13=1862-02-11, 元治元.12.3=1864-12-31, 文久4.2.8=1864-03-15, 慶応4.1.3=1868-01-27, 明治元.10.13=1868-11-26, 明治元.10.28=1868-12-11.

## 1. Kazunomiya, Taruhito, kōbu gattai, Tōsei Daisōtoku — **TRUE** (one small wording fix)

What is true:
- **和宮親子内親王** (かずのみや ちかこ ないしんのう).
  - Born 弘化3年閏5月10日 = **1846-07-03 (G)**. English Wikipedia's "1 August 1846" is wrong.
  - Eighth daughter of Emperor Ninkō and half-sister of Kōmei.
  - 「和宮」 was her childhood title. The personal name **親子** was granted only at her 内親王宣下 on 文久元年4月19日 (1861-05-28). It is taken from 『礼記』「父子親しみ」. So "Princess Chikako" is anachronistic for her before mid-1861.
- **Betrothal.** 嘉永4年7月12日 (1851-08-08), at Kōmei's order, she was promised to **有栖川宮熾仁親王** (たるひと, 1835–1895). She was 6, he was 17.
  - It was a 内約: no 結納 (betrothal gifts) had been exchanged.
  - English Wikipedia's "August 8, 1861" is the right day with the wrong year.
- **Breaking the engagement.**
  - 万延元年8月22日 (1860-10-06): Kampaku 九条尚忠 visited the Arisugawa house.
  - 8月23日: the house asked the 武家伝奏 for a "postponement" of the marriage. This was in effect a withdrawal.
  - 8月26日 (1860-10-10): the request was granted.
  - 万延元年10月18日 (**1860-11-30**): Kōmei formally consented to the marriage (降嫁勅許). His condition was that the bakufu carry out 攘夷 (expulsion of foreigners).
- **Journey and wedding.**
  - She left Kyoto from the 桂宮邸 on 文久元年10月20日 (1861-11-22).
  - She entered the 清水屋敷 in Edo Castle on 11月15日 (1861-12-16), and the 本丸大奥 on 12月11日 (1862-01-10).
  - **Wedding to 14th shōgun 徳川家茂: 文久2年2月11日 = 1862-03-11 (G).** English Wikipedia writes "11 February 1862", which is the lunar date mislabelled as Gregorian.
  - Iemochi and Kazunomiya were the same age. The marriage was the showpiece of 公武合体 (kōbu gattai). Rōjū 安藤信正 drove it, and 岩倉具視 argued for it at court.
- **Widowhood.**
  - Iemochi died at Osaka Castle on 慶応2年7月20日 (1866-08-29).
  - She took the tonsure on 12月9日 (1867-01-14) and became **静寛院宮** (せいかんいんのみや).
- **Tōsei Daisōtoku.** The office was **東征大総督**; its headquarters was the **東征大総督府**. The prompt's "Tōsei Daisōtokufu" names the headquarters, not the office Taruhito held.
  - Office established and Taruhito appointed: 慶応4年2月9日 (**1868-03-02**). He held it together with his post as 総裁 of the new government.
  - 2月15日 (1868-03-08): Emperor Meiji gave him the 節刀 and 錦旗, and he left Kyoto down the Tōkaidō. 西郷隆盛 was 下参謀.
  - Edo Castle was handed over on 4月11日 (1868-05-03). He entered Edo on 4月14日 (05-06) and the castle on 4月21日 (05-13).
  - He also became 会津征討大総督 on 5月19日. He was relieved as 東征大総督 on 10月28日 (1868-12-11).
- **Kazunomiya in 1868.**
  - She sent her lady 土御門藤子 to Kyoto with petitions to preserve the Tokugawa house. Fujiko reached Kyoto on 2月8日.
  - On 3月10日 and 11日 she sent envoys to the advancing army asking it to delay the attack.
  - Kotobank (Nipponica) says she petitioned "on the advance of 東征大総督有栖川宮熾仁親王".
  - The romantic image of Taruhito confronting his former fiancée is later embroidery. Japanese Wikipedia notes it was spread by Meiji novels and kōdan.
- **Death.** 明治10年 (1877) **9月2日**, at 箱根塔ノ沢 (Hakone), where she was being treated for beriberi. Cause: 脚気衝心 (beriberi heart failure). Age 32 by kazoedoshi (31 by Western count).
  - Buried at 増上寺 beside Iemochi. Posthumously raised to 一品 in 1883.
- Related: Taruhito died at the Arisugawa villa in 舞子 on **1895-01-15**. His death was officially announced as 1月24日, the day the body reached Tokyo, which is why Britannica/Kotobank give 1895.1.24. He then had a state funeral.

## 2. Arisugawa-no-miya extinct 1913; Takamatsu-no-miya inherits rites — **TRUE** (with nuances)

What is true:
- 10th and last head: **有栖川宮威仁親王** (たけひと). Born 文久2年1月13日 = 1862-02-11.
- His only son 栽仁王 (たねひと) died on 1908-04-07 while a naval cadet. The 1889 Imperial House Law (皇室典範, art. 42) banned adoption, so the house could not continue.
- **Death date has two versions.**
  - Actual: **大正2年 (1913) 7月5日**, at the 舞子 villa near Kobe (Kotobank 日本人名大辞典; English Wikipedia).
  - Official: **7月10日** 20:20. The body was brought to Tokyo "still gravely ill", and the death was announced as occurring at the Tokyo residence (宮内省告示第10号, 官報号外 1913-07-10).
  - Current Japanese Wikipedia follows the official date. A hidden editor's note there calls the 7月5日 date unsourced, but Kotobank gives it.
  - He was made Marshal-Admiral (元帥) on 7月7日, which fell between the two dates.
- **Takamatsu title.** On **1913-07-06** (宮内省告示第8号) Emperor Taishō's third son, 光宮**宣仁親王**, received the title **高松宮** to carry on the Arisugawa rites (祭祀) and property. 高松宮 was the Arisugawa house's original 1625 name.
  - Prince Nobuhito later married 徳川喜久子, Takehito's granddaughter through his daughter 實枝子女王.
- **Formal end of the house.** The house formally became extinct (絶家) only with the death of Takehito's widow **威仁親王妃慰子** on **1923-06-30**. She was the last Arisugawa imperial-family member.
  - "Extinct 1913" is the standard shorthand. Kotobank says 「大正2年（1913）威仁親王死去により廃絶」.
- The 高松宮 house itself ended in 2004 with Princess Kikuko's death.

## 3. Saionji Kinmochi, France, 東洋自由新聞, naichoku, 1884 rank — **TRUE**

What is true:
- **西園寺公望** (さいおんじ きんもち). Born 嘉永2年10月23日 (1849-12-07). He was the second son of 徳大寺公純; his eldest brother 徳大寺実則 was 宮内卿 in 1881. He was adopted into the 西園寺 house, a 清華家.
- **France.**
  - He left Japan at government expense in 明治3年12月 (lunar), i.e. January–February 1871 (G).
  - He reached Paris in spring 1871, during the Commune, which he disliked.
  - He studied at the Sorbonne, including under Émile Acollas. He befriended Clemenceau and Gambetta, and among Japanese students 中江兆民, 松田正久 and 光妙寺三郎.
  - He returned on **明治13年 (1880) 10月21日**. "c. 1871–1880" is correct.
  - NDL's text dates his departure "明治3（1870）年", which is the era year.
- **東洋自由新聞** (とうようじゆうしんぶん).
  - Founded **明治14年 (1881) 3月18日** as a daily in Tokyo.
  - Staff: **社長 西園寺公望**, 主筆 **中江兆民**, 副社長 飯塚西湖 (納), 幹事 松田正久; also 松沢求策, 光妙寺三郎 and others. Its line was French-style liberal people's-rights thought (自由民権).
  - The prompt's "president" is right. One Heibonsha blurb calls him 社長兼主筆, but all other entries give 中江 as 主筆.
- **Forced resignation.**
  - Court and government (岩倉具視, 三条実美, and his brother 徳大寺実則) pressed him to quit. In March he was given an imperial 内諭 (informal hint) and refused it.
  - On **4月8日** he was summoned to the 宮内省 and given an imperial **内勅** (ないちょく, confidential imperial order) through his brother, the 宮内卿. He gave in.
  - Kotobank: 「4月8日明治天皇の内勅によって」. Japanese Wikipedia (東洋自由新聞) gives his resignation as 4月9日.
  - 松沢求策 published a broadside exposing the 内勅 and was arrested.
  - The paper folded with **No. 34 on 4月30日 1881** (editorial 「東洋自由新聞顛覆す」).
- **1884 peerage.** On **明治17年 (1884) 7月7日**, under the 華族令 (Peerage Act), he was made **侯爵 (marquis)** (官報 第307号, 1884-07-08). He was raised to 公爵 (prince/duke) on 1920-09-07.
- Related: on 1881-11-24 he became 参事院議官補 under Itō Hirobumi, which began his career as Itō's protégé.

## 4. Women's names in Meiji koseki written in kana; Tsuda Umeko うめ→梅子 1902 — **PARTLY**

What is true:
- **General practice: TRUE.** Two-mora kana names were extremely common for commoner women in Meiji registers. Kanji names also occurred.
  - **明治安田生命, girls' names by birth year:** for 1912 births (明治45/大正元) the top ten are 千代, **ハル, ハナ**, 正子, 文子, **ヨシ**, 千代子, **キヨ**, 静子, **はる**. The katakana ハル and hiragana はる are counted as separate names, which shows the registered spelling. For 1913: 正子, 千代, 静子, キヨ, 文子, ヨシ, ハル, フミ, マサ, きみ.
    - Caveat: this ranking was compiled in 1989 from policyholders' names.
  - **三浦直人, Meiji University doctoral dissertation (2022), ch. 3.** Women's names of the period shifted between kanji and kana and added or dropped お and 子. A woman signing herself 〈春子〉 might be registered as はる, ハル, 春, はる子, ハル子 or 春子. He also notes that 子 was often a polite suffix rather than part of the registered name.
  - **尾脇秀和『女の氏名誕生』(ちくま新書 2024)** has sections titled 「女性名の片仮名化と識字率」, 「戸籍名『何子』の流行」 and 「『子』は付けなくていい」. These show the kana-ization of registered women's names is an established topic. I saw the table of contents only, not the text.
  - **Named examples.**
    - 与謝野晶子: real name 鳳**志やう** (しょう), kanji mixed with kana.
    - 瓜生繁子: birth name 益田**しげ**.
    - 大山捨松: childhood name 山川**さき**, later 咲子.
    - Counter-example in kanji: 樋口一葉 was registered as 奈津 but signed なつ, 夏 or 夏子.
- **Tsuda Umeko: partly.**
  - 津田梅子 was born 元治元年12月3日 (1864-12-31). Her name was **うめ, written むめ** (Japanese Wikipedia citing 山崎孝子 1988; Kotobank-Nipponica 「名はむめ」; 人物レファレンス lists 「津田 むめ」).
  - **When she changed it is disputed.**
    - Nipponica (Kotobank): **1900 (明治33)**.
    - Japanese Wikipedia (citing 橘木俊詔 2022) and the Waraku article: **1902 (明治35)**, when she left her father 津田仙's register (分籍) and set up her own. Japanese Wikipedia romanizes the registered name only as "UME", so the exact kana form in the register is not shown.
  - **"ムメ" in katakana is unverified.** No source I opened shows katakana.
  - **She used 梅子 publicly long before the formal change.** The 官報 of 1885-09-22 already lists 「宮内省御用掛…津田梅子」. Her English books and her gravestone say "Ume Tsuda".
  - Safe wording: "born うめ (written むめ); used 梅子 from the 1880s; formally registered as 梅子 c. 1900–1902."

## 5. 少女 read をとめ in Meiji; しょうじょ standard only c. 1900s via girls' magazines — **PARTLY**

What is true:
- **Both readings are old.**
  - 少女 has been a standard kanji spelling of **をとめ** since the Heian period. Per 日本国語大辞典's usage note, 「をとめ」も「少女」と記され. Kotobank's 精選版日本国語大辞典 files 「おとめ【少女・乙女】」 under the kanji 少女.
  - The Sino-Japanese reading **せうぢよ** is also old: 日本三代実録 867, and the ritsuryō age class 少女 (ages 17–20) in a 702 register.
  - So the Meiji choice of reading was stylistic (poetic/elegant をとめ versus plain せうぢよ), not chronological.
- **Original ruby in Meiji magazines (my counts from the NINJAL corpora):**

  | Source | 少女(をとめ) | 少女(せうぢよ) | other | no ruby |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 国民之友 1887 no.7, 湯浅半月 poem 「都の夢」 | 少女子《をとめご》 ×1 | – | – | 少女 otherwise unrubied |
  | 女学雑誌 1895 | 2 (島崎藤村「亡友反古帖」; 哀蝶「行く秋のおもひ」) | 2 (桜井鴎村「悪年少と家庭」; 天仙子「佳人の四季」) | – | 19 |
  | 女学世界 1909 | 11 | 20 | をんな 1 | 26 |
  | 婦人倶楽部 1925 | 8 | 16 | – | 18 |

  The しょうじょ reading was already in print by 1895, before any girls' magazine existed. It became the majority ruby by 1909, but をとめ ruby persisted into the 1920s, especially in literary and lyrical passages.
- **Girls' magazines fixed 少女 (しょうじょ) as the social category** of schoolgirl/adolescent girl:
  - 博文館『少年世界』 (from 1895) had girls' columns;
  - 『少女界』 (金港堂, 1902) was the first girls' magazine;
  - then 『少女世界』 (博文館, 1906), 『少女の友』 (実業之日本社, 1908), 『少女画報』 (1912).
  - Imada's thesis concerns the category, not the furigana. 今田絵里香『「少女」の社会史』 (勁草書房 2007), ch. 1 「『少女』の誕生――少女雑誌以前」, shows that 「少年」 in the 1870s–80s children's magazine 『穎才新誌』 covered girls as well. 「少女」 then split off as a gendered category tied to girls' secondary schooling and the urban new middle class (also 今田『「少年」「少女」の誕生』 2019, per 福田委千代's review).
- **Ōgai's 舞姫 (1890): don't cite the をとめ ruby as original.** The ja.wikisource transcription of 『水沫集』 (春陽堂 1892) prints 少女 without ruby. Aozora's modern editions add 少女《をとめ》 (現代日本文學大系) or 《おとめ》 (Shūeisha, Iwanami). Those are editors' readings.
- **Not found:** a statement by Honda Masuko or Imada Erika specifically about the をとめ/しょうじょ furigana. Treat any such attribution as UNVERIFIED.

## 6. 鳳雛 ほうすう = Pang Tong; 落鳳坡; Three Kingdoms known in Meiji — **TRUE**

What is true:
- **鳳雛** is read **ほうすう** (日本国語大辞典 also lists ほうす). It means a phoenix chick, i.e. a promising youth. Earliest Japanese example in NKD: 服部誠一『東京新繁昌記』 (1874–76).
- **The sobriquet is historical, not invented by the novel.**
  - 三国志 巻37 龐統伝, 裴松之注, quoting 『襄陽記』: 「諸葛孔明為臥龍，龐士元為鳳雛，司馬德操為水鏡」.
  - 巻35 注 has 司馬徽's 「此間自有伏龍、鳳雛」.
  - Japanese dictionaries give the idiom as **伏竜鳳雛**. 臥龍鳳雛 is also attested.
  - 龐統 (ほうとう), courtesy name 士元 (しげん), 179–214.
- **落鳳坡 is in the novel, not the history.**
  - Historically 龐統 was killed by a stray arrow (流矢) while besieging 雒県, aged 36.
  - In 『三国志演義』 he dies at **落鳳坡**. Mao edition ch. 63: 「此處地名落鳳坡」 / 「吾道號鳳雛，此處名落鳳坡，不利於吾」.
  - In the 嘉靖本 (1522) the section is titled 「落鳳坡箭射龐統」 (則125).
  - The Japanese 『通俗三国志』 was translated from the 李卓吾本, which belongs to the same textual family, so the episode should be there. I did not open the 通俗三国志 text itself.
  - A real place called 落鳳坡 with a 龐統 tomb exists today at 白馬関鎮, 羅江区, 徳陽, Sichuan.
- **The story was very widely known in Meiji.**
  - 湖南文山訳『通俗三国志』 (元禄2–5年, 1689–92) was the standard Japanese version for two centuries. 『絵本通俗三国志』 (天保7–12年, 1836–41, illustrated by 葛飾戴斗二世) added pictures.
  - NDL catalogue records for the Meiji period include:
    - 絵本通俗三国志: 1882 (著作館/清水市次郎, plates by 月岡芳年), 1883 (東京同益出版社), 1884 (菱花堂, 17 vols), 1887 (青柳国松), 1888 (銀花堂);
    - 『三国志銘々義伝』 (東洲堂 1883);
    - 『絵本三国志小伝』 (菱花堂 1885, 芳年);
    - **博文館『校訂通俗三国志』 上下 (1893, 帝国文庫)**.

## 7. 彼女 originally かのをんな, かのじょ later — **PARTLY (mostly wrong on timing)**

What is true:
- 彼女 was coined as a translation of Western third-person feminine pronouns (she, elle). Earlier Japanese used かれ for both sexes.
- デジタル大辞泉: 「西欧語からの訳語『かのおんな』の『おんな』を音読した語」. So a かのをんな stage existed conceptually.
- **But the カノジョ reading is attested very early:**
  - 日本国語大辞典: 明治9年 (1876) 『改正画引小学読本』 「彼女 カノジョ ムカウニヰルムスメ」;
  - 坪内逍遥『当世書生気質』 (1885–86) 「彼女(カノジョ)は活溌だ」.
  - NKD's usage note: at first it was felt as カノ+ジョ rather than one word; **as a one-word pronoun it spread widely only from the Taishō era**.
- **Original ruby (NINJAL corpora):**

  | Source | 彼女(かのぢよ) | other |
  |---|---|---|
  | 女学雑誌 1894 | **15** (e.g. 巌本善治) | – |
  | 女学雑誌 1895 | 2 | the form with の: 彼《か》の女《ぢよ》 1, 彼《か》の女《をんな》 1, 彼《あ》の女《ひと》 1 |
  | 女学世界 1909 | 4 | 彼《か》の女《ぢよ》 31, 彼女《かれ》 3 |
  | 婦人倶楽部 1925 | **209** | 彼女《かれ》 9 |

  国民之友 1888 has unrubied 彼女 ×7 and 彼の女 ×8. 二葉亭四迷『浮雲』 (1887–89) glosses 彼女 as 《あれ》.
- So in 1880s–90s print the ruby for 彼女 is almost always **かのぢよ**. **かのをんな mostly goes with the spelling 彼の女**, and other glosses (かれ, あれ) also occur.
- I found no rubied 彼女《かのをんな》 in the material checked. A 1880s author writing 彼女 would most plausibly gloss it かのぢよ, or write 彼の女 for かのをんな.

## 8. 加哩 / カレイ / カリー; first curry recipes 1872 — **PARTLY**

What is true:
- **The first recipes are confirmed.** Both books were published in **明治5年 (1872)**:
  - **敬学堂主人『西洋料理指南』** (東京, 雁金屋). Its curry uses 葱, 生姜, 蒜, 牛酪 (butter), 鶏, 海老, 鯛, 蠣, **赤蛙** (red frog), 小麦粉, and 「**カレー**」ノ粉.
  - **仮名垣魯文『西洋料理通』**, based on a Yokohama Englishman's notes. Its recipe uses cold leftover mutton, 葱, ボートル, 「**カリー粉**」 and flour, served ringed with rice.
- **First appearance of the word:** 福沢諭吉『増訂華英通語』 (1860), 「Curry **コルリ**」.
- **Other attested spellings:**
  - 1872 開拓使 records: 「タイスカリイ」 (i.e. ライスカリイ);
  - the Sapporo "らいすかれい" rule attributed to Clark (1876), which is traditional and not documented;
  - 1875 三田村多仲日誌: 「カレーライス」;
  - 『海軍割烹術参考書』 (1908): 「**カレイ**ライス」, 「カレイ粉」;
  - 『軍隊料理法』 (1910): 「カレー、ライス」;
  - 正岡子規's diary (1901): 「ライスカレー」;
  - 女学世界 1909: 「チキンカレー」, 「カレー粉」 (my corpus check).
- **加哩: UNVERIFIED for Meiji Japan.** It is a Chinese-style phonetic spelling (modern Chinese 咖喱, older 咖哩/加哩). I found no Meiji Japanese attestation: NDL catalogue searches returned 0 records, the corpora had no hits, and web searches found nothing. Use katakana (カレー, カリー, カレイ) for an 1880s Tokyo text. If 加哩 is used, mark it as invented.
- Related:
  - 1873: the Army's 幼年学校 served ライスカレー on Saturdays.
  - 1877: 風月堂 put it on its menu.
  - 1905: first domestic curry powder (蜂カレー, 大阪).
  - In the 1880s curry was a pricey Western-restaurant dish, not yet popular food.

## 9. 接吻 (seppun) as the Meiji word for kiss — **PARTLY** (right word, wrong origin story)

What is true:
- 接吻 was **not coined in Meiji**.
  - It is a Chinese word, found in Chinese Bible translations and vernacular novels.
  - It was first used in Japan for Dutch *kus* in 『道訳法児馬』 (Doeff Halma), dated 1815 by NKD and 文化13年 (1816) by Japanese Wikipedia.
  - 『和蘭字彙』 (1855–58) has 「Kussen 接吻スル」.
- **In Meiji it became the standard translation of English *kiss*.**
  - NKD's first Meiji example: 河島敬蔵訳『春情浮世の夢』 (1886, a Romeo and Juliet adaptation).
  - NKD: 「明治二十年代には、翻訳物だけでなく創作にも用いられるようになった」 (by the Meiji 20s it appeared in original fiction as well as translations).
  - Japanese Wikipedia (接吻) dates its establishment as the kiss-word to c. 明治20年 (1887).
- **Attestations I checked:**
  - 国民之友 1888: an editorial, and 二葉亭四迷訳 「あひびき（二）」 (no. 27): 「おづおづ接吻した」;
  - 『浮雲』 第2篇 (1888): 接吻《せっぷん》 (modern-kana edition);
  - 女学雑誌 1894–95: 接《せつ》吻《ぷん》 ×4 (巌本善治, 清水紫琴, 白衣子), plus **接吻《キツス》** (1895);
  - 女学世界 1909: 接吻 glossed せつぷん, **キス** and **くちづけ**.
- Older native words were 口吸ひ (くちすひ) and, colloquially, 口口 and 呂. くちづけ in the "kiss" sense dates only from Meiji (Nipponica).
- Usage note for the 1880s: 接吻 was a bookish, translated-novel word, often glossed キッス.

## 10. 黙示録 in the 1880 Meiji New Testament — **TRUE**

What is true:
- The 明治元訳 New Testament was finished by the 翻訳委員社中: Hepburn, S. R. Brown and D. C. Greene, with 奥野昌綱, 松山高吉 and 高橋五郎.
  - Translation work ended on 1879-11-03.
  - The last fascicles came out in **明治13年 (1880)**, among them **『約翰黙示録』**. The whole was issued as 『新約全書』, and a completion celebration was held in April 1880.
- **NDL bibliographic records for the 1880 fascicle:**
  - 『新約聖書約翰黙示録 全』, 横濱: 米國聖書會社, 1880, 46丁, 23 cm, 和装;
  - 版心 (running title in the fold): **新約全書約翰黙示録**; 見返し (endpaper): 「翻訳委員社中」;
  - reading given as **シンヤク セイショ ヨハネ モクシロク**.
- So the title was **約翰黙示録 (ヨハネもくしろく)**, with no の. The later form ヨハネの黙示録 / 默示録 belongs to the 1917 大正改訳.
- The original woodblock probably used the traditional glyph **默** (catalogues normalize to 黙). 默示録 itself comes from the earlier Chinese Protestant Bibles (約翰默示錄).
- Related: the Old Testament (旧約全書) was completed in 1887, giving the complete 明治元訳 Bible.
